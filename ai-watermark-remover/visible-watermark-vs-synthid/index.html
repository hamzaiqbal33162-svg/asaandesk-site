Visible watermark vs SynthID vs Content Credentials

AI pictures and videos can carry three very different kinds of label. Only one of them is the sparkle you can see, and it is the only one these tools touch.

Three different things

People often say "the AI watermark" as if there were one. AI pictures and videos from Google can carry three separate labels, made in three different ways, and they behave very differently when you edit a file. Knowing which is which explains what a watermark remover can and cannot do.

The visible sparkle

The sparkle is a small four-point star in the bottom-right corner of pictures from Gemini and Nano Banana and of videos from Veo and Flow. It is part of the pixels, blended in at a known strength, so anyone can see it and anyone can crop it out. Because its exact shape and strength are known, it can also be reversed precisely, which is what these tools do.

Its job is to tell a human viewer at a glance that the picture was made with AI. It carries no hidden data and no signature.

SynthID

SynthID is a watermark made by Google DeepMind that you cannot see. It is woven into the pixels of a picture, the frames of a video or the sound of audio while the content is generated, as tiny changes spread over the whole file. It is designed to survive common edits such as resizing, cropping, compression and colour changes.

Only special software can check for it; Google and some partners offer such checks. Removing the visible sparkle does not remove SynthID, and these tools make no attempt to. A picture cleaned here can still be identified as made with Google AI.

Content Credentials (C2PA)

Content Credentials follow an open standard (C2PA). They are a signed record inside the file's metadata that says which app made or changed the file and how, for example that it was created with a generative AI model. Apps and websites that support the standard can show it, and the signature shows whether the record was changed.

Because they live in metadata, Content Credentials disappear whenever a file is saved again without them, which happens with most editing apps and with these tools, since the cleaned picture is a new PNG. Your original file keeps them. You can check a file with our C2PA Checker.

What this means for you

  • Removing the sparkle changes how the picture looks in one corner, nothing more.
  • The picture is still AI-made and can still be recognised as such through SynthID.
  • Any duty to label AI media, from a law or a platform, stays exactly the same.
  • If you need proof of where a picture came from, keep the original file with its Content Credentials.
