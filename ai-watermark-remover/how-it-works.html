AI WATERMARK REMOVER · HOW IT WORKS

How the AI watermark remover works

The sparkle is not painted over the picture: it is blended in at a known strength. Knowing that strength exactly lets the tool undo it, pixel by pixel. This page explains each step and every check that can stop it.

The sparkle is blended, not painted

Gemini does not cover a patch of your picture. It mixes a white four-point star into the existing pixels, more strongly in the middle of the star and very lightly at its soft edges. For every pixel of the mark there is a fixed strength, called alpha, between 0 (no mark) and about 0.5 (half white). The new pixel is alpha × white + (1 − alpha) × the original.

Because the strength of every pixel is known, the formula can be turned around: the original = (new pixel − alpha × white) ÷ (1 − alpha). This is reverse alpha blending. It is not a guess or a fill: when the mark and its place are right, it gives back the original colours, apart from rounding by one or two levels.

The strengths come from calibration: the mark was captured on plain backgrounds, which shows its exact shape and strength. These calibrated masks, for the 48 and 96 pixel marks and two newer variants, are part of the open-source project our engine is based on (see the credits page).

Finding the mark from the picture size

Gemini makes pictures in a fixed list of sizes, and each size has its mark at a fixed place: a 48 pixel sparkle 32 pixels from the right and bottom edges on smaller outputs, a 96 pixel sparkle 64 pixels in on 2K and 4K outputs, and a few measured variants. The tool reads your picture's size, looks up every place the mark can be, and also tries a few pixels around each place in case the picture was padded or cropped by a pixel.

If the size is not on the list but is a scaled copy of a Gemini size, the places are scaled too. For other sizes the usual rule is tried, and the corner is searched for other sizes and distances, with a much stricter bar before anything changes.

Checking the evidence before changing anything

At each place the corner is compared with the mask in two ways: its brightness pattern (does it brighten exactly where the star is strong?) and its edges (are there edges exactly along the star's outline?). Both scores run from −1 to 1. The tool only goes on when both are clearly high, using the thresholds of the original project.

Then the mark is removed on a copy and checked again: the star's brightness pattern and edges must be gone, the corner must not become darker or blacker than before, and no visible star-shaped rest may remain. A white circle, a different logo or a texture that happens to look a little like the star fails these checks, and the picture is returned untouched with the message "No visible Gemini mark found. Your image was not changed."

Some pictures went through Gemini twice and carry the mark twice. When the edges of the star are still clearly there after one pass, a second pass is tried, and kept only if it takes them away.

Videos: the same idea on every frame

For a video, eight frames spread over its length are read from the corner. The tool looks at the places where Veo, the Gemini app and Flow put the sparkle for that video size, and picks the place that wins on most frames. It also measures how strong the mark is on each frame (compression can soften it) and uses the middle value.

Only when the mark is clearly there on the samples, and is present on every sample that has detail in the corner, is every frame cleaned. This runs inside ffmpeg.wasm, the browser version of the ffmpeg video tool: the corner is cut out, the reverse blending is applied with the measured mask, the corner is put back, and the video is saved as H.264 MP4. AAC and MP3 sound is copied as it is; other sound is converted to AAC.

Before you get the file, three frames of the finished video are checked once more. If the mark still shows, you get no file and the message says so. Compression can leave a very faint outline where the edges of the mark were; that is the limit of cleaning a video that was already compressed.

What stays the same, and what does not leave your device

Every pixel outside the mark is copied unchanged. Pictures are saved as PNG unless you ask for their own type. Nothing is uploaded: you can check in your browser's developer tools (Network tab) that no request carries your file. The only outside download is the video engine from unpkg.com, the first time you clean a video.

It removes only the visible Gemini sparkle drawn in the corner. It does not remove SynthID (the invisible watermark Google adds to the pixels), Content Credentials (C2PA), or anyone else's logo or ownership mark, and the result is still AI-made.
