What this income tax calculator works out

This income tax calculator estimates your US federal income tax for tax years 2025, 2026 and 2027, following the steps of IRS Form 1040. It tells you whether to expect a refund or a bill when you file, how much federal tax you pay in total, and which bracket your last dollar of income falls into.

Your income tax return is the yearly Form 1040 on which you report what you earned and settle the difference between the tax already withheld from your pay and the tax you really owe. The calculator does the same sums in advance, so you can plan withholding, estimated payments or a raise before the year ends.

It suits employees with a W-2, married couples, freelancers and sole traders with self-employment income, retirees with Social Security, and investors with dividends or capital gains. Every figure comes from the IRS revenue procedure for the year and from the Social Security Administration.

It is not a state, Pakistan, India or UK tax calculator. If you came here for Pakistan's salary tax, the section on FBR salary slabs further down explains the tax year 2027 rates from the Federal Board of Revenue. Tax rules change almost every year: the amounts here are those published by November 2026, including the 2027 figures the IRS released on 5 November 2026, and a later law could still change them.

How to use the calculator, step by step

Only the first block is needed for a quick estimate. The collapsed sections open when you need them, and any box left at zero is simply ignored.

  • Tax year: choose the year the income was earned. 2025 is the return filed in 2026, 2026 is filed in 2027, and 2027 is for planning ahead.
  • Filing status: one of the five IRS statuses (single, a joint or a separate return for a married couple, head of household, or qualifying surviving spouse). A joint return adds boxes for your spouse's wages and withholding.
  • Your wages for the year: box 1 of your W-2, which is already after pre-tax 401(k) and health insurance deductions. Federal income tax withheld is box 2, plus any other federal withholding.
  • Children under 17 and other dependents: they decide the child tax credit and the $500 credit for older children, parents or relatives you support.
  • Payments and ages: your age (and your spouse's) at the end of the year, which matters at 65 or older, and any estimated tax you paid with Form 1040-ES or carried over from last year's refund.
  • Self-employment: your net business profit from Schedule C (a loss as a minus number) and, if W-2 box 5 differs from box 1, your Medicare wages.
  • Other income: taxable interest, ordinary dividends and the qualified part of them, short- and long-term capital gains or losses, rent and royalties, other taxable income such as unemployment pay or IRA withdrawals, and the full Social Security benefits you received.
  • Adjustments and deductions: IRA contributions, student loan interest, qualified tips, the overtime premium, car loan interest on a new US-assembled car, your state and local income tax (as a rate or the amount withheld), property tax, mortgage interest, gifts to charity and other itemized costs.
  • Credits: child and dependent care costs, and college costs for up to four students with the choice of the American opportunity or the lifetime learning credit.

You never have to decide between the standard deduction and itemizing: the calculator works out both and uses the larger one.

Understanding your results

The big number is your estimated refund, or the tax you still owe, once everything withheld or paid during the year is counted. Next to it, total tax is your whole federal bill after credits and before payments.

2026 federal income tax brackets and how they work

There are seven federal rates: 10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35% and 37%. Each rate is charged only on the slice of taxable income inside its band. When a raise pushes you into a higher bracket, only the extra dollars pay the higher rate, so earning more never leaves you with less after tax.

Deductions: standard, itemized and the new 2025 to 2028 ones

A deduction lowers the income that is taxed; it does not cut the tax itself dollar for dollar. A $1,000 deduction saves $120 for someone in the 12% bracket and $240 in the 24% bracket. Deductions come in layers:

  • Adjustments, taken before AGI whether or not you itemize: half of self-employment tax, deductible IRA contributions and up to $2,500 of student loan interest.
  • The standard deduction, a flat amount for your filing status (table below), with an extra amount for each person 65 or older.
  • Itemized deductions, used instead of the standard one when they add up to more: state and local taxes, mortgage interest, gifts to charity, and medical costs above 7.5% of AGI.
  • The 2025 to 2028 deductions, allowed with either choice: qualified tips up to $25,000, the overtime premium up to $12,500 ($25,000 joint), up to $10,000 of interest on a loan for a new car assembled in the US, and $6,000 for each person 65 or older.
  • The qualified business income deduction: up to 20% of self-employment profit, but no more than 20% of taxable income before it.

State and local taxes (income or sales tax plus property tax) are capped at $40,000 for 2025, $40,400 for 2026 and $40,804 for 2027, or half that if married filing separately. The cap shrinks by 30% of the income above $500,000 (2025), $505,000 (2026) or $510,050 (2027), but never below $10,000.

The new deductions fade out at higher incomes. Tips and overtime drop by $100 for every $1,000 of modified AGI above $150,000 ($300,000 joint), car loan interest by $200 per $1,000 above $100,000 ($200,000 joint), and the senior deduction by 6% of income above $75,000 ($150,000 joint). Married people must file jointly to claim the tips, overtime and senior deductions.

Charity rules changed for 2026: people who do not itemize can deduct up to $1,000 of cash gifts ($2,000 joint), while itemizers count only the part of their gifts above 0.5% of AGI. People in the 37% bracket also get a slightly smaller benefit from itemizing from 2026.

Tax credits: child, care, college and earned income

A credit comes straight off your tax, so it is worth more than a deduction of the same size. Non-refundable credits can bring your income tax down to zero but no further; refundable credits are paid to you even when you owe nothing.

The child tax credit and the credit for other dependents shrink together by $50 for every $1,000 of income above $200,000, or $400,000 on a joint return. The refundable part is 15% of earned income above $2,500, up to the cap in the table.

The child and dependent care credit is a percentage of your care costs, counting at most $3,000 when one child or dependent is cared for and $6,000 when there are several. For 2025 the rate falls from 35% to 20% as income rises. From 2026 it starts at 50% for the lowest incomes, is 35% from about $45,000 of AGI and falls toward 20% above $75,000 ($150,000 joint).

For college, the American opportunity credit is worth up to $2,500 per student in the first four years (all of the first $2,000 of costs and 25% of the next $2,000), and up to 40% of it is refundable. The lifetime learning credit is 20% of up to $10,000 of costs per return, so at most $2,000, and is not refundable.

The earned income credit helps low and moderate earners: it grows with earnings, levels off, then phases out, and the calculator applies the IRS figures for your number of children. It is lost when investment income passes $12,200 in 2026.

Social Security, Medicare and the other taxes in your total

Employees pay 7.65% of wages in payroll tax, often called FICA: 6.2% for Social Security on wages up to the yearly wage base, and 1.45% for Medicare with no limit. The employer pays the same again. The wage base is $176,100 for 2025, $184,500 for 2026 and $192,300 for 2027, according to the Social Security Administration.

An employee's FICA is withheld separately; it is not part of the income tax shown here and it does not lower taxable income. To see it on each payslip, use the Take-Home Paycheck Calculator. The calculator does add these other federal taxes to your total:

  • Self-employment tax: 15.3% (12.4% for Social Security up to the wage base, plus 2.9% for Medicare) on 92.35% of your net profit, once that base reaches $400. Half of it is deducted before AGI.
  • Additional Medicare tax: 0.9% on wages and self-employment income above $200,000 ($250,000 joint, $125,000 married filing separately). Employers start withholding it once your wages pass $200,000.
  • Net investment income tax: 3.8% of the smaller of your interest, dividends, gains and rent, or the amount by which your income passes the same $200,000, $250,000 or $125,000 thresholds.
  • Alternative minimum tax: a second calculation with an exemption of $90,100 (single) or $140,200 (joint) in 2026; you pay the difference only when it comes out higher than regular tax. Here it adds back state and local taxes but no other AMT items.

The 0.9% and 3.8% thresholds are written into the law and are not raised for inflation, so more households reach them as pay grows.

Worked examples

Each example uses the IRS figures for 2026 unless it says otherwise, and you can repeat it by typing the same values into the calculator.

  • Single, $65,000 in wages, $6,500 withheld: $65,000 − $16,100 = $48,900 taxable. Tax is $1,240 + 12% of $36,500 ($4,380) = $5,620, an effective rate of 8.6%, so the refund is $880. The same wages owed $5,749 in 2025 (that year the last slice was taxed at 22%) and owe $5,552 in 2027.
  • Married filing jointly, $120,000 in wages, two children under 17, $6,000 withheld: taxable income is $120,000 − $32,200 = $87,800. Tax is $2,480 + 12% of $63,000 ($7,560) = $10,040, and the child tax credit of 2 × $2,200 brings it to $5,640, a refund of $360.
  • Head of household, $58,000 in wages, one child, $4,000 of day care costs, $3,000 withheld: taxable income is $33,850 and tax before credits is $3,708. The $2,200 child tax credit and a $1,050 care credit (35% of the first $3,000 of costs) leave $458, so the refund is $2,542.
  • Single freelancer with $60,000 of profit, no wages and $10,000 of estimated payments: self-employment tax is $60,000 × 92.35% × 15.3% = $8,477.73. Half of it brings AGI to $55,761; after the $16,100 standard deduction and a $7,932 qualified business income deduction, taxable income is $31,729 and income tax is $3,559. Total tax is $12,037, so $2,037 is still owed.
  • Single restaurant server with $52,000 in wages, of which $8,000 is tips and $3,000 is overtime premium, $4,000 withheld: both deductions come on top of the standard deduction, so taxable income is $24,900 and tax is $2,740 instead of $4,060. The refund is $1,260.

Looking for a Pakistan income tax calculator? How FBR salary slabs work

This page works out US federal tax only, so it cannot give a correct figure for salary taxed in Pakistan. Pakistan's income tax is set by the Income Tax Ordinance, 2001, and the rates change with each year's Finance Act. Below is a short guide to the salary rates, taken from the Federal Board of Revenue (FBR).

A Pakistani tax year runs from 1 July to 30 June and is named after the year in which it ends, so tax year 2027 is 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027, the year usually written as 2026-27. The salary table applies when salary is more than 75% of your taxable income, and your employer deducts the tax from each month's pay under section 149.

For tax year 2026 (2025-26) the first three slabs were the same, but the rates above Rs 2,200,000 were 23%, 30% and 35%, with 35% starting at Rs 4,100,000, and salaried people paid a 9% surcharge on their tax once taxable income passed Rs 10 million. The Finance Act 2026 added the extra slabs and removed that surcharge for salaried individuals. The same Rs 6,000,000 salary paid Rs 1,281,000 in tax year 2026.

Business people and other non-salaried individuals use a different, higher table. For filing a return or any case beyond these slabs, check the FBR's own guidance or ask a tax adviser.

Tips, common mistakes and related calculators

  • Use W-2 box 1, not your salary before 401(k) and health deductions, or the estimate will come out too high.
  • Do not take Social Security and Medicare off your wages yourself; employees cannot deduct them.
  • Qualified dividends are part of ordinary dividends (box 1b sits inside box 1a), so enter the full box 1a amount in the first box.
  • Tips and overtime you enter should already be inside your wages; the deduction takes them out again, so do not add them to wages a second time.
  • State income tax is extra. As of 2026, eight states, including Texas, Florida, Nevada and Tennessee, have no individual income tax, Washington taxes only capital gains, California's top rate is 13.3%, and ten states also allow county or city income taxes.
  • If you expect to owe, change your withholding with a new Form W-4 or make estimated payments during the year rather than waiting for April.

How it works

Pick the tax year and your filing status, then enter your wages and the federal tax already withheld. Open the sections below for a spouse, business profit, investment income, deductions and credits. The calculator follows the Form 1040 steps: it adds up your income, works out how much of any Social Security is taxable, takes off adjustments (half of self-employment tax, IRA, student loan interest) to reach adjusted gross income, then takes the larger of the standard deduction or your itemized deductions, plus the 2025 to 2028 deductions for tips, overtime, car loan interest and people 65 or older, and the qualified business income deduction. Tax on the rest uses the brackets for your status, with the 0%, 15% and 20% rates on qualified dividends and long-term gains. It adds the alternative minimum tax, the 3.8% investment income tax, self-employment tax and the 0.9% Additional Medicare tax, takes off your credits, and compares the total with what you have paid.

The formula

  • Adjusted gross income = total income (with only the taxable part of Social Security) − half of self-employment tax − IRA − student loan interest
  • Taxable income = AGI − larger of (standard deduction + extra at 65+) or itemized deductions − tips, overtime, car loan interest and senior deductions − QBI deduction
  • Self-employment tax = 92.35% of profit × (12.4% up to the Social Security wage base + 2.9%)
  • Income tax = bracket tax on ordinary income + 0% / 15% / 20% on qualified dividends and long-term gains
  • Total tax = income tax + AMT + 3.8% NIIT + self-employment tax + 0.9% Additional Medicare tax − credits
  • Owe (or refund) = total tax − tax withheld − estimated payments

A worked example

A single filer earns $65,000 in wages in 2026 and has $6,500 withheld. Taxable income is $65,000 − $16,100 standard deduction = $48,900. Tax: 10% of $12,400 = $1,240, plus 12% of $36,500 = $4,380, so $5,620, and the refund is $880. The same wages in 2025 gave $5,749 of tax, because the 2026 bands and standard deduction are higher.

Questions

How do I calculate income tax on my salary?

Start with your yearly wages after pre-tax deductions (W-2 box 1) and subtract the standard deduction for your filing status, for example $16,100 for a single filer in 2026. Charge each bracket rate on the slice of income inside it, add the slices, then subtract credits such as the child tax credit. The calculator does every step and shows each bracket slice.

What is the income tax formula, with an example?

Tax = the sum of (income inside each bracket × that bracket's rate) − credits, where income means taxable income after deductions. For a single filer in 2026 with $30,000 of taxable income: 10% of $12,400 = $1,240 plus 12% of $17,600 = $2,112, so $3,352 before credits.

Is this an income tax calculator for Pakistan?

No, it works out US federal income tax only. Pakistan's salary tax follows the FBR slabs: for tax year 2027 (July 2026 to June 2027), salary up to Rs 600,000 a year is tax free and the rates rise from 1% to 35% above Rs 7,000,000. The Pakistan section above lists every slab with an example.

What is the income tax rate on salary in Pakistan for 2026-27?

Under the Finance Act 2026 there are eight salary slabs for tax year 2027, charged at 0%, 1%, 11%, 20%, 25%, 29%, 32% and 35% on the income above each slab's start, plus a fixed amount. For example, Rs 2,400,000 a year pays Rs 116,000 + 20% of Rs 200,000 = Rs 156,000. Check the FBR in case a later law changes the rates.

Which tax year should I pick?

Pick the year the income was earned. 2025 is the return you file in spring 2026, 2026 is the one you file in 2027, and 2027 lets you plan ahead with the figures the IRS has already published.

What is the difference between my marginal and effective tax rate?

The marginal rate is the bracket your last dollar of taxable income falls into, so it shows what tax you would pay on a raise or a bonus. The effective rate is your total tax divided by total income, which is always lower because the first slices are taxed at 10% and 12% and the standard deduction is not taxed at all.

Should I take the standard deduction or itemize?

Itemize only when your state and local taxes, mortgage interest, charity and large medical bills add up to more than the standard deduction for your status. Most filers come out ahead with the standard deduction. The calculator compares both and shows which one it used next to Deductions.

Are Social Security and Medicare taken off before income tax?

No. For employees, the 7.65% payroll tax is not deductible, so it does not lower taxable income. If you are self-employed, half of your self-employment tax is an adjustment, and self-employment tax itself is worked out on 92.35% of your profit.

How do the tips, overtime and senior deductions work?

From 2025 to 2028 you can deduct qualified tips up to $25,000, the extra half of overtime pay up to $12,500 ($25,000 on a joint return), interest on a loan for a new US-assembled car up to $10,000, and $6,000 for each person 65 or older. Each one shrinks at higher incomes, and you get them with the standard deduction too. Married people must file jointly for all but the car loan interest.

Can I use it for business income?

Yes, if you are a sole proprietor or single-member LLC owner who reports profit on Schedule C: enter the net profit under Self-employment and the calculator adds self-employment tax and the qualified business income deduction. It does not cover corporations, partnership details, farm income or the extra limits for specified service businesses at high incomes.

Does it include state income tax for California, Texas, New York or Florida?

No, it estimates federal tax only. Texas and Florida have no state individual income tax, while California and New York tax income at graduated rates on a separate state return. Your state tax only enters here as an itemized deduction, through the state and local income tax rate or the amount withheld.

Why might my refund differ from this estimate?

It is an estimate of federal tax only. Things it does not cover, such as state income tax, special credits, farm or foreign income, can change the result. Your real return uses whole-dollar lines, so small differences of a dollar or two are normal.
