How it works

On the Grow money tab, enter a starting amount, the yearly rate, how often it compounds and the number of years; you can also add a deposit every month. The result uses the compound interest formula and shows the balance year by year. On the Convert a rate tab, enter a rate and how it is compounded, then pick another compounding: the calculator finds the rate that grows money by exactly the same amount in a year, for example a monthly APR as a yearly APY, and the rate for a single period.

The formula

  • Growth: A = P × (1 + r ÷ m)^(m × t), with P the starting amount, r the yearly rate, m the times it compounds a year and t the years; continuous: A = P × e^(r × t)
  • One year's growth factor F = (1 + r ÷ m)^m (continuous: e^r); APY = F − 1
  • The same growth with m₂ compoundings a year: r₂ = m₂ × (F^(1 ÷ m₂) − 1); continuous: r₂ = ln F
  • Rate for one period = r₂ ÷ m₂; a day is 1/365.25 of a year

A worked example

6% compounded monthly grows money by (1 + 0.06 ÷ 12)^12 = 1.0616778 in a year, so it equals 6.16778% compounded yearly (the APY). Going the other way, an 8% APY is 12 × (1.08^(1/12) − 1) = 7.72084% compounded monthly, or 0.64340% a month. $10,000 at 6% compounded monthly for 10 years grows to $10,000 × 1.005^120 = $18,193.97.

Questions

What is the difference between APR and APY?

APR is the quoted yearly rate before compounding; for most loans and cards it is the monthly rate times 12. APY is what the money really grows by in one year once the interest earns interest. A 6% APR charged monthly is a 6.168% APY. Savings accounts usually quote APY, loans usually quote APR.

How do I convert APR to APY?

Open Convert a rate, enter the APR, choose Monthly (or however often it compounds) and show it compounded Yearly (APY). The formula is APY = (1 + APR ÷ 12)^12 − 1 for monthly compounding.

Is daily compounding much better than monthly?

Only slightly. 5% compounded monthly is a 5.116% APY; compounded daily it is 5.127%, and even continuous compounding only reaches 5.127%. The rate itself matters far more than how often it compounds.

What is continuous compounding?

It is the limit of compounding more and more often, every instant. Money then grows by e^r in a year (e ≈ 2.71828), so 5% continuous is e^0.05 − 1 = 5.127% a year. Some bonds and many textbook problems use it.

Why does the converter use 365.25 days?

To average in the leap day every fourth year. It changes the result only in the far decimal places; 12% compounded daily is 12.02807% compounded twice a month either way to five decimals.
