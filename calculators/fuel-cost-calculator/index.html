What this fuel cost calculator works out

This fuel cost calculator tells you how much fuel a trip needs and what it will cost, from three numbers: the distance, your vehicle's fuel economy and the fuel price. It works in kilometres or miles, in litres or gallons, and in rupees, pounds, dollars or any other currency, so it suits a drive across Pakistan as well as a road trip in the US or UK.

The answer is a fuel budget, not a quote. Tolls, parking and wear on the car are not included, and real fuel use moves with speed, traffic, load and weather.

How to use the fuel cost calculator

  • Trip distance: type the one-way distance and pick miles or km. A map app's driving route gives a good figure.
  • Fuel economy: type your car's figure and pick its unit: miles per US gallon, miles per UK gallon, litres per 100 km, km per litre or litres per mile.
  • Fuel price: type the pump price, choose per US gallon or per litre, and pick your currency from the menu (Rs for Pakistani rupees).
  • More options: tick Round trip to double the distance, and set Split the cost between to share it among the people in the car.
  • Read the cost and the fuel needed at the top, then the steps, which show every conversion, and the table of other fuel economy figures.

How to calculate fuel cost by hand

The fuel cost formula is: fuel cost = distance ÷ fuel economy × fuel price, with all three in matching units. With km per litre, divide the kilometres by the km/L figure to get litres, then multiply by the price per litre. With L/100 km, multiply the kilometres by the figure and divide by 100. With mpg, divide the miles by the mpg to get gallons, then multiply by the price per gallon.

The fuel cost per km is simply the price per litre ÷ km per litre: Rs 265 ÷ 13 = Rs 20.38 a km. The fuel cost per mile is the price per gallon ÷ mpg: $3.20 ÷ 28 = $0.114 a mile.

In Excel or Google Sheets, put the distance in A2, the km/L or mpg figure in B2 and the price in C2, then use =A2/B2*C2. For L/100 km use =A2*B2/100*C2. When the units do not match, for example a UK mpg figure with a price per litre, convert first, or let this calculator do it.

Worked examples for a trip, a bike and a commute

  • US road trip: 450 miles each way, round trip, at 28 mpg with gas at $3.20 a gallon. The 900 miles use 32.14 US gallons (121.67 litres) and cost $102.86, or $34.29 each for three people and $0.114 a mile.
  • UK journey: 180 miles in a car that does 48 mpg (UK) with fuel at £1.38 a litre. The trip uses 3.75 UK gallons, which is 17.05 litres, and costs £23.53, about 13.1p a mile. The car's figure equals 5.89 L/100 km.
  • European trip: 600 km at 6.5 L/100 km with fuel at €1.70 a litre uses 39 litres and costs €66.30. The same car is 36.19 mpg in US gallons.
  • Pakistan motorcycle commute: 30 km a day for 26 working days is 780 km. A bike that gives 50 km per litre needs 15.6 litres, which at Rs 265 a litre is Rs 4,134 a month, or Rs 5.30 a km.
  • Prices here are examples. Petrol and diesel prices change, so type the price at your pump on the day you travel.

Fuel economy units and how to convert them

Countries quote fuel economy in different units, and some of them run in opposite directions: a higher mpg or km/L is better, but a lower L/100 km is better. The calculator converts between them with the exact factors 1 mile = 1.609344 km, 1 US gallon = 3.785411784 litres and 1 UK gallon = 4.54609 litres.

A UK gallon is about 20% bigger than a US gallon, so the same car shows a higher mpg figure in the UK. That is why the menu keeps the two mpg units apart.

Why real fuel use is often higher

Official and dashboard figures are measured or averaged under set conditions. The U.S. Department of Energy's fueleconomy.gov site gives these effects of the way a car is driven and kept:

  • Aggressive driving (fast starts, hard braking, speeding) can lower fuel economy by roughly 15% to 30% at highway speeds and 10% to 40% in stop-and-go traffic.
  • Idling burns about a quarter to half a US gallon of fuel an hour, depending on the engine and air conditioning.
  • An extra 100 pounds (about 45 kg) of load cuts mpg by about 1%. A large roof box can cut it by about 6% to 17% on the highway and 10% to 25% at 65 to 75 mph.
  • Under-inflated tyres lower mileage by about 0.2% for each 1 psi drop in average pressure, and the recommended grade of motor oil can improve it by 1% to 2%.
  • In very hot weather air conditioning can reduce a conventional car's fuel economy by more than 25%. In cold weather, city fuel economy is roughly 15% lower at 20 °F (−7 °C) than at 77 °F (25 °C), and up to 24% lower on short trips.

For a safe budget, read the what-if table one step below your usual figure, or use your own measured fuel economy from the gas mileage calculator.

Fuel cost for business mileage and tax (UK and US)

In the UK, HMRC publishes advisory fuel rates that employers can use to repay business mileage in company cars, or to work out what an employee repays for private fuel. HMRC reviews them every quarter (1 March, 1 June, 1 September and 1 December). The rates apply only to company cars, and the table below gives them by engine size.

In the US, the IRS standard mileage rate for business use in 2026 is 72.5 cents a mile from January 1 to June 30 and 76 cents a mile from July 1 to December 31 (70 cents in 2025). That rate is meant to cover the whole cost of running the car, not just fuel, so it is higher than the fuel cost per mile this calculator shows.

Ways to cut the fuel cost of a trip

  • Share the ride. Split the cost between shows each person's share; four people in one car pay a quarter each.
  • Check tyre pressures when the tyres are cold, and take off an empty roof box or rack before a long drive.
  • Drive smoothly and keep to a steady speed; on the highway, cruise control usually helps.
  • Plan the route to avoid long jams and idling, and combine short errands into one trip, since short trips on a cold engine use the most fuel per km.
  • When choosing a car or bike, compare its figure in the what-if table: the difference over a year of driving is often larger than people expect.

How it works

Type how far you will drive, how much fuel your car uses and what the fuel costs. Fuel economy can be in miles per US or UK gallon, litres per 100 km, kilometres per litre or litres per mile, and the price can be per litre or per gallon: the calculator turns everything into litres and kilometres first, so mixed units are fine. It then multiplies the fuel needed by the price. Under More options you can double the distance for a round trip and split the cost between the people in the car. A table shows the same trip at other fuel economy figures, so you can see what a thirstier or a more frugal car would cost.

The formula

  • Fuel (US gallons) = miles ÷ mpg
  • Fuel (litres) = km × L/100 km ÷ 100, or km ÷ (km/L)
  • Cost = fuel × price (gallons are turned into litres when the price is per litre: 1 US gallon = 3.785411784 L)
  • 1 mile = 1.609344 km; 1 UK gallon = 4.54609 L
  • Cost per person = cost ÷ number of people

A worked example

A 300-mile trip in a car that does 25 mpg needs 300 ÷ 25 = 12 US gallons. At $3.50 a gallon that is 12 × 3.50 = $42.00. The same car is 9.41 L/100 km, so with fuel at Rs 280 a litre the 482.8 km trip uses 45.42 litres and costs about Rs 12,719.

Questions

How do I calculate fuel cost per km?

Divide the price of one litre by the kilometres your vehicle covers on a litre. With petrol at Rs 265 a litre and a car that does 13 km/L, that is Rs 20.38 a km. If your figure is in L/100 km, multiply it by the price and divide by 100.

How do I calculate fuel cost for a trip?

Divide the distance by the fuel economy to get the fuel needed, then multiply by the fuel price. For 780 km on a bike that gives 50 km/L, that is 15.6 litres, which costs Rs 4,134 at Rs 265 a litre. Tick Round trip if you are coming back the same way.

How do I calculate fuel cost per mile in the UK?

UK cars quote miles per UK gallon, but fuel is priced per litre, so turn the price into a price per UK gallon first (× 4.54609) and divide by the mpg. At £1.38 a litre and 48 mpg (UK) that is about 13.1p a mile. Choose miles per UK gallon and per litre here and the calculator does the conversion.

What is the fuel cost formula in Excel?

With the distance in A2, the km/L or mpg in B2 and the price in C2, use =A2/B2*C2. For a figure in L/100 km use =A2*B2/100*C2. Keep the units matched: km with km/L and a price per litre, or miles with mpg and a price per gallon.

Where do I find my car's fuel economy?

The best figure is your own: fill the tank, note the distance until the next fill and divide (the gas mileage calculator does this for you). Otherwise use the figure in the owner's manual or an official rating, and expect real driving to use a little more.

Can I mix units, such as mpg with a price per litre?

Yes. Every figure is turned into litres and kilometres before the sums, so an mpg car with a price per litre, or a distance in miles with L/100 km, all work.

Why is the real cost often higher?

Fuel economy changes with speed, traffic, hills, cold weather, roof boxes, heavy loads and air conditioning. The U.S. Department of Energy notes that aggressive driving alone can lower highway fuel economy by about 15% to 30%. For a safe budget, read the cost at a slightly worse figure in the what-if table.

How do I share the cost of a road trip?

Open More options, tick round trip if you are coming back, and set the number of people. The calculator shows each person's share of the fuel. Tolls and parking are not included.

Does it work for a motorcycle, a diesel car or a truck?

Yes. The sums are the same for any vehicle and any liquid fuel: type its own fuel economy and the price of its fuel, such as diesel per litre. For a truck that carries different loads, use the figure for the load on that trip.

What is fuel cost adjustment in an electricity bill?

It is a different charge from vehicle fuel. In Pakistan, the Fuel Charges Adjustment (FCA) on electricity bills is decided each month by NEPRA for the ex-WAPDA distribution companies, after a public hearing, to pass changes in the fuel cost of generating power on to consumers. This calculator does not work it out; it covers fuel for driving.
