What is TDEE, and why does it matter?

Your TDEE, short for total daily energy expenditure, is the number of calories your body uses in 24 hours. This TDEE calculator estimates it from your sex, age, height, weight and activity level, then shows how much to eat to keep, lose or gain weight. If you eat about your TDEE, your weight stays roughly the same; eat less and you lose, eat more and you gain.

TDEE is also what people mean by maintenance calories. It is always higher than your BMR (basal metabolic rate), because BMR covers only the energy you would use lying completely still, while TDEE adds digestion, daily tasks and exercise. The result is an estimate with a typical error of around 10%, so treat it as a starting point you fine-tune with your own weight records.

How to use the TDEE calculator

  • Choose Metric or US units, then pick Male or Female.
  • Enter your age (18 to 80), weight and height.
  • Pick the activity level that matches a normal week. BMR only shows your resting energy alone; the other six levels run from sedentary to extra active.
  • Under Formula and units you can switch the BMR formula (Katch-McArdle asks for your body fat percentage) and show the results in kilojoules instead of kcal.
  • Read your TDEE, your BMR, your BMI with its WHO class and healthy weight range, and the daily amounts to lose or gain weight. Loss rates that would take you under 1,200 kcal (women) or 1,500 kcal (men) are left out, with a note saying why.

What makes up your TDEE

Energy scientists split daily expenditure into three parts. The split below comes from the FAO, WHO and UNU report on human energy requirements (2004) and a 2004 review of diet-induced thermogenesis; the activity share is simply what is left, and it is the part that varies most between people.

The three parts of total daily energy expenditure
PartTypical share of TDEEWhat it covers
Basal metabolic rate (BMR)About 45% to 70%Heart, brain, lungs, liver, kidneys and muscles at complete rest
Diet-induced thermogenesisAbout 5% to 15%Digesting, absorbing and storing food; higher for protein, lower for fat
Physical activityThe restExercise plus everyday movement: walking, standing, housework, fidgeting

Because BMR is the largest part for most people, size and muscle matter a lot: a heavier or more muscular body burns more at rest. Because activity is the most changeable part, adding a daily walk changes TDEE far more than any food or supplement said to boost metabolism.

Choosing the right activity level

The activity factor is the number your BMR is multiplied by. In the FAO, WHO and UNU report the same idea is called the physical activity level (PAL = TDEE ÷ BMR): 1.40 to 1.69 describes a sedentary or lightly active lifestyle, 1.70 to 1.99 an active or moderately active one, and 2.00 to 2.40 a vigorous one, with values above 2.40 hard to keep up for long.

The US dietary guidelines describe moderately active as activity equal to walking about 1.5 to 3 miles (2.4 to 4.8 km) a day at 3 to 4 miles per hour on top of daily life, and active as walking more than 3 miles (4.8 km) a day. That is a handy way to think about step counts: the distance you cover on foot matters more than the exact number of steps.

Worked examples

  • Metric, Lahore: a 31-year-old woman, 163 cm and 64 kg, who exercises every day (active, 1.55). Her BMR is 1,343 kcal, so her TDEE is about 2,081 kcal a day. Her BMI is 24.1, normal. To lose 0.5 kg a week she would eat about 1,581 kcal; the 1 kg a week rate is not offered because it would take her under 1,200 kcal.
  • Metric, Rawalpindi: a 38-year-old man, 175 cm and 88 kg, with an office job and no exercise (sedentary). His BMR is 1,789 kcal and his TDEE 2,147 kcal. His BMI of 28.7 is overweight, with a healthy range of 56.7 to 76.6 kg at his height. Eating 1,647 kcal a day would lose about 0.5 kg a week.
  • US units, Revised Harris-Benedict: a 45-year-old man, 6 ft 0 in and 200 lb, exercising 1 to 3 times a week. His BMR is 1,926 kcal and his TDEE 2,648 kcal; 1 lb a week of loss means about 2,148 kcal a day.

Using your TDEE to lose weight, and how accurate it is

To lose weight, eat below your TDEE: 250 kcal less for about 0.25 kg (0.5 lb) a week, 500 kcal less for about 0.5 kg (1 lb). Weigh yourself under the same conditions for 2 to 3 weeks. If the weekly average falls faster or slower than planned, your real TDEE differs from the estimate, so move your intake by 100 to 200 kcal a day.

The gold standard for measuring TDEE in free-living people is doubly labelled water. A 2021 study of thousands of such measurements found that, after allowing for body size and lean mass, energy use is fairly stable from about age 20 to 60 and falls after 60. Prediction formulas were mostly built on North American and European volunteers; a 2005 review noted that older adults and US ethnic minorities were underrepresented, so South Asian users in particular should check the estimate against their own weight trend.

Special cases: breastfeeding, pregnancy, teens and trackers

  • Breastfeeding: the US dietary guidelines add about 330 kcal a day in the first six months and 400 kcal in the second six months; the Pakistan Dietary Guidelines suggest about 500 kcal extra. Add these to your TDEE yourself, as the calculator does not.
  • Pregnancy: no extra energy is usually needed in the first trimester, then about 340 kcal a day in the second and 452 kcal in the third (US guidelines); follow your doctor's advice.
  • Teenagers: this calculator starts at 18. For ages 15 to 17 the Calorie Calculator works, but growing teens should not diet without a doctor.
  • Fitness trackers: watch and phone estimates of calories burned can be useful for trends, but treat them as estimates too; use either the tracker or the activity factor for your exercise, not both.

How it works

Total daily energy expenditure (TDEE) is everything you burn in a day: your basal metabolic rate (BMR) at rest, the energy used to digest food, and all your movement and exercise. The calculator estimates the BMR with the Mifflin-St Jeor formula (or Revised Harris-Benedict or Katch-McArdle under Formula and units) and multiplies it by a factor for your activity level. Eating about your TDEE keeps your weight steady; about 500 kcal a day less loses roughly 1 lb (0.5 kg) a week. Loss targets under 1,500 kcal a day for men or 1,200 kcal for women are not offered, because going that low needs a doctor's care.

The formula

  • BMR (Mifflin-St Jeor): men 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age + 5; women the same − 161
  • TDEE = BMR × activity factor (1.2 sedentary, 1.375 light, 1.465 moderate, 1.55 active, 1.725 very active, 1.9 extra active), rounded to whole kcal
  • Lose 0.25 / 0.5 / 1 kg (0.5 / 1 / 2 lb) a week: TDEE − 250 / 500 / 1,000 kcal; gain: the same amounts added
  • BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²; WHO healthy range 18.5 to 25
  • 1 kcal = 4.1868 kJ

A worked example

A 30-year-old man, 185 cm and 95 kg with 18% body fat, who trains hard 6 to 7 times a week: Katch-McArdle BMR = 370 + 21.6 × (95 × 0.82) ≈ 2,053 kcal, so TDEE ≈ 2,053 × 1.725 ≈ 3,541 kcal a day. His BMI is 95 ÷ 1.85² ≈ 27.8, in the overweight class, even though much of his weight is muscle.

Questions

What is the difference between TDEE and BMR?

BMR is the energy you would use lying still all day. TDEE adds everything else: walking, work, exercise and digesting food. For most people TDEE is 1.2 to 1.9 times their BMR.

How do I calculate TDEE from BMR?

Multiply your BMR by your activity factor. With a BMR of 1,500 kcal and exercise 1 to 3 times a week (1.375), TDEE = 1,500 × 1.375 ≈ 2,063 kcal a day. The calculator works out the BMR for you first.

Which activity level should I choose?

Think of a normal week, not your best one. Exercise means 15 to 30 minutes with your heart rate up, intense exercise 45 to 120 minutes, very intense 2 hours or more. If you are unsure, pick the lower level; most people overestimate how active they are.

Is a TDEE calculator accurate?

It gives an estimate that is usually within about 10% for healthy adults, but individuals can be further off. The only way to know your real TDEE is to compare what you eat with how your weight changes over a few weeks, then adjust.

How do I use TDEE to lose weight as a woman?

Take your TDEE and eat 250 to 500 kcal a day less, staying at or above 1,200 kcal. A 31-year-old woman with a TDEE of 2,081 kcal, for example, would eat about 1,581 kcal to lose 0.5 kg a week. Adding activity is the safer way to make a bigger gap.

What is my TDEE while breastfeeding?

Work out your TDEE as usual, then add the extra for milk production: about 330 kcal a day in the first six months and 400 kcal later by US guidelines, or about 500 kcal by the Pakistan Dietary Guidelines. Do not diet hard while breastfeeding without your doctor's advice.

Is there a different TDEE formula for Asians?

No separate formula is in common use; the standard formulas are applied to everyone. Because they were built mostly on Western volunteers, treat the result as a first guess and adjust it to your own weight trend.

Why are some weight-loss amounts missing?

If a loss target would put you under 1,200 kcal a day (women) or 1,500 kcal (men), it is left out and a note explains why. Below those amounts it is hard to get enough nutrients, so only do it with a doctor. Moving more is the safer way to make a bigger deficit.

Why does my BMI say overweight when I am muscular?

BMI uses only height and weight, so it cannot tell muscle from fat. Athletes often score as overweight. A body fat estimate, such as the Body Fat Calculator, gives a better picture.
