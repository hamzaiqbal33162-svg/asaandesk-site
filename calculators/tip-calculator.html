What the tip calculator works out

A tip calculator turns a bill and a tip percentage into the tip amount, the total to pay and, when friends share the bill, what each person owes. It saves the mental arithmetic at the end of a meal, a haircut or a taxi ride, and it gets the cents right when a bill does not divide evenly.

The maths is the same in any currency: dollars, rupees, pounds or dirhams. Only the symbol changes, and you can pick it from the currency menu at the top of the calculator.

How to use this tip calculator

  • Choose your currency symbol at the top (US dollar by default; Pakistani rupee, pound, euro, Indian rupee, dirham, riyal, Canadian and Australian dollar are also listed).
  • Type the Bill amount. It starts at 50.
  • Set the Tip in percent. It starts at 15% and accepts 0% to 100%.
  • Set the Number of people sharing the bill, from 1 to 1,000.
  • Optional: under More options, Round up the tip, the total or each person's share to a whole amount. The default is No rounding.

Understanding the results

The top line shows the Tip and the Total to pay. With two or more people you also get the Tip per person and the Total per person. Because each share is rounded to the cent, a note tells you when the shares fall a cent or two short of the total (or go over it) and how many people should pay one cent more or less.

When you round up, the tip changes, so an extra line shows the Tip as a share of the bill, for example 10.03% instead of 10%. Below that, a table repeats the sum at 5%, 10%, 12%, 14%, 15%, 18%, 20%, 25%, 30% and 50% (plus your own rate), with each person's share when the bill is split.

How to calculate a tip, with or without a calculator

The tip formula is tip = bill × rate ÷ 100, and the total is the bill plus the tip. To find the tip percentage you left, reverse it: tip ÷ bill × 100. A $9 tip on a $60 bill is 9 ÷ 60 × 100 = 15%.

In your head, start from 10%: move the decimal point one place left, so 10% of $64 is $6.40. Double it for 20% ($12.80). For 15%, add half of the 10% to itself ($6.40 + $3.20 = $9.60). For 18%, take 20% and subtract 2% (one fifth of the 10%): $12.80 − $1.28 = $11.52.

Worked examples: splitting the bill in dollars and rupees

  • Four friends in New York, a bill of $86.40 and an 18% tip: the tip is $86.40 × 0.18 = $15.552, rounded to $15.55, and the total $101.95. Each share is $101.95 ÷ 4 = $25.4875, shown as $25.49; four of those make $101.96, so the note says one person can pay $25.48. Each person's part of the tip is $3.89.
  • Three friends at a restaurant in Lahore, a bill of Rs 7,850 and a 10% tip: the tip is Rs 785 and the total Rs 8,635. Each share is Rs 2,878.33, one paisa short, so one person pays Rs 2,878.34. Choose Each person's share, to a whole amount and each pays Rs 2,879: the total becomes Rs 8,637 and the tip Rs 787, or 10.03% of the bill.
  • Rounding the total for cash: a $47.30 bill at 15% gives a $7.10 tip (7.095 rounded half up) and a $54.40 total. Round the total up and you pay $55, a tip of $7.70, or 16.28%.

How much to tip in the US (2026 guide)

Tipping customs are not law and they shift over time. The rates below follow the Emily Post Institute's general tipping guide, a long-standing US etiquette reference, as checked in November 2026.

Tipping in other countries and service charges

  • Japan: the national tourism organisation says tipping is not customary in restaurants, bars, taxis or hotels, and a tip may be politely refused.
  • United Kingdom: since 1 October 2024 the Employment (Allocation of Tips) Act 2023 requires employers to pass on to workers, without deductions, all the tips, gratuities and service charges that the business receives or controls.
  • United States: a charge the restaurant adds itself, such as 18% for a large group, is a service charge, not a tip, under both the IRS and Labor Department rules. Do not add a second tip by mistake.
  • Pakistan and elsewhere: read the bill first. If a service charge line is printed, a tip is already included; if not, choose your own rate and type the amount before tax, as tax is not part of the service.

Tips, wages and tax for workers in the US

Under the federal Fair Labor Standards Act an employer may pay a tipped worker as little as $2.13 an hour in cash and count up to $5.12 an hour of tips (the tip credit) toward the $7.25 federal minimum wage. If tips fall short, the employer must make up the difference. State laws can require more, so check your state's rate.

Tips are taxable income, and employees must report cash tips to their employer unless they total less than $20 in a month. The "No Tax on Tips" deduction lets eligible workers deduct up to $25,000 of qualified tips a year for 2025 to 2028, reduced above $150,000 of modified income ($300,000 for joint returns). These are the rules as of 2026, and tax rules change.

Shared tip pools are often split by hours worked: each person gets pool × their hours ÷ everyone's hours. With $600 in the pool and staff who worked 30, 20 and 10 hours, the shares are $300, $200 and $100.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Tip on the bill before tax if that is your habit: type the pre-tax amount, then add the tax yourself.
  • With a coupon or discount, many people tip on the full price, since the service was the same.
  • Check for an automatic service charge before adding a tip.
  • When splitting, use the note about the extra cent so the bill is covered exactly.
  • Round up per person when everyone pays cash; the calculator shows what that does to the tip percentage.

How it works

Type the bill, the tip rate and how many people are paying. The tip is the bill times the rate, rounded to the nearest cent, and the total is the bill plus the tip. With more than one person you also get each person's tip and share; when the shares cannot split the total to the exact cent, a note tells you who pays one cent more. Under More options you can round the tip, the total or each person's share up to a whole amount, which makes paying in cash easier. The table under the result shows the tip and total for the same bill at the usual rates, so you can compare 15%, 18% and 20% at a glance. Pick your currency symbol at the top; the maths is the same in any currency.

The formula

  • Tip = bill × tip rate ÷ 100, rounded to the nearest cent
  • Total = bill + tip
  • Each person's share = total ÷ number of people, rounded to the nearest cent
  • Tip percentage = tip ÷ bill × 100
  • Rounded up: total = next whole amount (or each share = next whole amount × people), tip = total − bill

A worked example

A bill of $55 with a 15% tip: the tip is $55 × 0.15 = $8.25 and the total $63.25. Split three ways each person pays $21.08; three shares make $63.24, so one person pays $21.09.

Questions

How do I calculate a tip on a bill?

Multiply the bill by the tip rate and divide by 100. A 15% tip on $48 is 48 × 15 ÷ 100 = $7.20, so the total is $55.20. The calculator does this and rounds to the cent.

How do I calculate the tip percentage I paid?

Divide the tip by the bill and multiply by 100. Leaving $12 on a $70 bill is 12 ÷ 70 × 100 = 17.1%. When you round up, the calculator shows this as Tip as a share of the bill.

How much should I tip at a restaurant?

In the US, 15% to 20% of the bill before tax is the usual guide for table service, and around 10% or nothing for counter service and takeout. In many other countries a service charge is already on the bill, so check it before adding more.

How do I work out a 20% tip in my head?

Find 10% by moving the decimal point one place left, then double it. On a $43 bill, 10% is $4.30, so 20% is $8.60. For 15%, add half of the 10%: $4.30 + $2.15 = $6.45.

Should I tip on the amount before or after tax?

Many people tip on the amount before tax, since tax is not part of the service. If you prefer that, type the bill without the tax and add the tax yourself at the end.

Why do the shares not add up to the total?

Each share is rounded to the nearest cent, so three shares of $63.25 are $21.08 each, one cent short. The note under the result says how many people should pay a cent more so the bill is covered exactly.

Can I round the tip up?

Yes. Under More options choose to round the tip, the total or each person's share up to a whole amount. The result then shows the new tip and the tip as a share of the bill.

Can I use it in Pakistani rupees?

Yes. Choose Rs Pakistani rupee in the currency menu; the maths does not change. A 10% tip on a Rs 6,400 bill is Rs 640, for a total of Rs 7,040.

Is this a gratuity calculator for end-of-service pay in Pakistan?

No. Here gratuity means a tip on a bill. End-of-service gratuity paid to employees is set by labour law and the employer's scheme and depends on wages and years of service, not on a bill.

Is an automatic gratuity the same as a tip?

Not legally in the US. A charge the business adds to the bill, such as 18% for a large party, counts as a service charge under IRS and Labor Department rules, because the customer did not choose it. You do not need to tip again on top unless you want to.
