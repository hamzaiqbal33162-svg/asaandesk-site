What the day of the week calculator tells you

This day of the week calculator names the weekday of any date from January 1 of the year 1 to December 31, 9999. Type a birthday, an exam or wedding date, or a date from a history book, and the answer appears at once, with no button to press.

Beside the weekday you get the date's place in its year: its day number, the days left, which Saturday (or Monday, or Friday) of the year it is, its ISO week number and whether the year is a leap year. A table lists the weekday of the same date over the next ten years, and a month calendar marks the day.

How to use it

  • Pick or type a date in the Date box. Any year from 1 to 9999 works; dates before the year 1 (BC dates) are not supported.
  • Read the headline: it names the weekday, with the ISO week and the day of the year under it. For dates before 1924 it says "On the Gregorian calendar" instead.
  • Check the rows for the day number, the days left, the nth weekday of the year, the ISO week, the leap year answer and how many days the date is from today.
  • Open the table of coming years to see the weekday of the same date over the next ten years. For February 29 it lists the next ten leap years.
  • For a date before 1924, also read the Julian calendar row and the note under it.

How to calculate the day of the week by hand

Zeller's congruence turns a Gregorian date into a number from 0 to 6: h = (q + ⌊13(m + 1) ÷ 5⌋ + K + ⌊K ÷ 4⌋ + ⌊J ÷ 4⌋ + 5J) mod 7. Here q is the day of the month and m the month, counted from 3 for March to 12 for December, with January and February taken as months 13 and 14 of the year before. K is the last two digits of the year and J the first two. The answer h = 0 means Saturday, 1 Sunday, 2 Monday and so on up to 6 for Friday. Many books write −2J in place of +5J; both leave the same remainder after dividing by 7.

Worked example, March 23, 2027: q = 23, m = 3, K = 27 and J = 20. ⌊13 × 4 ÷ 5⌋ = 10, ⌊27 ÷ 4⌋ = 6, ⌊20 ÷ 4⌋ = 5 and 5 × 20 = 100. The total is 23 + 10 + 27 + 6 + 5 + 100 = 171, and 171 = 24 × 7 + 3, so h = 3: a Tuesday, the same answer the calculator gives.

John Horton Conway's doomsday rule, from 1973, is easier to do in your head. In any year these dates share one weekday, the "doomsday": 4/4, 6/6, 8/8, 10/10, 12/12, May 9 and September 5, July 11 and November 7, March 14, the last day of February, and January 3 (January 4 in a leap year). For 2000 to 2099 start from Tuesday (Wednesday for the 1900s). Divide the last two digits of the year by 12, then add the whole quotient, the remainder and the remainder divided by 4 (whole part only), and count that many days on.

For 2026: 26 ÷ 12 = 2 remainder 2, and 2 ÷ 4 = 0, so 2 + 2 + 0 = 4 days after Tuesday, which is Saturday. April 4, 2026 was indeed a Saturday. For 2027 the count is 2 + 3 + 0 = 5, giving Sunday, so March 14, 2027 is a Sunday and March 23, nine days later, a Tuesday.

Day of the week formula in Excel and Google Sheets

With a date in cell A1, =TEXT(A1,"dddd") shows its weekday name, such as Tuesday. =WEEKDAY(A1) returns a number from 1 for Sunday to 7 for Saturday; =WEEKDAY(A1,2) counts from 1 for Monday to 7 for Sunday, and type 3 counts from 0 for Monday to 6 for Sunday. Google Sheets uses the same three WEEKDAY types, and Excel's =ISOWEEKNUM(A1) gives the ISO week number shown here.

Microsoft notes that Excel treats 1900 as a leap year, a choice kept for compatibility with Lotus 1-2-3, so WEEKDAY returns wrong values for dates before March 1, 1900. For those dates use this calculator, which gives February 28, 1900 as a Wednesday.

Gregorian, Julian and ISO calendars

Pope Gregory XIII introduced the Gregorian calendar in 1582. Thursday, October 4, 1582 was followed by Friday, October 15: ten dates were dropped, but the weekdays carried on in order. Its leap year rule gives an average year of 365.2425 days, and the whole pattern repeats every 400 years, which hold 146,097 days, exactly 20,871 weeks. That is why one simple count of days can find the weekday of any date.

Britain and its colonies changed later, under the Calendar (New Style) Act 1750: Wednesday, September 2, 1752 was followed by Thursday, September 14. Russia changed in 1918 and Greece in 1923. Enter September 14, 1752 here and the Julian row shows September 3, 1752, the name the old calendar would have given that Thursday.

This calculator uses the Gregorian calendar for every date, carried back before 1582 as well. To look up a date written in the Julian calendar, add the gap of that period first (10 days after October 1582, 11 days in 1752), or find the Gregorian date whose Julian row matches. The Julian date October 4, 1582 is the Gregorian October 14, 1582, a Thursday.

ISO 8601 counts Monday as day 1 of the week, and its week 1 is the week holding the year's first Thursday. A year has 52 or 53 ISO weeks; it has 53 when it starts on a Thursday, or when it is a leap year that starts on a Wednesday. January 1, 2026 was a Thursday, so 2026 has a week 53, and January 1, 2027 belongs to it.

Where the weekday names come from

The seven-day names go back to the Romans, who named the days after the Sun, the Moon and the five planets they could see. English swapped four of them for Germanic gods, while French and Spanish kept most of the Latin names.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Check the order of day and month. 03/04/2026 means March 4 to some writers and April 3 to others; the calendar under the result shows which one you entered.
  • Do not guess next year's weekday by adding one day. A February 29 in between adds two, so use the coming-years table.
  • February 29 exists only in leap years. February 29, 2026 is not a real date, so the calculator asks for another one.
  • For history dates before 1924, find out which calendar the source used before you trust the weekday.
  • "From today" counts from the date on your own device, so it changes each day you open the page.

How it works

Pick any date and the weekday appears straight away, with its day number in the year, the days left in that year, which Monday, Tuesday and so on of the year it is, the ISO week number, whether the year is a leap year and how far the date is from today. Old dates also show the same day on the Julian calendar, and a table lists the weekday of the same date in the next 10 years.

The formula

  • Day number = days since Jan 1, 1970 on the Gregorian calendar; weekday = (day number + 4) mod 7, where 0 is Sunday
  • Leap year: divisible by 4, except century years, which must be divisible by 400 (2000 yes, 2100 no)
  • Day of the year = date − Jan 1 + 1; days left = 365 or 366 − day of the year
  • Nth weekday of the year = (day of the year − 1) ÷ 7, rounded down, + 1; a weekday comes 53 times when it is one of the year's first 1 (or 2 in a leap year) weekdays
  • ISO week: weeks start on Monday and week 1 is the one with the year's first Thursday

A worked example

Nov 21, 2026: Jan 1, 2026 was a Thursday and Nov 21 is day 325, so it is 324 days later. 324 = 46 × 7 + 2, and two days after Thursday is Saturday. It is the 47th Saturday of 2026, with 40 days and 5 Saturdays left in the year.

Questions

What day of the week was I born?

Enter your date of birth in the Date box and the headline names the weekday. For example, June 15, 2003 was a Sunday, the 24th Sunday of that year. The table under the result shows which weekday your birthday falls on in each of the next ten years.

How do I calculate the day of the week from a date?

Count the days from a date whose weekday you know and divide by 7; the remainder tells you how many weekdays to move on. Zeller's congruence and Conway's doomsday rule are shortcuts for the same count, and both are shown step by step in the guide above.

What is the day of the week formula?

A common one is Zeller's congruence: h = (q + ⌊13(m + 1) ÷ 5⌋ + K + ⌊K ÷ 4⌋ + ⌊J ÷ 4⌋ + 5J) mod 7, with January and February counted as months 13 and 14 of the year before. h = 0 is Saturday, 1 Sunday, and so on to 6 for Friday.

How do I find the day of the week from a date in Excel?

Use =TEXT(A1,"dddd") for the weekday name, or =WEEKDAY(A1) for a number from 1 (Sunday) to 7 (Saturday). Add 2 as a second argument, =WEEKDAY(A1,2), to count from Monday instead. Google Sheets accepts the same formulas.

What is the first day of the week?

Under the ISO 8601 standard, Monday is day 1 and weeks run Monday to Sunday, which is what the ISO week number here uses. Excel's and Google Sheets' WEEKDAY functions count Sunday as day 1 unless you choose another type, so it depends on the system you follow.

Does the calculator use the Julian calendar?

It works on the Gregorian calendar for every date, but for dates before 1924 it also shows the same day on the Julian calendar. Countries switched at different times: most of Catholic Europe in 1582, Britain and its American colonies in 1752, Russia in 1918, Greece in 1923. A date in an old record may be a Julian one, so check which calendar it used.

Can I find the day of the week for BC dates?

No. The calculator covers the years 1 to 9999 only, so a date before the year 1 cannot be entered. For the earliest supported dates it shows the Julian date as well, since records from those centuries used the Julian calendar.

What is the ISO week number?

It numbers the weeks of a year from Monday to Sunday, with week 1 being the week that holds the first Thursday of the year. So January 1 can belong to week 52 or 53 of the year before, as January 1, 2027 does (week 53 of 2026).

Why do some years have 53 Saturdays?

A common year is 52 weeks and 1 day, and a leap year 52 weeks and 2 days. The weekday of January 1 (and of January 2 in a leap year) therefore comes 53 times, and every other weekday 52 times. 2028 starts on a Saturday and is a leap year, so it has 53 Saturdays and 53 Sundays.

How does it know the weekday of a date long ago?

The Gregorian calendar repeats exactly every 400 years (146,097 days, a whole number of weeks), so every date can be turned into a count of days from a known day. That count divided by 7 gives the weekday, and the same rule works for any year from 1 to 9999.
