How it works

BMI is your weight in kilograms divided by your height in metres squared. For adults (20 and over) the World Health Organization classes run from severe thinness below 16 to obese class III at 40 and above, with 18.5 to 25 counted as healthy; the healthy weight range is those two BMIs times your height squared. BMI Prime is your BMI divided by 25, so anything over 1.00 is above the healthy range. The Ponderal Index divides weight by height cubed, which keeps it fairer for very short or very tall people. Children and teens grow fast, so a single cut-off does not work for them: from 2 to 19 the BMI is compared with children of the same age and sex on the CDC 2000 growth charts and given as a percentile, and weight and height get their own percentiles too.

The formula

  • BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²; in US units the pounds and inches are converted exactly (1 lb = 0.45359237 kg, 1 in = 2.54 cm)
  • Adult classes (WHO): under 16 severe thinness, 16 to 17 moderate thinness, 17 to 18.5 mild thinness, 18.5 to 25 normal, 25 to 30 overweight, 30 to 35 obese class I, 35 to 40 obese class II, 40 and over obese class III
  • Healthy weight = 18.5 × height² to 25 × height²; BMI Prime = BMI ÷ 25; Ponderal Index = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)³
  • Children 2 to 19: z = ((BMI ÷ M)^L − 1) ÷ (L × S) with the CDC L, M and S values for the child's sex and age in months, and the percentile is the share of the normal curve below z
  • Child classes (CDC): under the 5th percentile underweight, 5th to under the 85th healthy weight, 85th to under the 95th overweight, 95th and above obesity (120% of the 95th percentile or more: severe obesity)

A worked example

A 30-year-old who is 175 cm tall and weighs 70 kg: 1.75² = 3.0625, so BMI = 70 ÷ 3.0625 ≈ 22.9, in the normal class. The healthy range for that height is 18.5 × 3.0625 ≈ 56.7 kg to 25 × 3.0625 ≈ 76.6 kg, BMI Prime is 22.9 ÷ 25 ≈ 0.91 and the Ponderal Index is 70 ÷ 1.75³ ≈ 13.1 kg/m³.

Questions

What is a healthy BMI?

For adults, 18.5 up to 25. Under 18.5 is underweight, 25 to 30 is overweight and 30 or more is obese. Some health services in South Asia use lower cut-offs (about 23 for overweight and 27.5 for obesity), because health risks start at a lower BMI there, so ask your doctor which applies to you.

Why do children get a percentile instead of a class?

A child's BMI changes a lot as they grow, and boys and girls grow differently. So the BMI is compared with children of the same sex and age on the CDC growth charts. The 65th percentile means the BMI is higher than that of about 65 out of 100 children of the same age and sex.

What are BMI Prime and the Ponderal Index?

BMI Prime is your BMI divided by 25, the top of the healthy range, so 0.92 means 8% under the upper limit and 1.20 means 20% over it. The Ponderal Index divides weight by height cubed instead of squared, which makes it fairer for very short or very tall people.

Is BMI accurate for everyone?

No. BMI cannot tell muscle from fat, so athletes can read as overweight, and older people who have lost muscle can read as normal while carrying a lot of fat. Waist size, body fat percentage and a doctor's check give a fuller picture.

Why does my child's exact age in months matter?

Growth chart values change month by month. A whole number of years is taken as the child's birthday; add the months since the last birthday for a closer result, especially for young children who grow fastest.
