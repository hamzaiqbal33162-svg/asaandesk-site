What a log is in math

A log calculator finds the exponent you need. The logarithm log_b(x) is the power you must raise the base b to in order to get x. Since 2⁵ = 32, log₂(32) = 5; since 10² = 100, log₁₀(100) = 2. A log undoes a power the same way subtraction undoes addition.

Three bases cover almost every use. Base 10 is the common log, written log or lg, and it counts how many times you multiply by 10. Base e (about 2.718281828459) is the natural log, written ln, which appears in growth, decay and calculus. Base 2 is the binary log, written log₂ or lb, which counts doublings and bits in computing.

Logs turn huge ranges into small, readable numbers. A million is 10⁶, so its common log is just 6. That is why scales for acidity (pH), sound (decibels) and earthquakes use logarithms.

There is no rule for the log of a sum: log(x + y) does not split. The power rule is the one that solves equations such as 2ⁿ = 1,000, because taking logs brings the unknown down from the exponent.

Worked examples

Base 2: log₂(1024). Ask what power of 2 gives 1,024. Change of base gives ln 1024 ÷ ln 2 = 6.9314718055995 ÷ 0.69314718055995 = 10, and the check 2¹⁰ = 1024 confirms it.

A fraction answer: log₈(2). Since 8 is 2³, the cube root of 8 is 2, so the answer is 1/3. The page shows 0.33333333333333 and the exact answer 1/3.

A number below 1: log₂(1/8) = −3, because 2⁻³ = 1/8. Logs of numbers between 0 and 1 are always negative when the base is above 1.

Natural log: ln(10) = 2.302585092994. This is the factor between the two most common logs: ln(x) = 2.302585 × log₁₀(x).

Antilog (solve for the number): log₃(x) = 4 means x = 3⁴ = 81. Solve for the base: log_b(81) = 4 means b⁴ = 81, so b = 81^(1/4) = 3.

Logs in real life: pH, decibels and doubling

pH: chemists define pH as minus the base-10 log of the hydrogen ion activity, which for dilute solutions is close to the concentration in moles per litre. Pure water at about 10⁻⁷ mol/L has pH 7. A solution with 0.00032 mol/L has log₁₀(0.00032) = −3.4948500216801, so its pH is about 3.49. Each pH step is a factor of 10 in acidity.

Decibels: a power ratio in decibels is 10 × log₁₀(P ÷ P₀). A sound with 1,000 times the reference power is 10 × 3 = 30 dB louder, and every extra 10 dB means ten times the power.

Doubling and bits: log₂ tells you how many doublings reach a number. log₂(1,000,000) = 19.931568569324, so 20 bits can count past a million and a search that halves the list each time needs at most 20 steps for a million items.

How to calculate log on a scientific, basic or Excel calculator

Scientific calculator: the log key is base 10 and the ln key is base e. For log base 2, or any other base, use change of base: type log(x) ÷ log(2). For example log(100) ÷ log(2) = 2 ÷ 0.30103 = 6.64386. Some models also have a log□□ key where you type the base directly.

Without a calculator, split the number into a power of 10 and a part between 1 and 10. For 3,450 that is 10³ × 3.45, so log(3,450) = 3 + log(3.45) = 3 + 0.53782 = 3.53782. The whole part (3) is called the characteristic and the decimal part the mantissa, which is what printed log tables list.

On a basic calculator with only a square-root key you can estimate ln(x): press √ fifteen times, subtract 1 and multiply by 32,768 (that is 2¹⁵). For x = 2 this gives about 0.6914 on an 8-digit display, close to the true 0.69315. Divide by 2.302585 to turn it into a base-10 log.

In Excel, =LOG(number, base) takes any base and uses 10 when the base is left out, so =LOG(8, 2) returns 3. =LN(x) and =LOG10(x) give the natural and common logs.

Common mistakes and related tools

  • Taking the log of 0 or a negative number: no real power of a positive base gives them, so the page shows an error.
  • Splitting a sum: log(2 + 8) is log 10 = 1, not log 2 + log 8 = 0.90309.
  • Mixing up log and ln on a calculator: ln(100) is 4.60517, log(100) is 2.
  • Dividing logs the wrong way round in change of base: the number goes on top, ln(x) ÷ ln(b).
  • Related tools: Exponent Calculator for the reverse operation, Scientific Calculator for longer expressions and Half-Life Calculator for decay problems that use ln.

How it works

A logarithm answers one question: to what power must the base be raised to give the number? So log₁₀(1000) = 3 because 10³ = 1000. Choose what you want to find, pick a base (10, e, 2 or type your own) and fill in the other values. The answer comes with the change-of-base step that works on any calculator, ln(x) ÷ ln(b), and a check that raises the base back to the power. Whole answers and simple fractions are found exactly, so log₈(2) shows 1/3 as well as 0.33333333333333.

The formula

  • log_b(x) = y means b^y = x (x > 0, b > 0 and b ≠ 1)
  • Change of base: log_b(x) = ln(x) ÷ ln(b) = log₁₀(x) ÷ log₁₀(b)
  • Solve for the number: x = b^y
  • Solve for the base: b = x^(1/y), when y ≠ 0
  • ln means the log with base e ≈ 2.718281828459

A worked example

log₂(1/8): 2 to what power gives 1/8? Since 2³ = 8, the power −3 gives 1/8, so the answer is −3. By change of base: ln(0.125) ÷ ln(2) = −2.0794415416798 ÷ 0.69314718055995 = −3.

Questions

What is the difference between log and ln?

ln is the natural log, with base e (about 2.718). log on its own usually means base 10 in school and science, and base 2 is common in computing. They differ only by a fixed factor: ln(x) = log₁₀(x) × 2.302585093.

How do you calculate log base 2 on a calculator?

Divide the common log of the number by the common log of 2: log₂(x) = log(x) ÷ log(2). For 100 that is 2 ÷ 0.30103 = 6.64386. On this page, just tap base 2 and type the number.

How do you calculate log without a calculator?

Write the number as a power of 10 times a value between 1 and 10, then add the known log of that value. With log 2 = 0.30103 and log 3 = 0.47712 you can build the logs of 4, 5, 6, 8 and 9 from the product and power rules.

What is the formula for changing the base of a log?

log_b(x) = ln(x) ÷ ln(b), or equally log(x) ÷ log(b) with base-10 logs. Any base works in the division as long as you use the same one on top and bottom.

What is an antilog?

The antilog undoes a log: the antilog of y in base b is bʸ. The antilog of 2 in base 10 is 100. Choose The number x on this page to find it.

Why must x be positive?

Any positive base raised to any power gives a positive number. No power of 10 equals 0 or −5, so the log of 0 or of a negative number has no real answer.

Why can the base not be 1?

1 to any power is still 1, so it can never reach other numbers, and log₁(8) has no answer. The base also has to be positive, so bases such as 0 or −2 are not allowed.

How do I find the base when I know x and y?

Choose The base b, then type x and y. log_b(81) = 4 means b⁴ = 81, so b is the fourth root of 81, which is 3. The calculator does this with b = x^(1/y).

How is log used to calculate pH?

pH is minus the base-10 log of the hydrogen ion activity, roughly the concentration in mol/L. Type the concentration as x with base 10 and change the sign of the answer: 0.00032 gives −3.49, so the pH is about 3.49.

How do I calculate a log in Excel?

Use =LOG(number, base), for example =LOG(8, 2) for 3. If you leave out the base Excel uses 10, and =LN(number) gives the natural log.
