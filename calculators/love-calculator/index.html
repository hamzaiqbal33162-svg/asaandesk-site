What this love calculator is (and is not)

A love calculator is a name game: type your name and your crush's, partner's or best friend's name, and it shows a love percentage from 0% to 100% with a playful message. People use it at parties, in class, as a prank on a friend or simply to smile for a minute.

It is not a test of real feelings. The score on this page comes only from the letters of the two names, through a fixed mixing formula, so it cannot know anything about the people, their personalities or how they treat each other. Nobody can measure love with arithmetic, and two names with a low score can belong to a very happy couple. Enjoy it as a game and nothing more.

How to use the love calculator by name

  • Type a name in First name (it starts with Romeo) and another in Second name (it starts with Juliet). First names, full names or nicknames all work, up to 100 characters each.
  • Press Check the match.
  • Read the Love match percentage, the short message under it, the Match level and the love meter, a gauge coloured by level.
  • Try other spellings or full names for fun: each spelling gives its own fixed score.
  • Share the page address if you like. The two names travel in the link, so your friend sees the same names and the same score.

What the love percentage and levels mean

The score is one whole number from 0 to 100, and the message depends on the band it falls in. The bands are spread evenly, so every level is about as likely as any other for a random pair of names.

How the love calculator formula works

First both names are cleaned up: changed to lower case, with spaces, hyphens, dots, apostrophes and other punctuation removed. Then the two cleaned names are put in alphabetical order and joined with a bar, such as juliet|romeo, so the order you typed them in does not matter.

That text goes through a hash: the 32-bit FNV-1a function followed by the bit mixer used in MurmurHash3. A hash turns any text into a big number in a way that looks random but is completely repeatable. The love percentage is that number divided by 101, keeping the remainder, which always lands between 0 and 100.

Because there is no randomness on each visit, the same two names give the same score forever, on any phone or computer. Other love calculator sites use their own hidden formulas, so the same names can score very differently on another site.

How to calculate love percentage on paper with names: FLAMES

Long before apps, school friends worked out love results on paper. The best known is FLAMES, where each letter stands for a result; in the most common version they are Friends, Lovers, Affection, Marriage, Enemies and Siblings.

  • Write both names and cross out the letters they share, one for one. For Emma and Noah, the A appears in both, so one A goes from each name.
  • Count the letters left: E, M, M from Emma and N, O, H from Noah make 6.
  • Write F L A M E S and count along it by that number, starting at F. The sixth letter is S, so S is struck out.
  • Keep counting 6 from the next letter, wrapping round to the start, and strike out each letter you land on. For Emma and Noah the order is S, F, A, L, E.
  • The last letter left is the answer: here M, for Marriage.

Other paper methods count how often the letters of a word such as LOVES appear in both names and add neighbouring digits until two are left. Every method gives a different answer for the same names, which is part of the fun.

How it works

This is a game, just for fun. Type two names and you get a score from 0% to 100%, a short message and a love meter. The score is made from the letters of the two names only: both names are written in lower case, spaces and punctuation are dropped, the two are put in alphabetical order and run through a fixed mixing formula (a hash) that turns them into a number from 0 to 100. So the same two names always give the same score, whichever name you type first and however you use capital letters, but a small change in spelling gives a completely different one. It knows nothing about the people, so please do not take it seriously.

The formula

  • Each name: lower case, spaces and punctuation removed ("Mary-Jane" and "mary jane" are the same)
  • The two names are sorted alphabetically and joined: first|second
  • Score = 32-bit FNV-1a hash of that text, mixed with the murmur3 finaliser, modulo 101 (0 to 100)
  • Message: 0-19 just say hello, 20-39 a small spark, 40-59 good friends, 60-79 lovely match, 80-100 perfect pair

A worked example

Romeo and Juliet score 69%, a Lovely match, and so do juliet and ROMEO, because the names are put in lower case and in alphabetical order first. Bilal and Zara score 1%, but Bilal and Zarah score 75%: one extra letter changes everything.

Sources

  • Fowler-Noll-Vo hash function (FNV-1a)
  • MurmurHash3 finaliser (bit mixing)

Questions

Is the love calculator real?

No, it is just for fun. The score comes only from the letters of the two names and says nothing about real feelings or how well two people get along.

How does the love calculator work?

It cleans up both names, sorts them alphabetically, mixes their letters with a hash formula and keeps the remainder after dividing by 101. That gives a score from 0% to 100% that is always the same for the same two names.

How do I calculate love percentage by names?

Type both names in the boxes and press Check the match. On paper you can play FLAMES instead: cross out shared letters, count the rest and count around the word FLAMES.

Why does the order of the names not matter?

Before the score is worked out the two names are put in alphabetical order, and capital letters, spaces and punctuation are ignored. So Ali and Sara gives the same score as SARA and ali.

Why did a small spelling change give a very different score?

The score comes from a hash, a formula that mixes every letter so that any change, even one letter, gives an unrelated number. Bilal and Zara score 1% but Bilal and Zarah score 75%.

Why does another love calculator give a different score?

Every site uses its own formula, and most keep it hidden. There is no single correct love percentage for two names.

Can I use a love calculator by date of birth?

This one uses names only. Dates of birth would just be another set of numbers to mix, and they would not make the result any more real.

Is the love calculator safe for kids?

Yes. It shows only a percentage and a gentle message, needs no sign-up and stores nothing.

Are the names saved anywhere?

No. The score is worked out in your browser and nothing is stored. The names are only kept in the page address, so a link you share opens with the same two names and score.
