What the distance calculator measures

This distance calculator finds the straight-line distance between two points in three settings: two points on a flat graph (2D), two points in space (3D), and two places on Earth given by latitude and longitude. The first two use the distance formula from coordinate geometry and show every step; the third measures over the curved surface of the Earth, which is how far apart two cities are as the crow flies.

For graph points you also get the slope, the angle and the equation of the line through them, so one entry answers most coordinate geometry questions. For places on Earth you get kilometres, miles, nautical miles and metres, plus the compass direction to set off in.

How to use the distance calculator

  • 2D points: type x and y for Point 1 (x₁, y₁) and Point 2 (x₂, y₂). The page opens with (1, 2) and (4, 6). Negative numbers and decimals are fine.
  • 3D points: type x, y and z for each point. The example is (1, 2, 3) to (4, 6, 15).
  • Places on Earth: under Type the coordinates as, choose Decimal degrees or Degrees, minutes, seconds. In decimal degrees, south latitudes and west longitudes are negative (New York is about 40.71, −74.01). Latitude runs from −90 to 90 and longitude from −180 to 180.
  • In degrees, minutes, seconds, type the parts with spaces or symbols and end with N or S (latitude) or E or W (longitude): 24 51 39 N, or 67°0′37″E. Decimal minutes such as 24 51.65 N also work. The example opens with Karachi and Lahore.
  • Results update as you type; open the steps to see the working.

Understanding the results

  • Distance (2D and 3D): to 14 significant digits. When the coordinates are whole numbers and the answer is not, the Exact distance is shown in surd form too, such as √29 for (2, 3) to (7, 5).
  • Slope (m): the rise over the run, ΔY ÷ ΔX, as a decimal and as a fraction. A vertical line has no slope (it would mean dividing by 0), and a horizontal line has slope 0.
  • Angle with the x-axis: arctan of the slope, negative when the line falls from left to right.
  • Line through both points: y = mx + b in decimals and exact fractions, plus where it crosses the y-axis and the x-axis. A picture shows the points, the line and the ΔX and ΔY legs.
  • 3D also lists ΔX, ΔY and ΔZ, the three steps between the points.
  • Places on Earth: the Distance in km (to 0.1 km), In miles, Nautical miles and Metres, and the Start direction from point 1, in degrees clockwise from north with its compass point (42.7°, NE, from Karachi towards Lahore). The steps convert the coordinates both ways between decimal and degrees-minutes-seconds.

The distance formula, with worked examples

Plot the two points and draw a right triangle under the line between them: one leg is the horizontal gap ΔX = x₂ − x₁, the other the vertical gap ΔY = y₂ − y₁. The Pythagorean theorem then gives the distance: d = √(ΔX² + ΔY²). You can take the points either way round: each gap is squared, so its sign drops out.

Example with negative coordinates, from (−3, 5) to (2, −7): ΔX = 2 − (−3) = 5 and ΔY = −7 − 5 = −12, so d = √(5² + (−12)²) = √(25 + 144) = √169 = 13. The calculator adds the slope −12/5 = −2.4 and the line y = −2.4x − 2.2.

Example with decimals, from (1.5, −2) to (4, 4): ΔX = 2.5 and ΔY = 6, so d = √(6.25 + 36) = √42.25 = 6.5.

In 3D, add the third gap: d = √(ΔX² + ΔY² + ΔZ²). The longest straight line inside a room 4 m long, 3 m wide and 2.5 m high runs from (0, 0, 0) to (4, 3, 2.5): √(16 + 9 + 6.25) = √31.25 ≈ 5.59 m, so a 5.5 m pole just fits corner to corner.

For school: the length of a line segment and the midpoint of two points are part of Coordinate geometry in the Cambridge O Level Mathematics syllabus (4024, exams in 2025 to 2027). The midpoint is the average of the coordinates, ((x₁ + x₂) ÷ 2, (y₁ + y₂) ÷ 2), so the midpoint of (1, 2) and (4, 6) is (2.5, 4).

How distance is calculated between two latitude and longitude points

On a globe the shortest route between two places is not a straight line on a flat map but an arc over the surface. If the Earth were a perfect ball, the haversine formula with a mean radius of 6,371 km would give that arc. The real Earth is slightly flattened at the poles, so this calculator measures on the WGS-84 ellipsoid, the model GPS uses (equatorial radius 6,378,137 m, flattening 1 ÷ 298.257223563), using Thaddeus Vincenty's inverse method from 1975.

The difference is small but real. For London to New York the ellipsoid gives 5,585.2 km, while a round Earth of radius 6,371.0088 km gives about 5,570.2 km, 15 km (about 0.27%) less. For Karachi to Lahore the two differ by under 1 km. For two places almost exactly opposite each other on the globe, where Vincenty's method does not settle, the calculator falls back to the round-Earth formula and says so.

Degrees, minutes and seconds turn into decimal degrees as degrees + minutes ÷ 60 + seconds ÷ 3,600. Karachi's 24° 51′ 39″ N is 24 + 51 ÷ 60 + 39 ÷ 3,600 = 24.8608°; a longitude of 0° 7′ 33″ W (London) becomes −0.1258°.

Straight-line distance versus distance by road

The Earth distance here is the shortest path over the surface, the way an aircraft flies. A road or rail route has to follow valleys, bridges, motorway junctions and city streets, so it is always longer, and on winding mountain roads it can be much longer. For driving or walking distance, use a maps app's route; this page is for the direct distance, for checking a route's directness, or for working with coordinates.

Once you know a road distance, the Fuel Cost Calculator turns it into the cost of a trip in PKR or any currency, and the Mileage Calculator works out what a journey costs per kilometre or mile.

Distance in physics: speed, time and displacement

In physics, distance usually means how far something travels, and the distance formula there is distance = speed × time (s = vt). A car averaging 90 km/h for 3 hours covers 270 km; a runner at 10 km/h for 45 minutes (0.75 h) covers 7.5 km. Average speed is the distance travelled divided by the time taken. The Speed Calculator solves for any one of the three.

Distance and displacement are different. Displacement is the change in position, a straight line from start to finish with a direction, which is what this calculator's 2D and 3D tabs measure between two points. Distance travelled follows the actual path: walk 3 km out and 3 km back and you have covered 6 km, but your displacement is zero.

The same idea estimates how far away lightning is. The US National Weather Service says to count the seconds between the flash and the thunder and divide by 5 to get miles; since a mile is about 1.6 km, dividing by 3 gives roughly the kilometres. A 12-second gap means about 2.4 miles, or 4 km.

Common mistakes and related calculators

  • Subtracting a negative: x₂ − x₁ with x₁ = −3 is x₂ + 3. Brackets in the steps keep the signs clear.
  • Forgetting the square root: √(ΔX² + ΔY²), not ΔX² + ΔY².
  • Mixing up latitude and longitude: latitude (north-south) comes first. Karachi is about 24.9° N, 67.0° E, not 67.0° N.
  • Dropping the minus sign for south or west in decimal degrees, which moves the point to the other side of the world.
  • Treating the Earth distance as a driving distance.

How it works

On a flat graph the distance between two points is the long side of a right triangle whose other sides are the steps across (ΔX) and up (ΔY), so Pythagoras gives d = √(ΔX² + ΔY²). The 2D tab also gives the slope, the angle with the x-axis and the equation of the line through both points, in decimals and in exact fractions, and draws the points. In 3D a third step (ΔZ) joins the sum under the root. For places on Earth, type each latitude and longitude, as decimal degrees (south and west are negative) or as degrees, minutes and seconds with N, S, E or W. The distance is measured over the surface of the WGS-84 ellipsoid, the slightly flattened Earth model that GPS uses, with Vincenty's formula, which is accurate to well under a metre.

The formula

  • 2D: d = √((x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²)
  • 3D: d = √((x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)² + (z₂ − z₁)²)
  • Slope m = ΔY ÷ ΔX, angle θ = arctan(ΔY ÷ ΔX), line y = m x + b with b = y₁ − m x₁
  • Degrees-minutes-seconds: decimal = degrees + minutes ÷ 60 + seconds ÷ 3,600 (negative for S and W)
  • Earth: Vincenty's inverse formula on WGS-84 (a = 6,378,137 m, f = 1 ÷ 298.257223563)
  • 1 mile = 1.609344 km, 1 nautical mile = 1.852 km

A worked example

From (1, 2) to (4, 6): ΔX = 3 and ΔY = 4, so d = √(3² + 4²) = √25 = 5. The slope is 4 ÷ 3 = 1.333…, the line is y = (4/3)x + 2/3, and it rises at 53.13° to the x-axis. On Earth, Karachi (24.8607, 67.0011) to Lahore (31.5204, 74.3587) is about 1,032.2 km, or 641.4 miles, in a straight line over the surface.

Questions

How do I calculate the distance between two points?

Subtract the x values and the y values, square both differences, add them and take the square root: d = √((x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²). From (1, 2) to (4, 6) that is √(9 + 16) = 5. Type the points in the 2D tab and every step is shown.

What is the distance formula in coordinate geometry?

It is d = √((x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²), which is the Pythagorean theorem applied to the horizontal and vertical gaps between the points. In three dimensions a (z₂ − z₁)² term joins the sum under the root.

How is distance calculated between two latitude and longitude points?

The coordinates are treated as points on the Earth's surface and the shortest path between them over the surface is measured. A simple method treats the Earth as a sphere (the haversine formula); this calculator uses the more accurate WGS-84 ellipsoid with Vincenty's formula, the same Earth model GPS uses.

How far is Karachi from Lahore?

In a straight line over the Earth's surface, about 1,032 to 1,034 km (around 642 miles), depending on which points in each city you take. The road route is longer, so check a maps app for the driving distance.

Is the Earth distance the same as the driving distance?

No. It is the shortest path over the Earth's surface, as a plane might fly. Roads bend around hills, rivers and buildings, so a driving route is always longer, sometimes by a lot on mountain roads.

How do I type degrees, minutes and seconds?

Choose Degrees, minutes, seconds and type each value with spaces or symbols, then N or S for latitude and E or W for longitude, such as 38 53 51.36 N or 77°2′11.76″W. Decimal minutes such as 38 53.856 N work too.

How can I calculate the distance between two coordinates in Excel?

With latitudes in B1 and B2 and longitudes in C1 and C2 (decimal degrees), the round-Earth haversine formula is =2*6371*ASIN(SQRT(SIN(RADIANS(B2-B1)/2)^2+COS(RADIANS(B1))*COS(RADIANS(B2))*SIN(RADIANS(C2-C1)/2)^2)), in km. It can differ from the ellipsoid result here by a few tenths of a percent on long routes.

What is the distance formula in physics?

Distance = speed × time, often written s = vt. A bus at 60 km/h for 2.5 hours travels 150 km. Rearranged, speed = distance ÷ time and time = distance ÷ speed.

What is the difference between distance and displacement?

Distance is the length of the path actually travelled; displacement is the straight-line change in position from start to finish, with a direction. A full lap of a 400 m track is 400 m of distance but zero displacement.

How do I calculate the distance of lightning?

Count the seconds from the flash to the thunder and divide by 5 for miles, the rule the US National Weather Service gives, or by about 3 for kilometres. Light arrives almost at once, while sound takes roughly 5 seconds to cover a mile.

What if the line between the two points is vertical?

When both points have the same x, the line is vertical: its slope is undefined, its angle is 90° and its equation is x = that value. When both have the same y, the slope is 0 and the line is horizontal.
