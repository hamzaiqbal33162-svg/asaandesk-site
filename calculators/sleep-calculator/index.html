How it works

Sleep comes in cycles of light sleep, deep sleep and dream (REM) sleep that last about 90 minutes each, and waking at the end of a cycle tends to feel easier than waking in the middle of one. Choose whether you know your wake-up time or your bedtime and type it. The calculator counts back (or forward) whole cycles, adding the minutes you usually need to fall asleep, and lists the times for 1 to 7 cycles. Five or six cycles, about 7.5 to 9 hours, suit most adults. The second tab simply works out the bedtime or wake-up time for a set amount of sleep.

The formula

  • Bedtime = wake-up time − time to fall asleep − cycles × cycle length
  • Wake-up time = bedtime + time to fall asleep + cycles × cycle length
  • Set amount of sleep: bedtime = wake-up time − sleep, or wake-up time = bedtime + sleep
  • Times past midnight wrap around the 24-hour clock (12 AM is midnight)

A worked example

To wake up at 6:00 AM with 15 minutes to fall asleep and 90-minute cycles: 5 cycles are 7 hours 30 minutes, so go to bed at 6:00 AM − 15 minutes − 7 hours 30 minutes = 10:15 PM. Six cycles (9 hours) give 8:45 PM.

Questions

Why 90 minutes?

During the night the brain moves through light sleep, deep sleep and REM sleep again and again. One round takes roughly 90 minutes on average, though it can be anywhere from about 70 to 120 minutes. If you know your own cycles are shorter or longer, change the cycle length under More options.

Which time should I pick?

The two times at the top, 5 or 6 full cycles, give 7.5 to 9 hours of sleep, which fits the 7 or more hours most adults need. Four cycles (6 hours) is a short night, and 1 or 2 cycles suit a nap more than a night's sleep.

What does time to fall asleep mean?

Most people take 10 to 20 minutes to drift off after lying down. That time is added before the first cycle starts, so the bedtimes are when to be in bed, not when to be asleep.

Is this medical advice?

No. It is a simple guide based on average sleep cycles. If you often feel tired after a full night, snore loudly or wake many times, talk to a doctor.
