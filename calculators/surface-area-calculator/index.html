What surface area is

A surface area calculator measures the outside skin of a solid: the total area of all the faces and curved parts you could touch, paint or wrap. If you unfolded a cardboard box flat, the area of that flat sheet would be the box's surface area. It is counted in square units, such as square meters (m²), square feet (ft²) or square centimeters (cm²).

Three terms come up again and again. Total surface area (TSA) is every face added together. Lateral surface area is only the sides, leaving out the base and top: the four walls of a box or the label of a tin. Curved surface area (CSA) is the lateral area of a round solid such as a cylinder, cone or sphere. Schoolbooks use all three, and this page shows each part separately so you can build whichever one a question asks for.

Surface area is different from volume. Volume is how much a solid holds and is counted in cubic units; surface area is how much material covers it. A tank's volume decides how much water it stores, while its surface area decides how much steel or paint it needs. For the space inside, use the Volume Calculator.

How to use the surface area calculator

  • Pick the shape from the tabs: Sphere, Cone, Cube, Cylinder, Box, Capsule, Spherical cap, Frustum, Ellipsoid or Pyramid.
  • Choose the Unit: feet, inches, yards, miles, meters, centimeters, millimeters, kilometers, micrometers, nanometers or angstroms. Every size box then uses it.
  • Type the sizes. Sphere, Cone, Cylinder and Capsule ask for a Radius (r); Cone, Cylinder, Box, Frustum and Pyramid ask for a Height (h). Box adds Length (l) and Width (w), Cube needs the Edge length (a), and Pyramid the Base edge (a).
  • Capsule asks for the Height of the straight part (h), the cylinder between the two half spheres, not the full length.
  • Spherical cap: fill in any two of Base radius (r), Ball radius (R) and Cap height (h), and the third is worked out.
  • Frustum asks for the Top radius (r), the Bottom radius (R) and the Height (h). Ellipsoid asks for the three semi-axes a, b and c, each half of a full width.
  • Mixed units: open Units and tick Use a different unit for each length, then pick a unit beside each box, for example a box 2 ft long, 18 in wide and 1 ft high.

Understanding the results and square units

The headline gives the total surface area in your unit. Under it, every part is listed on its own: Top, Bottom and Side (lateral) for a cylinder; Base and Side (lateral) for a cone; Top and bottom, Front and back, and Two ends for a box; Base and Four sides (lateral) for a pyramid; Flat base and Curved cap for a spherical cap. Add only the parts you need when a shape is open, such as a tank without a lid.

The steps put your numbers into each formula and show the exact multiple of π when it is short, such as 24π, before the decimal. A collapsed table gives the total in square meters, square centimeters, square millimeters, square feet, square inches, square yards, acres and hectares, and a labelled picture shows which size goes where.

With mixed units, the answer is worked out in each unit you used, so a box measured in feet and inches shows the total in ft² and in in². The ellipsoid result carries an approximate sign, explained below.

Cube and cuboid (rectangular prism)

A cube has six equal square faces, so its surface area is 6a². For a storage box with 40 cm edges: 6 × 40² = 6 × 1,600 = 9,600 cm², which is 0.96 m² of cardboard before any flaps or overlap.

A cuboid, also called a rectangular prism or box, has three pairs of matching rectangles: top and bottom (l × w each), front and back (l × h each) and the two ends (w × h each). That is why the formula is 2(lw + lh + wh). For the default 5 ft × 3 ft × 4 ft box: 2 × 5 × 3 = 30, 2 × 5 × 4 = 40 and 2 × 3 × 4 = 24, so the total is 94 ft².

Painting a room is a cuboid problem. For a room 12 ft long, 10 ft wide and 10 ft high, the page gives Front and back 240 ft² and Two ends 200 ft², so the four walls cover 440 ft². The ceiling is half of Top and bottom, 120 ft². Subtract doors and windows, then divide by the coverage printed on the paint tin.

Cylinder, pipe and capsule

A cylinder unrolls into two circles and a rectangle. The circles each have area πr². The rectangle is as tall as the cylinder and as wide as the circle's circumference, 2πr, so its area is 2πrh. Total surface area = 2πr² + 2πrh = 2πr(r + h), and the curved surface area is 2πrh on its own.

Example, a roof water tank: an overhead tank with a radius of 0.6 m and a height of 1.2 m has Top and Bottom of 1.1309733552923 m² each and a Side of 4.5238934211693 m², 6.785840131754 m² in all. If it stands on the roof and you paint only the side and the lid, that is 4.5239 + 1.1310 = 5.6549 m². At, say, 10 m² per litre per coat, two coats need about 1.13 litres; use the coverage on your own tin.

Pipes and tubes: for paint, insulation or wrapping, use only the Side. A pipe 10 cm across (r = 0.05 m) and 6 m long has an outside curved area of 0.6π = 1.8849555921539 m². For the inside surface too, run it again with the inner radius and add the two.

A capsule is a cylinder with a half sphere on each end. Its two half spheres make one full sphere, 4πr², plus the straight side 2πrh. With r = 3 ft and a straight part of 4 ft, that is 36π + 24π = 60π ≈ 188.50 ft².

Sphere, hemisphere, spherical cap and ellipsoid

A sphere's surface area is 4πr², exactly four times the area of a circle with the same radius. A ball with a radius of 10 cm has 4 × π × 10² = 400π = 1,256.6370614359 cm², about 0.126 m². Doubling the radius makes the area four times larger, because the radius is squared.

A hemisphere (half a ball) has a curved surface of 2πr². A solid hemisphere also has a flat circular face of πr², so its total is 3πr². For r = 3 that is 27π ≈ 84.82 square units. On this page, use Spherical cap with the cap height equal to the ball radius to get both parts.

A spherical cap is the piece cut off a ball by a flat slice, like the top of a dome. Its curved area is 2πRh, where R is the ball's radius and h the cap's height, and its flat base is a circle of radius r = √(2Rh − h²). For R = 5 ft and h = 2 ft the base radius is 4 ft, the curved cap is 20π ≈ 62.83 ft² and the base is 16π ≈ 50.27 ft².

An ellipsoid is a stretched or squashed sphere with three semi-axes a, b and c. When all three are equal it is a sphere, and when two are equal (a spheroid, like an egg or the Earth) exact formulas exist. With three different semi-axes there is no simple exact formula; the true value needs elliptic integrals.

This page uses Knud Thomsen's approximation, 4π((aᵖbᵖ + aᵖcᵖ + bᵖcᵖ) ÷ 3)^(1/p) with p = 1.6, which stays within about 1.06% of the true value and is much closer for shapes near a sphere. For a = b = c = 5 it gives 314.15926535898, exactly the sphere value 4π × 5² = 100π. For the default 3 × 2 × 1 it gives about 48.94.

Cone, frustum, pyramid, and shapes not on the tabs

A cone's curved surface unrolls into a sector of a circle with area πrl, where l = √(r² + h²) is the slant height. Add the base πr² for the total. An ice cream cone 6 cm across (r = 3 cm) and 10 cm tall has a slant height of √109 ≈ 10.44 cm and a curved area of 98.397570688857 cm²; with a paper lid the total is 126.67190457117 cm².

A frustum is a cone with its tip cut off, the shape of a bucket, a lampshade or a flower pot. Its side is π(R + r) times its slant height √((R − r)² + h²). For a bucket with a top radius of 15 cm, a bottom radius of 11 cm and a height of 28 cm, the side is 2,310.2991278424 cm² and the bottom 380.13271108436 cm²; an open bucket needs about 2,690.43 cm² of metal.

A square pyramid has one square base and four triangles. Each triangle has area ½ × a × slant height, so the four together are 2a × √((a/2)² + h²). The default pyramid (a = 6, h = 4) has a slant height of 5, sides of 60 and a base of 36, total 96 square units.

On a giant scale, the Great Pyramid of Giza had a base of about 230.3 m and an original height of about 146.6 m. Those sizes give four sloping faces of about 85,863 m², and 138,901 m² with the base, about 13.9 hectares.

  • Triangular prism: two triangles plus three rectangles. Surface area = 2 × (½ × base × triangle height) + (side₁ + side₂ + side₃) × length. A prism with a 3-4-5 right-triangle end and a length of 10 has 2 × 6 + 12 × 10 = 132 square units.
  • Any prism: 2 × area of the end + perimeter of the end × length. This also covers hexagonal prisms and L-shaped beams.
  • Open box or tank without a lid: take the Box total and subtract one l × w face. For 5 × 3 × 4 that is 94 − 15 = 79.
  • Composite solids, such as a cylinder topped with a cone: add the outside parts only and leave out the faces that touch each other. The page's part-by-part list makes this easy.
  • Flat shapes such as a rectangle or a circle have an area, not a surface area. Use the Area Calculator or the Circle Calculator for those.

Surface area to volume ratio

The surface area to volume ratio (SA:V) divides the outside area by the space inside. For a cube it is 6a² ÷ a³ = 6 ÷ a, and for a sphere 4πr² ÷ (4/3)πr³ = 3 ÷ r. Both fall as the object grows, because area grows with the square of the size and volume with the cube.

In science this ratio explains why small things lose heat and exchange gases quickly relative to their size, and why crushed or powdered solids react or dissolve faster than one lump: splitting a solid exposes more surface for the same amount of material.

Common mistakes and tips

  • Using the diameter as the radius. Halve the width across a circle first.
  • Using the vertical height where the slant height belongs, or the other way round. The page asks for the vertical height h and works out the slant for you.
  • Counting faces that are not there: an open tank has no top, a pipe has no ends, a room's floor is not painted.
  • Forgetting to square the units: an answer in feet is in ft², not ft.
  • Rounding π to 3.14 early. Keep π, as the steps do, and round only the final answer.
  • Forgetting waste: real cardboard, tiles and sheet metal need overlaps and offcuts, so buy a little more than the bare surface area.
  • For a person's skin area used in medicine, use the Body Surface Area Calculator instead.

How it works

Pick the shape, choose the unit and type its sizes. The total surface area appears as you type, together with each part on its own (the base, the top and bottom, the curved or slanted side) so you can leave out a part you do not need, such as the open top of a tank. A labelled picture shows which size is which, and the steps work each formula out with your numbers, with the exact multiple of π when it is a short number. If your sizes are in different units, open Units and give each box its own unit: everything is then worked out in each unit you used.

The formula

  • Sphere: 4πr²
  • Cone: base πr², side πr√(r² + h²)
  • Cube: 6a²; rectangular box: 2(lw + lh + wh)
  • Cylinder: top and bottom πr² each, side 2πrh
  • Capsule: two half spheres of 2πr² each, side 2πrh
  • Spherical cap: flat base πr², curved cap 2πRh, with r = √(2Rh − h²)
  • Conical frustum: top πr², bottom πR², side π(R + r)√((R − r)² + h²)
  • Square pyramid: base a², four sides 2a√((a/2)² + h²)
  • Ellipsoid (approximate, Knud Thomsen): 4π((aᵖbᵖ + aᵖcᵖ + bᵖcᵖ) ÷ 3)^(1/p) with p = 1.6

A worked example

A cylinder with a radius of 2 ft and a height of 6 ft: top and bottom are π × 2² = 4π ft² each, the side is 2 × π × 2 × 6 = 24π ft², so the total is 32π, about 100.53 square feet.

Questions

What is the formula for calculating surface area?

It depends on the shape: 6a² for a cube, 2(lw + lh + wh) for a box, 2πr² + 2πrh for a cylinder, 4πr² for a sphere and πr² + πr√(r² + h²) for a cone. In every case you add the areas of all the outside faces. The table above lists all ten shapes on this page.

How do you calculate the surface area of a cylinder?

Add the two circles, 2 × πr², and the curved side, 2πrh. For r = 3 and h = 4 that is 18π + 24π = 42π ≈ 131.95 square units. Leave out a circle for an open tank.

How do you calculate the surface area of a rectangular prism?

Multiply each pair of sides, add the three products and double the sum: 2(lw + lh + wh). A box 5 × 3 × 4 gives 2(15 + 20 + 12) = 94. The page lists each pair of faces so you can drop the lid.

How do you calculate the surface area of a sphere?

Square the radius, multiply by π and then by 4. A sphere with a radius of 10 cm has 400π ≈ 1,256.64 cm². If you know the diameter, halve it first.

What is the difference between lateral and total surface area?

The lateral (side) area is only the curved or slanted wall: the side of a cylinder, the sloping part of a cone or the four triangles of a pyramid. The total adds the flat ends, such as the base, top and bottom. Both are shown, part by part.

What is the surface area of a cube with 3 cm sides?

Each face is 3 × 3 = 9 cm² and a cube has six faces, so the surface area is 54 cm². In general it is 6a² for an edge of a.

How do I work out the paint needed for a round tank without a lid?

Choose Cylinder, enter the radius and height, then add the Bottom and the Side (lateral) values and leave out the Top. Divide that area by the coverage on the paint tin, for example 10 square meters per liter.

How do I calculate the surface area of a triangular prism?

Add the two triangular ends and the three rectangular sides: 2 × (½ × base × height) + perimeter of the triangle × length. A 3-4-5 triangle prism 10 long has 12 + 120 = 132 square units.

Why is the ellipsoid result marked approximate?

An ellipsoid with three different semi-axes has no simple exact surface formula. The well-known approximation by Knud Thomsen used here is never off by more than about 1.06 %, and much less for shapes close to a sphere.

What is the surface area to volume ratio?

It is the outside area divided by the volume. A cube with 1 cm edges has a ratio of 6 : 1, while one with 10 cm edges has 0.6 : 1, because volume grows faster than area as things get bigger.

Can I mix feet and inches?

Yes. Open Units, switch on a different unit for each length and pick the unit next to each box. The whole answer, every part included, is then given in square feet and in square inches.
