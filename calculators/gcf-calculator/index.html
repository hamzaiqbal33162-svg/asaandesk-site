What the GCF is

The greatest common factor (GCF) of two or more whole numbers is the biggest number that divides all of them with no remainder. The factors of 24 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12 and 24, and the factors of 36 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18 and 36. The common ones are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 12, so the GCF of 24 and 36 is 12.

The same number has three names. American books say GCF or greatest common divisor (GCD), while Pakistani and British books, including Matric and O-level texts, usually say highest common factor (HCF). This GCF calculator gives the answer with the steps of two school methods, so you can copy the working into a notebook.

How to use the GCF calculator

  • Type the numbers in the Whole numbers box, separated by commas or spaces. The page starts with 48, 180, 300.
  • Use two numbers or as many as you need; they must be positive whole numbers, and they can be very long.
  • Read the Greatest common factor (GCF) at the top. If the answer is 1, the page tells you the numbers are co-prime.
  • Under it you get the GCF in prime factors and the Least common multiple (LCM) of the same numbers.
  • Open the steps for Method 1 (prime factors) and Method 2 (Euclid's algorithm), and the table of prime factors of each number.

Four ways to find the GCF, with steps

Listing factors. Write all the factors of each number and pick the largest one they share, as with 24 and 36 above. This is fine for small numbers but slow for big ones.

Prime factorization. Split each number into primes and multiply the primes they all share, each as few times as it appears in any of them. For 48, 180 and 300: 48 = 2⁴ × 3, 180 = 2² × 3² × 5 and 300 = 2² × 3 × 5². Every number has at least 2² and 3¹, and 5 is missing from 48, so the GCF is 2² × 3 = 12.

Ladder (or cake) method. Write the numbers in a row and divide them all by a prime that goes into every one, again and again. 48, 180, 300 divided by 2 gives 24, 90, 150; by 2 again gives 12, 45, 75; by 3 gives 4, 15, 25. No prime divides 4, 15 and 25 together, so stop: the GCF is the product of the primes on the side, 2 × 2 × 3 = 12.

Euclid's division method. Divide the larger number by the smaller, then the divisor by the remainder, until the remainder is 0. For 1,071 and 462: 1,071 = 2 × 462 + 147, then 462 = 3 × 147 + 21, then 147 = 7 × 21 + 0. The last divisor, 21, is the GCF. This is the quickest way for large numbers, because you never need their prime factors.

For any two numbers, GCF × LCM equals the two numbers multiplied: 12 × 72 = 864 = 24 × 36. So once you know the GCF, the LCM is quick: LCM = a × b ÷ GCF. This shortcut works only for two numbers at a time. For three or more, find the GCF of the first two and then the GCF of that answer with the next number: GCF(48, 180) = 12, and GCF(12, 300) = 12.

Word problems that use the GCF

The GCF answers questions about the largest equal pieces or groups. A floor 360 cm by 480 cm is to be covered with the largest square tiles that fit without cutting. GCF(360, 480) = 120, so 120 cm tiles work, 3 by 4 of them, 12 tiles in all.

A teacher has 24 pencils and 36 erasers and wants identical packs with nothing left over. The largest number of packs is GCF(24, 36) = 12, each with 2 pencils and 3 erasers. Simplifying fractions is the same idea: 48/180 divided top and bottom by the GCF, 12, gives 4/15 in one step.

GCF of fractions, decimals and algebra terms

Fractions. A common rule is GCF of fractions = GCF of the numerators ÷ LCM of the denominators. For 2/3 and 4/9 that is GCF(2, 4) ÷ LCM(3, 9) = 2/9, and indeed 2/3 = 3 × 2/9 and 4/9 = 2 × 2/9.

Decimals. This calculator takes whole numbers, so scale first. For 1.2 and 0.18, multiply both by 100 to get 120 and 18, find GCF(120, 18) = 6, then divide back by 100: 0.06.

Algebra. The GCF of terms such as 6x²y and 9xy³ is the GCF of the numbers times each shared letter at its lowest power: 3xy. Taking it out is the first step in factoring, for example 6x² + 9x = 3x(2x + 3), a standard question in O-level and SAT algebra.

How to get the GCF on a calculator

Many graphing calculators have it built in. On a TI-84 Plus CE, press MATH, move right to the NUM menu and choose gcd(, then type two numbers separated by a comma: gcd(48, 180) gives 12. For three numbers, nest it: gcd(gcd(48, 180), 300). Ordinary pocket calculators have no GCF key, so use Euclid's divisions on them, or type the whole list here.

Tips and common mistakes

  • The GCF can never be bigger than the smallest number. If the smallest number divides all the others, it is the GCF: GCF(8, 24, 40) = 8.
  • Do not mix up GCF and LCM. The GCF is at most the smallest number; the LCM is at least the largest.
  • In prime factors, take each shared prime the fewest times it appears, not the most (that gives the LCM).
  • A GCF of 1 is a real answer: 8 and 9 share no factor except 1, so they are co-prime even though neither is prime.

How it works

Type two or more positive whole numbers, separated by commas or spaces. The greatest common factor (GCF) is the largest whole number that divides every one of them exactly. It is worked out two ways as you type: from the primes the numbers share, and with Euclid's algorithm, the long division method many school books teach for the HCF.

The formula

  • With prime factors: GCF = the product of the primes found in every number, each taken the fewest times it appears
  • Euclid's algorithm: GCF(a, b) = GCF(b, r), where a = q × b + r; stop when r = 0, the last divisor is the GCF
  • GCF(a, b, c) = GCF(GCF(a, b), c), and so on for longer lists
  • For two numbers: GCF(a, b) × LCM(a, b) = a × b

A worked example

GCF of 48 and 180: 48 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 and 180 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 × 5. Both contain 2 twice and 3 once, so the GCF is 2 × 2 × 3 = 12. With Euclid: 180 = 3 × 48 + 36, then 48 = 1 × 36 + 12, then 36 = 3 × 12 + 0, so the GCF is 12.

Questions

How do I calculate the GCF of 2 numbers?

Divide the larger by the smaller, then divide the smaller by the remainder, and repeat until the remainder is 0; the last divisor is the GCF. You can also split both into primes and multiply the primes they share. Type the two numbers above to see both methods worked out.

How do I calculate the GCF of 3 numbers?

Find the GCF of the first two, then the GCF of that answer and the third number. For 48, 180 and 300: GCF(48, 180) = 12 and GCF(12, 300) = 12. The calculator does this for any number of values.

What is the GCF of 24 and 36?

12. The factors 24 and 36 share are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 12, and 12 is the largest. Their LCM is 72, and 12 × 72 = 24 × 36 = 864.

How do I find the GCF and LCM together?

Write each number in primes. The GCF takes each shared prime the fewest times it appears, the LCM takes every prime the most times. For two numbers you can also use LCM = a × b ÷ GCF. This page shows both results.

How do I find the GCF fast?

Check first whether the smallest number divides all the others; if it does, it is the GCF. Otherwise use Euclid's division method, which needs only a few divisions even for large numbers.

How do I get the GCF on a TI-84 calculator?

Press MATH, go right to NUM and pick gcd(, then type the two numbers with a comma between them and close the bracket. For more numbers, put one gcd( inside another.

Can I find the GCF of fractions or decimals?

Yes, by hand. For fractions, divide the GCF of the numerators by the LCM of the denominators. For decimals, multiply all of them by the same power of 10 to make whole numbers, find the GCF here, then divide back.

Are GCF, GCD and HCF the same thing?

Yes. Greatest common factor, greatest common divisor and highest common factor are three names for the same number. Pakistani and British school books usually say HCF.

What does it mean when the GCF is 1?

The numbers are co-prime: no whole number other than 1 divides all of them. Two different primes, such as 17 and 31, are always co-prime, but so are 8 and 9.

Why is 0 not allowed?

Every number divides 0, so a list with 0 in it says nothing about 0. To keep the answer clear, only positive whole numbers are accepted.

Where is the GCF used?

To simplify fractions (divide the top and bottom by their GCF), to split things into the largest equal groups, and as a step towards the least common multiple.
