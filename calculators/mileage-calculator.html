What this mileage calculator works out

This mileage calculator plans the cost and time of a drive. From a trip distance, your average speed, your car's fuel economy and the fuel price, it gives the fuel cost, the driving time and the fuel needed, plus the cost per mile or kilometre. It can make the trip a round trip and split the cost between passengers.

If you do not know the distance, the second tab measures it between two places from their map coordinates and links to the road route. Mileage can also mean a car's fuel economy, the "mileage" or fuel average in km per litre; to measure that from a fill-up, use our Gas Mileage Calculator.

How to use the mileage calculator

  • Trip distance tab: type the one-way road distance and pick miles or km.
  • Between two places tab: paste the start and end points as latitude, longitude (like 31.5204, 74.3587). Open the route link in the result, read the road distance, and type it in "Road distance from the map" for exact figures.
  • Average speed: in mph or km/h, including slow stretches but not stops.
  • Fuel economy: in mpg (US), mpg (UK), L/100 km, km/L or litres per mile, whichever your car or bike uses.
  • Fuel price: per US gallon or per litre, in the currency you choose.
  • Under More options: tick "Round trip (there and back)" and set "Split the cost between" for shared rides.

The mileage formulas

Driving time is distance ÷ speed. Fuel needed is distance ÷ fuel economy when economy is in miles per gallon or km per litre, or distance × L/100 km ÷ 100 when it is in litres per 100 km. Fuel cost is fuel × price. Everything is converted with exact factors: 1 mile is 1.609344 km and 1 US gallon is 3.785411784 litres.

The straight-line distance between two places uses Vincenty's formula on the WGS-84 ellipsoid, the shape of the Earth that GPS uses, so it is accurate to within a few millimetres. A road is always longer than that line, often by 10% to 40%, which is why the page asks for the road distance.

Worked examples: a road trip and a drive in Pakistan

  • Road trip in miles: 280 miles each way, there and back, at an average of 60 mph in a car doing 28 mpg, with gas at $3.25 a gallon and three people sharing. The round trip is 560 miles (901.2 km), takes 9 h 20 min of driving and needs 20 US gallons, so the fuel costs $65.00, or $21.67 each, about $0.116 a mile.
  • Lahore to Islamabad from coordinates: with Lahore at 31.5204, 74.3587 and Islamabad at 33.6844, 73.0479, the straight line is 269.7 km (167.6 miles). If the route link shows a road distance of 380 km and you type it in, the road is 1.41 times the straight line. At 100 km/h, 14 km/L and an example price of Rs 265 a litre, the drive takes 3 h 48 min and needs 27.14 litres, about Rs 7,192.86 of petrol.
  • Motorbike commute: 60 km of riding at 35 km/h on a bike that does 45 km/L needs 1.33 litres; at the same example price that is Rs 353.33, about Rs 5.89 per km.
  • Europe in L/100 km: 500 km at 90 km/h in a car using 6.5 L/100 km needs 32.5 litres; at €1.65 a litre the fuel costs €53.63 and the drive takes 5 h 33 min.

Mileage reimbursement and the 2026 IRS rate

Many employers repay business driving at a flat rate per mile instead of the fuel you bought. In the United States the IRS standard mileage rate for business use in 2026 is 72.5 cents a mile from January 1 to June 30 and 76 cents a mile from July 1 to December 31; for 2025 it was 70 cents. The 2026 rate for medical and military moving trips is 20.5 cents and then 23.5 cents, and for charity driving 14 cents.

To work out a claim, multiply the business miles in your log by the rate in force on the dates you drove. The flat rate covers fuel, wear, insurance and depreciation, so it is higher than the fuel cost this page shows. Rates change, so check the IRS page, or your employer's policy, for the year you claim. Outside the US, use the rate your employer or tax authority publishes.

Tips for accurate trip planning

  • Use the road distance whenever you can. The straight line is a lower limit, useful for flights or for a quick sense of how far apart two places are.
  • Pick a realistic average speed. Motorway stretches may average 90 to 100 km/h (55 to 65 mph), but city streets and traffic can halve that; add time for breaks and fuel stops.
  • Use your real fuel economy. A figure measured from your own fill-ups is better than the brochure figure, and a loaded car, roof box or air conditioning uses more.
  • Check the price unit: a price per litre and a price per gallon differ by a factor of about 3.79.
  • For a long drive, plan where to refuel: divide your tank size by the fuel economy to see how far one tank goes.

Finding coordinates and distances between places

In most map apps you can long-press a place on a phone, or right-click it on a computer, to see and copy its latitude and longitude. Latitude comes first; south and west are negative, or you can write N, S, E and W instead, like 31.5204 N, 74.3587 E.

The calculator does not look up place names, so nothing you type is sent to a map service. The route link opens OpenStreetMap only when you click it, and its road distance can be typed back in. For straight-line distances between points without the trip costs, see our Distance Calculator.

How it works

If you already know the road distance (from a map app or a road sign), type it in the Trip distance tab. If you only know the places, use Between two places and paste each place's latitude and longitude, the way map apps copy them (40.7128, -74.0060). The calculator measures the straight line between them over the Earth's surface, the same way GPS does, and gives a link that opens the driving route on OpenStreetMap, a free map, where you can read the exact road distance. Type that number in the optional road box and every figure switches to the road. Driving time comes from your average speed, and the fuel and its cost from your car's fuel economy and the fuel price. Under More options you can make it a round trip and split the cost between passengers.

The formula

  • Straight-line distance: Vincenty's formula on the WGS-84 ellipsoid (a = 6,378,137 m, f = 1 ÷ 298.257223563)
  • Driving time = distance ÷ average speed
  • Fuel (litres) = km × L/100 km ÷ 100; fuel (US gallons) = miles ÷ mpg
  • Fuel cost = fuel × price; 1 mile = 1.609344 km, 1 US gallon = 3.785411784 L

A worked example

New York (40.7128, -74.0060) to Boston (42.3601, -71.0589) is 190.4 miles in a straight line. The road route is longer, about 215 miles. At an average of 55 mph that takes 215 ÷ 55 = 3.91 hours, or 3 h 55 min, and a car doing 25 mpg needs 215 ÷ 25 = 8.6 gallons, which at $3.50 a gallon is $30.10.

Questions

How do I calculate mileage for a trip?

Enter the trip distance, your average speed, your car's fuel economy and the fuel price. The calculator divides the distance by the economy for the fuel, multiplies by the price for the cost, and divides the distance by the speed for the time. 280 miles each way at 28 mpg and $3.25 a gallon costs $65.00 there and back.

What is the mileage formula?

Fuel needed = distance ÷ fuel economy (miles per gallon or km per litre), fuel cost = fuel × price, and driving time = distance ÷ average speed. For a car's own mileage, divide the distance driven by the fuel used to refill the tank.

How do I calculate mileage per litre?

Divide the kilometres driven by the litres used: 412 km on 31.5 litres is 13.08 km/L. Our Gas Mileage Calculator does it from two odometer readings and a fill-up; this page then uses that km/L to plan trips.

How do I calculate the mileage of a bike or scooty?

The same way as a car: fill the tank, ride, refill and divide the kilometres by the litres. Then enter that km/L here; a bike doing 45 km/L needs 1.33 litres for 60 km.

What is the mileage reimbursement rate for 2026?

The IRS business rate is 72.5 cents per mile from January 1 to June 30, 2026, and 76 cents per mile from July 1 to December 31, 2026. Multiply your logged business miles by the rate for the dates you drove, and check the IRS page for any change.

How do I calculate mileage for taxes or work?

Keep a log of each business trip with the date, purpose and miles, add up the miles, and multiply by the rate your employer or tax authority uses, such as the IRS standard mileage rate in the US. Commuting from home to a regular workplace usually does not count as business driving.

Why is the straight-line distance shorter than the drive?

Roads bend around hills, rivers, buildings and private land, so a drive is almost always longer than the straight line between two points, often by 10% to 40%. That is why the result says so and gives a route link: read the road distance there and type it in the road box.

How do I find a place's latitude and longitude?

In most map apps, long-press (phone) or right-click (computer) the place: the coordinates appear and can be copied, like 40.7128, -74.0060. Latitude comes first; south and west are negative, or you can write N, S, E and W instead.

Why can I not just type the city names?

Turning names into map points and working out a road route needs an outside map service. This calculator does all its maths in your browser and does not send your places to any service; the route link opens OpenStreetMap only when you click it.

How accurate is the driving time?

It is the distance divided by the average speed you choose, with no stops, traffic or speed limits. On motorways 55 to 65 mph (90 to 100 km/h) is a fair average; in towns it can be half that. Add time for breaks.
