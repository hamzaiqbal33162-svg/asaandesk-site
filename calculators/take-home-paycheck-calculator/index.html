What is take-home pay?

Take-home pay, also called net pay, is what actually reaches your bank account on payday. It is your gross pay, the salary or wages before anything is taken out, minus taxes and deductions: federal income tax withholding, Social Security and Medicare (together called FICA), state and city income tax where they apply, and pre-tax deductions such as 401(k) contributions and health insurance.

This take-home paycheck calculator works out each of those amounts for one paycheck and for the year, for the 2026 and 2027 tax years. It follows the IRS percentage method that employers use with Form W-4, and it also tells you what to write on your own W-4 so the amount withheld matches your situation.

The basic formula is simple: take-home pay = gross pay − taxes − deductions. The hard part is the federal income tax, which depends on your filing status, your dependents, other jobs and deductions. That is the part this calculator does for you.

Your pay stub shows the same pieces. Gross pay is at the top, then each tax and deduction for this pay period, then net pay at the bottom, usually with year-to-date totals beside each line. If your stub does not match this calculator, compare line by line: the usual causes are a different W-4, a deduction you forgot, or a state or local tax.

How to use the take-home paycheck calculator

  • Enter your Yearly pay from this job before tax (default 80,000). If you are self-employed, enter your yearly profit.
  • Choose How often you are paid: daily (260 working days), weekly (52), every two weeks (26, the default), twice a month (24), monthly (12), quarterly, twice a year or once a year.
  • Pick your Filing status: single, married filing jointly or surviving spouse, married filing separately, or head of household.
  • Choose whether You are an employee or self-employed, and the Tax year (2026, or 2027 for paychecks from January).
  • Open Deductions from your pay for 401(k), 403(b) or TSP contributions and for pre-tax health insurance, HSA and FSA money, each as a yearly amount.
  • Open Family and age for children under 17, other dependents, and whether you or your spouse are 65 or older.
  • Open Other jobs and income if you have income not from a job, or another job, or a spouse who works; enter the pay from the other jobs.
  • Open Deductions on your tax return for itemized deductions, IRA and student loan interest adjustments, qualified tips, qualified overtime premium, car loan interest on a new US-assembled car and cash gifts to charity.
  • Open State and city tax to enter your state and city income tax rates.

Most people only need the first three boxes. The optional sections make the federal figure closer to your real paycheck when your situation is more complex.

Federal tax numbers for 2026 and 2027

Federal withholding uses the tax brackets and standard deduction for the year, from IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32 for 2026 and Rev. Proc. 2026-35 for 2027. The standard deduction is 16,100 for single filers in 2026 (16,600 in 2027), 32,200 for married couples filing jointly (33,200) and 24,150 for heads of household (24,925).

Payroll taxes do not use brackets. Social Security is 6.2% of wages up to the wage base, 184,500 in 2026 and 192,300 in 2027, and stops above it. Medicare is 1.45% of all wages, and employers must withhold another 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax on wages above 200,000 in a year. On the default paycheck, taxable income is 80,000 − 16,100 = 63,900, so the year's tax is 10% of 12,400, plus 12% of 38,000, plus 22% of 13,500: 8,770, or 337.31 per paycheck.

How the employer gets from the W-4 to the number on your stub: IRS Publication 15-T's percentage method turns each paycheck into a yearly amount, adds Step 4(a) other income, takes off Step 4(b) deductions and the standard deduction built into the tables, works out the tax at your status's brackets, subtracts the Step 3 credits, divides by the number of paychecks and adds any Step 4(c) extra. Step 2 (multiple jobs) raises the amount for households with more than one job, because each employer otherwise applies the full standard deduction and the low brackets to its own pay.

The 2026 Form W-4 also covers deductions added in 2025 for the years 2025 to 2028: up to 25,000 of qualified tips, up to 12,500 of qualified overtime premium (25,000 joint), up to 10,000 of interest on a loan for a new US-assembled car, and 6,000 for each person 65 or older, each reduced at higher incomes. Enter them under Deductions on your tax return and the calculator puts them into Step 4(b).

Your employer also pays its own 6.2% Social Security and 1.45% Medicare on your wages. That share does not come out of your paycheck, so it does not lower take-home pay, but it is why self-employed people pay both halves, 15.3%, as self-employment tax.

Worked examples: 401(k), family, two jobs and self-employed

Saving in a 401(k). With 6,000 a year going to a 401(k) on the default 80,000 salary, taxable pay falls to 74,000, minus the 16,100 standard deduction is 57,900, which has 7,450 of 2026 tax, or 286.54 per paycheck. Social Security and Medicare do not change. Take-home pay falls only from 2,504.23 to 2,324.23, although 230.77 per paycheck goes into savings, because the 401(k) saves 50.77 of tax each time. Add 3,000 a year of pre-tax health insurance and take-home is 2,243.06, with Social Security and Medicare lower too.

A married couple with children. A married couple filing jointly, with one job paying 100,000 and two children under 17, has 124.62 of federal tax withheld per paycheck after the Step 3 child credits, and takes home 3,427.31 every two weeks, or 89,110 a year.

Two jobs in one household. If you earn 60,000 and your spouse earns 40,000, filing jointly, the calculator uses the 2026 Form W-4 Multiple Jobs table. Withholding on your job rises to 270.38 per paycheck and take-home is 1,860.77. If you leave Step 2 blank, you could instead pay 1,047.50 a quarter with Form 1040-ES.

A single parent. Head of household with a 60,000 salary and one child: federal withholding is only 67.23 per paycheck and take-home pay is 2,063.92.

Self-employed. With 80,000 of yearly profit, nobody withholds for you. The calculator shows 352.35 of Social Security and 82.40 of Medicare (self-employment tax) and 205.53 of income tax to set aside per two-week period, 4,161.87 a quarter in estimated tax, and 2,436.64 left per period.

Next year. The same default paycheck in 2027 has 326.52 of federal tax withheld instead of 337.31, because the brackets and standard deduction rise with inflation, so take-home pay is 2,515.02.

Tips, overtime and age. If 10,000 of a 50,000 yearly pay is qualified tips, federal withholding falls from 146.92 to 100.77 per paycheck and take-home rises to 1,675.19. With 5,000 of qualified overtime premium on 60,000, withholding falls from 193.08 to 170.00. At 65 or older, the extra standard deduction and the senior deduction cut it to 155.92 on 60,000.

Other income and itemizing. With 5,000 a year of interest or rent on top of the default 80,000 salary, withholding rises to 379.62 per paycheck so that tax is covered, or you can leave it off the W-4 and pay 275.00 a quarter instead. If you expect 25,000 of itemized deductions, above the 16,100 standard deduction, withholding falls to 262.00 and take-home pay rises to 2,579.54.

State and city income tax: Texas, Florida, California and others

Nine states have no broad-based individual income tax: Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington and Wyoming. If you live and work in one of them, leave the state rate at 0 and your take-home pay is the same as in the salary table above. A take-home paycheck in Texas or Florida is therefore mainly federal tax, Social Security and Medicare.

Fifteen states use a single flat rate in 2026, including Illinois, Pennsylvania, Michigan, North Carolina, Colorado and Arizona; enter that rate. States with graduated rates, such as California and New York, charge more on higher income, so enter your effective rate: your expected state tax for the year divided by your pay. The calculator applies the state and city rates to gross pay.

California also withholds State Disability Insurance (SDI). For 2026 the California EDD rate is 1.3% with no wage limit, so add 1.3 to your state rate. On the default 80,000 salary, 1.3% is 40.00 per paycheck; a combined 5% state rate would take 153.85 and leave 2,350.38. Some cities and counties add a local income tax; enter it in the city box.

Bonuses, pre-tax deductions and getting your W-4 right

A few rules decide how much of a bonus or a pre-tax deduction reaches your bank account, and how close your withholding comes to the tax you really owe.

  • Bonuses are supplemental wages. Employers may withhold a flat 22% of federal income tax from a bonus paid separately, and withholding on supplemental wages above 1 million in a year is 37%. The bonus is then taxed with the rest of your income on your return, so the flat rate is only a prepayment.
  • The 401(k), 403(b) and TSP elective deferral limit is 24,500 for 2026 and 25,500 for 2027 (more with catch-up contributions from age 50). These contributions lower income tax but not Social Security or Medicare.
  • Pre-tax health insurance, HSA and FSA money taken through a cafeteria plan is free of income tax, Social Security and Medicare, which is why it has its own box.
  • Fill in Steps 3 and 4 on the W-4 for your highest-paying job only. If you have two jobs, or you and your spouse both work, use Step 2, or your withholding will usually be too low.
  • Check your withholding after a life change, such as marriage, a new baby or a second job, so you are not surprised by a bill or a big refund.
  • Withholding is not your final tax. Your return settles the year; the Income Tax Calculator estimates the refund or amount owed.
  • Common mistake: using monthly pay in the yearly box. Every amount in this calculator is per year, including the 401(k) and health deductions.
  • Common mistake: counting a 401(k) as lowering Social Security. It does not; only cafeteria-plan health, HSA and FSA money does.
  • Common mistake: ignoring a second job. Each employer withholds as if its job were your only one, so two jobs without Step 2 usually leave you owing tax in April.

Take-home pay outside the US: UK and Pakistan

This calculator follows US rules only. Other countries take tax from pay in a similar way, through the employer, but with their own rates. In the UK, for the 2026 to 2027 tax year, the Personal Allowance is £12,570; income tax is 20% from £12,571 to £50,270, 40% up to £125,140 and 45% above, with different bands in Scotland. Employee National Insurance is 8% on earnings from £242 to £967 a week and 2% above.

In Pakistan, the employer deducts income tax from salary every month under section 149 of the Income Tax Ordinance. The FBR's withholding tax rate card for tax year 2027 (1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027, under the Finance Act 2026) gives the yearly salary slabs below; your monthly deduction is the year's tax spread over the months. Rates change with every budget.

How it works

Enter your yearly pay, how often you are paid and your filing status. The calculator splits the year into paychecks and takes off Social Security (6.2% up to the yearly wage base), Medicare (1.45%, plus the 0.9% your employer withholds on pay above $200,000), your state and city rates, and your pre-tax deductions. Federal income tax follows the IRS percentage method for a 2020-or-later Form W-4: your taxable pay for the year, minus the standard deduction built into the tables and the deductions in Step 4(b), taxed at the brackets for your status, minus the Step 3 credits for children and other dependents. If you or your spouse have another job, the Multiple Jobs Worksheet amount is added. You also get the W-4 numbers to give your employer. If you are self-employed, it shows self-employment tax and the income tax to set aside from each payment instead.

The formula

  • Gross pay per paycheck = yearly pay ÷ paychecks a year (52 weekly, 26 every two weeks, 24 twice a month, 12 monthly, 260 daily)
  • Social Security = 6.2% × pay up to the wage base; Medicare = 1.45% × pay + 0.9% × pay above $200,000 (401(k) money counts, cafeteria-plan health money does not)
  • Federal withholding for the year = tax on (taxable pay + Step 4(a) − Step 4(b) − standard deduction) − Step 3 credits + Multiple Jobs amount
  • Step 4(b) = itemized deductions above the standard deduction (or cash gifts to charity) + extra standard deduction at 65+ + tips, overtime, car loan interest and senior deductions + other adjustments
  • Take-home pay = gross pay − federal − Social Security − Medicare − state − city − pre-tax deductions

A worked example

A single person earning $80,000 a year, paid every two weeks, with $6,000 a year going to a 401(k): taxable pay is $74,000, minus the $16,100 standard deduction = $57,900, which has $7,450 of 2026 tax, or $286.54 per paycheck. Social Security is 6.2% of $80,000 ÷ 26 = $190.77 and Medicare $44.62. Take-home pay is $3,076.92 − $286.54 − $190.77 − $44.62 − $230.77 = $2,324.23.

Questions

How do I calculate my take-home pay?

Start with gross pay for the pay period, then subtract federal income tax withholding, Social Security (6.2%), Medicare (1.45%), any state and city tax, and pre-tax deductions such as a 401(k). The calculator works out the federal part from your filing status and W-4 details.

What is take-home pay on 100k?

For a single filer paid every two weeks in 2026 with no state tax and no pre-tax deductions, it is 3,045.38 per paycheck, or about 79,180 a year. A state income tax, 401(k) contributions or health insurance would lower it.

What is take-home pay on 50,000?

About 42,355 a year for a single filer in 2026 in a state with no income tax: 1,629.04 every two weeks or 3,529.58 a month. On 40,000 it is about 34,320, and on 30,000 about 26,285.

How much is taken out of my paycheck in Texas?

Texas has no state income tax, so only federal income tax, Social Security and Medicare come out, plus any deductions you choose. On 80,000 a year, single, paid every two weeks, that is 337.31 + 190.77 + 44.62, leaving 2,504.23.

How do I calculate take-home pay in California?

Work out the federal part as usual, then add California's tax. Enter your effective California income tax rate plus the 1.3% SDI for 2026 in the state rate box, because the calculator applies a flat state rate to gross pay.

How is a bonus taxed on my paycheck?

Employers may withhold a flat 22% of federal income tax from a bonus paid separately, plus Social Security and Medicare; above 1 million of supplemental wages in a year the rate is 37%. Your return then taxes the bonus with your other income.

Why is my federal tax lower than my tax bracket suggests?

Only the part of your pay above the standard deduction is taxed, and it moves through the brackets: 10% on the first slice, 12% on the next and so on. So someone in the 22% bracket usually has well under 22% of their pay withheld.

Does my 401(k) lower Social Security and Medicare?

No. 401(k), 403(b) and TSP contributions lower income tax only. Pre-tax health insurance, HSA and FSA money taken through a cafeteria plan is free of income tax, Social Security and Medicare, which is why they are two separate boxes here.

What should I put on my W-4?

Use the numbers in the W-4 table under the result. Fill in Steps 3 and 4 on one W-4 only, the one for your highest-paying job. If you would rather not tell your employer about other income or a second job, leave Steps 2, 4(a) and 4(c) blank and pay the quarterly amount shown with Form 1040-ES instead.

I am self-employed. What do I set aside?

Choose self-employed. Nobody withholds for you, so the calculator shows self-employment tax (15.3% on 92.35% of your profit, less above the wage base) and the extra income tax your business adds after half of that tax and the qualified business income deduction. Pay it to the IRS each quarter.

Why does Social Security stop late in the year for high earners?

Social Security tax only applies up to the yearly wage base (184,500 in 2026, 192,300 in 2027). This calculator spreads it evenly over the year; on real paychecks it is taken from each check until your pay reaches the limit, then stops.

Can I use it for take-home pay in the UK or Pakistan?

No, it follows US federal and state rules. The guide above lists the UK's 2026 to 2027 income tax bands and National Insurance rates and Pakistan's salary slabs for tax year 2027, which work the same way: tax is taken by the employer from each paycheck.
