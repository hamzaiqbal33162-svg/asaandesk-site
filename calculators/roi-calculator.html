What an ROI calculator tells you

Return on investment (ROI) measures how much an investment earned compared with what it cost. An ROI calculator takes the amount you put in and the amount you got back, or what the investment is worth now, and shows the gain or loss in money and as a percentage of the amount invested.

ROI on its own ignores time, so this calculator also gives the yearly, or annualized, ROI. That is the steady compound rate per year that would have turned your money into the same result. It lets you compare a share you held for 4 years with a property you held for 10, or with the rate on a savings account.

How to use the ROI calculator

  • Amount invested: everything you put in, including fees, commissions, taxes paid on purchase and improvement costs.
  • Amount you got back: what you sold it for after selling costs, or what it is worth today, plus any dividends, rent or interest you received along the way.
  • Time held: choose Length in years (decimals allowed, such as 2.5) or Start and end dates.
  • Currency: choose dollars, Pakistani rupees or another currency; ROI is a percentage, so the currency only changes how the gain is shown.

The amount invested must be above 0 and the time must be more than 0 years; with dates, the start date has to be before the end date.

ROI formula and annualized ROI, with examples

The ROI formula is ROI = (amount returned − amount invested) ÷ amount invested × 100. The annualized ROI formula is (amount returned ÷ amount invested)^(1 ÷ years) − 1, the same idea as a compound annual growth rate (CAGR). In Excel or Google Sheets, with the amount invested in B1, the amount returned in B2 and the years in B3, use =(B2-B1)/B1 for ROI and =(B2/B1)^(1/B3)-1 for the yearly ROI.

Example 1, shares in dollars: $8,000 grows to $11,600 in 4 years. The ROI is 45.00% and the yearly ROI is 9.73%.

Example 2, shares in rupees with dates: you bought Pakistan Stock Exchange shares for Rs 250,000 on 15 March 2021 and they were worth Rs 410,000 on 22 November 2026. The gain is Rs 160,000, an ROI of 64.00% over 5.690 years, which is 9.08% a year.

Example 3, a loss: $6,000 that falls to $4,800 over 2 years is an ROI of −20.00%, or −10.56% a year.

ROI on property, projects and marketing

Rental or investment property: count the price plus stamp duty, transfer fees, agent fees and renovation as the amount invested, and the sale price after costs plus the net rent received as the amount returned. For example, if a house cost Rs 9,000,000 in all and, after 5 years, you sold it and collected rent for a total of Rs 12,700,000, the ROI is 41.11% and the yearly ROI is 7.13%. The Rental Property Calculator goes into cash flow, cap rate and yield in more detail.

Projects and marketing: use the extra profit the spending brought in, not the sales. If $2,500 of advertising produced sales with $5,600 of gross profit, the ROI is 124.00%. Over a quarter of a year the calculator shows a yearly ROI of 2,417.63% and warns that it assumes the same growth for a whole year, which a single campaign rarely repeats.

Short holdings in general: 10% earned in 6 months is 21.00% a year, because the 10% compounds twice. Treat yearly figures for holdings under a year with care.

What ROI does not tell you

  • Risk: two investments with the same ROI can carry very different chances of loss. Compare like with like.
  • Money added or taken out along the way: ROI treats everything as invested at the start and returned at the end. For several cash flows at different dates, the IRR Calculator gives a truer yearly rate, and the Average Return Calculator handles deposits and withdrawals.
  • Inflation: a 9% yearly ROI during 8% inflation is only about 1% of real growth; the Inflation Calculator shows the buying power.
  • Taxes: use amounts after tax on gains, dividends or rent if you want the return you actually keep.
  • ROIC is a different measure: return on invested capital compares a company's operating profit after tax with all the capital invested in the business, and is used to judge companies rather than your own investments.

Tips and related calculators

  • Include every cost on the invested side and every receipt on the returned side; leaving out fees is the most common reason an ROI looks too good.
  • Compare yearly ROI, not total ROI, when the holding periods differ.
  • Use a realistic current value for investments you still hold, and say so when you report the ROI.
  • Related tools: the Investment Calculator to project growth, the Compound Interest Calculator, the Payback Period Calculator to see how long a project takes to repay its cost, and the Margin Calculator for profit on sales.

How it works

The gain is what you got back minus what you put in, and the ROI is that gain as a percentage of the amount invested. Because a 50% gain over one year is far better than the same gain over ten, the calculator also gives the yearly ROI: the steady compound rate that would turn the amount invested into the amount returned over the time you held it. With dates, the time is counted in whole years from the start date plus the share of the last year.

The formula

  • Gain = amount returned − amount invested
  • ROI = gain ÷ amount invested × 100
  • Yearly ROI = (amount returned ÷ amount invested)^(1 ÷ years) − 1
  • Years between dates = whole years from the start date + days since the last anniversary ÷ days in that year

A worked example

Invest $8,000 in shares and get back $11,600 after 4 years. The gain is $3,600, so the ROI is 3,600 ÷ 8,000 = 45%. The yearly ROI is 1.45^(1 ÷ 4) − 1 = 9.73% a year, less than 45 ÷ 4 = 11.25% because the growth compounds.

Questions

How do I calculate ROI?

Subtract the amount invested from the amount you got back, divide by the amount invested and multiply by 100. $8,000 that grows to $11,600 has a gain of $3,600 and an ROI of 45%.

How do I calculate ROI percentage per year?

Divide the amount returned by the amount invested, raise it to the power 1 ÷ years and subtract 1. For a 45% gain over 4 years that is 1.45^(1 ÷ 4) − 1 = 9.73% a year.

How do I calculate ROI in Excel or Google Sheets?

With the amount invested in B1 and the amount returned in B2, ROI is =(B2-B1)/B1 formatted as a percent. Add the years in B3 and the yearly ROI is =(B2/B1)^(1/B3)-1.

How do I calculate ROI on a rental property?

Add the purchase price and every buying and repair cost for the amount invested, and the sale price after costs plus the net rent you received for the amount returned. Then enter the years you owned it to get the yearly ROI.

What is a good ROI?

It depends on the risk and the time. Compare the yearly ROI, not the total, with what you could have earned elsewhere over the same time, such as a savings account or a broad stock index.

Why is the yearly ROI lower than ROI divided by the years?

Gains compound: each year's growth builds on the growth before it. A 45% gain over 4 years needs only 9.73% a year, not 11.25%, because the later years grow from a bigger amount.

Should costs and income be included?

Yes, for a fair figure. Add fees, taxes and repair costs to the amount invested, and add dividends, rent or interest you received to the amount you got back.

What does ROI not show?

It treats all the money as going in at the start and coming out at the end. If you added or took out money along the way, the IRR or average return calculators give a truer yearly rate.
