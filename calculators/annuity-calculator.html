How it works

This covers the saving years of an annuity, before any payout. Enter what you start with, what you add each year and each month, the yearly growth rate and the number of years. The rate is a yearly rate that is turned into the monthly rate that gives exactly the same growth over a year (its 12th root), and the balance grows month by month. Additions made at the start of a period earn that period's growth; additions at the end start earning in the next one. A yearly addition goes in in the first month of each year (start) or after the twelfth month (end).

The formula

  • Monthly rate j = (1 + rate ÷ 100)^(1/12) − 1
  • Each month: balance = (balance + additions at the start) × (1 + j) + additions at the end
  • Closed form: end balance = P(1 + R)^N + A((1 + R)^N − 1) ÷ R + M((1 + j)^12N − 1) ÷ j, each addition term × (1 + R) or (1 + j) when made at the start; R = rate ÷ 100
  • Total additions = N × (A + 12 × M); total return = end balance − starting amount − total additions

A worked example

Start with $20,000 and add $10,000 at the start of each year for 10 years at 6%. The first month holds $30,000, which grows by j = 1.06^(1/12) − 1 ≈ 0.4868%, so $146.03 in month 1 and $31,800 after a year (exactly 6% on $30,000). After 10 years the balance is $175,533.38: you added $100,000 on top of the $20,000, so $55,533.38 is growth.

Questions

Is the growth rate compounded monthly?

The rate is a yearly rate. It is turned into the monthly rate that gives the same yearly growth, so $100 with no additions grows to exactly $106 in a year at 6%. A rate quoted as 6% compounded monthly would grow a little more.

What is the difference between start and end of each period?

Additions at the start of each period (an annuity due) earn growth in the period they are made, so they end up worth more. Additions at the end (an ordinary annuity) start earning one period later.

Can I add both yearly and monthly amounts?

Yes. Both use the same timing, and both appear in the schedules. Leave either one at 0 if you do not use it.

What happens after the saving years?

That is the payout phase. Use the Annuity Payout Calculator with the end balance from here to see how much you can take out each month, or how long a payment will last.
