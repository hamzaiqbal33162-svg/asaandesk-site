What long division is

A long division calculator splits a big division into small steps you can do in your head. Instead of dividing 935 by 4 all at once, you divide one part of the number at a time, from left to right, and carry what is left over into the next part. It is the written method taught in primary school for dividing by numbers too big for times tables alone.

Every division has four parts. The dividend is the number being shared (935), the divisor is the size of each group or the number of groups (4), the quotient is how many whole times the divisor fits (233), and the remainder is what is left over (3). They always fit one check: divisor × quotient + remainder = dividend.

Short division is the same method with the subtractions done mentally and only the carried remainders written small next to the digits. Long division writes every multiply and subtract line out in full, which is why it suits two-digit and longer divisors and why teachers ask for it as working.

How to use the long division calculator with steps

How to do long division step by step

  • Divide: look at the first digit of the dividend. If the divisor is bigger, take the first two digits instead. Ask how many whole times the divisor fits and write that digit above.
  • Multiply: multiply the divisor by the digit you just wrote and put the product under the part you divided.
  • Subtract: take the product away. The result must be smaller than the divisor; if it is not, your quotient digit was too small.
  • Bring down: copy the next digit of the dividend next to the difference to make a new number.
  • Repeat until no digits are left. What remains is the remainder, or write a decimal point, bring down zeros and keep going for a decimal answer.
  • Check: multiply the quotient by the divisor and add the remainder. You should get the dividend back.

A short way to remember the order is Divide, Multiply, Subtract, Bring down, the four moves in every cycle. The calculator's steps follow exactly that order, so you can compare them line by line with your own working.

Worked examples

With a remainder, 935 ÷ 4: 4 goes into 9 two times (8), leaving 1. Bring down 3 to make 13; 4 goes in three times (12), leaving 1. Bring down 5 to make 15; 4 goes in three times (12), leaving 3. Answer: 233 R3, which is 233 3/4 or 233.75.

Past the decimal point, 100 ÷ 7 to 6 places: 7 into 10 is 1 (remainder 3), 7 into 30 is 4 (remainder 2), so 14 R2. Write the decimal point and bring down zeros: 20 gives 2, 60 gives 8, 40 gives 5, 50 gives 7, 10 gives 1 and 30 gives 4. The answer 14.285714 keeps going, because the remainder 2 has come back and the digits 285714 repeat forever.

Decimal divisor, 2.45 ÷ 0.07: move both decimal points 2 places to the right to get 245 ÷ 7. 7 into 24 is 3 (21, leaving 3), bring down 5 to make 35, and 7 into 35 is 5 exactly. So 2.45 ÷ 0.07 = 35.

Money in PKR, 75,000 ÷ 12: sharing a PKR 75,000 bill between 12 people. 12 into 75 is 6 (72, leaving 3), bring down 0 to make 30 (2 times, leaving 6), bring down 0 to make 60 (5 times, leaving 0), bring down the last 0 (0 times). Each person pays PKR 6,250 and nothing is left over.

A negative dividend, −100 ÷ 7: divide the sizes, 100 ÷ 7 = 14 R2, and put the minus sign on the answer, −14 2/7. The page also gives the form with a remainder that is never negative, −100 = 7 × (−15) + 5, which some textbooks and programming languages use.

Long division with decimals

When the dividend has a decimal point, divide as usual and put the decimal point in the answer directly above the one in the dividend. When the divisor has a decimal point, move it to the right until the divisor is whole, and move the dividend's point the same number of places, adding zeros if needed. Multiplying both numbers by the same power of 10 never changes the answer.

Examples: 3.5 ÷ 4 becomes 35 ÷ 40 and equals 0.875 (7/8). 1,250 ÷ 0.25 becomes 125,000 ÷ 25 and equals 5,000. The calculator does the scaling for you and shows it in an As whole numbers row.

Beyond numbers: HCF, polynomials and quick checks

The long division method for HCF (the highest common factor, or GCD) divides the larger number by the smaller, then divides the old divisor by the remainder, and repeats until the remainder is 0. For 84 and 36: 84 ÷ 36 = 2 R12, then 36 ÷ 12 = 3 R0, so the HCF is 12. This is Euclid's algorithm, and our GCF Calculator does it for any set of numbers.

Polynomial long division uses the same divide, multiply, subtract, bring down cycle with terms instead of digits. For (x² + 5x + 6) ÷ (x + 2): x² ÷ x = x, subtract x(x + 2) = x² + 2x to leave 3x + 6, then 3x ÷ x = 3, subtract 3(x + 2) to leave 0. The quotient is x + 3. This page divides numbers only.

Before dividing, divisibility rules tell you whether a remainder is coming. A number divides by 3 when its digit sum does (9 + 3 + 5 = 17, so 935 does not), by 4 when its last two digits do (35 does not), and by 5 when it ends in 0 or 5 (935 does).

Common mistakes and tips

  • Forgetting a zero in the quotient: when the divisor does not fit after bringing down a digit, write 0 before bringing down the next one. 6,250 = 75,000 ÷ 12 has a 0 at the end for that reason.
  • A remainder as big as the divisor means the quotient digit was too small; go up by one.
  • Moving only one decimal point: shift the dividend and the divisor by the same number of places.
  • Mixing up the order: 4 ÷ 935 is not 935 ÷ 4. The dividend is the number being shared.
  • Always finish with the check multiplication; it catches most slips.

How it works

Type the dividend (the number being divided) and the divisor. The calculator works the division the way it is written on paper: take the first digits that the divisor goes into, write how many times it goes, multiply, subtract, bring down the next digit, and repeat. The layout shows the working in columns and the steps say each move in words. Decimals are first made whole by moving both decimal points, and under More options the division can carry on past the decimal point.

The formula

  • dividend = divisor × quotient + remainder, with the remainder smaller than the divisor
  • Each cycle: bring down a digit, quotient digit = how many whole times the divisor fits, subtract that many divisors
  • With decimals: multiply both numbers by 10, 100, … until they are whole; the quotient does not change
  • Mixed number = quotient and remainder ÷ divisor; the decimal is the remainder divided on

A worked example

935 ÷ 4: 4 goes into 9 two times (8), leaving 1; bring down the 3 to make 13, 4 goes in three times (12), leaving 1; bring down the 5 to make 15, 4 goes in three times (12), leaving 3. So 935 ÷ 4 = 233 R3 = 233 3/4 = 233.75. Check: 4 × 233 + 3 = 935.

Questions

How do you do long division step by step?

Divide the first part of the dividend by the divisor, multiply, subtract, then bring down the next digit and repeat until no digits are left. What is left at the end is the remainder. Check by multiplying the quotient by the divisor and adding the remainder.

What do dividend, divisor, quotient and remainder mean?

In 935 ÷ 4 = 233 R3, 935 is the dividend, 4 is the divisor, 233 is the quotient (how many whole times 4 fits) and 3 is the remainder (what is left over).

How do I divide decimals by long division?

Move the decimal point of both numbers the same number of places to the right until both are whole. 3.5 ÷ 4 becomes 35 ÷ 40; the answer, 0.875, is the same. The calculator does this for you and says so in the steps.

How do I get decimal places instead of a remainder?

Open More options and choose how many decimal places to work out. The division then writes a decimal point, keeps bringing down zeros, and shows the digits it found and the rounded answer.

How do I write the remainder as a fraction or decimal?

Put the remainder over the divisor: 935 ÷ 4 = 233 R3 = 233 3/4. Divide that fraction on to get the decimal, 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75, so the answer is 233.75. The calculator shows all three forms.

What is the long division formula?

Dividend = divisor × quotient + remainder, where the remainder is at least 0 and smaller than the divisor. For 100 ÷ 7 that is 100 = 7 × 14 + 2.

What is the difference between long division and short division?

Both follow the same steps. Short division keeps the multiplying and subtracting in your head and writes only small carried remainders, so it suits one-digit divisors. Long division writes every line, which makes two-digit and longer divisors easier to follow.

How do I find the HCF by the long division method?

Divide the larger number by the smaller, then divide the divisor by the remainder, and keep going until the remainder is 0. The last divisor is the HCF: for 84 and 36 it is 12.

What happens with negative numbers?

The division is done on the sizes and the sign is put on the answer, so −100 ÷ 7 = −14 R2, which is the mixed number −14 2/7. A note also gives the form with a remainder that is never negative: −100 = 7 × (−15) + 5.

Why can you not divide by 0?

Division asks how many groups of the divisor make the dividend. No number of zeros ever adds up to 935, so there is no answer, and the page shows an error instead.
