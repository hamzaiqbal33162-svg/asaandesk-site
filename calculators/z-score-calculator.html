What a z-score is

A z-score (also called a standard score) tells you how far a value is from the average, measured in standard deviations. A z-score of 0 is exactly at the mean, +2 is two standard deviations above it and −1.5 is one and a half below it. Because it has no units, a z-score lets you compare values from different scales, such as a maths mark and an English mark, or a child's height and weight.

When the data follow a normal (bell-shaped) distribution, each z-score also gives a probability: the share of values below it, above it or between two points. This z-score calculator does both, from a raw value or straight from a z-score, and replaces a printed z-table.

How to use the z-score calculator

  • Between two z-scores: type the Left z-score (Z₁) and the Right z-score (Z₂). The page starts with −1 and 0.
  • The bell curve picture shades the area that the main answer describes.

Understanding the results

With the default value, z = (5 − 3) ÷ 2 = 1: the value is one standard deviation above the mean. P(x < 5) = 0.84134, so about 84.13% of values are lower and 5 sits at the 84th percentile. P(x > 5) = 0.15866 are higher, and P(3 < x < 5) = 0.34134 lie between the mean and the value.

The converter shows the same five areas for any z-score, or the z-score for an area you type. The between tool gives the area inside two z-scores, the area outside them and each tail; from −1 to 0 the area is 0.34134.

The z-score formula, with worked examples

For a single value, z = (x − μ) ÷ σ: subtract the mean, then divide by the standard deviation. For the mean of a sample of n values, divide by the standard error instead: z = (x̄ − μ) ÷ (σ ÷ √n).

  • Comparing two exams. A maths mark of 78 in a class with mean 65 and standard deviation 10 has z = 1.3. An English mark of 85 with mean 80 and standard deviation 4 has z = 1.25. The maths mark is relatively better (about the 90.3rd percentile against the 89.4th), even though the English mark is higher.
  • Quality control. A mill fills 1 kg bags of flour with a mean of 1,005 g and a standard deviation of 4 g. A 998 g bag has z = (998 − 1,005) ÷ 4 = −1.75, and P(x < −1.75) = 0.04006, so about 4% of bags weigh less than 998 g if the weights are normal.
  • A sample mean. Scores have μ = 500 and σ = 100. A group of 25 students averages 540. The standard error is 100 ÷ √25 = 20, so z = (540 − 500) ÷ 20 = 2, and the chance of a group average this high by luck alone is P(x > 2) = 0.02275.

Z-score vs standard deviation, percentile and normalization

The standard deviation measures how spread out a whole data set is, in the data's own units. A z-score describes one value, in standard deviations. If marks have a standard deviation of 10, a mark 15 above the mean has z = 1.5. The Standard Deviation Calculator gives μ and σ from a list of numbers.

A percentile is the left-tail area times 100, so z = 1 is the 84th percentile and z = −1 the 16th. Z-score normalization in data science applies the formula to every value of a column, so the column ends up with mean 0 and standard deviation 1 and different features can be compared or fed into a model on the same scale.

Z-scores in child growth and bone density

Health reports also use z-scores. In WHO child growth standards, stunting means a height-for-age more than two standard deviations below the WHO median, that is a z-score below −2. These growth z-scores are read from WHO reference tables for the child's age and sex, so use a growth chart or ask a health worker rather than this page for a child's measurements.

In a bone density (DXA) report, the Z-score compares your bone density with people of your own age and sex, while the T-score compares it with healthy young adults. A result is an estimate to discuss with your doctor, not a diagnosis on its own.

Z-scores in Excel and on a TI-84

  • Excel and Google Sheets: =STANDARDIZE(x, mean, standard_dev) gives the z-score, =NORM.S.DIST(z, TRUE) the left-tail area and =NORM.S.INV(p) the z-score for a left-tail area. For example =NORM.S.INV(0.975) returns 1.96.
  • TI-84: press 2nd then VARS for the DISTR menu. normalcdf(lower, upper, 0, 1) gives an area between two z-scores (use −1E99 for minus infinity), and invNorm(area, 0, 1) gives the z-score with that area to its left.

Common mistakes

  • Using the wrong standard deviation: for a sample mean, divide by σ ÷ √n, not σ.
  • Reading the wrong tail: a printed table may give the area from the mean, the left tail or the right tail. Here every area is labelled.
  • Assuming normality: the areas are only right when the data are roughly bell-shaped. The z-score itself is still a valid distance from the mean.
  • Forgetting the sign: −1.5 is below the mean, and its left-tail area is small (0.06681).

How it works

A z-score says how many standard deviations a value sits above or below the mean. In the first tool, type the value, the mean and the standard deviation: you get the z-score, the share of values below it (its percentile), above it and between it and the mean, with the area shaded on a bell curve. The second tool works like a z-table in both directions: type a z-score to get all five areas, or type one area (for example a right tail of 0.05) to get the z-score. The full table of areas is under the result. The third tool gives the area between two z-scores and the areas outside them.

The formula

  • z = (x − μ) ÷ σ
  • P(x < z) = Φ(z) = ½ × (1 + erf(z ÷ √2))
  • P(x > z) = 1 − Φ(z); from the mean: |Φ(z) − 0.5|
  • P(−z < x < z) = 2Φ(|z|) − 1; two tails = 2 × (1 − Φ(|z|))
  • Between two z-scores: P(z₁ < x < z₂) = Φ(z₂) − Φ(z₁)

A worked example

A test has a mean of 70 and a standard deviation of 8, and you scored 82. Your z-score is (82 − 70) ÷ 8 = 1.5. The area left of 1.5 is about 0.93319, so you scored higher than about 93.3% of test takers.

Questions

How do I calculate a z-score?

Subtract the mean from the value and divide by the standard deviation: z = (x − μ) ÷ σ. A score of 82 with mean 70 and standard deviation 8 gives z = 12 ÷ 8 = 1.5.

What does a negative z-score mean?

The value is below the mean. A z-score of −1 is one standard deviation below the mean; about 15.9% of values from a normal distribution are lower than it.

How do I turn a z-score into a percentile?

Use the left-tail area, P(x < Z), and multiply by 100. A z-score of 1 gives 0.84134, so the value is at about the 84th percentile.

What is the z-score formula for a sample mean?

z = (x̄ − μ) ÷ (σ ÷ √n), where σ ÷ √n is the standard error. A sample of 25 with mean 540, from scores with μ = 500 and σ = 100, has z = 40 ÷ 20 = 2.

How do I calculate a z-score in Excel?

Use =STANDARDIZE(x, mean, standard_dev), or type =(A1-mean)/sd. =NORM.S.DIST(z, TRUE) then gives the share of values below that z-score.

How do I calculate a z-score on a TI-84?

Work out (x − μ) ÷ σ on the home screen. For areas, use normalcdf(lower, upper, 0, 1) from the DISTR menu (2nd VARS), and invNorm(area, 0, 1) to go from an area back to z.

Which z-score cuts off the top 5%?

Type 0.05 into the right-tail box of the converter. The answer is Z = 1.6449. For 5% split over both tails (2.5% each), type 0.05 into the two-tails box to get 1.96.

What is a z-score used for?

To compare values on different scales, to find percentiles, to flag unusual values (beyond about ±3), and in hypothesis tests and confidence intervals. Growth charts and bone density reports use z-scores too.

What is the difference between a z-score and a standard deviation?

The standard deviation describes the spread of a whole data set in its own units. A z-score describes one value as a number of standard deviations from the mean, with no units.

Is this the same as a z-table?

Yes, but more exact. A printed z-table gives the area to four or five decimals for z to two decimals; here any z-score works and the areas are worked out directly. The table under the converter shows the classic layout of areas from the mean.
