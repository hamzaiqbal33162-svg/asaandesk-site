What a bra size calculator tells you

A bra size calculator turns two tape measurements into a size label such as 34B. The number is the band, the part that goes around your ribcage and carries most of the support. The letter is the cup, and it does not stand for a fixed volume: it shows how much larger your bust is than your band.

That is why the same letter means different things on different bands. A 32D and a 38D do not hold the same amount, because the D only says the bust is about 4 inches bigger than the band in each case. The calculator works out the band and cup from your measurements, then shows the matching label in eight size systems used around the world.

How to measure your bra size at home

  • Use a soft cloth or plastic tape, the kind tailors use, and stand straight in front of a mirror with your arms relaxed by your sides.
  • Wear a thin, unpadded bra, or none. A padded or push-up bra adds to the bust measurement and gives too big a cup.
  • Band (underbust): wrap the tape snugly around your ribcage, right under the bust where the band of a bra sits. Keep it level all round and breathe out normally.
  • Bust: wrap the tape loosely around the fullest part of the bust, usually across the nipples, without squeezing. Check in the mirror that it is level at the back.
  • Write both numbers down to the nearest half inch or half centimetre, and measure twice. If the two tries differ, use the average.

How to use this bra size calculator

The "From measurements" tab needs only two boxes. Type your bust into Bust and your underbust into "Band (underbust)". The US / Metric switch at the top chooses inches or centimetres, and you can also type a unit such as 86cm or 34in straight into a box.

The "Convert a size" tab is for a size you already wear. Choose the Size system on your label (US / Canada, UK, Europe, France, Belgium and Spain, Australia and New Zealand, UK dress size, or the band + 4 method), then pick the Band and the Cup from the lists.

  • The three large results are your size in the first three systems; the rows under them give the other systems.
  • "Bust minus underbust" shows the difference that decides the cup, in inches and centimetres.
  • The table lists the band, cup and full size in every system, and the steps show exactly how the band and cup were rounded.
  • If the difference is more than 8 inches, the calculator says the cup is outside its table; full-bust brands and a professional fitting cover those sizes.

The bra size formula: band and cup

The band is your underbust in inches rounded to the nearest even number, because inch bands come in even sizes only (28, 30, 32 and so on). An underbust of 31.5 inches becomes a 32 band; 29 inches, exactly halfway, goes up to 30. Then subtract the underbust from the bust and count the whole inches of difference to get the cup.

Worked examples in centimetres and inches

Metric example: an underbust of 78 cm and a bust of 92 cm. The underbust is 30.71 inches, so the US and UK band is 30. The difference is 14 cm, or 5.51 inches, which is 5 whole inches. The calculator gives 30DD/E in the US, 30DD in the UK, 70G in Europe, 85G in France, Belgium and Spain, and 10DD in Australia.

Inch example: an underbust of 31.5 inches and a bust of 36 inches. The band rounds to 32 and the difference is 4.5 inches, so 4 whole inches and a D cup: 32D in the US and the UK. In centimetres the difference is 11.4 cm, five 2 cm steps, so the European size is 70E and the French size 85E. With the band + 4 method the same person would be labelled 36D.

Bra size in Pakistan: inches, centimetres and brand charts

Bras sold in Pakistan carry labels from more than one system, so the safest start is your own measurement. If your tape is in inches, set the switch to US and enter both numbers in inches; the US / Canada and UK rows then give the familiar 32, 34, 36 labels. If a shop or brand sizes in centimetres, the European row is the one to read.

For example, an underbust of 86 cm (about 33.9 inches) with a bust of 91.5 cm is a difference of 5.5 cm, or 2.2 inches. That is 34B in inch sizes and 75B in European sizes, so 34B in centimetres means a band of about 84 to 89 cm under the bust with a bust about 5 to 7.5 cm larger. A fitting guide by the European brand Triumph, published in The Express Tribune in 2015, measures the band in centimetres and rounds it to the nearest 5, the European way.

Local brands each publish their own chart and some still add inches to the band, so compare your measurements with the chart of the brand you are buying rather than relying on a size from another label. When ordering online, buy one bra first and check the fit before ordering more.

Sister sizes and signs of a bad fit

A sister size keeps roughly the same cup volume on a different band: go one band up and one cup down, or one band down and one cup up. The sisters of 34C are 36B and 32D. Try one when the cups fit but the band feels too tight or too loose.

Poor fit is common. In a 2008 study of 30 young women with upper back or chest pain, 80% wore the wrong size, most of them a bra that was too small. A 2003 study found that every one of 102 women referred for breast reduction surgery was wearing a badly fitting bra.

  • The band rides up at the back: it is too loose, so try a smaller band (and a sister cup).
  • Red marks, digging straps or shoulder pain: the band is not carrying the weight, or the cup is too small.
  • Breast tissue spills over the top or sides: go up a cup.
  • The centre panel between the cups does not lie flat on your chest: the cups are usually too small.
  • Wrinkles or gaps in the cups: try a smaller cup or a different shape.

Small bust, large bust, teens and other cases

  • Small bust: a difference under 1 inch gives AA. Many brands start at A, so a smaller band with an A cup can fit better than a loose AA.
  • Large bust: the table here goes up to an 8-inch difference. Beyond that the calculator still gives your band and tells you the cup is outside its table; full-bust brands make cups well past H.
  • Teens and tweens: growing bodies change size quickly, so measure again every few months and choose soft, stretchy styles while the size keeps moving.
  • Men and trans women: the same two measurements work for anyone; the band follows the ribcage and the cup follows the difference. Some people prefer a band one size up for comfort.
  • Sagging or soft tissue: measure while leaning forward slightly as well as standing, and use the larger bust number if the two differ a lot.

How it works

Measure two things with a soft tape: your band (snug and level, right under the bust) and your bust (loosely around the fullest part, wearing a bra without padding). The band size is the underbust rounded to an even number of inches. The cup letter comes from how much bigger the bust is than the band: in US, UK and Australian sizes every inch of difference is one cup, and in European and French sizes every 2 cm is one cup. The same size is then read across the band and cup lists of each system. To change a size you already wear into another system, use the Convert tab.

The formula

  • US and UK band = underbust in inches, rounded to the nearest even number (33 → 34, 32.5 → 32)
  • European, French and dress-size bands: underbust rounded to a whole inch, plus 1 if that is odd, then read across the band lists
  • Difference = bust − underbust
  • US, UK and Australian cup = whole inches of the difference: 0 AA, 1 A, 2 B, 3 C, 4 D, 5 DD (US DD/E), 6 E (US DDD/F) ...
  • European and French cup = whole 2 cm steps of the difference: AA, A, B, C, D, E, F, G, H, I
  • Band lists side by side: US/UK 28 to 60 in, EU 60 to 140 cm, France 75 to 155 cm, UK dress size 4 to 36, Australian dress size 6 to 38, band + 4 method 32 to 64

A worked example

Underbust 34 in and bust 38 in: the band is 34, the difference is 4 inches, so the cup is D: 34D in the US and UK. In centimetres the difference is 10.2 cm, five 2 cm steps, so the European size is 75E and the French size 90E. In Australia it is dress size 12D.

Questions

How do I calculate my bra size at home?

Measure your underbust snugly and your bust loosely at the fullest part, both with a soft tape. Round the underbust to the nearest even inch for the band, then count the whole inches of difference for the cup: 1 is A, 2 is B, 3 is C, 4 is D. The calculator does this for you and shows every system.

How do I measure for a bra?

Stand straight with your arms down. For the band, wrap the tape snugly and level around your ribcage right under the bust. For the bust, measure loosely around the fullest part without squeezing. Wear a thin, unpadded bra or none, and measure twice to be sure.

How do I calculate bra size in cm?

Set the switch to Metric and enter both measurements in centimetres. European band labels go in 5 cm steps (70, 75, 80) and are read across from your band, and the European cup goes up one letter for every 2 cm of difference. The US and UK sizes are shown too, worked out in inches.

What does bra size A, B, C, D mean?

The letter shows how much bigger the bust is than the band, not a fixed cup size. In inch sizes A is about 1 inch of difference, B 2 inches, C 3 inches and D 4 inches. So a 32C and a 38C have different cup volumes.

What is bra size 34B?

A 34 band, for an underbust of about 33 to 35 inches (84 to 89 cm), with a bust about 2 inches larger. In Europe it is 75B, in France 90B and in Australia 12B. Its sister sizes are 36A and 32C.

What is a sister size?

Cup letters grow with the band, so going one band up and one cup down (34C to 36B) keeps about the same cup volume, and so does one band down with one cup up (34C to 32D). Try a sister size when the band feels too tight or too loose but the cups fit.

Why is my European cup letter different?

European and French sizes count the cup in 2 cm steps of the difference, while US and UK sizes count whole inches (2.54 cm). A difference of 10.2 cm (4 inches) is a D in inches but an E in European sizes, so the letters often drift apart above a C.

What is the band + 4 method?

An older way of sizing that adds about 4 inches to the measured underbust. A few brands still label their bras this way, so if a band feels very loose, check which method the brand uses.

Which bra size chart is used in Pakistan?

You will find both inch labels (32B, 34C) and European centimetre labels (70B, 75C) in Pakistan, and each brand has its own chart. Measure yourself in inches or centimetres, enter the numbers here, and compare the matching row with the brand's chart.

Is the calculated size exact?

No. It is a good starting point, but brands, styles and fabrics fit differently. Try the size on, and a sister size, before you buy several bras. A professional fitting is the most reliable.
