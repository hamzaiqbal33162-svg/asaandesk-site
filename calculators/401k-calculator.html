Year by year

How it works

The first tab follows your 401(k) year by year until you retire. Each year your pay rises, you put in your percentage of it and your employer adds its match. Your own part is capped at the IRS limit for your age (with the catch-up from 50, and the higher catch-up at 60 to 63), pay above the IRS pay limit is not counted, and you and your employer together stay under the total additions limit; the limits grow with inflation in later years. Deposits come in through the year, so they earn half a year of return at first. At retirement the page shows three ways to spend the balance by your life expectancy. The second tab shows what an early withdrawal leaves after income tax and the 10% penalty, unless an IRS exception applies. The third tab finds the range of contribution rates that collects the whole match without reaching the IRS limit before the year ends.

The formula

  • Your deposit = your % × pay (pay capped at the 401(a)(17) limit), at most the 402(g) limit + the catch-up for your age
  • Match = pay × (tier-1 % × the part of your % up to its limit + tier-2 % × the part between the two limits)
  • Yearly growth = balance × R + (your deposit + match) × R ÷ 2; balance ← balance + deposits + growth
  • Same yearly amount: B × R ÷ (1 − (1 + R)^−Y); same monthly amount: B × (R/12) ÷ (1 − (1 + R/12)^−12Y); rising monthly amount: the same with (R − inflation) ÷ 12
  • Early withdrawal: you receive = amount − amount × (federal + state + local rate) − 10% × the part no exception covers

A worked example

At 30 on $75,000, putting in 10% with a 50% match on the first 3% of pay: the first year you add $7,500 and your employer $1,125. With $35,000 today and 6% a year, that year grows by 35,000 × 6% + 8,625 × 3% = $2,358.75 to $45,983.75. With 3% pay rises the balance reaches $1,711,800 at 65, which pays $149,243 a year until 85.

Questions

How much can I put in my 401(k)?

For 2026 you can defer $24,500 of your pay, plus an $8,000 catch-up from age 50, or $11,250 instead at ages 60 to 63. For 2027 the figures are $25,500, $8,500 and $11,750. Employer money does not count toward these, but you and your employer together are capped at $72,000 in 2026 and $75,000 in 2027.

How much should I contribute to get the whole match?

At least the percentage of pay your employer matches up to, often 3% to 6%. Use the third tab: it shows the lowest rate that collects the full match and the highest rate before you reach the IRS limit early in the year.

What does it cost to cash out early?

Before 59½ you usually pay income tax plus a 10% penalty, so a $10,000 withdrawal at 25% federal and 5% state tax leaves about $6,000. Leaving your job in or after the year you turn 55, disability and a few other exceptions remove the penalty but not the tax.

Why is today's balance not in my contributions?

It is money you already saved, so it is shown on its own line. The total money in adds it to your future contributions and the employer match; everything above that is investment growth.

Will the limits really rise every year?

The IRS raises them with inflation, usually in $500 steps. The projection grows them with the inflation you enter, which is close to what happens over time.
