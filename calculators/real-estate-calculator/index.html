What this real estate calculator shows

This real estate calculator gives a buyer the three numbers that decide whether a home is within reach: the full monthly cost of owning it, the cash needed to buy it, and the income that cost fits. It goes beyond the loan payment by adding property tax, home insurance, mortgage insurance and HOA or condo fees.

Below the snapshot, the page gathers all our property tools in one place: mortgage and affordability calculators for buying, payoff, refinance and home equity calculators for owners, rent and rental property calculators for renters and investors, and measuring tools such as square footage, concrete and tile calculators for building work.

How to use the real estate calculator

  • Choose your currency at the top, then enter the Home price (default 400,000).
  • Enter the Down payment as a % of the price (default 20%) or switch the unit to an amount.
  • Set the Interest rate (default 6.5% a year) and the Loan term (default 30 years).
  • Open Costs and income to change Property tax (default 1.1% of the price a year), Home insurance per year (1,500), HOA or condo fee per month (0), Mortgage insurance (PMI) under 20% down (0.5% of the loan a year) and Closing costs (3% of the price).
  • Optionally enter Your household income before tax, per year to see what share of it the home would take.

Replace the defaults with real figures as soon as you have them: the property tax bill for the home, an insurance quote and the HOA fee from the listing. They can move the monthly cost more than a small change in the rate.

Understanding the results

The two headlines are the Monthly cost of owning and the Cash needed to buy. The rows add the Loan amount, the Loan payment (principal and interest), the Total interest over the loan and the Income that fits this home. With your income entered, Share of your income compares the cost with it: up to 28% is within the usual guide, 28% to 36% may still be approved, and above 36% is likely to stretch your budget.

Real estate formulas to know

These are the formulas behind most real estate questions, from buying a home to judging a rental. Our linked calculators work them out for you.

Buying property in Pakistan: taxes and loan rules

In Pakistan, the costs of buying include FBR advance tax as well as provincial stamp duty and registration fees. Under the FBR's withholding tax rate card for tax year 2027 (updated to 30 June 2026 under the Finance Act 2026), a buyer pays advance tax under section 236K of 1.25% of the fair market value if on the active taxpayers list, and 10.5% to 18.5% if not, rising with the value. A seller pays under section 236C: 2.75% of the gross sale price for active taxpayers and 11.5% for others.

For bank home finance, the State Bank of Pakistan's Prudential Regulations for Housing Finance (updated 18 August 2026) set a maximum loan-to-value of 90:10, a tenor of up to 30 years and a cap of 65% of net disposable income for all installments together. That cap uses take-home pay, unlike the 28% guide here, which uses income before tax.

Enter these purchase taxes and fees in rupees as part of the closing costs %, and check the current rate card and your province's stamp duty before you budget, because both change with each budget.

Tips before you buy

  • Budget for upkeep. Repairs, maintenance and utilities are not in the monthly cost here, and older homes need more.
  • Look up the actual property tax. Rates differ a lot between counties and cities, so use the bill for the home you want, not the default 1.1%.
  • Plan for PMI to end. On a conventional loan, the CFPB explains that you can ask to cancel PMI when the balance is scheduled to reach 80% of the original value, and that it ends automatically at 78%.
  • Keep cash after closing. An emergency fund protects you from the first surprise repair.
  • Compare renting. The Rent vs Buy Calculator shows when owning beats renting over time.

How it works

This page has two parts. The snapshot at the top takes a home price, a down payment (as a % or an amount), a rate and a term, and adds the loan payment, property tax, insurance, mortgage insurance under 20% down and any HOA fee into one monthly cost of owning. It also shows the cash you need on the day (down payment plus closing costs) and the yearly income at which that monthly cost is 28% of income before tax, a common lender guide; add your own income under Costs and income to see your share. Below it are all our property calculators, grouped by what you want to do next.

The formula

  • Loan L = price − down payment; loan payment M = L × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n), r = rate ÷ 1200, n = years × 12
  • Monthly cost = M + price × tax % ÷ 12 + insurance ÷ 12 + PMI (L × PMI % ÷ 12, under 20% down) + HOA fee
  • Cash needed = down payment + price × closing cost %
  • Income that fits = monthly cost × 12 ÷ 0.28; share of your income = monthly cost × 12 ÷ your income

A worked example

A 400,000 home with 20% down is a 320,000 loan; at 6.5% over 30 years the loan payment is 2,022.62. Tax at 1.1% adds 366.67 a month and 1,500 of insurance adds 125, so owning costs 2,514.28 a month. With 3% closing costs you need 80,000 + 12,000 = 92,000 to buy, and the cost fits an income of about 107,755 a year.

Questions

What does the monthly cost of owning include?

The loan payment (principal and interest), property tax, home insurance, mortgage insurance when you put down less than 20%, and any HOA or condo fee. Repairs, utilities and upkeep come on top, so keep a separate budget for them.

How much income do I need for a 400,000 house?

With 20% down at 6.5% over 30 years, 1.1% property tax and 1,500 of insurance, the monthly cost is 2,514.28, which is 28% of an income of about 107,755 a year. With 10% down, PMI and a bigger loan raise that to about 125,019.

Why 28% of income?

Many lenders like housing costs to stay at or under about 28% of your income before tax, and all your debts together under about 36%. It is a guide, not a rule: some loans allow more, and a lower share leaves more room in your budget.

How do I calculate real estate taxes?

Multiply the home's taxable value by the local tax rate. This calculator uses a yearly % of the price (1.1% by default) and divides it by 12; replace it with your area's rate or the actual tax bill. In Pakistan, also budget for the FBR advance tax on purchase and the provincial stamp duty.

How do I calculate a real estate agent's commission?

Multiply the sale price by the agreed commission rate, then split it between the listing and buying brokers and their agents as their agreements say. The commission calculator finds the total from the price and rate, or the rate from the price and commission.

How do I calculate the cap rate of a property?

Divide the net operating income (rent and other income minus operating costs, not the loan payment) by the price and multiply by 100. The rental property calculator works it out along with cash flow and cash-on-cash return.

What taxes do I pay when buying property in Pakistan?

Advance tax under section 236K, 1.25% of the fair market value for active taxpayers and 10.5% to 18.5% for others in tax year 2027, plus provincial stamp duty and registration fees. The seller pays advance tax under section 236C. Check the FBR's current rate card before you buy.
