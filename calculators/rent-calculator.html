How it works

Enter your income before tax, per year or per month, and what you pay each month on loans and cards. Lenders and money advisers often use two limits: all your debt payments plus housing should stay under about 36% of your income before tax, and under 28% if you want room to breathe. The calculator takes your debt payments away from both limits, and what is left is the rent you can afford. It also shows a third of your income, a limit many landlords use when they check tenants.

The formula

  • Monthly income I = yearly income ÷ 12 (or the monthly income you typed)
  • Most rent you can afford = 36% × I − monthly debt payments
  • Comfortable rent = 28% × I − monthly debt payments
  • Landlord check = I ÷ 3

A worked example

With $80,000 a year and no debt, I = $6,666.67 a month. The most rent is 0.36 × 6,666.67 = $2,400, a comfortable rent is 0.28 × 6,666.67 = $1,867, and a third of income is $2,222.

Questions

Should I use my income before or after tax?

The 28% and 36% limits are set against income before tax, so that is what the calculator expects. If you only know your take-home pay, enter it anyway: the result will simply be lower and safer.

Which debts count?

Count the payments you must make every month: car loans, student loans, personal loans and the minimum on each card. Do not count your current rent, food or utility bills.

Why is there a landlord check?

Many landlords and letting agents only accept a tenant whose rent is at most a third of their income before tax, or who earns about three times the rent. It is a quick way to see if an application is likely to pass.

What if the result is zero?

Then your debt payments already use up the 36% limit, and any rent would stretch your budget. Paying off a card or a small loan first, or sharing the rent, frees up room.

Does it work in other currencies?

Yes. Pick your currency symbol at the top of the calculator. The percentages are the same in any country; the symbol only changes how the amounts are shown.
