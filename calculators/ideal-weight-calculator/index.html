What the ideal weight calculator shows

An ideal weight calculator estimates what you might weigh for your height and sex. Doctors call this ideal body weight (IBW). The idea goes back to the 1960s and 1970s, when pharmacists needed a quick reference weight to dose medicines, and the formulas still serve that purpose in hospitals today.

This calculator gives four results, from the Robinson, Miller, Devine and Hamwi formulas, their average as the headline, and the healthy weight range from the World Health Organization's BMI band of 18.5 to 25. For children and teenagers aged 2 to 17 it switches to the CDC growth charts. Treat every number as an estimate and a starting point for a conversation with your doctor, not a weight you must reach.

How to use this calculator, step by step

  • Choose metric (cm and kg) or U.S. units (feet, inches and pounds). Results appear in the same unit system.
  • Pick Male or Female; the formulas use different base weights for each.
  • Enter your age. From 18 the adult formulas apply; for ages 2 to 17 an Extra months box appears for a closer child result.
  • Enter your height. The four formulas need at least 5 ft (152.4 cm); for shorter adults only the healthy BMI range is shown.
  • Read the average at the top, each formula and the healthy range below it, and the bar chart that compares the formulas.

The four ideal weight formulas and where they come from

All four share one shape: a base weight at 5 ft plus a fixed number of kilograms for each extra inch. That is why they are written in inches even in metric countries; the calculator converts centimetres for you.

Hamwi (1964) was first published in pounds: 106 lb for a man at 5 ft plus 6 lb per inch, and 100 lb for a woman plus 5 lb per inch. The metric version used here, 48 kg plus 2.7 kg per inch for men, gives almost the same numbers. Hamwi's method assumes a medium frame; dietitians often take off 10% for a small frame and add 10% for a large one.

Devine (1974) was proposed for dosing the antibiotic gentamicin and became the standard hospital formula. Robinson and Miller (both 1983) were later re-fits of the same idea with a smaller slope per inch. A 2000 review by Pai and Paloucek traced the formulas back to their origins and stressed that they were made for dosing, not as health targets.

Worked examples

A man 5 ft 10 in tall. He is 10 inches over 5 ft, so Robinson gives 52 + 19 = 71.0 kg, Miller 56.2 + 14.1 = 70.3 kg, Devine 50 + 23 = 73.0 kg and Hamwi 48 + 27 = 75.0 kg. The average is 72.3 kg (159 lb), and his healthy BMI range is 58.5 to 79.0 kg.

A woman in Pakistan, 157.5 cm tall. Divide by 2.54 to get 62.0 inches, so x = 2.0. Robinson gives 52.4 kg, Miller 55.8 kg, Devine 50.1 kg and Hamwi 49.9 kg, an average of 52.1 kg. Her WHO healthy range is 45.9 to 62.0 kg. Because health risks for South Asians start at a lower BMI, NICE in the UK suggests treating a BMI of 23 as the start of overweight, which at her height is 57.1 kg.

A child. For a 10-year-old boy 140 cm tall, the CDC growth charts give a healthy BMI for age of 14.2 to 19.4, so his healthy weight range is 27.8 to 38.0 kg, with 32.6 kg at the middle BMI for his age.

How hospitals use ideal body weight

Some medicines spread mainly through lean tissue, so dosing them by total weight in a person with obesity would give too much. Pharmacists then use IBW, usually the Devine value, or an adjusted body weight that adds 40% of the excess: IBW + 0.4 × (actual weight − IBW). For a 5 ft 10 in man weighing 100 kg that is 73 + 0.4 × 27 = 83.8 kg.

On a ventilator, breath size is set from predicted body weight, which is the Devine formula written in centimetres: 50 + 0.91 × (height in cm − 152.4) for men and 45.5 + 0.91 × (height in cm − 152.4) for women. The landmark ARDS Network trial (2000) found that 6 mL per kg of predicted body weight saved more lives than larger breaths.

Factors that change your ideal weight

  • Muscle: athletes and people who lift weights can sit well above every formula and still be lean. Body fat percentage tells you more than IBW for them.
  • Frame size: people with a broad frame and heavy bones weigh more at the same height. The formulas assume a medium frame.
  • Sex: women's formulas start lower because women have less muscle and bone mass on average, even though they carry more body fat.
  • Age: the formulas do not change with age after growth stops, but muscle tends to fall and fat to rise over the years, so waist size is a useful extra check.
  • Ethnic background: the formulas are the same for everyone, but people of South Asian, Chinese and some other backgrounds face higher risks at a lower BMI, so a weight near the middle or lower part of the WHO range may suit them better.
  • Children and babies: under 18 only growth charts apply. The CDC recommends WHO growth charts for babies under 2 and its own charts from age 2, which is why this calculator starts at 2 years.

Limits of ideal weight formulas and tips

  • IBW ignores body composition, fat distribution and health conditions. It is a reference weight, not a health score.
  • The four formulas can differ by 4 kg or more for the same person, and by over 10 kg for very tall men; the healthy BMI range shows how wide the healthy band really is.
  • Below 5 ft the formulas do not apply, which is why the calculator then shows only the healthy BMI range.
  • Use your waist as a second check: keeping it below half your height is a simple health target.
  • If you are pregnant, follow pregnancy weight gain advice instead; see the Pregnancy Weight Gain Calculator.
  • Aim for habits rather than a number: regular activity, enough sleep and a diet built on vegetables, fruit, pulses and whole grains.

How it works

Four well-known formulas give an ideal body weight from height and sex alone. Each starts from a base weight at 5 feet and adds a fixed amount for every inch above that. They were made in the 1960s to 1980s, mostly to work out medicine doses, so they are a rough guide rather than a target. The healthy BMI range (18.5 to 25) gives a wider band that suits most adults. Children and teens up to 17 grow fast, so for them the range comes from the CDC BMI-for-age chart instead: the 5th to 85th percentile BMI for their age and sex, times their height squared.

The formula

  • x = height in inches − 60 (inches over 5 ft); 1 inch = 2.54 cm
  • Robinson (1983): men 52 kg + 1.9 kg × x; women 49 kg + 1.7 kg × x
  • Miller (1983): men 56.2 kg + 1.41 kg × x; women 53.1 kg + 1.36 kg × x
  • Devine (1974): men 50 kg + 2.3 kg × x; women 45.5 kg + 2.3 kg × x
  • Hamwi (1964): men 48 kg + 2.7 kg × x; women 45.5 kg + 2.2 kg × x
  • Ideal weight shown at the top = the average of the four formulas
  • Healthy range for adults = 18.5 × height (m)² to 25 × height (m)²; children: the 5th and 85th percentile BMI for age × height²

A worked example

A man 5 ft 10 in tall is 10 inches over 5 ft. Robinson: 52 + 1.9 × 10 = 71 kg (156.5 lb); Miller: 56.2 + 1.41 × 10 = 70.3 kg (155.0 lb); Devine: 50 + 2.3 × 10 = 73 kg (160.9 lb); Hamwi: 48 + 2.7 × 10 = 75 kg (165.3 lb). Their average is 72.3 kg (159.4 lb), inside the healthy BMI range of 128.9 to 174.2 lb.

Questions

How do I calculate my ideal weight?

Count the inches you are over 5 ft, multiply by the formula's amount per inch and add its base weight. With the Devine formula a 5 ft 6 in woman gets 45.5 + 2.3 × 6 = 59.3 kg. This calculator works out all four formulas and their average for you.

How do I calculate ideal weight in kg from height in cm?

Divide your height in cm by 2.54 to get inches, subtract 60, then use a formula. For 170 cm: 170 ÷ 2.54 = 66.9 inches, so x = 6.9, and Devine gives 50 + 2.3 × 6.9 = 65.9 kg for a man.

What is the ideal weight for a 5'4 female?

The four formulas give 54.3 to 58.5 kg, an average of 55.8 kg (123 lb). The healthy BMI range at that height is wider, about 48.9 to 66.1 kg (108 to 146 lb).

What is the ideal weight for a 5'10 male in kg?

The formulas give 70.3 to 75.0 kg, an average of 72.3 kg (159 lb). His healthy BMI range is about 58.5 to 79.0 kg.

Which formula is the best?

None is clearly best. Devine is the one most used in hospitals for medicine doses, Robinson and Miller were fitted later to newer data, and Hamwi gives the highest values for men. The average is a fair middle point, and the healthy BMI range shows how much room there is around it.

Does ideal weight depend on age?

For adults the formulas use only height and sex, so age does not change the result. Age matters for children, whose healthy range comes from growth charts for their exact age and sex.

Is the ideal weight different for Pakistani or Indian adults?

The formulas are the same, but South Asians tend to have more body fat and higher diabetes risk at the same BMI. NICE suggests treating a BMI of 23 as the start of overweight for them, so aiming for the middle of the range is sensible.

Why does the calculator not use my weight?

Ideal weight formulas use only height and sex. To see where your own weight sits, use the BMI calculator, and for body composition use the body fat calculator.

Why are the formulas missing for people under 5 feet?

All four formulas start at 5 feet and add weight for every inch above it, so they were never meant for shorter adults and give odd results there. For them only the healthy BMI range is shown.

How is a child's healthy weight worked out?

From the CDC growth charts: the BMI at the 5th and 85th percentile for the child's age and sex, times the height squared. Add the months since the last birthday for a closer result. Babies under 2 are checked on WHO growth charts by a doctor or health visitor.

Can athletes use an ideal weight calculator?

Only as a rough reference. Muscle is dense, so a fit athlete can be well above every formula with a low body fat. Body fat percentage and waist size are better guides for them.
