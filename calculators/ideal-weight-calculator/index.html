How it works

Four well-known formulas give an ideal body weight from height and sex alone. Each starts from a base weight at 5 feet and adds a fixed amount for every inch above that. They were made in the 1960s to 1980s, mostly to work out medicine doses, so they are a rough guide rather than a target. The healthy BMI range (18.5 to 25) gives a wider band that suits most adults. Children and teens up to 17 grow fast, so for them the range comes from the CDC BMI-for-age chart instead: the 5th to 85th percentile BMI for their age and sex, times their height squared.

The formula

  • x = height in inches − 60 (inches over 5 ft)
  • Robinson (1983): men 52 kg + 1.9 kg × x; women 49 kg + 1.7 kg × x
  • Miller (1983): men 56.2 kg + 1.41 kg × x; women 53.1 kg + 1.36 kg × x
  • Devine (1974): men 50 kg + 2.3 kg × x; women 45.5 kg + 2.3 kg × x
  • Hamwi (1964): men 48 kg + 2.7 kg × x; women 45.5 kg + 2.2 kg × x
  • Healthy range for adults = 18.5 × height (m)² to 25 × height (m)²; children: the 5th and 85th percentile BMI for age × height²

A worked example

A man 5 ft 10 in tall is 10 inches over 5 ft. Robinson: 52 + 1.9 × 10 = 71 kg (156.5 lb); Miller: 56.2 + 1.41 × 10 = 70.3 kg (155.0 lb); Devine: 50 + 2.3 × 10 = 73 kg (160.9 lb); Hamwi: 48 + 2.7 × 10 = 75 kg (165.3 lb). Their average is 72.3 kg (159.4 lb), inside the healthy BMI range of 128.9 to 174.2 lb.

Sources

  • McCarron and Devine (1974): Clinical pharmacy case studies (gentamicin therapy), Drug Intelligence and Clinical Pharmacy
  • Robinson et al. (1983): Determination of ideal body weight for drug dosage calculations, American Journal of Hospital Pharmacy
  • Pai and Paloucek (2000): The origin of the ideal body weight equations, Annals of Pharmacotherapy

Questions

Which formula is the best?

None is clearly best. Devine is the one most used in hospitals for medicine doses, Robinson and Miller were fitted later to newer data, and Hamwi gives the highest values for men. The average is a fair middle point, and the healthy BMI range shows how much room there is around it.

Why does the calculator not use my weight?

Ideal weight formulas use only height and sex. To see where your own weight sits, use the BMI calculator, and for body composition use the body fat calculator.

Why are the formulas missing for people under 5 feet?

All four formulas start at 5 feet and add weight for every inch above it, so they were never meant for shorter adults and give odd results there. For them only the healthy BMI range is shown.

How is a child's healthy weight worked out?

From the CDC growth charts: the BMI at the 5th and 85th percentile for the child's age and sex, times the height squared. Add the months since the last birthday for a closer result.
