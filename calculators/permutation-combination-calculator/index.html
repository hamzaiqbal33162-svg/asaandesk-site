How it works

Type how many items there are (n) and how many you pick (r). A permutation counts the different orders: for gold, silver and bronze among 10 runners there are 10 × 9 × 8 = 720 ways. A combination counts only which items are picked, not their order: a team of 3 from 10 people can be formed in 720 ÷ 6 = 120 ways, because each team of 3 can be lined up in 3! = 6 orders. Every answer is worked out with exact whole-number maths, so even 100 choose 50 is correct to the last of its 30 digits. Under More options you can also count when an item can be picked more than once, such as digits in a PIN.

The formula

  • P(n, r) = n! ÷ (n − r)! = n × (n − 1) × … × (n − r + 1)
  • C(n, r) = n! ÷ (r! × (n − r)!) = P(n, r) ÷ r!
  • With repetition: arrangements = n^r, selections = C(n + r − 1, r)
  • n! = 1 × 2 × … × n, and 0! = 1

A worked example

A lottery draws 5 numbers from 52. If the order of the draw mattered there would be P(52, 5) = 52 × 51 × 50 × 49 × 48 = 311,875,200 outcomes. Order does not matter for a ticket, so divide by the 5! = 120 orders of each set: C(52, 5) = 2,598,960 different tickets.

Questions

When do I use a permutation and when a combination?

Ask whether a different order gives a different result. Seats, passwords, rankings and race places are permutations. Teams, committees, pizza toppings and lottery tickets are combinations, because the same items in another order are the same group.

Why must r be no larger than n?

Without repetition you cannot pick more different items than exist, so P(5, 7) and C(5, 7) are 0 and the calculator tells you so. If an item can be picked again, tick the with-repetition option: then 7 picks from 5 items are possible.

How big can the numbers be?

n can go up to 1,000,000. Results up to 120 digits are shown in full, longer ones as about m × 10^e with every digit in the Exact values table and the copy button. Past 20,000 digits only the size is worked out, from logarithms, to 10 significant digits.

What are the with-repetition counts?

If each pick can be any of the n items again, there are n^r ordered arrangements (a 4-digit PIN has 10^4 = 10,000) and C(n + r − 1, r) unordered selections (3 scoops from 5 ice-cream flavours, repeats allowed, give C(7, 3) = 35 choices).
