What permutation and combination mean

Permutations and combinations are two ways of counting choices. In simple words, a permutation is an arrangement, where order matters, and a combination is a selection, where order does not. Picking a captain and a vice-captain from 15 players is a permutation (Ali then Hamza is different from Hamza then Ali); picking 2 players to carry the drinks is a combination (the same two people either way).

This permutation and combination calculator gives both counts at once, nPr and nCr, exact to the last digit, with the formula filled in with your numbers. It also counts the cases where an item can be picked again.

How to use the calculator

  • Total number of items (n): how many things you choose from, such as 10 runners or 52 cards (0 to 1,000,000).
  • Number of items chosen (r): how many you pick or arrange (the page starts with n = 10, r = 3).
  • Read Permutations P(n, r) (order matters) and Combinations C(n, r) (order does not matter), plus the Orders of each selection, r!, which links the two: permutations = combinations × r!.
  • Under More options, tick Also count with repetition when an item can be picked again; you then also get n^r arrangements and C(n + r − 1, r) selections.
  • Very long answers show as about m × 10^e, with every digit in the Exact values table and a copy button. The steps show the factorial formula with your numbers.

Permutation and combination formulas, with and without repetition

Two questions decide the formula: does order matter, and can the same item be used more than once? The table uses n = 10 items and r = 3 picks for every case, so you can compare the four answers.

The factorial n! means n × (n − 1) × … × 1, and 0! is defined as 1 so that the formulas still work when r = 0 or r = n. Always C(n, r) = C(n, n − r): choosing 3 people to go is the same as choosing the 7 who stay.

Worked examples

  • Cricket squad: a team of 11 picked from a squad of 15 is C(15, 11) = 1,365 possible teams. The batting order of those 11 can then be set in 11! = 39,916,800 ways.
  • Handshakes: if 20 people each shake hands once with everyone else, the handshakes are pairs, so C(20, 2) = 190.
  • Seating: 8 guests in 3 numbered chairs is P(8, 3) = 8 × 7 × 6 = 336 arrangements; choosing which 3 sit, ignoring the chairs, is C(8, 3) = 56.
  • PIN codes: a 4-digit PIN with repeats allowed has 10^4 = 10,000 options; with no digit used twice there are P(10, 4) = 5,040.
  • Lottery: a 6-from-49 draw has C(49, 6) = 13,983,816 combinations, so one ticket has a 1 in 13,983,816 chance of matching all six.

Arranging the letters of a word

When all the letters are different, a word of n letters can be arranged in n! ways: MATH gives 4! = 24 arrangements. When letters repeat, divide by the factorial of each repeat count, because swapping identical letters makes no new word. BOOK has 4! ÷ 2! = 12 arrangements, and PAKISTAN, with two A's among 8 letters, has 8! ÷ 2! = 20,160.

To do this here, set n and r to the number of letters to get n! as the permutation count, then divide by the repeat factorials. Words with more repeats, such as LETTER (two E's and two T's), need 6! ÷ (2! × 2!) = 180.

Permutations and combinations in FSc, A-level and entry tests

In Pakistan this topic is Chapter 7, Permutation, Combination and Probability, of the Punjab Textbook Board's FSc Part 1 mathematics book, and it is also in the Probability and Statistics 1 part of Cambridge International A-level Mathematics (9709). Typical questions ask for arrangements of a word, committees with conditions (at least one woman, two particular people never together) and circular seating, where n people round a table can sit in (n − 1)! ways because turning the whole table changes nothing.

For conditional questions, split the count into cases and add them, or count everything and subtract the cases you do not want. A committee of 3 from 6 men and 4 women with at least one woman is C(10, 3) − C(6, 3) = 120 − 20 = 100.

Permutations and combinations on a calculator or in Excel

TI-84 Plus CE: type n, press MATH, move right to the PROB menu, choose nPr or nCr, then type r and press enter. Casio fx-570ES PLUS and fx-991ES PLUS: nPr and nCr are shifted functions printed above the keys, so type n, press SHIFT and the key marked nPr or nCr, then type r.

In Excel or Google Sheets, =PERMUT(10,3) gives 720 and =COMBIN(10,3) gives 120; =COMBINA(10,3) gives 220, the count with repetition. Handheld calculators and spreadsheets round very large results, while this page keeps every digit.

How it works

Type how many items there are (n) and how many you pick (r). A permutation counts the different orders: for gold, silver and bronze among 10 runners there are 10 × 9 × 8 = 720 ways. A combination counts only which items are picked, not their order: a team of 3 from 10 people can be formed in 720 ÷ 6 = 120 ways, because each team of 3 can be lined up in 3! = 6 orders. Every answer is worked out with exact whole-number maths, so even 100 choose 50 is correct to the last of its 30 digits. Under More options you can also count when an item can be picked more than once, such as digits in a PIN.

The formula

  • P(n, r) = n! ÷ (n − r)! = n × (n − 1) × … × (n − r + 1)
  • C(n, r) = n! ÷ (r! × (n − r)!) = P(n, r) ÷ r!
  • With repetition: arrangements = n^r, selections = C(n + r − 1, r)
  • n! = 1 × 2 × … × n, and 0! = 1

A worked example

A lottery draws 5 numbers from 52. If the order of the draw mattered there would be P(52, 5) = 52 × 51 × 50 × 49 × 48 = 311,875,200 outcomes. Order does not matter for a ticket, so divide by the 5! = 120 orders of each set: C(52, 5) = 2,598,960 different tickets.

Questions

When do I use a permutation and when a combination?

Ask whether a different order gives a different result. Seats, passwords, rankings and race places are permutations. Teams, committees, pizza toppings and lottery tickets are combinations, because the same items in another order are the same group.

How do I calculate permutation and combination by hand?

For nPr, multiply n by the next r − 1 smaller numbers: P(10, 3) = 10 × 9 × 8 = 720. For nCr, divide that by r!: 720 ÷ 6 = 120. Cancelling before multiplying keeps the numbers small.

How do I calculate permutations and combinations on a TI-84 Plus?

Type n, press MATH, go right to PROB, pick 2: nPr or 3: nCr, type r and press enter. For 10 nCr 3 you get 120.

How do I calculate permutation and combination in a scientific calculator?

On most scientific calculators, type n, press SHIFT (or 2nd) and the key labelled nPr or nCr, then type r and press =. Very large answers are rounded, so use this page for exact digits.

How do I calculate permutations and combinations in Excel?

Use =PERMUT(n, r) for permutations and =COMBIN(n, r) for combinations; =COMBINA(n, r) counts selections with repetition. For example =COMBIN(8, 2) gives 28.

How many ways can the letters of a word be arranged?

Take the factorial of the number of letters and divide by the factorial of each repeat count. BOOK has 4! ÷ 2! = 12 arrangements and LETTER has 6! ÷ (2! × 2!) = 180.

Why is 0! equal to 1?

There is exactly one way to arrange nothing, and defining 0! = 1 keeps the formulas correct: C(5, 5) = 5! ÷ (5! × 0!) = 1. It also makes n! = n × (n − 1)! true for n = 1.

Why must r be no larger than n?

Without repetition you cannot pick more different items than exist, so P(5, 7) and C(5, 7) are 0 and the calculator tells you so. If an item can be picked again, tick the with-repetition option: then 7 picks from 5 items are possible.

How big can the numbers be?

n can go up to 1,000,000. Results up to 120 digits are shown in full, longer ones as about m × 10^e with every digit in the Exact values table and the copy button. Past 20,000 digits only the size is worked out, from logarithms, to 10 significant digits.

What are the with-repetition counts?

If each pick can be any of the n items again, there are n^r ordered arrangements (a 4-digit PIN has 10^4 = 10,000) and C(n + r − 1, r) unordered selections (3 scoops from 5 ice-cream flavours, repeats allowed, give C(7, 3) = 35 choices).
