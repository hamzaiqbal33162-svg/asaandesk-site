What is the marriage tax penalty, and what is a marriage bonus?

In the US, married couples usually file one joint federal return instead of two single ones. A marriage penalty is the extra tax a couple pays on that joint return compared with what the same two people would pay as single filers; a marriage bonus is the tax they save. This marriage tax calculator works out both sides with the IRS figures for 2025, 2026 or 2027 and shows which one applies to you.

Most joint brackets and the joint standard deduction are exactly twice the single ones, so couples with similar incomes often see little change, and couples where one partner earns much more than the other usually get a bonus. Penalties appear where the joint amounts are less than double: the top brackets, the 0.9% Additional Medicare tax and the 3.8% net investment income tax, and credits such as the earned income credit that are reduced by the couple's combined income.

How to use the marriage tax calculator

  • Tax year: 2027, 2026 or 2025.
  • Your and your partner's wages or business income, and pre-tax retirement savings such as a 401(k), 403(b) or deductible IRA.
  • The filing status each of you would use if not married: Single, Head of household or Qualifying surviving spouse. Head of household needs a qualifying child or relative; without one, single is used and a warning explains why.
  • Children under 17 for each of you.
  • Self-employment and age: tick if the income is from self-employment, and enter ages (65 and over adds to the standard deduction).
  • Investment income: interest and non-qualified dividends, qualified dividends, long-term and short-term capital gains, and rent, royalties and other passive income.
  • Deductions and dependents: other dependents aged 17 or older, and the standard deduction or itemized deductions for each person.
  • State tax: a flat state and city rate for each person, which stays the same married or not.

Understanding the results

The headline says Marriage penalty, Marriage bonus or no difference, with the amount a year. Under it, the difference is split into income tax and the Medicare and investment taxes, followed by the federal total as two single filers and as a married couple. The Single or married table shows every line for you, your partner, the two of you unmarried, and married: all income, federal income tax, any net investment income tax, Social Security, Medicare, state tax, retirement savings, take-home pay and the marginal tax rate.

A minus income tax means refundable credits, such as the refundable part of the child tax credit and the earned income credit, are larger than the tax, so the IRS pays the difference. Social Security is worked out per person on wages, so it does not change with marriage.

The 2026 standard deduction is $16,100 for a single filer, $24,150 for a head of household and $32,200 for a married couple filing jointly. The 0.9% Additional Medicare tax starts at $200,000 of wages for a single filer but $250,000 for a couple, and the 3.8% net investment income tax uses the same $200,000 and $250,000 thresholds; neither pair doubles.

Worked examples

  • Two similar middle incomes (the starting figures): $70,000 and $40,000 of wages in 2026. Single, the income taxes are $6,570 and $2,620; married, $8,840. The couple gets a small bonus of $350, because part of the $70,000 earner's income moves from the 22% bracket into the joint 12% bracket.
  • One earner: $150,000 and no income. Federal tax falls from $36,209 to $26,815, a bonus of $9,394 a year.
  • Two high earners: $500,000 and $450,000 of wages. Married, they pay $4,332 more: $2,982 of income tax, because the joint 37% bracket starts at $768,700 rather than twice $640,600, plus $1,350 of Additional Medicare tax, because the joint threshold is $250,000 instead of $400,000.
  • A single parent marrying: a head of household earning $32,000 with two children marries a partner earning $60,000. Alone, the parent's refundable credits exceed the tax (income tax of −$9,008), including $5,608 of earned income credit. On a joint return with $92,000 of income, the earned income credit is gone and the couple pays $6,268 more a year.

What else changes when you marry

  • Filing separately: married couples can also file separate returns, which use their own brackets (in 2026 the 37% rate starts at $384,350 of taxable income for each spouse, half the joint figure). This calculator compares single returns with a joint return only.
  • Head of household status ends for both of you once you file jointly, which removes its wider brackets and larger standard deduction.
  • Credits and deductions that phase out with income, such as the earned income credit, use your combined income, as the single-parent example shows.
  • Itemized deductions are added together on a joint return; if one of you itemizes and the other does not, check whether the joint total beats the joint standard deduction.
  • State taxes have their own rules. The calculator uses the flat rate you type for each person, so a state where marriage changes the brackets, such as California, may differ from the result here.

Marriage tax outside the US: the UK Marriage Allowance

Searches for a marriage tax calculator in the UK usually mean the Marriage Allowance, which works very differently from US joint filing. GOV.UK explains that a married person or civil partner who earns less than the Personal Allowance (usually £12,570) can transfer £1,260 of it to a partner who pays basic-rate tax (income from £12,571 to £50,270, or a lower limit in Scotland). That cuts the partner's tax by up to £252 a year, and a claim can be backdated to the 2022 to 2023 tax year. It cannot be combined with Married Couple's Allowance. This calculator covers US federal tax only.

Tips, and related calculators

  • Run the calculator for the year you plan to marry: IRS Publication 501 treats you as married for the whole year if you are married on 31 December, the final day of your tax year.
  • Update your Form W-4 after the wedding, especially if both of you work, so that withholding matches the joint tax.
  • If one of you is self-employed or has large investment income, open those sections, because the Medicare and investment taxes are where many penalties come from.

How it works

Enter each person's yearly wages or business income and any pre-tax retirement savings, the filing status each of you would use if not married, and any children. More income types, deductions, other dependents, ages and state tax rates are under the sections below. The calculator works out three federal returns with the same IRS rules: one for each of you as a single filer (or head of household), and one married filing jointly with everything combined. It compares income tax after credits, Social Security, Medicare with the 0.9% Additional Medicare tax, and the 3.8% investment income tax, and shows the difference as a marriage penalty (more tax) or a marriage bonus (less tax).

The formula

  • Each return: taxable income = income − pre-tax savings (− half of self-employment tax) − standard or itemized deduction (− QBI deduction)
  • Income tax = bracket tax (0/15/20% on qualified dividends and long-term gains) − child tax credit ($500 per other dependent) − earned income credit
  • Social Security = 6.2% of wages up to the wage base; Medicare = 1.45% + 0.9% above $200,000 single or $250,000 for a married couple
  • Penalty (+) or bonus (−) = federal tax married − (your tax single + partner's tax single)

A worked example

In 2026 one partner earns $150,000 and the other has no income. Filing alone, the earner pays $36,209 of federal income tax, Social Security and Medicare. As a married couple filing jointly, the 2026 standard deduction doubles to $32,200 and the wider joint brackets apply, so the total is $26,815: a marriage bonus of $9,394 a year.

Questions

What is a marriage penalty?

It is the extra tax a couple pays on a joint return compared with what they would pay as two single people. It mostly shows up at very high incomes (the 37% bracket starts well below twice the single amount), for the 0.9% Medicare and 3.8% investment taxes, and for people who lose the earned income credit or head of household status.

Why do many couples get a marriage bonus?

Joint brackets and the joint standard deduction are twice the single ones for most incomes. When one partner earns much more than the other, the joint return spreads that income over the wider brackets, so the couple pays less than the higher earner would alone.

How is income tax calculated for married couples?

On a joint return, both incomes are added, the joint standard deduction ($32,200 in 2026) or itemized deductions are taken off, and the joint brackets are applied to what is left. Credits for children and other dependents then reduce the tax.

How do I calculate my taxes married filing jointly vs single?

Work out each person's tax as a single filer and add the two, then work out one joint return with everything combined, and compare. This calculator does all three returns with the same IRS rules and shows the difference.

Does marriage change Social Security or state tax here?

Social Security is worked out per person on wages, so it stays the same. State tax uses the flat rate you enter for each person. Medicare can change: the 0.9% extra tax starts at $250,000 for a couple, not two times $200,000.

Why can the income tax be a minus number?

Refundable credits such as the additional child tax credit and the earned income credit can be more than the tax, and the IRS pays the rest. That often happens for a single parent filing as head of household, and it is a big reason marriage can cost such a parent money.

Is there a 2026 marriage tax calculator?

Yes, pick 2026 as the tax year; 2025 and 2027 are also available. Each year uses the IRS inflation figures for that year, so the same incomes can give slightly different results.

What is the UK marriage tax allowance?

It lets a lower-earning spouse or civil partner transfer £1,260 of their Personal Allowance to a basic-rate partner, saving up to £252 a year, according to GOV.UK. It is separate from US joint filing, which this calculator handles.
