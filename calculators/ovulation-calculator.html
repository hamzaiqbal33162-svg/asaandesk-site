When do I ovulate?

This ovulation calculator estimates the day you are most likely to ovulate, your fertile window, the best days for sex if you want to get pregnant, the earliest sensible day for a pregnancy test and the date your next period is due. It repeats the pattern for your next six cycles on a calendar.

Ovulation is the release of an egg from an ovary. ACOG explains that in a 28-day cycle it happens about 14 days before the next period starts, and that the gap between ovulation and the next period is the steadiest part of the cycle. That is why the calculator counts back 14 days from your next expected period. It is an estimate for a regular cycle, not medical advice.

How to use the ovulation calculator

  • Enter the first day of your last period, the first day of bleeding.
  • Choose your usual cycle length, from 22 to 44 days, counted from the first day of one period to the first day of the next. If your cycles vary, use your average and read the dates loosely.
  • Read your most likely ovulation day and ovulation window at the top, then the best days for sex, the test day, the next period and the due date if you conceive this cycle.
  • Scroll down for the next six cycles in a table and on a colour-coded calendar.

How the ovulation date is calculated

The formula is simple: ovulation day = first day of your last period + (cycle length − 14) days. The calculator then marks two days either side of it as the ovulation window, and the five days before ovulation through two days after as the best days for sex, because sperm can wait in the body for the egg.

The fertile window

ACOG says an egg survives for about 24 hours after ovulation, while sperm usually survive about 3 days and sometimes up to 5. The NHS adds that sperm can survive in the fallopian tubes for up to 7 days. So the chance of pregnancy comes mostly from sex in the days before ovulation, not after it.

A well-known study by Wilcox and colleagues (1995) followed 221 women trying to conceive. Pregnancies came only from sex during a six-day span ending on the day of ovulation, and the chance per cycle rose from about 10% for sex five days before ovulation to about 33% on the day itself. A later analysis by the same team (2000) found that only about 30% of women had their fertile window wholly within cycle days 10 to 17, and that the timing could be unpredictable even in regular cycles.

Ways to pin down ovulation

  • Cervical mucus: just before ovulation the mucus increases and becomes thin and slippery, and after ovulation it gets thicker and less noticeable, according to ACOG.
  • Basal body temperature: your temperature at full rest rises slightly, by about 0.5 to 1°F, at ovulation and stays higher until the end of the cycle. ACOG notes it shows only that ovulation has already happened; the most fertile days are the 2 to 3 days before the rise, so a few months of charts show your pattern.
  • Urine hormone tests: ovulation tests and electronic fertility monitors detect hormones in urine to flag your fertile days.
  • Combining signs: the symptothermal method uses temperature and cervical mucus together instead of relying on one sign.

Irregular periods

The NHS describes regular cycles anywhere from 21 to 35 days as normal, and says ovulation usually happens 10 to 16 days before the next period. If your cycle length changes from month to month, a calendar estimate can miss your fertile days. Track your cycles for a few months, use the shortest and longest lengths to see the range of dates, and add mucus checks or ovulation tests. Very irregular or missing periods are worth discussing with a doctor.

Safe days and choosing a baby's sex

Calendar dates alone are not reliable birth control. ACOG reports that with typical use of fertility awareness methods, 12 to 24 women out of 100 become pregnant in the first year, compared with fewer than 1 to 5 with perfect use. The Standard Days method, for example, treats days 8 to 19 as fertile and works only for cycles that are always 26 to 32 days long. Talk to a doctor or a family planning service about a method that suits you.

Some people search for an ovulation calculator for a boy or a girl. The 1995 Wilcox study found that cycles producing boys and girls had similar timing of sex, and concluded that for practical purposes timing has no influence on the baby's sex.

When to see a doctor

The NHS says more than 8 in 10 couples where the woman is under 40 conceive within a year if they have regular sex every 2 or 3 days. ACOG recommends a fertility check after 1 year of trying, or after 6 months if you are older than 35, and the NHS also suggests seeing a GP sooner from age 36 or if you already know of a possible fertility problem.

How it works

Enter the first day of your last period and how long your cycles usually are. In most cycles the egg is released about 14 days before the next period starts, so ovulation is placed at your cycle length minus 14 days after the period began. The window of two days either side covers normal variation, and because sperm can survive several days, sex in the five days before ovulation can also lead to a pregnancy. The calendar repeats the same pattern for your next six cycles.

The formula

  • Most likely ovulation day O = first day of last period + (cycle length − 14) days
  • Ovulation window = O − 2 to O + 2 days
  • Best days for sex to get pregnant = O − 5 to O + 2 days
  • Pregnancy test from O + 9 days; next period = first day of last period + cycle length
  • Due date if you conceive this cycle = first day of last period + 280 + (cycle length − 28) days

A worked example

A period that started on November 1, 2026 with a 28-day cycle puts ovulation on November 1 + 14 = November 15. The ovulation window is November 13 to 17, the best days for sex are November 10 to 17, a pregnancy test is worth taking from November 24, and the next period is due on November 29.

Questions

How do I calculate my ovulation days?

Take your usual cycle length, subtract 14, and add that many days to the first day of your last period. For a 28-day cycle that is 14 days after the period started. The calculator also marks two days either side as your ovulation window.

When am I most fertile?

In the two or three days before ovulation and on the day itself. An egg lives for about a day after release, while sperm can live for up to five days, so sex shortly before ovulation gives the best chance of pregnancy.

When do I ovulate with a 30-day cycle?

About 16 days after your period starts, on cycle day 17 if the first day of bleeding is day 1. Your best days for sex are then about cycle days 12 to 19.

How accurate is an ovulation calculator?

It uses averages for a regular cycle. Even with regular periods, ovulation can come a few days earlier or later, and with irregular cycles the estimate is weaker. Ovulation test kits, tracking your temperature or cervical mucus, or a doctor's scan can show it more exactly.

Can I use an ovulation calculator with irregular periods?

Only as a rough guide. Enter your average cycle length, then check the dates for your shortest and longest cycles too, and use cervical mucus or ovulation tests to see when you actually ovulate.

Can I use these dates to avoid pregnancy?

Not on their own. Calendar dates are not reliable birth control, because ovulation moves from cycle to cycle. ACOG reports 12 to 24 pregnancies per 100 women in the first year of typical use of fertility awareness methods. Talk to a doctor or a family planning service about reliable methods.

Can timing sex choose a boy or a girl?

No. A 1995 study of 221 women found that timing sex in relation to ovulation had, for practical purposes, no influence on the baby's sex.

When should I take a pregnancy test?

The NHS says most tests are most reliable from the first day of a missed period. Some very sensitive tests work a little earlier, and this calculator marks a first test day about 9 days after ovulation, but a negative result that early may still turn positive later.

When should I see a doctor about getting pregnant?

ACOG recommends a fertility check after 1 year of trying, or after 6 months if you are older than 35. See a doctor sooner if your periods are very irregular or you know of a health problem that affects fertility.
