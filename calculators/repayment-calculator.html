What the repayment calculator works out

A repayment calculator answers the two questions every borrower asks: how much do I have to pay each time to clear this debt by a certain date, and how long will it take if I pay a fixed amount? It works for any fixed-rate debt, such as a personal loan, car loan, student loan, a credit card balance you stop adding to, or a home loan.

Repayment means paying back what you borrowed together with the interest. With equal payments, each one first covers the interest that has built up since the last payment, and the rest reduces the balance. The calculator follows that month by month, or week by week, and shows the payment or the time, the total you repay, the total interest and a full repayment schedule.

How to use the repayment calculator, step by step

  • Choose your currency and a tab: Pay off in a set time (find the payment) or Pay a set amount (find the time).
  • Enter the Amount you owe and the yearly Interest rate.
  • Choose How often you pay, from daily and weekly through every 2 weeks, twice a month and monthly to quarterly, twice a year or yearly.
  • Set time: enter Pay it off in, years and months. Set amount: enter the Payment each time.
  • Under More options, change Interest compounds only if your lender compounds on a different schedule from your payments, for example daily on a monthly-paid card.

Loan repayment formula: how to calculate repayments

The loan repayment formula is P = B × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n), with B the balance, r the interest rate for one payment period and n the number of payments. For monthly payments on a yearly rate, r is usually the yearly rate ÷ 12.

Pakistan example: a personal loan of Rs 500,000 at 24% a year over 3 years has r = 0.02 and n = 36, so P = 500,000 × 0.02 ÷ (1 − 1.02^−36) = Rs 19,616.43 a month. Over the loan you pay Rs 706,191.35, of which Rs 206,191.35 is interest. The first payment carries Rs 10,000 of interest (2% of the balance) and Rs 9,616.43 of principal; the share of principal grows every month.

To find the time for a fixed payment, use n = −ln(1 − B × r ÷ P) ÷ ln(1 + r). With Rs 25,000 a month on the same loan, n = −ln(1 − 10,000 ÷ 25,000) ÷ ln(1.02) = 25.8, so 26 payments with a smaller last one of Rs 19,936.41. A payment has to be larger than B × r, Rs 10,000 here, or the balance never falls.

In Excel or Google Sheets, =PMT(24%/12, 36, -500000) returns the payment and =NPER(24%/12, -25000, 500000) the number of payments. A repayment schedule in Excel is a table where each row computes interest = previous balance × r, principal = payment − interest, and new balance = previous balance − principal.

Repayments for cars, credit cards, student loans and mortgages

  • Car loan: $25,000 at 7% over 5 years is $495.03 a month and $4,701.80 of interest. Paying every 2 weeks over the same 5 years at the same yearly rate is $228.18 each time, with slightly less interest, $4,662.99, because the balance falls a little sooner.
  • Credit card: if you stop using the card, $5,000 at 22% takes 4 years 4 months at $150 a month and costs $2,798.05 of interest; at $250 a month it is gone in 2 years 1 month for $1,285.72. Many cards compound daily: choosing Daily under More options for a 2-year payoff raises the payment from $259.39 to $259.88. The Credit Card Payoff Calculator handles minimum payments.
  • Student loans: enter the balance and rate of each loan, or their total with an average rate, to see a payment or a payoff date. Plans whose payment depends on income work differently and are not modelled here; the Student Loan Calculator covers student loans in more detail.
  • Home loans: the maths is the same, but taxes, insurance and long terms matter more; use the Mortgage Calculator or the Mortgage Payoff Calculator.

Repayment capacity: how much can you afford?

Lenders look at how much of your income already goes to debts. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) defines the debt-to-income ratio as all your monthly debt payments divided by your gross monthly income, and notes that different loan products and lenders set different limits. With $2,000 of monthly debt payments and $6,000 of gross monthly income, the ratio is 33%.

To find a repayment you can carry, start from your take-home pay, subtract rent or housing, bills, food and existing debt payments, and keep a buffer for surprises. Then use the Pay a set amount tab with that figure to see how long the debt will take. The Debt-to-Income Ratio Calculator works out the lender's view.

Ways to repay a loan faster, and common mistakes

  • Pay a little more than the minimum. On the Rs 500,000 loan, raising the payment from Rs 19,616.43 to Rs 25,000 cuts about 10 months and Rs 61,254.94 of interest.
  • Pay more often. Payments every 2 weeks lower the balance sooner; half your monthly payment every 2 weeks also adds one extra monthly payment a year.
  • Put windfalls such as a bonus or tax refund toward the balance, and ask your lender to apply extra money to principal.
  • Check for early repayment charges before paying a loan off; some lenders charge a fee or extra profit for early settlement.
  • Match the rate to the frequency: the calculator turns the yearly rate into a rate per payment for you, so always enter the yearly rate.
  • Do not keep borrowing on the same debt. The results assume the balance only goes down.

How it works

Enter what you owe and the yearly interest rate, and choose how often you pay. On Pay off in a set time, give the years and months you want to be debt-free in: the calculator finds the payment that clears the balance exactly then. On Pay a set amount, give the payment you can make each time: it works out how many payments that takes, with a smaller last payment so you never overpay. Interest compounds as often as you pay unless you choose otherwise under More options.

The formula

  • Rate per payment r = (1 + i ÷ m)^(m ÷ p) − 1, where i is the yearly rate, m the times interest compounds a year and p the payments a year (continuous: r = e^(i ÷ p) − 1)
  • A year is 52 weeks (26 two-week periods) or 365.25 days
  • Set time: n = years × p payments, payment P = B × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n); at 0% P = B ÷ n
  • Set amount: n = −ln(1 − B × r ÷ P) ÷ ln(1 + r) payments; at 0% n = B ÷ P
  • A set payment must be more than B × r, the interest one period adds

A worked example

A personal loan of Rs 500,000 at 24% a year, repaid monthly over 3 years: r = 0.24 ÷ 12 = 0.02 and n = 36, so P = 500,000 × 0.02 ÷ (1 − 1.02^−36) = Rs 19,616.43 a month and Rs 206,191.35 of interest in all. Paying Rs 25,000 a month instead takes n = −ln(1 − 10,000 ÷ 25,000) ÷ ln(1.02) = 25.8 payments, about 2 years 2 months, and saves Rs 61,254.94 of interest.

Questions

How do I calculate repayments on a loan?

Use P = B × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n), with B the amount owed, r the rate per payment (the yearly rate ÷ 12 for monthly payments) and n the number of payments. Rs 500,000 at 24% over 36 months gives Rs 19,616.43 a month.

How long will it take to repay my loan?

Choose Pay a set amount and enter your payment. The calculator uses n = −ln(1 − B × r ÷ P) ÷ ln(1 + r) and shows the years and months, the number of payments and the smaller last payment.

What is a repayment schedule?

A table of every payment with how much goes to interest, how much to principal and the balance left after it. The calculator shows it by year and by payment.

Does paying every two weeks instead of monthly save interest?

A little, because the balance drops sooner and less interest builds up. The bigger saving comes when you pay half the monthly amount every two weeks: that is 26 half payments a year, one extra monthly payment, so the debt ends sooner.

What is the smallest payment that works?

It must be more than the interest that builds up in one period, the balance times the rate per period. With 10,000 at 10% paid monthly that is 83.33, so any payment above it clears the debt eventually, and every bit more shortens the time a lot.

Which compounding should I choose?

Leave it on As often as you pay unless your lender states otherwise. Many card and loan agreements compound daily; choosing Daily then gives a slightly higher cost for the same yearly rate.

What is the loan repayment formula in Excel?

=PMT(rate/12, months, -amount) gives the monthly payment, for example =PMT(24%/12, 36, -500000) = 19,616.43. =NPER(rate/12, -payment, amount) gives the number of payments for a fixed payment.

Can I use this as a loan repayment calculator in Pakistan?

Yes. Choose Rs, enter the loan amount, the yearly markup or profit rate from your bank and the tenure. For KIBOR-linked loans whose rate is revised, run it again with the balance left and the new rate.

Why is the number of payments a whole number when the time is not?

A payment you choose rarely divides the balance evenly. The calculator counts every payment, including a smaller last one that only clears what is left, and adds up what you really pay.
