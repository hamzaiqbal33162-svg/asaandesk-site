How it works

Pick any date and the weekday appears straight away, with its day number in the year, the days left in that year, which Monday, Tuesday and so on of the year it is, the ISO week number, whether the year is a leap year and how far the date is from today. Old dates also show the same day on the Julian calendar, and a table lists the weekday of the same date in the next 10 years.

The formula

  • Day number = days since Jan 1, 1970 on the Gregorian calendar; weekday = (day number + 4) mod 7, where 0 is Sunday
  • Leap year: divisible by 4, except century years, which must be divisible by 400 (2000 yes, 2100 no)
  • Day of the year = date − Jan 1 + 1; days left = 365 or 366 − day of the year
  • Nth weekday of the year = (day of the year − 1) ÷ 7, rounded down, + 1; a weekday comes 53 times when it is one of the year's first 1 (or 2 in a leap year) weekdays
  • ISO week: weeks start on Monday and week 1 is the one with the year's first Thursday

A worked example

Nov 21, 2026: Jan 1, 2026 was a Thursday and Nov 21 is day 325, so it is 324 days later. 324 = 46 × 7 + 2, and two days after Thursday is Saturday. It is the 47th Saturday of 2026, with 40 days and 5 Saturdays left in the year.

Questions

How does it know the weekday of a date long ago?

The Gregorian calendar repeats exactly every 400 years, so every date can be turned into a count of days from a known day. That count divided by 7 gives the weekday. The same rule works for any year from 1 to 9999.

Why is there a Julian date for old dates?

Before the Gregorian calendar, Europe used the Julian calendar, and countries switched at different times: most of Catholic Europe in 1582, Britain and its American colonies in 1752, Russia in 1918, Greece in 1923. A date in an old record may be a Julian one, so both are shown for dates before 1924.

What is the ISO week number?

It numbers the weeks of a year from Monday to Sunday, with week 1 being the week that holds the first Thursday of the year. So Jan 1 can belong to week 52 or 53 of the year before, as Jan 1, 2027 does (week 53 of 2026).

Why do some years have 53 Saturdays?

A common year is 52 weeks and 1 day, and a leap year 52 weeks and 2 days. The weekday of Jan 1 (and of Jan 2 in a leap year) therefore comes 53 times, every other weekday 52 times.
