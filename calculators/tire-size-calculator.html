What a tire size calculator tells you

A tire size calculator reads the size code on a tire's sidewall, such as 195/65R15, and turns it into real dimensions: how tall the whole tire is, how high the sidewall is, how wide the tread is, how far the tire rolls in one turn and how many turns it makes in a mile or a kilometre. It can also list other sizes that fit a bigger or smaller wheel without changing the overall height much, and compare two tires to show the effect on your speedometer.

Those numbers matter whenever you change wheels or tires. A tire that is noticeably taller or shorter than the original changes the speedometer and odometer readings, the gearing, and the clearance in the wheel arch, and can upset the anti-lock brakes and stability control that expect a certain wheel speed. Checking the overall diameter before you buy avoids most of those surprises.

The same code is used for tires and, in British and Pakistani spelling, tyres: 195/65R15 means exactly the same on both sides of the Atlantic.

What the tire size numbers mean

Take 195/65R15 91H as an example. Each part of the code has a fixed meaning:

  • Optional prefix: P for passenger car (sized to the US TRA standard), LT for light truck, ST for special trailer, T for a temporary spare. No prefix usually means a metric size to the European ETRTO standard.
  • 195: the nominal section width, the tire's widest point from sidewall to sidewall, in millimetres.
  • 65: the aspect ratio, the sidewall height as a percentage of the width. Here the sidewall is 65% of 195 mm, which is 126.75 mm. A lower number means a shorter, sportier sidewall.
  • R: the construction. R is radial, used on almost all modern cars; D is diagonal (cross-ply) and B is bias-belted.
  • 15: the wheel (rim) diameter in inches that the tire fits.
  • 91: the load index, the most weight one tire may carry at its rated pressure; 91 means 615 kg (1,356 lb).
  • H: the speed rating, the highest speed the tire is designed to sustain under that load; H is 210 km/h (130 mph).

Off-road and light-truck tires are often sized in inches instead, such as 31x10.50R15: about 31 inches tall, 10.5 inches wide, radial, for a 15-inch wheel. The calculator accepts both styles. The load index and speed rating do not change the dimensions, so you can leave them off when you type the size.

Speed ratings and load index

When you change size, the new tires should have at least the load index and speed rating your car's maker specifies. The speed rating is the letter at the end of the code.

The load index is a number code for weight: 91 means 615 kg (1,356 lb) per tire, 94 means 670 kg (1,477 lb) and 95 means 690 kg (1,521 lb). Multiply by four for the most the set can carry, and never fit tires with a lower index than the car maker asks for.

Tires also carry a DOT code in the US whose last four digits give the week and year of manufacture: 2325 means week 23 of 2025. Older codes, from before 2000, used three digits.

How to calculate tire size in inches, step by step

  • Sidewall height = width × aspect ratio ÷ 100. For 195/65R15: 195 × 65 ÷ 100 = 126.75 mm.
  • Overall diameter = wheel diameter × 25.4 + 2 × sidewall, because the tire has a sidewall above and below the wheel: 15 × 25.4 + 2 × 126.75 = 634.5 mm.
  • In inches, divide by 25.4: 634.5 ÷ 25.4 = 24.98 in, about 25.0 in.
  • Circumference = π × diameter = 1,993.3 mm, the distance covered in one turn.
  • Revolutions per mile = 1,609,344 mm ÷ 1,993.3 mm = 807.4; per km, 1,000,000 ÷ 1,993.3 = 501.7.
  • Difference between two tires = (new diameter − old diameter) ÷ old diameter × 100%.

Worked examples: bigger wheels and bigger tires

Plus one: 16 to 17 inch wheels. A car on 205/60R16 tires (652.4 mm, 25.7 in tall) moving to 17-inch wheels gets a list of 65 sizes within 3% for that wheel. Among sizes of similar width: 205/55R17 is 0.75% taller, 215/50R17 is 0.86% shorter, 225/50R17 is 0.67% taller and 215/55R17 is 2.44% taller. The closer to 0%, the less the speedometer changes.

The 215/55R17 choice in detail. Compared with 205/60R16, its sidewall is 3.86% lower and its tread 4.88% wider. The speedometer reads 2.38% low: when it shows 60 you are doing about 61.5, and at 100 about 102.4. The odometer counts 100 when you have really covered 102.44.

Small car upgrade. Going from 175/70R14 to 185/60R15 changes the overall diameter by only 0.40% (601 mm to 603 mm), so the speedometer barely moves, while the tread is 5.71% wider and the sidewall 9.39% lower.

Bigger off-road tires. Replacing 265/65R17 with 285/70R17 on the same wheel makes the tire 7.02% taller (30.6 in to 32.7 in). The calculator warns that this is over 3%: at 60 on the speedometer the real speed is about 64.2, and the speedometer reads 6.56% low. A change that large also needs a check of clearance, gearing and the speedometer calibration.

Speedometer, odometer and gearing after a size change

A speedometer does not measure speed directly; it counts how fast the wheels turn and assumes the original tire size. A taller tire covers more ground in each turn, so the car is going faster than the speedometer says and the odometer under-counts the distance. A shorter tire does the opposite. The Compare tab gives the exact percentage and a table from 20 to 120.

A taller tire also acts like a higher gear: the engine turns more slowly for a given road speed, and the car feels less eager to accelerate. A shorter tire does the reverse. Many fitters suggest keeping the overall diameter within about 3% of the original, which is why that is the default limit for the alternatives list.

How to find your tire size, and tips before you buy

  • Read the size on the sidewall of the tires now fitted, or check the tire information placard (usually on the driver's door frame) and the owner's manual for the sizes the maker approves.
  • Keep the load index and speed rating at least as high as the original specification.
  • Check the rim width as well as the diameter: each tire size suits a range of rim widths.
  • Make sure a wider or taller tire clears the suspension, brakes and wheel arch at full steering lock and over bumps.
  • Replace tires in pairs on the same axle, or all four on many four-wheel-drive and all-wheel-drive cars, so the wheels turn at matching speeds.
  • Remember that sizes are nominal: real tires differ slightly, and under the car's weight the rolling diameter is a little smaller than the figure here.

How it works

A tire size such as 225/45R17 holds three numbers: the tread width in millimetres (225), the sidewall height as a percentage of that width (45, the aspect ratio) and the wheel diameter in inches (17); the R means radial. Type the code as printed on the tire and the calculator works out the sidewall height, the overall diameter, the circumference and how many times the tire turns in a mile and in a kilometre, and draws it to scale. To fit a different wheel, tick the box and enter the new wheel diameter: you get every size in 5 mm and 5% steps that keeps the overall diameter within 3% of the original. The Compare tab puts two tires side by side and shows how far your speedometer and odometer would be off with the second one.

The formula

  • Sidewall (mm) = width × aspect ratio ÷ 100
  • Overall diameter (mm) = wheel (in) × 25.4 + 2 × sidewall
  • Circumference = π × diameter; revolutions per mile = 1,609,344 ÷ circumference (mm)
  • Real speed = speedometer reading × new diameter ÷ old diameter
  • Inch sizes (31x10.50R15): overall diameter 31 in, width 10.5 in, wheel 15 in

A worked example

For 195/65R15 the sidewall is 195 × 65 ÷ 100 = 126.75 mm, so the overall diameter is 15 × 25.4 + 2 × 126.75 = 634.5 mm (25.0 in) and the tire turns 807.4 times a mile. Swapping 205/60R16 (652.4 mm) for 215/55R17 (668.3 mm) makes the tire 2.44% larger, so at 60 on the speedometer you are really doing about 61.5.

Questions

What do the numbers in 225/45R17 mean?

225 is the tread width in millimetres, 45 is the sidewall height as a percentage of the width (so 101 mm here), R means radial construction and 17 is the wheel diameter in inches. Numbers after the size, such as 94W, are the load index and speed rating; they do not change the dimensions.

How do I calculate tire size in inches?

Multiply the width by the aspect ratio and divide by 100 for the sidewall in mm, double it, add the wheel diameter × 25.4, and divide by 25.4. 195/65R15 comes to 634.5 mm, or 24.98 inches.

How do I calculate the tire size difference?

Work out both overall diameters, subtract the old from the new and divide by the old. 215/55R17 against 205/60R16 is (668.3 − 652.4) ÷ 652.4 = 2.44% larger; the Compare tab does it for every measurement.

Can I fit a different tire size?

Usually only if the overall diameter stays within about 3% of the original, so the speedometer, gearing and stability systems keep working, and the tire fits the rim width and the wheel arch. The alternatives list checks the diameter only: confirm the load index, speed rating and clearance with your car's handbook or a tire fitter.

What size tires fit my rims?

The last number of the code must match the wheel diameter, and the rim width must suit the tire's width. To keep the same overall height on a new wheel size, tick the alternatives box and type the new wheel diameter.

Why does a bigger tire make my speedometer read low?

The speedometer counts wheel turns. A tire with a larger diameter travels further on each turn, so the car moves faster than the turns suggest. A 2.44% larger tire shows 60 when you are doing about 61.5, and the odometer counts fewer miles than you drive.

How do I find the tire size on my car?

It is moulded into the sidewall of each tire, for example 195/65R15 91H. The sizes the car maker approves are also on the tire placard, usually inside the driver's door, and in the owner's manual.

What does 31x10.50R15 mean?

It is an inch size: about 31 inches tall, 10.5 inches wide, radial, for a 15-inch wheel. The calculator gives its sidewall as 8.0 in (203 mm) and 650.6 revolutions per mile.

What is a tire's aspect ratio?

The sidewall height as a percentage of the tread width. On a 205/55 tire the sidewall is 55% of 205 mm, about 113 mm; a lower aspect ratio means a shorter sidewall.

Are the sizes exact?

They are the nominal sizes from the code. Real tires of the same size differ by a few millimetres between makes, and the rolling diameter under the car's weight is slightly smaller, so treat small differences as estimates.
