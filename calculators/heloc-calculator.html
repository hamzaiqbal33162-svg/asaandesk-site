How it works

A HELOC has two stages. During the draw period you can borrow from the line and usually pay only the interest, so the balance does not go down. When the draw period ends, the repayment period starts: the balance is paid off in equal monthly payments of principal and interest, which is when the payment jumps. The calculator assumes you use the whole line (or the amount you type) at the start and that the rate stays the same, then adds up every payment. With costs switched on it adds the closing cost and the yearly fee, and shows the yearly rate that includes them. The second tab shows how big a line your equity may allow: lenders let all loans on the home together reach a share of its value, often 80% to 85%.

The formula

  • Draw period: payment = amount used × r, with r = yearly rate ÷ 12; the balance stays the same
  • Repayment period: payment = amount × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−m), with m = repayment years × 12
  • Total paid = draw payment × draw months + repayment payment × m; total interest = total paid − amount used
  • Cost of the line = total interest + closing cost + yearly fee × draw years
  • Yearly cost with fees: the monthly rate at which all payments and fees are worth the amount used minus the closing cost, times 12
  • Line you may get = lender's limit % × home value − mortgage owed

A worked example

A $50,000 line at 8% with a 5-year draw and 15-year repayment: 50,000 × 0.08 ÷ 12 = $333.33 a month for 5 years, then $477.83 a month for 15 years. All 240 payments add up to $106,008.69, of which $56,008.69 is interest.

Questions

Why does my HELOC payment go up after the draw period?

During the draw period most HELOCs need only the interest, so none of the balance is paid off. When repayment starts, the whole balance has to be cleared over a shorter time, so each payment carries principal as well as interest. Plan for that jump.

Is the rate fixed?

Usually not. Most HELOC rates are the prime rate plus a margin and change when prime moves. The calculator keeps one rate so you can compare; try a higher rate to see how the payments could change.

What is the APR of a HELOC?

US rules define a credit line's APR as its interest rate, without the closing cost or yearly fees, which is why the two match here. To compare the full cost, look at the yearly cost with fees and the cost of the line.

Do I have to use the whole line?

No. You pay interest only on what you use. Open More options and type the amount you plan to use to see payments on that.

How big a line can I get?

Lenders often let all loans on the home reach 80% to 85% of its value. With a $600,000 home and $250,000 owed, an 80% lender may offer up to $230,000. Your credit and income also count.
