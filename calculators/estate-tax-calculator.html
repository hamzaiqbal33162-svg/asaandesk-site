What this estate tax calculator estimates

An estate tax calculator estimates the US federal tax due on everything a person owns when they die, before it passes to their heirs. The estate tax, sometimes called the death tax, is paid by the estate itself, not by the people who inherit. It only applies to large estates: for a death in 2026, the first $15 million per person is exempt.

This calculator follows the steps of Form 706, the federal estate tax return. It adds up the assets, takes off debts, final costs, gifts to charity, state death taxes and anything left to a US-citizen spouse, adds back large lifetime gifts and applies the exemption for the year of death. You can compare deaths in 2025, 2026 and 2027, and add a late spouse's unused exemption.

It is an estimate for planning, not tax or legal advice. Real estates involve valuations, trusts and elections that an estate attorney or tax adviser should review, and exemption amounts change with the law and with inflation.

How to use the estate tax calculator

  • Year of death: 2025, 2026 or 2027. Each year has its own exemption.
  • Main assets: Home and other real estate; then Stocks, bonds and other investments; Bank accounts and CDs; and Retirement accounts. Use market value on the date of death, not what was paid for them.
  • Debts: mortgages, loans and card balances owed at death.
  • More assets: Cars, boats and other belongings, Life insurance payout (counted when the person owned the policy or it pays the estate) and Business interests and anything else owned.
  • Debts, costs and gifts at death: Funeral, settlement and legal costs; amounts Left to charity; State estate or inheritance tax paid; and what is Left to a spouse who is a US citizen.
  • Lifetime gifts and a late spouse: Taxable gifts made during life (only the part of each gift above the yearly allowance), the Gift tax already paid on them (leave blank to let the calculator estimate it) and the Unused exemption from a late spouse, the DSUE amount shown on that spouse's Form 706.

How estate tax is calculated

Step 1 is the gross estate: the fair market value on the date of death of everything the person owned or controlled, including homes, investments, bank accounts, retirement accounts, business interests, personal belongings and life insurance they owned. Step 2 is the taxable estate: subtract debts, funeral and administration costs, gifts to qualified charities, state death taxes and property passing to a surviving spouse.

Step 3 adds back adjusted taxable gifts, the lifetime gifts that went over the yearly allowance, because the gift tax and the estate tax share one exemption. Step 4 applies the rate schedule in section 2001(c) of the tax code to that total to get a tentative tax. Step 5 subtracts any gift tax already payable on the lifetime gifts and the unified credit, which is the same schedule applied to the exemption.

Because the credit wipes out the tax on the first $15 million, and every bracket above $1 million is taxed at 40%, the practical estate tax formula is: tax = 40% × (taxable estate + adjusted taxable gifts − exemption), never less than zero. So the estate tax rate that matters is 40%, but it applies only to the slice above the exemption.

Estate tax exemption by year

The exemption, officially the basic exclusion amount, has risen sharply since 2001. Legislation enacted in 2025 (Public Law 119-21) set it at $15 million for 2026 and indexes it to inflation from 2027.

In 2010 estates could choose to pay no estate tax at all, with heirs taking over the person's original cost basis instead of a stepped-up basis. The executor must file Form 706 when the gross estate plus adjusted taxable gifts is above the exemption for the year of death, even if deductions bring the tax to zero.

Worked estate tax examples

An estate just over the exemption. With the defaults, a person dying in 2026 owns $17 million and owes $500,000, so the taxable estate is $16.5 million. That is $1.5 million above the $15 million exemption, and the tax is 40% of it: $600,000. The heirs receive $15.9 million, and the effective rate on the whole estate is 3.6%.

Charity and lifetime gifts. An $18.7 million estate leaves $1 million to charity and has $550,000 of debts and costs, so the taxable estate is $17.15 million. The person also made $1 million of taxable gifts during life. Added together, $18.15 million is $3.15 million above the exemption, so the tax is $1,260,000. Without the charitable gift, the tax would have been $400,000 higher.

A married couple using portability. A husband died in 2024 and left everything to his wife, so none of his $13.61 million exemption was used, and his executor elected portability on a timely Form 706. The wife dies in 2026 with $25 million. Her exemption is $15 million plus his unused $13.61 million, $28.61 million in total, so no federal estate tax is due. Without the portability election, $10 million would have been taxable and the tax would have been $4 million.

A large estate. A single person dying in 2026 with $40 million and no deductions owes 40% of $25 million: $10 million. The heirs receive $30 million, and the effective rate is 25%. A typical estate of $5 million, by contrast, owes nothing federally, though it may owe state tax.

Spouses, gifts and filing rules

Everything left to a surviving spouse who is a US citizen is deductible (the unlimited marital deduction), so the first death in a married couple usually owes no federal tax. Property left to a spouse who is not a US citizen qualifies only through a qualified domestic trust (QDOT). Portability then lets the survivor use the late spouse's unused exemption, but only if the executor elects it on a Form 706 filed on time. Executors of estates that did not otherwise have to file may make the election late, up to the fifth anniversary of the death.

Gifts during life share the same exemption. Each person may give up to $19,000 per recipient in 2025 and 2026 ($20,000 in 2027) without using any of it, and a married couple giving jointly can give $38,000 per recipient in 2026. Tuition paid directly to a school, medical bills paid directly to the provider, gifts to a spouse, gifts to qualified charities and gifts to political organizations are not taxable gifts at all. Larger gifts are reported on Form 709 and use up part of the lifetime exemption; tax is only paid once the exemption is gone.

Form 706 is due 9 months after the date of death, and Form 4768 gives an automatic 6-month extension to file. The executor may value the estate on the date of death or, if it lowers both the gross estate and the tax, on the alternate valuation date 6 months later.

State estate and inheritance taxes, and other countries

Some states tax estates far below the federal level, and a few charge an inheritance tax that the heirs pay, at rates that depend on their relationship to the person who died. Enter any state death tax paid in the calculator; it is deducted from the federal taxable estate.

Ways to reduce estate tax

  • Give within the yearly allowance: $19,000 per person per recipient in 2026, every year, moves money out of the estate without touching the exemption.
  • Pay tuition or medical bills directly to the school or provider; there is no limit on these.
  • Leave money to charity, which is fully deductible from the estate.
  • For married couples, make sure the first executor files Form 706 to elect portability, even when no tax is due.
  • Keep life insurance out of the estate by having someone else, often an irrevocable life insurance trust, own the policy.
  • Consider the alternate valuation date when markets fall in the 6 months after death.

Heirs also benefit from a stepped-up basis: inherited property generally takes its fair market value on the date of death (or the alternate valuation date) as its new cost, so a gain built up during the person's life is not taxed when the heirs sell. A will, beneficiary forms on retirement accounts and insurance, and, where useful, a living trust keep the estate moving quickly and in the way the person wanted.

Tips and related tools

  • Value assets at market value on the date of death, not at purchase price.
  • Count retirement accounts and life insurance the person owned; they are part of the gross estate even when they pass straight to a named beneficiary.
  • Remember income tax: heirs who withdraw from an inherited traditional IRA or 401(k) pay income tax on it, separately from any estate tax.
  • Check your state's rules; a state estate tax can apply to estates far below $15 million.
  • Keep copies of every gift tax return (Form 709). The executor needs them to add lifetime gifts back and to show how much of the exemption is already used.

How it works

Pick the year of death and enter what the person owned, then the debts. More assets, final costs, gifts to charity, state death taxes and what goes to a US-citizen spouse are under the sections below, with taxable lifetime gifts and a late spouse's unused exemption. The calculator follows Form 706: the taxable estate is the gross estate minus those subtractions. Lifetime taxable gifts are added back, the 2001(c) rate table gives a tentative tax, and the gift tax already due on those gifts and the credit for the exemption come off. Above about $1 million the rate is 40%, so in practice the tax is 40% of the amount above the exemption. The table shows the same estate in each year from 2025 to 2027.

The formula

  • Gross estate = real estate + investments + cash + retirement + belongings + life insurance + other
  • Taxable estate = gross estate − debts − funeral and settlement costs − charity − state death tax − amount left to a US-citizen spouse
  • Tentative tax = 2001(c) table on (taxable estate + lifetime taxable gifts): $345,800 + 40% of the part above $1,000,000
  • Estate tax = tentative tax − gift tax due on the lifetime gifts − credit for the exemption (the same table on the exemption + any unused exemption of a late spouse)

A worked example

In 2026 an estate of $18.7 million has $1.55 million of debts, costs and gifts to charity, so the taxable estate is $17.15 million. With $1 million of lifetime taxable gifts, $18.15 million is $3.15 million above the $15 million exemption, and the tax is 40% of that: $1,260,000.

Questions

How is estate tax calculated?

Add up everything the person owned at market value, subtract debts, costs, charity, state death tax and what goes to a US-citizen spouse, then add back lifetime taxable gifts. Anything above the exemption, $15 million for a 2026 death, is taxed at 40%.

How big does an estate have to be to owe federal estate tax?

For a death in 2026, the taxable estate plus lifetime taxable gifts has to be more than $15 million ($15.48 million in 2027, $13.99 million in 2025). A married couple can pass up to twice that, because what goes to a US-citizen spouse is not taxed and the survivor can use the late spouse's unused exemption.

What is the estate tax rate?

The federal rate schedule runs from 18% to 40%, but the exemption's credit covers all the lower brackets, so in practice every dollar above the exemption is taxed at 40%. The effective rate on the whole estate is much lower, 3.6% on a $16.5 million taxable estate in 2026.

What is the estate tax exemption in 2026?

$15 million per person for deaths in 2026, set by legislation enacted in 2025, and $15.48 million in 2027 after the inflation adjustment. It was $13.99 million in 2025.

How much estate tax will my children pay on their inheritance?

Usually none. The federal estate tax is paid by the estate before anything is distributed, there is no federal inheritance tax on heirs, and most estates are below the exemption. A few states do charge an inheritance tax, often with lower rates for children.

Why are lifetime gifts added back?

The gift tax and the estate tax share one exemption. Gifts above the yearly allowance ($19,000 per person in 2026) use up part of it, so they are added to the estate before the exemption is taken off. Any gift tax already due on them is then subtracted, so the same gift is never taxed twice.

Is life insurance part of the estate?

Yes, when the person who died owned the policy or it pays out to the estate. A policy owned by someone else, for example by an irrevocable life insurance trust, usually stays out of the estate.

What is an estate tax return and when is it due?

It is IRS Form 706, filed by the executor 9 months after the date of death, with an automatic 6-month extension on Form 4768. It is required when the gross estate plus adjusted taxable gifts exceeds the exemption, and it is also how a surviving spouse claims portability.

Does my state charge estate tax too?

Several states and Washington, D.C. have an estate tax, and a few have an inheritance tax paid by heirs, often with far lower exemptions than the federal one: Illinois taxes estates above $4 million and Massachusetts requires a return above $2 million. California has none. Enter any state death tax paid; it is subtracted from the federal taxable estate.

Is there an estate tax in Canada?

No. Instead, a person is treated as having sold their capital property at market value just before death, and any gain is taxed on their final income tax return, unless it passes to a spouse or common-law partner. This calculator covers only the US federal estate tax.
