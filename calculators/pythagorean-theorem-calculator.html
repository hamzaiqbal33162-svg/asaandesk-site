What the Pythagorean theorem says

The Pythagorean theorem (Pythagoras' theorem) says that in every right triangle, the square of the hypotenuse equals the sum of the squares of the two legs: a² + b² = c². The legs a and b are the two sides that meet at the right angle, and the hypotenuse c is the side opposite it, always the longest. So if the legs are 3 and 4, then c² = 9 + 16 = 25 and c = 5.

Because the formula links all three sides, any two of them give the third. This Pythagorean theorem calculator finds the missing side exactly, in simplest square-root form such as 3√15, and as a decimal, then adds both acute angles, the area, the perimeter and the height to the hypotenuse, with a labelled picture and every step.

How to use the Pythagorean theorem calculator

  • Fill in exactly two of Leg a, Leg b and Hypotenuse c, and leave the one you want empty. The page opens with a = 3, b = 4 and c empty.
  • To find c, give both legs. To find a or b, give the other leg and the hypotenuse; the leg must be shorter than c.
  • Each side takes a whole number or a decimal (1.25). For a side with a square root, such as 2√5, type 2 in the side box and 5 in the × √ (optional) box beside it, or type 2√5, 2 sqrt 5 or sqrt(5) straight into the side box.
  • Fractions such as 3/4 are not accepted; type them as decimals (0.75). Decimals are kept exact, so 0.75 and 1 give a hypotenuse of exactly 1.25.
  • Use one unit for all three sides. For feet and inches, change everything to inches first (5 ft 6 in = 66 in), or everything to decimal feet.

Understanding the results

  • The headline is the missing side. When it is a square root that does not come out whole, the exact form is shown first (√2, 3√15) with the decimal underneath; otherwise the decimal comes first, with a fraction if it has one.
  • Angle α (faces a) and Angle β (faces b): the two acute angles in degrees, also in degrees-minutes-seconds and radians. They always add up to 90°.
  • Area: a × b ÷ 2, since the legs are the base and height.
  • Perimeter: a + b + c, exact (15 + 3√15) when a root is involved.
  • Height h to the hypotenuse: the perpendicular from the right angle to c, a × b ÷ c.
  • The All values table lists every value as a decimal and in exact form, marks the two sides you gave, and the picture labels your triangle.

Pythagorean theorem formulas with worked examples

Rearranging a² + b² = c² gives one formula for each side: c = √(a² + b²) when the hypotenuse is missing, a = √(c² − b²) when a is missing, and b = √(c² − a²) when b is missing. Subtract when you want a leg, add when you want the hypotenuse.

Find c: with a = 3 and b = 4, c = √(9 + 16) = √25 = 5. The angles are 36.87° and 53.13°, the area 6, the perimeter 12 and the height to the hypotenuse 12/5 = 2.4.

Find b: with a = 5 and c = 13, b = √(169 − 25) = √144 = 12, so the triangle has area 30 and perimeter 30.

Find a leg in surd form: with b = 3 and c = 12, the steps read a = √(12² − 3²) = √(144 − 9) = √135 = 3√15 ≈ 11.618950038622, because 135 = 9 × 15 and √9 = 3.

Legs of 1 and 1 give c = √2 ≈ 1.4142135623731 and two 45° angles, the shape of a square cut along its diagonal.

Pythagorean triples

A Pythagorean triple is a set of three whole numbers that fit a² + b² = c² exactly. Any multiple of a triple is also one, so 3-4-5 gives 6-8-10, 9-12-15 and 12-16-20.

What the Pythagorean theorem is used for in real life

  • Squaring a corner in construction: measure 3 units along one wall and 4 along the other; if the diagonal between the marks is exactly 5, the corner is a right angle. Builders use 6-8-10 or 3-4-5 in feet or metres for larger layouts.
  • Ladders: a 12 ft ladder with its foot 3 ft from the wall reaches √(144 − 9) = 3√15 ≈ 11.62 ft up the wall.
  • Diagonals of rectangles: a room 12 ft by 16 ft measures exactly 20 ft corner to corner; a plot of land 30 ft by 60 ft has a diagonal of √4,500 = 30√5 ≈ 67.08 ft; and a screen 48 in wide and 27 in tall has a diagonal of about 55.07 in, which is how screen sizes are quoted.
  • Isosceles triangles: the height splits one into two right triangles. With equal sides of 10 and a base of 12, the height is √(10² − 6²) = 8, so the area is 12 × 8 ÷ 2 = 48.
  • Coordinates: the distance between two points is the hypotenuse of a triangle with legs ΔX and ΔY. From (1, 2) to (4, 6) the legs are 3 and 4, so the distance is 5. The Distance Calculator does this for any two points, in 3D too.
  • In three dimensions, apply it twice: a box 3 × 4 × 12 has a floor diagonal of 5 and a space diagonal of √(5² + 12²) = 13.

Proof, converse and the law of cosines

One short proof uses the height h. Dropping it from the right angle onto the hypotenuse splits c into two parts, p next to a and q next to b, and makes two smaller triangles that are similar to the whole one. Similarity gives a ÷ c = p ÷ a and b ÷ c = q ÷ b, so a² = cp and b² = cq. Adding them, a² + b² = c(p + q) = c². Euclid gave a different proof, with squares drawn on each side, as Proposition 47 of Book I of the Elements.

The converse is just as useful: if the three sides of a triangle satisfy a² + b² = c², the angle opposite c is a right angle. 7, 24 and 25 pass (49 + 576 = 625), so they make a right triangle; 6, 7 and 9 do not (36 + 49 = 85, not 81). If a² + b² is more than c² the largest angle is acute, and if it is less the angle is obtuse.

For triangles without a right angle, the law of cosines extends the theorem: c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos C. When C = 90°, cos C = 0 and it becomes a² + b² = c². The Triangle Calculator solves any triangle this way.

For school, in Excel, common mistakes and related calculators

Pythagoras' theorem is section 6.1 of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics syllabus (4024, exams in 2025 to 2027), followed by sine, cosine and tangent in right-angled triangles and problems in two and three dimensions. Answers in surd form, such as 3√15, are what the exact result here gives, and Paper 1 allows no calculator.

In Excel or Google Sheets, =SQRT(A1^2 + B1^2) gives the hypotenuse from legs in A1 and B1 (or =SQRT(SUMSQ(A1, B1))), and =SQRT(C1^2 − B1^2) gives a leg. In Python, math.hypot(3, 4) returns 5.0.

  • Adding when you should subtract: a missing leg is √(c² − b²), not √(c² + b²).
  • Using the theorem on a triangle without a right angle; use the law of cosines instead.
  • Forgetting the square root at the end: c² = 25 means c = 5.
  • Mixing units, such as one side in feet and another in inches.

How it works

Fill in two sides of a right triangle and leave the side you want empty. The legs a and b are the two sides that meet at the right angle; the hypotenuse c is the long side across from it. A side can be a whole number, a decimal, or a number times a square root: for 2√5 type 2 in the side box and 5 in the box next to it (or type 2√5 or 2 sqrt 5 straight into the side box). The missing side is worked out exactly, so √16 becomes 4 and √20 becomes 2√5, and also as a decimal. The angles, area, perimeter and the height to the hypotenuse follow, with every step written out.

The formula

  • a² + b² = c², so c = √(a² + b²) and a = √(c² − b²)
  • √(k² × m) = k√m: take square factors out of the root (√20 = √(4 × 5) = 2√5)
  • α = arcsin(a ÷ c), β = 90° − α
  • Area = a × b ÷ 2; perimeter = a + b + c; height to the hypotenuse h = a × b ÷ c

A worked example

Leg a = 2 and hypotenuse c = 2√5: b = √(c² − a²) = √(20 − 4) = √16 = 4. Then α = arcsin(2 ÷ 4.47214) ≈ 26.565°, β ≈ 63.435°, the area is 2 × 4 ÷ 2 = 4, the perimeter is 6 + 2√5 ≈ 10.47214 and the height to the hypotenuse is 8 ÷ 2√5 = 4√5/5 ≈ 1.78885.

Questions

How do I calculate the Pythagorean theorem to find c?

Square both legs, add them and take the square root: c = √(a² + b²). For legs of 8 and 15, c = √(64 + 225) = √289 = 17. Type the two legs and leave Hypotenuse c empty.

What is the formula if b is missing?

b = √(c² − a²): square the hypotenuse, subtract the square of the known leg and take the root. With a = 5 and c = 13, b = √(169 − 25) = 12.

Can I find the sides with only the hypotenuse?

Not from the theorem alone: a hypotenuse of 10 fits legs of 6 and 8, but also 1 and √99, and endlessly many others. You need a second side, or an angle, in which case the Right Triangle Calculator uses sine and cosine instead.

How do I use the Pythagorean theorem with feet and inches?

Change every measurement to one unit first. For legs of 5 ft 6 in and 7 ft 6 in, use 66 in and 90 in: c = √(66² + 90²) ≈ 111.61 in, which is 9 ft 3.6 in.

Which side is the hypotenuse?

The hypotenuse is the side across from the right angle, and it is always the longest side. The two shorter sides, which meet at the right angle, are the legs.

What does 2√5 mean?

It means 2 times the square root of 5, about 4.47214. Square roots that do not come out whole are kept in this exact form, with any square factor taken out (√20 = 2√5), and the decimal is shown next to it.

What is the converse of the Pythagorean theorem?

If the sides of a triangle satisfy a² + b² = c², the triangle has a right angle opposite c. It is how you check whether three lengths, such as 20, 21 and 29, make a right triangle: 400 + 441 = 841 = 29².

Why do I get an error that a leg must be shorter than c?

In a right triangle the hypotenuse is longer than either leg, because c² = a² + b² is more than a² or b² alone. If the leg you typed is as long as c or longer, no right triangle fits.

Does it work with decimals?

Yes. Decimals are handled exactly as fractions (2.5 is 5/2), so the answer is still exact: legs 1.5 and 2 give a hypotenuse of exactly 2.5.
