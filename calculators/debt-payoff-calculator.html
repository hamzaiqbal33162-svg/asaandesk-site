What a debt payoff calculator does

A debt payoff calculator turns a list of loans and cards into one plan: the month you become debt-free, the total interest, and exactly what to pay on each debt each month. This one takes up to 20 debts of any kind (credit cards, car loans, student loans, personal loans, even a mortgage), adds any extra money you can find, and sends that extra to one debt at a time until everything is cleared.

The key idea is a fixed monthly total. When one debt is paid off, you keep paying the same amount and its freed payment rolls on to the next debt. Each debt then falls faster than the one before, which is why a plan like this can cut years off the time it takes to become debt-free, even with no extra money at all.

How to use the debt payoff calculator

  • Number of debts: choose how many rows you need, from 1 to 20.
  • For each debt, enter a Name (optional), the Balance owed, the Monthly payment (what you pay now, or the minimum) and the Interest rate (APR). Use 0 for an interest-free debt.
  • Extra you can pay each month: money on top of the payments above. 0 is fine.
  • Pay extra to: Highest rate first (the avalanche method) or Smallest balance first (the snowball method).
  • More extra payments (optional): Extra once a year (paid in month 1, then every 12 months), a One-time extra payment with the month it is Paid in, and When a debt is paid off, where you can choose to Move its payment to the next debt or Stop paying it.
  • Pick your currency at the top; the plan updates as you type.

If a debt's monthly payment does not cover the interest it adds each month, the calculator says so and shows the amount you need to pay above, because that balance would never fall on its own.

Debt avalanche or debt snowball?

Both methods pay every debt its regular payment and send the extra to one target. The avalanche targets the highest interest rate, so each extra unit of money stops the most expensive interest; with a fixed monthly total it normally costs the least interest. The snowball targets the smallest balance, so whole debts disappear sooner and you have fewer bills to track. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau describes the trade-off simply: the highest-rate method saves the most money, while the snowball shows progress quickly but may cost more in the long run.

With the starting debts, the difference is modest: the avalanche costs $7,552.82 of interest and the snowball $7,842.26, and both finish in 53 months. The gap grows when a large debt carries the highest rate, because the snowball leaves it untouched for longer. The calculator always shows the other order's interest, so you can decide whether quick wins are worth the extra cost to you.

Worked example: three debts in Pakistani rupees

Choose Pakistani rupee and enter three debts at assumed rates (use the rates on your own statements): a credit card of Rs 180,000 at 40% with Rs 9,000 a month, a personal loan of Rs 600,000 at 24% with Rs 23,000, and a motorbike loan of Rs 150,000 at 28% with Rs 8,000. Add Rs 5,000 extra a month, so the family pays Rs 45,000 every month.

Highest rate first clears the card in month 18 and the motorbike loan in month 20, and the household is debt-free in 28 months (2 years 4 months) with Rs 327,711.08 of interest. Smallest balance first would clear the motorbike loan first but cost Rs 340,336.99 and take a month longer. Paying only each debt's own payment, with no extra and no rollover, would take 3 years 2 months and cost Rs 427,879.69, so the plan saves about Rs 100,169.

Before settling any Pakistani loan early, ask the bank for its early settlement charges and an exact payoff figure; some loans charge a fee for paying ahead, and that cost should be weighed against the interest saved.

Payoff amount, payoff quote and payoff date

The balance on your statement is not always what it takes to close a loan. The CFPB explains that a payoff amount also includes interest up to the day you pay, any unpaid fees and any prepayment penalty, so it differs from the current balance. A payoff quote, payoff letter or payoff statement is the lender's written figure for a given date; for loans secured by a home, the servicer must provide an accurate one when you ask.

To estimate the payoff date of a single debt by hand, use the number of payments formula: n = −ln(1 − r × B ÷ P) ÷ ln(1 + r), where B is the balance, P the monthly payment and r the monthly rate. In Excel or Google Sheets, =NPER(rate/12, −payment, balance) gives the same count. For the $4,500 card at 22.99% paid at $220 a month, it is about 26.2 payments, so the card is cleared in month 27, as the plan shows.

Ways to find extra money, and biweekly payments

  • Build a budget and look for one or two groups to trim, such as eating out, subscriptions or travel; even $50 a month shortens the plan.
  • Send windfalls such as a tax refund, bonus or gift straight to the target debt with the one-time extra.
  • Use the yearly extra for an annual bonus or a 13th-month salary.
  • Paying half the monthly payment every two weeks gives 26 half-payments, or 13 full payments a year instead of 12. To model it, add one-twelfth of your monthly payments as the monthly extra.
  • Ask card issuers for a lower rate, or move a high-rate balance to a cheaper loan, then rerun the plan with the new rates.
  • Keep a small emergency fund while you pay down debt, so a surprise bill does not go back on a card.

When a payoff plan is not enough: counseling, settlement and bankruptcy

If the payments do not fit your income even after cuts, there are other routes in the US, each with costs. The Federal Trade Commission notes that a nonprofit label does not guarantee that a credit counseling service is free or affordable, and that a debt management plan can take 48 months or more. A debt settlement company cannot collect its fees before it settles a debt, but settlement can leave you owing more in late fees and interest if it fails, hurts your credit, and forgiven debt may count as taxable income.

Bankruptcy is the last resort: Chapter 7 generally sells non-exempt assets to pay creditors, and Chapter 13 sets a court-approved plan that repays some debts over three to five years while you keep your property. You must get credit counseling from a government-approved organization before filing, and the FTC says bankruptcy stays on a credit report for 10 years.

Common mistakes, and related calculators

  • Entering the minimum payment as a fixed amount when a card's minimum falls as the balance falls. Keep paying the starting amount, as the plan assumes.
  • Adding new purchases to a card you are paying off, which moves the debt-free date back every month.
  • Forgetting fees and penalties when comparing early payoff with saving or investing.
  • Stopping a cleared debt's payment instead of rolling it on; the calculator shows how much longer that takes.

How it works

List each debt with what you still owe, what you pay on it each month and its interest rate, then add any extra you can pay every month, once a year or once. Each month every debt gets its interest and its own payment. The extra money goes to one debt at a time: the one with the highest rate, or the one with the smallest balance if you prefer quick wins. When a debt is cleared, its payment moves on to the next debt (you can turn this off), so your monthly total stays the same and each debt goes faster than the one before.

The formula

  • Each debt, each month: interest = balance × APR ÷ 100 ÷ 12, added to the balance
  • Each debt then gets its own payment, or the whole balance if that is smaller
  • Extra money this month = (all payments + the monthly extra) − the payments made, plus the yearly extra in months 1, 13, 25 … and the one-time extra in its month
  • The extra goes to the target debt up to its balance, then to the next: highest APR first, or smallest starting balance first
  • Without moving payments on, only the extras go to the next debt
  • Interest of a debt = what you paid on it − what you owed at the start

A worked example

With the starting debts, a credit card ($4,500 at 22.99%, $120 a month), a car loan ($15,000 at 7.49%, $380), a student loan ($20,000 at 5.8%, $230) and a personal loan ($3,000 at 12.5%, $120), plus $100 extra a month, you pay $950 every month. The extra goes to the card first, which is clear in month 27; its money then joins the personal loan, the car loan and finally the student loan. You are debt-free in 53 months (4 years 5 months) with $7,552.82 of interest, against $12,291.19 over 9 years 6 months if each debt only got its own payment.

Questions

How do I calculate my debt payoff date?

For one debt, the number of payments is n = −ln(1 − r × B ÷ P) ÷ ln(1 + r), with B the balance, P the payment and r the monthly rate. For several debts with payments rolling from one to the next, it has to be worked out month by month, which is what this calculator does; the result shows the month each debt and all debts are cleared.

Highest rate first or smallest balance first?

Highest rate first (the avalanche) costs the least interest. Smallest balance first (the snowball) clears whole debts sooner, which helps some people stay motivated. The results show the interest of the other order too, so you can see the difference.

What is a debt payoff plan?

It is a schedule that says what to pay on each debt every month until all of them are gone. This calculator writes it for you in the Plan for each debt table, for example $220 a month in months 1-26, then the last amount in month 27.

Is this a debt payoff calculator with extra payments?

Yes. You can add an extra amount every month, once a year and once, in any month you choose. The results show how much interest the extras save and how much sooner you are debt-free.

What does moving a payment to the next debt do?

When a debt is paid off you keep paying the same monthly total and send that freed payment to the next debt. It is the main reason a plan like this clears debts years sooner; if you stop paying a cleared debt, only the extra money moves on.

What is a loan payoff amount?

It is what you must pay on a given date to close the loan completely: the balance plus interest up to that day, unpaid fees and any prepayment penalty. Ask your lender for a payoff quote or letter before you send the final payment.

How do I calculate debt payoff in Excel?

For one debt, =NPER(rate/12, −payment, balance) gives the number of months. For several debts, list each month in rows with interest = balance × rate/12 and the payment applied, then send any leftover to the target debt; the Month by month table here does the same and is ready to compare.

Why does it say a debt can never be paid off?

If a debt's monthly payment is not more than the interest it adds each month, its balance never goes down on its own. Raise that payment above the interest shown in the message.

When are the yearly and one-time extras paid?

The yearly extra is paid in month 1 and then every 12 months. The one-time extra is paid in the month you choose, counted from your first payment.

Can I add a mortgage or a car loan?

Yes, any debt with a balance, a monthly payment and an interest rate works. Up to 20 debts can be listed. A mortgage usually has the lowest rate, so the avalanche method pays it last.
