What is depreciation?

Depreciation is the way accounts spread the cost of a long-lasting asset, such as a machine, a car, a laptop, furniture or a rental building, over the years it is used, instead of treating the whole price as an expense on the day you buy it. Each year's share is the depreciation expense, and what is left on the books is the book value.

This depreciation calculator builds that year-by-year schedule for one asset. You choose straight line, declining balance (including double declining) or sum of the years' digits, and it shows each year's expense, the running total and the book value at the start and end of every year.

Depreciation is an accounting estimate, not a market price. A car's book value after three years can be far from what a buyer would pay. For the drop in a car's resale value, compare the price you paid with current asking prices for the same model and age.

How to use the depreciation calculator

  • Choose one of the three options under Method: Straight line (the same every year), Sum of the years' digits, or Declining balance.
  • Enter the Cost of the asset (default 12,000), including delivery and setup costs that are part of getting it ready to use.
  • Enter the Value left at the end (salvage), what you expect to sell it for when you stop using it (default 2,000). Use 0 if nothing.
  • Enter the Useful life in years (default 5). Only straight line accepts part years such as 27.5.
  • For declining balance, set the Declining factor: 2 for double declining balance, 1.5 for 150%. The yearly rate is the factor divided by the life.
  • Under More options, Round amounts to whole units is on by default; the last year takes the rounding difference.
  • Under Part first year, tick The first year is a part year, enter the Date it went into use, choose how to count the first year and set the month and day your accounting year starts.

Pick your currency at the top. The schedule and a chart of book value and yearly depreciation appear under the result.

Understanding the results

On straight line the headline is Depreciation a year. On declining balance it is the Yearly depreciation rate, factor ÷ life. Every method also shows Depreciation in the first year and the Amount to depreciate (cost − salvage value).

In the schedule, Book value at start is what the asset is carried at when the year begins, the percentage column shows the share taken that year, Depreciation is that year's expense, Total so far is accumulated depreciation and Book value at end is what is left. With a part first year, two more rows show how long the asset was in use in the first accounting year and how that time is counted.

Worked examples: a part year, a rental building and a car in Pakistan

A part first year. The default 12,000 asset goes into use on 15 April 2026 in a January-to-December accounting year. With the half-year rule, 2026 gets 1,000, the years 2027 to 2030 get 2,000 each, and 2031 takes the last 1,000, ending at the 2,000 salvage value.

A US rental building. US tax law depreciates residential rental property over 27.5 years by straight line with the mid-month convention, and land is not depreciated. Enter the building's cost only, say 275,000, salvage 0, life 27.5, and a service date of 10 April 2026 counted as whole months plus half for a part month. The first year is counted as 8.5 months and gets 7,083; each full year after that gets 10,000.

A car in Pakistan, in rupees. Pakistan's tax law uses the written-down value method at a fixed rate, which is declining balance on the book value left. For a Rs 5,000,000 car at the 15% rate for motor vehicles, set life 10 and factor 1.5. Year 1 is Rs 750,000, year 2 Rs 637,500, year 3 Rs 541,875 and year 4 Rs 460,594. The calculator writes off the rest in the final year; the tax rate simply continues on what is left.

Depreciation for tax: US MACRS and Pakistan's tax rates

Tax depreciation often follows its own rules, separate from the accounts. In the US, most business property placed in service after 1986 uses MACRS. It sets fixed recovery periods, treats salvage value as zero and, for 3, 5, 7 and 10-year property, uses 200% declining balance that switches to straight line in the year straight line gives an equal or larger deduction. IRS Publication 946 (for 2025 returns) lists, for example, cars and trucks as 5-year property, office furniture as 7-year property, residential rental property at 27.5 years and nonresidential real property such as offices and shops at 39 years.

This calculator does not switch methods or use the IRS tables, so its declining balance figures will not match MACRS exactly. Use it to understand the method and to plan, and use the IRS tables or tax software for the return.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Do not depreciate land. Split a property's price between land and building and depreciate only the building.
  • Include the costs of getting the asset ready to use, such as delivery and installation, in the cost.
  • Keep salvage value realistic. Setting it too high understates the expense every year.
  • Use the same method every year for the same asset unless the accounting rules let you change it.
  • Do not confuse amortization with depreciation. Amortization spreads the cost of intangible assets such as patents and software licences, while depreciation covers physical assets.

How it works

Depreciation spreads the cost of an asset, less what it will be worth at the end, over the years you use it. Pick a method, enter the cost, the salvage value and the useful life. Straight line takes the same amount every year. Declining balance takes a fixed percentage of whatever book value is left, so it front-loads the cost; the last year brings the book value down to the salvage value. Sum of the years' digits also starts high and falls by the same step each year. If the asset went into use part way through your accounting year, tick the part first year option: the first year gets only its share, every later year is a blend of two asset years, and one extra year at the end takes the rest.

The formula

  • Amount to depreciate B = cost − salvage value
  • Straight line: B ÷ life each year
  • Declining balance: rate = factor ÷ life; each year rate × book value at the start (never below salvage)
  • Sum of the years' digits: S = N(N + 1) ÷ 2; year k gets (N − k + 1) ÷ S × B
  • Part first year with share f: accounting year t = f × asset year t + (1 − f) × asset year t − 1
  • Book value at end of a year = book value at start − that year's depreciation

A worked example

An 11,000 machine worth 1,000 after 5 years has B = 10,000. Straight line gives 2,000 a year. Double declining balance uses 2 ÷ 5 = 40% of the book value: 4,400 in year 1, then 2,640, 1,584 and 950.40, and the last year takes the remaining 425.60 down to the 1,000 salvage value.

Questions

How do I calculate depreciation?

Subtract the salvage value from the cost, then spread that amount over the useful life with your chosen method. By straight line, a 12,000 asset with 2,000 salvage over 5 years depreciates 2,000 a year. The calculator builds the full schedule for any of the three methods.

What is the straight line depreciation formula?

Yearly depreciation = (cost − salvage value) ÷ useful life. It gives the same expense every year, and the book value falls in a straight line to the salvage value at the end of the life.

What is the reducing balance depreciation formula?

Each year's depreciation = rate × book value at the start of the year, where the rate here is factor ÷ life. Because the book value shrinks, the expense falls every year. The calculator stops at the salvage value and puts any remainder in the last year.

What is double declining balance?

Declining balance with a factor of 2: twice the straight-line rate, applied to the book value left each year. For a 5-year life that is 40% a year, so a 12,000 asset gets 4,800 in year 1 and 2,880 in year 2.

What is the depreciation rate in Pakistan?

For tax, the Income Tax Ordinance 2001 sets rates on written-down value: 10% for buildings, 15% for furniture, plant and machinery and motor vehicles, and 30% for computer hardware, per the FBR text amended up to 30 June 2022. Check the current Finance Act before filing, as rates can change.

How do I calculate car depreciation?

For accounts, enter the car's cost, an expected resale value and the years you will keep it, and pick declining balance, which fits cars that lose more value early. For tax, use the rules where you file, for example 15% of written-down value for motor vehicles in Pakistan or 5-year property under US MACRS.

How do I calculate depreciation on a rental property?

Depreciate the building only, not the land. In the US, residential rental property uses straight line over 27.5 years with the mid-month convention, so choose straight line, life 27.5, tick the part first year and count it as whole months plus half for a part month.

What does the half-year rule do?

It treats every asset as going into use in the middle of the year, so the first year gets half a year's depreciation and the half left over is taken in one extra year at the end.

Is this the same as US tax (MACRS) depreciation?

No. MACRS uses fixed IRS tables, treats salvage value as zero and switches from declining balance to straight line. This calculator follows the general accounting methods, which are close but not the same.

What is the difference between depreciation and amortization?

Both spread a cost over time. Depreciation is used for physical assets such as machines, vehicles and buildings, while amortization is used for intangible assets such as patents, and the same word also describes paying down a loan.
