How it works

Pick the tax year and your filing status, then enter your wages and the federal tax already withheld. Open the sections below for a spouse, business profit, investment income, deductions and credits. The calculator follows the Form 1040 steps: it adds up your income, works out how much of any Social Security is taxable, takes off adjustments (half of self-employment tax, IRA, student loan interest) to reach adjusted gross income, then takes the larger of the standard deduction or your itemized deductions, plus the 2025 to 2028 deductions for tips, overtime, car loan interest and people 65 or older, and the qualified business income deduction. Tax on the rest uses the brackets for your status, with the 0%, 15% and 20% rates on qualified dividends and long-term gains. It adds the alternative minimum tax, the 3.8% investment income tax, self-employment tax and the 0.9% Additional Medicare tax, takes off your credits, and compares the total with what you have paid.

The formula

  • Adjusted gross income = total income (with only the taxable part of Social Security) − half of self-employment tax − IRA − student loan interest
  • Taxable income = AGI − larger of (standard deduction + extra at 65+) or itemized deductions − tips, overtime, car loan interest and senior deductions − QBI deduction
  • Self-employment tax = 92.35% of profit × (12.4% up to the Social Security wage base + 2.9%)
  • Income tax = bracket tax on ordinary income + 0% / 15% / 20% on qualified dividends and long-term gains
  • Total tax = income tax + AMT + 3.8% NIIT + self-employment tax + 0.9% Additional Medicare tax − credits
  • Owe (or refund) = total tax − tax withheld − estimated payments

A worked example

A single filer in 2026 earns $80,000 in wages. Taxable income is $80,000 − $16,100 standard deduction = $63,900. Tax: 10% of $12,400 = $1,240, plus 12% of $38,000 = $4,560, plus 22% of $13,500 = $2,970, so $8,770. With $9,000 withheld, the refund is about $230.

Questions

Which tax year should I pick?

Pick the year the income was earned. 2025 is the return you file in spring 2026, 2026 is the one you file in 2027, and 2027 lets you plan ahead with the figures the IRS has already published.

Are Social Security and Medicare taken off before income tax?

No. For employees, the 7.65% payroll tax is not deductible, so it does not lower taxable income. If you are self-employed, half of your self-employment tax is an adjustment, and self-employment tax itself is worked out on 92.35% of your profit.

How do the tips, overtime and senior deductions work?

From 2025 to 2028 you can deduct qualified tips up to $25,000, the extra half of overtime pay up to $12,500 ($25,000 on a joint return), interest on a loan for a new US-assembled car up to $10,000, and $6,000 for each person 65 or older. Each one shrinks at higher incomes, and you get them with the standard deduction too. Married people must file jointly for all but the car loan interest.

What is the credit for other dependents?

It is a $500 credit for each dependent who is too old for the child tax credit, such as a child of 17 or more or a parent you support. Both credits shrink by $50 for every $1,000 of income above $200,000 ($400,000 on a joint return).

Why might my refund differ from this estimate?

It is an estimate of federal tax only. Things it does not cover, such as state income tax, special credits, farm or foreign income, can change the result. Your real return uses whole-dollar lines, so small differences of a dollar or two are normal.
