Year by year

How it works

Enter what is in your Roth IRA today, how much you add each year (or tick the box to always put in the most allowed), your age, retirement age and the return you expect. Each year the deposit is capped at the IRS limit for your age, which rises by the catch-up amount from 50. The Roth balance grows untaxed. To show what the tax shelter is worth, the same deposits also go into an ordinary taxable account that pays your tax rate on each year's growth. Under More options you can pick the tax year and check whether your income lets you contribute directly.

The formula

  • Deposit each year = the smaller of your amount and the IRS limit for your age (limit + catch-up from age 50)
  • Roth: B ← B × (1 + R) + deposit (the deposit goes in at the end of the year)
  • Taxable: T ← T × (1 + R × (1 − tax rate)) + deposit; tax that year = T × R × tax rate
  • Roth growth = Roth balance − money put in; Roth advantage = Roth balance − taxable balance
  • Income check (IRS worksheet): allowed = limit − limit × (MAGI − start of range) ÷ width of range, rounded up to $10, at least $200

A worked example

With $30,000 today and $7,500 a year from age 30 to 65 at 6%, the Roth grows to $1,066,343. You put in $292,500, so $773,843 is tax-free growth. In a taxable account at a 25% tax rate the money grows at 6% × 0.75 = 4.5% a year and ends at $751,245 after $152,915 of tax, so the Roth is $315,098 ahead.

Questions

How much can I put in a Roth IRA?

For 2026 and 2027 the limit is $7,500 a year, or $8,600 if you are 50 or older. It is one limit shared by all your traditional and Roth IRAs, and you cannot put in more than you earned from work that year.

Does my income matter?

Yes. For 2026 the limit shrinks for single filers with a MAGI between $153,000 and $168,000 and for married couples filing jointly between $242,000 and $252,000; above the range you cannot contribute directly. The 2027 ranges are $158,000 to $173,000 and $250,000 to $260,000.

Why compare with a taxable account?

A Roth's benefit is that its growth is never taxed. Putting the same deposits in an ordinary account that pays tax on each year's interest or gains shows how much that is worth to you in dollars.

Can I take my money out early?

You can take out what you put in at any time, tax- and penalty-free. Growth is tax-free once you are 59½ and the account is at least five years old; taking growth out earlier can mean tax plus a 10% penalty.

Will the limit stay the same in future years?

The IRS raises the limit with inflation in $500 steps, so it will probably grow. This projection keeps the chosen year's limit for every future year, which may slightly understate what a maxed-out account can reach.
