What the circle calculator finds

A circle is every point that lies the same distance from one centre point. That one distance, the radius, fixes everything else about it, so if you know any one of the radius, diameter, circumference or area, this circle calculator works out the other three, shows each circle formula with your numbers in it, and draws your circle with its labels.

The four measurements are easy to mix up. The radius (r) runs from the centre to the edge. The diameter (d) goes straight across through the centre and is always twice the radius. The circumference (C) is the length all the way round, which is the perimeter of a circle. The area (A) is the flat space inside, measured in square units such as cm² or square feet.

How to use the circle calculator

  • Pick the tab for what you know: Radius, Diameter, Circumference or Area. Each tab has one box, so there is never a clash between two values you typed.
  • Type the number. The boxes open with radius 5, diameter 10, circumference 30 and area 100 as examples; any positive number works.
  • Choose a Unit (optional): millimeters, centimeters, meters, kilometers, inches, feet, yards or miles. The unit is carried to the answers, with the area in that unit squared. It labels the numbers; it does not convert between units.
  • Read the answers as you type: no button press is needed.

Understanding the results

  • The big numbers are the three values you did not type. Usually the area comes first; when you start from the area, the radius comes first instead.
  • Your radius (or diameter, circumference or area) repeats the value you gave, so a screenshot shows the whole question.
  • π multiples: when you give the radius or the diameter, the circumference and area are also written as a multiple of π, such as 10π and 25π for a radius of 5. Maths teachers often ask for answers "in terms of π", and these are exact. From a circumference or an area the radius itself involves π, so only decimals are shown.
  • Results have 14 significant digits. Round them to suit the question: 78.539816339745 is 78.54 to two decimal places.
  • The steps show each formula rearranged for the value you gave, and the picture labels r, d and C on your circle.

Circle formulas: area, circumference, radius and diameter

Every circle formula comes from two facts: the diameter is twice the radius, and the circumference divided by the diameter is always the same number, π. The area formula πr² follows from those, and the rest are the same formulas turned round.

Worked examples with steps

Area of a circle with a diameter of 12 feet, for example a round rug or room. On the Diameter tab with feet chosen, the steps read: r = 12 ÷ 2 = 6 ft; C = πd = π × 12 = 12π = 37.699111843078 ft; A = πd² ÷ 4 = π × 12² ÷ 4 = 36π = 113.09733552923 ft². So the floor area is about 113.1 square feet, and a border round the edge needs about 37.7 feet.

Radius from the circumference. A round pond measures 30 m along its edge. On the Circumference tab with meters chosen, that gives r = 30 ÷ 2π = 4.7746482927569 m, d = 30 ÷ π = 9.5492965855137 m, and A = 30² ÷ 4π = 71.619724391353 m².

Radius from the area. A circle of area 100 cm² has r = √(100 ÷ π) = 5.6418958354776 cm and C = √(4π × 100) = 35.44907701811 cm.

A Pakistan example. A round lawn in a house in Lahore is 20 feet across. Its area is 100π = 314.15926535898 ft², about 314 square feet of grass to seed or turf, and its edge is 20π, about 62.8 feet of border bricks. For a round concrete slab 3 m across and 10 cm thick, the area is 2.25π = 7.068583470577 m², and multiplying by the 0.1 m thickness gives about 0.707 m³ of concrete; the Concrete Calculator adds the bags and a waste margin.

About π, and why 22/7 gives a slightly different answer

π (pi) is the ratio of any circle's circumference to its diameter. It never ends and never repeats: 3.14159 26535 89793 23846 … This calculator uses the full double-precision value, 3.141592653589793. That is the same 15 decimal places NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory says it uses for its most accurate interplanetary navigation, so it is far more than any homework or building job needs.

School questions often say to use π ≈ 3.14 or π ≈ 22/7 (3.142857…). Both are a little off, so your answer will differ slightly from the calculator's. For a radius of 7, 22/7 gives an area of exactly 154 and a circumference of exactly 44, while the true values are 153.94 and 43.98. Going backwards, an area of 154 gives a true radius of 7.0014. If the question names an approximation, use it and round as asked.

For O Level students in Pakistan and elsewhere: the Cambridge O Level Mathematics syllabus (4024, exams in 2025 to 2027) prints A = πr² and C = 2πr on the formula list, says answers may be asked for in terms of π, and treats arc length and sector area as fractions of the circumference and area. Its Paper 1 allows no calculator, which is where the π-multiple answers here help you check your working.

Other parts of a circle: arc, chord, sector and the circle equation

  • Arc: a piece of the circumference. For a central angle θ in radians, the arc length is s = rθ; in degrees, s = 2πr × θ ÷ 360.
  • Sector: the slice between two radii, like a slice of pizza. Its area is ½r²θ in radians, or πr² × θ ÷ 360 in degrees. A sprinkler that waters 20 feet out through 90° covers a quarter circle, 100π ≈ 314.16 ft².
  • Chord: a straight line joining two points on the circle. The diameter is the longest chord. Splitting the triangle formed by two radii and the chord in half gives the chord length 2r sin(θ ÷ 2).
  • Tangent: a line that touches the circle at one point, at right angles to the radius there.
  • On a graph, a circle with centre (h, k) and radius r has the equation (x − h)² + (y − k)² = r²; with the centre at the origin that is x² + y² = r². Read r from the equation (the square root of the right-hand side) and type it into the Radius tab for the area and circumference.

Tips, common mistakes and related calculators

  • Using the diameter in πr²: halve it first, or use πd² ÷ 4.
  • Mixing up 2πr and πr²: the circumference has r (a length); the area has r² (square units).
  • Comparing round things by diameter: a 16-inch pizza (64π ≈ 201 in²) has four times the area of an 8-inch one (16π ≈ 50.3 in²), and one 12-inch pizza (36π ≈ 113 in²) is more food than two 8-inch pizzas (about 100.5 in²).
  • Finding the radius from a measured circumference is often easier than finding the centre: wrap a tape round a pipe or tree, then use the Circumference tab.

For other shapes, use the Area Calculator. Cylinders, cones and spheres are in the Volume Calculator and the Surface Area Calculator.

How it works

Pick the value you know (radius, diameter, circumference or area) and type it. The other three appear as you type, to 14 significant digits, with the formula for each one rearranged for the value you gave. When you give the radius or the diameter, the circumference and area are also shown as a multiple of π (a radius of 5 gives 10π and 25π). Choose a unit if you like: lengths keep it and the area is given in that unit squared.

The formula

  • Diameter d = 2r
  • Circumference C = 2πr = πd
  • Area A = πr² = πd² ÷ 4 = C² ÷ (4π)
  • From the circumference: r = C ÷ (2π), d = C ÷ π
  • From the area: r = √(A ÷ π), d = √(4A ÷ π), C = √(4πA)

A worked example

A circle with an area of 100 cm² has a radius of √(100 ÷ π) = 5.6418958354776 cm, a diameter of twice that, 11.283791670955 cm, and a circumference of √(4π × 100) = 35.44907701811 cm.

Questions

How do I calculate the area of a circle with the diameter?

Halve the diameter to get the radius, then use A = πr², or use A = πd² ÷ 4 in one go. A circle 12 cm across has r = 6 cm and an area of 36π, about 113.1 cm². Choose the Diameter tab and the calculator shows both steps.

How do I calculate the area of a circle in square feet?

Measure the radius or diameter in feet, choose feet as the unit, and the area is given in ft². A round patio 10 feet across has an area of 25π, about 78.5 square feet. If you measured in inches, divide the area in square inches by 144 to get square feet.

How do I calculate the circumference of a circle?

Multiply the diameter by π, or the radius by 2π. A wheel 70 cm across has a circumference of 70π, about 219.9 cm, which is how far it rolls in one turn.

Is the perimeter of a circle the same as its circumference?

Yes. Perimeter is the general word for the distance round a shape, and circumference is the special name for a circle's perimeter. Both use C = 2πr.

How do I find the radius if I only know the circumference?

Divide the circumference by 2π. For a circumference of 30 m the radius is 30 ÷ 6.2832 = 4.7746 m. Choose the Circumference tab and type it; the steps show this.

How do I find the radius from the area?

Divide the area by π and take the square root: r = √(A ÷ π). An area of 50 m² gives r = √15.915, about 3.99 m. The Area tab does this and then gives the diameter and circumference too.

What is the difference between circumference and area?

The circumference is a length: the distance once around the edge, like a string laid on the line. The area is the flat space inside, in square units. A circle of radius 5 cm has a circumference of about 31.4 cm and an area of about 78.5 cm².

Why is the answer sometimes shown as 25π?

When the radius is known exactly, the area πr² and the circumference 2πr are neat multiples of π, which is how they are written in school work when a question says "leave your answer in terms of π". The decimal value is shown next to it.

Should I use 3.14, 22/7 or the full value of π?

Use whatever the question asks for. For real measurements the full value is best, and this calculator uses π to about 16 significant digits. With 22/7 a radius of 7 gives an area of exactly 154; the true area is 153.94.

How do I find the equation of a circle?

With centre (h, k) and radius r, the equation is (x − h)² + (y − k)² = r². A circle centred at (2, −3) with radius 4 is (x − 2)² + (y + 3)² = 16. Going the other way, the square root of the number on the right is the radius you can type in here.
