How it works

Pick the value you know (radius, diameter, circumference or area) and type it. The other three appear as you type, to 14 significant digits, with the formula for each one rearranged for the value you gave. When you give the radius or the diameter, the circumference and area are also shown as a multiple of π (a radius of 5 gives 10π and 25π). Choose a unit if you like: lengths keep it and the area is given in that unit squared.

The formula

  • Diameter d = 2r
  • Circumference C = 2πr = πd
  • Area A = πr² = πd² ÷ 4 = C² ÷ (4π)
  • From the circumference: r = C ÷ (2π), d = C ÷ π
  • From the area: r = √(A ÷ π), d = √(4A ÷ π), C = √(4πA)

A worked example

A circle with an area of 100 cm² has a radius of √(100 ÷ π) = 5.6418958354776 cm, a diameter of twice that, 11.283791670955 cm, and a circumference of √(4π × 100) = 35.44907701811 cm.

Questions

What is the difference between circumference and area?

The circumference is a length: the distance once around the edge, like a string laid on the line. The area is the flat space inside, in square units. A circle of radius 5 cm has a circumference of about 31.4 cm and an area of about 78.5 cm².

How do I find the radius if I only know the circumference?

Divide the circumference by 2π. For a circumference of 30 m the radius is 30 ÷ 6.2832 = 4.7746 m. Choose the Circumference tab and type it; the steps show this.

Why is the answer sometimes shown as 25π?

When the radius is known exactly, the area πr² and the circumference 2πr are neat multiples of π, which is how they are written in school work. The decimal value is shown next to it.

How many digits of π are used?

The full double-precision value, about 3.141592653589793, so the results are exact to the 14 significant digits shown.
