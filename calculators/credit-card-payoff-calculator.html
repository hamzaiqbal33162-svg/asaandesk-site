How it works

Enter the total you can put towards your cards each month and, for each card, what you owe, its minimum payment and its interest rate. Each month every card first gets its interest added and its minimum paid. Whatever is left of your budget goes to one card at a time: the one with the highest rate (the avalanche method) or the one with the smallest balance (the snowball method). When a card is paid off, the money that went to it moves on to the next card, so your monthly total stays the same until every card is clear.

The formula

  • Each card, each month: interest = balance × APR ÷ 100 ÷ 12, added to the balance
  • Each card then gets its minimum payment (or the whole balance if that is smaller)
  • Money left = your budget − the minimums paid; it goes to the target card, up to its balance, then to the next one
  • Target order: highest APR first (avalanche) or smallest starting balance first (snowball); ties keep your list order
  • Interest of a card = what you paid on it − what you owed at the start

A worked example

Three cards: $4,600 at 18.99% (minimum $100), $3,900 at 19.99% ($90) and $6,000 at 15.99% ($120), with $500 a month. The minimums take $310, so $190 extra goes to the 19.99% card: $280 a month until it is clear in month 16. Then its money moves to the 18.99% card ($380 a month), and finally all $500 goes to the last card. All three are paid off in 38 months, and the interest is about $4,471.

Questions

Which is better, highest rate first or smallest balance first?

Highest rate first always costs the least interest, because the money goes where interest grows fastest. Smallest balance first clears whole cards sooner, which some people find easier to stick with. The results show both, so you can see what the quicker wins cost.

Why must the budget cover all the minimum payments?

Missing a minimum payment usually brings a late fee, a mark on your credit record and often a higher penalty rate. The plan only works if every card gets at least its minimum each month.

What does "What to pay" in the card table mean?

It is the payment plan for that card: how much to pay on it each month and for which months. The amount goes up when a card before it is paid off and its money moves on.

Does it include new spending on the cards?

No. It assumes you stop using the cards while paying them off. New purchases or fees make the plan longer.

Can I add more than three cards?

Yes. Change the number of cards (up to 20) and fill in the new rows. A row with no balance is left out.
