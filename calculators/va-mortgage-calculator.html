What a VA mortgage calculator shows

A VA mortgage calculator works out the monthly payment on a home loan guaranteed by the US Department of Veterans Affairs (VA). VA loans are for eligible service members, veterans and some surviving spouses, and they differ from other mortgages in three ways that change the numbers: you can buy with no down payment, there is no monthly mortgage insurance, and most borrowers pay a one-time VA funding fee that is usually added to the loan.

This calculator applies the VA's own funding fee table to your down payment and loan history, then shows the loan amount with the fee, the monthly principal and interest, the full monthly cost with property tax and insurance, the total interest and a full payment schedule. You can also see what extra payments or paying every two weeks would save.

How to use the VA mortgage calculator

  • Home price: the price you agree to pay. VA loans need no down payment as long as the price is not above the appraised value.
  • Down payment: as a % of the price or a dollar amount. Leave it at 0 for a no-down-payment loan; 5% or 10% lowers the funding fee.
  • Loan term (up to 30 years) and Interest rate (% a year).
  • Is this your first VA loan?: First VA loan, or I have had one before. A later use costs more only when you put less than 5% down.
  • VA funding fee (optional): You are (active duty or a veteran, Reserve or National Guard, a surviving spouse who gets DIC, or one without DIC), the disability exemption box, and whether the fee is added to the loan or paid in cash at closing.
  • Taxes and costs (optional): property tax per year (as % of the price or an amount), home insurance, HOA or building fee and other yearly costs, with yearly rises if you expect them.
  • Extra payments and dates (optional): extra every month, every year or once, a list of one-time payments, the first payment date and the every-two-weeks comparison.

Amounts are in US dollars, because VA loans are a US programme. If the down payment covers the whole price, the calculator stops, since there is no loan to work out.

The VA funding fee in 2026

The funding fee is a one-time charge paid to the VA on most VA-backed loans. For a home purchase or construction loan, the rates in force since 7 April 2023, and still listed by the VA in November 2026, depend on your down payment and whether you have used the benefit before. On a purchase, the funding fee is the only cost you may roll into the loan; every other fee is paid at closing.

Reserve and National Guard members pay the same rates as other veterans.

Worked VA loan examples

You buy a $365,000 home with nothing down on your first VA loan, at 6.25% for 30 years, with property tax of 1.1% of the price and $1,800 a year of home insurance. The funding fee is 2.15% × $365,000 = $7,847.50, which is added to give a loan of $372,847.50. The payment is $2,295.69 a month, and with $334.58 of tax and $150 of insurance it is $2,780.27. Over 30 years you would pay $453,599.53 of interest.

In the first month, $1,941.91 of the payment is interest and only $353.77 lowers the balance; by year 30 almost all of each payment repays the loan. The table shows how the down payment, a second use of the benefit and the exemption change the same purchase.

Property tax varies a lot by state and county, so type your own rate. On the same no-down-payment loan, a 0.8% tax rate gives a monthly cost of $2,689.02 with insurance, and 1.8% gives $2,993.19, a difference of more than $300 a month for the same house and loan. Ask the county assessor or the listing for the current tax bill, and get a quote for home insurance before you set your budget.

VA loan, FHA loan or conventional loan

The VA says its purchase loans need no down payment as long as the price is not above the appraised value, need no private mortgage insurance (PMI) or mortgage insurance premiums (MIP), and come with fewer closing costs, some of which the seller may pay. An FHA loan, by contrast, needs a down payment and charges both an upfront and a yearly mortgage insurance premium, and a conventional loan with less than 20% down usually needs PMI.

Running the same $365,000 home at 6.25% for 30 years through our FHA Loan Calculator with the minimum 3.5% down ($12,775) gives $2,851.83 a month with mortgage insurance, tax and insurance, against $2,780.27 for the VA loan with nothing down. The FHA buyer also pays $37,709.04 of yearly premium over the loan.

A conventional loan with 20% down avoids mortgage insurance too, but it needs much more cash. The same home with $73,000 down through the Mortgage Calculator costs $1,797.89 a month for principal and interest, or $2,282.48 with tax and insurance, on a $292,000 loan. That is about $498 a month less than the VA loan with nothing down, in return for $73,000 at closing. For many buyers the question is how long it would take to save that much, and what the home would cost by then.

The trade-offs of a VA loan are the funding fee, the need for a Certificate of Eligibility, and the rule that you must live in the home you buy. Compare total cost, not just the rate, and see the Mortgage Calculator for a conventional loan.

Who can get a VA loan, and how entitlement works

The VA's minimum service rules depend on when and how you served. Current service members qualify after 90 continuous days. Veterans of the Gulf War era (2 August 1990 to the present) generally need 24 continuous months, or the full period of at least 90 days they were called to active duty. National Guard and Reserve members qualify with 90 days of non-training active-duty service or 6 creditable years. Surviving spouses may qualify if they receive certain types of DIC, or if the service member is missing in action or a prisoner of war. You prove eligibility with a Certificate of Eligibility (COE), which you or your lender can request online.

Entitlement is the amount the VA promises to pay your lender if you default. With full entitlement there is no VA loan limit, although you still have to afford the loan and the appraisal has to support the price. For larger loans, the VA guarantees up to 25% of the loan amount, and basic entitlement is $36,000 for loans of $144,000 or less.

If part of your entitlement is still tied up in another VA loan, county loan limits apply. The VA's method is: county one-unit limit × 25%, minus the entitlement already used, equals your remaining entitlement, and most lenders will lend up to four times that with no down payment. In a county with an $850,000 limit and $40,000 of entitlement in use, that is $212,500 − $40,000 = $172,500, so up to $690,000 with nothing down.

You can use the benefit more than once. On a later use the fee with less than 5% down rises to 3.3%, but with 5% or 10% down it is the same 1.5% or 1.25% as on a first loan. That makes a down payment worth more the second time: on the $365,000 home, a later-use loan with nothing down carries a fee of $12,045.00, while 5% down cuts it to $5,201.25, a saving of $6,843.75 on the fee alone, on top of the smaller loan.

How much VA loan can I afford? DTI and residual income

VA underwriting uses two tests. The first is the debt-to-income ratio: the monthly housing payment (principal, interest, tax and insurance), any homeowners or association fees and your long-term debts, divided by your gross income. The VA's standard in 38 CFR 36.4340 is 41% or less; a higher ratio needs extra justification.

The second is residual income: what is left each month after the housing payment, debts and other obligations, to cover living costs. The guideline depends on your region, your family size and the loan amount. If your ratio is above 41% but your residual income is at least 20% above the guideline, the extra review is not needed.

Now suppose you earn $7,500 a month before tax and also pay $450 a month on a car loan. Housing plus debts is $3,230.27, so the ratio is (2,780.27 + 450) ÷ 7,500 = 43.07%, a little above the 41% standard. The lender would then look closely at your residual income; if it is at least 20% above the guideline for your region and family, the loan can still be approved without the extra review. Paying off the car loan, putting 5% down or choosing a cheaper home would bring the ratio under 41%.

Closing costs, paying off early and refinancing a VA loan

Closing costs on a VA loan can include the lender's origination fee, discount points, the credit report, the VA appraisal, title insurance, recording fees, hazard insurance and property taxes. Buyer and seller can negotiate who pays them; the VA does not limit credits toward closing costs overall, but it limits seller's concessions to no more than 4% of the home's reasonable value. Only the funding fee can be added to a purchase loan, so plan cash for the rest.

Extra payments go straight to the balance. On the $372,847.50 loan above, $200 extra every month ends the loan after 290 payments instead of 360, in March 2051 instead of December 2056, and saves $103,144.43 of interest for $57,800.00 of extra payments. Paying half the payment every two weeks, which adds up to one extra monthly payment a year, would clear it in about 24.3 years and save $100,772.70 of interest.

Most of each early payment is interest, so the balance falls slowly at first. On the example loan, only $4,369.01 of the $27,548 paid in year 1 reduces the balance; by year 20 the principal part is $14,281.36 and in year 30 it is $26,637.84. Later, if rates fall, the Interest Rate Reduction Refinance Loan (IRRRL) lets you refinance an existing VA-backed loan into a new one, usually at a lower rate or from an adjustable to a fixed rate. You must already have a VA loan on the home and certify that you live in it or used to. The funding fee is 0.5%, so on a $340,000 balance it would be $1,700, and the VA allows the closing costs to be included in the new loan. A cash-out refinance, which replaces your loan with a bigger one and pays you the difference, carries the purchase rates of 2.15% on first use and 3.3% after. Someone who takes over (assumes) your VA loan pays a 0.5% fee. Use the Refinance Calculator to compare the old and new loans.

VA mortgage tips and related tools

  • Get your COE early, so the lender can confirm your entitlement before you make an offer.
  • Compare the funding fee with your cash: putting 5% down saves 0.65% of the loan in fees on a first use, and 1.8% on a later one.
  • Check whether you are exempt. A disability rating, even one granted shortly before closing, can remove the fee entirely.
  • Ask about rates from several lenders; the VA guarantees the loan but does not set the rate.
  • Veterans with severe service-connected disabilities who received a Specially Adapted Housing grant can get Veterans' Mortgage Life Insurance (VMLI) of up to $200,000, paid to the lender, if they apply before age 70.
  • Related tools: the Mortgage Calculator, the FHA Loan Calculator, the Refinance Calculator for an IRRRL or cash-out refinance, and the Mortgage Amortization Calculator.

How it works

Type the home price, your down payment (it can be zero), the loan term and the interest rate, and say whether this is your first VA loan. The calculator looks up the VA funding fee for a home purchase: the rate depends on how much you put down and on whether you have used a VA loan before. It is a share of the loan, and most people add it to the loan, so the amount you borrow is the price minus the down payment plus the fee. Veterans who get VA disability compensation and surviving spouses who get DIC pay no fee. The monthly payment is the level payment that clears the loan over the term. VA loans have no monthly mortgage insurance. Property tax, insurance and other costs, extra payments and the every-two-weeks comparison work as in our mortgage calculator.

The formula

  • Base loan B = home price − down payment
  • Funding fee rate (purchase, from 7 April 2023): under 5% down 2.15% first use, 3.3% later use; 5% to under 10% down 1.5%; 10% or more 1.25%
  • Fee F = rate × B (0 if exempt)
  • Loan L = B + F when the fee is added to the loan, or B when it is paid at closing
  • Monthly payment M = L × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n), with r = yearly rate ÷ 12 and n = years × 12

A worked example

A $365,000 home with nothing down, first VA loan: the fee is 2.15% of $365,000 = $7,847.50, so the loan is $372,847.50. At 6.25% for 30 years the payment is $2,295.69 a month, or $2,780.27 with 1.1% property tax and $1,800 a year of home insurance. Putting 5% down drops the fee to 1.5% and the payment to $2,167.02.

Questions

How do I calculate a VA mortgage payment?

Take the price minus the down payment, add the funding fee if you finance it, then use M = L × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n) with r the yearly rate ÷ 12 and n the months. A $372,847.50 loan at 6.25% for 30 years is $2,295.69 a month before tax and insurance.

What is the VA funding fee?

A one-time charge that keeps the VA home loan program running. For a purchase it is 2.15% of the loan with less than 5% down on a first VA loan, 3.3% on a later one, 1.5% with 5% to under 10% down and 1.25% with 10% or more. Most borrowers add it to the loan instead of paying cash.

Who does not pay the funding fee?

Veterans who get VA compensation for a service-connected disability, surviving spouses who get Dependency and Indemnity Compensation, active-duty service members with a Purple Heart, and a few others. Tick the exemption box or pick the surviving spouse with DIC.

Does a VA disability rating change my VA mortgage?

It removes the funding fee if you receive, or are eligible for, VA compensation for a service-connected disability. On a $365,000 no-down-payment loan that saves $7,847.50 and lowers the payment from $2,295.69 to $2,247.37 at 6.25%.

Do VA loans have mortgage insurance?

No. The VA says its purchase loans need no private mortgage insurance or mortgage insurance premium, which is why the payment is often lower than an FHA loan for the same price.

Can I include taxes, insurance and closing costs?

Taxes, home insurance, HOA and other yearly costs are in the Taxes and costs section and appear in Monthly with costs. Closing costs other than the funding fee cannot be added to a VA purchase loan, so they are paid in cash at closing.

Is there a limit on how much I can borrow?

With full entitlement the VA sets no loan limit, though the lender still checks your income, debts and the appraisal. With part of your entitlement in use, county loan limits apply to the no-down-payment amount.

How is VA home loan entitlement calculated?

With entitlement in use, multiply the county one-unit loan limit by 25% and subtract the entitlement already used; lenders usually lend up to four times what is left without a down payment. With full entitlement there is no limit.

What debt-to-income ratio do I need for a VA loan?

The VA standard is 41% or less of gross income for the housing payment plus long-term debts. A higher ratio can still be approved, especially if your residual income is well above the VA guideline for your region and family size.

What is VA mortgage life insurance?

Veterans' Mortgage Life Insurance covers up to $200,000 of the mortgage for veterans and service members with severe service-connected disabilities who received a Specially Adapted Housing grant. It is paid to the lender, and you must apply before age 70.

What is a VA mortgage?

A home loan from a private lender, such as a bank or mortgage company, that the US Department of Veterans Affairs guarantees in part. The guarantee lets eligible service members, veterans and some surviving spouses buy with no down payment and no mortgage insurance, usually in return for a one-time funding fee.

What is the VA mortgage rate today?

The VA guarantees the loan but does not set the rate; each lender sets its own, and rates change daily with the market and your credit. Get quotes from several lenders on the same day and enter each rate here to compare the payment and total interest; on our example, every half point changes the payment by about $120 a month.
