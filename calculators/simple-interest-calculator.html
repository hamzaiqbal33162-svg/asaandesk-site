How it works

Pick what you want to find with the tabs: the end balance, the starting amount, the interest rate or the time. Fill in the other three; the rate can be per year or per month and the time in years or months, and the calculator converts them to match. Simple interest is paid only on the starting amount, so the same interest is added every period and the balance grows in a straight line. The worked steps show the formula with your numbers.

The formula

  • Interest I = P × r × t (P = starting amount, r = rate per period, t = time in the same periods)
  • End balance A = P + I = P × (1 + r × t)
  • Starting amount P = A ÷ (1 + r × t)
  • Rate r = (A ÷ P − 1) ÷ t; time t = (A ÷ P − 1) ÷ r
  • A rate per year with a time in months: t = months ÷ 12

A worked example

$20,000 at 3% a year for 10 years earns I = 20,000 × 0.03 × 10 = $6,000, so the end balance is $26,000. Going the other way, $40,000 that must reach $50,000 at 4% a year needs t = (50,000 ÷ 40,000 − 1) ÷ 0.04 = 6.25 years.

Questions

What is the difference between simple and compound interest?

Simple interest is always worked out on the starting amount, so you earn the same interest every year. Compound interest is worked out on the growing balance, so the interest itself earns interest. Over a long time compound interest gives much more; use the Interest Calculator for it.

Where is simple interest used?

In short loans between people, some car and personal loans that quote a flat rate, bonds that pay out their coupons instead of reinvesting them, and many school maths problems. Most bank savings accounts and mortgages use compound interest.

Can I enter the rate per month?

Yes. Switch the rate to % a month. With a time in years, the calculator turns the years into months first (2 years = 24 months) so both use the same period. The rate tab always gives the answer per year, with the monthly rate under it.

Why is the last row of the table shorter?

If the time is not a whole number of periods, for example 6.25 years, the last row covers only the part that is left (a quarter of a year) and earns that share of a year's interest.
