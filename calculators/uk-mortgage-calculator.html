How it works

Enter the property price, your deposit as a percentage or in pounds, the mortgage term and the yearly interest rate. The mortgage is the price minus the deposit. A repayment mortgage pays off capital and interest together with one fixed monthly amount, worked out month by month at a twelfth of the yearly rate. The interest-only payment covers just the interest, so the whole loan is still owed at the end. Yearly costs are added as one twelfth a month, and the stamp duty estimate applies the England and Northern Ireland bands to the price.

The formula

  • Mortgage L = price − deposit; loan to value = L ÷ price
  • Monthly rate r = yearly rate ÷ 100 ÷ 12; number of repayments n = years × 12
  • Repayment M = L × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n); at 0% interest, M = L ÷ n
  • Interest-only payment = L × r
  • Stamp duty = the sum over the bands of (part of the price in the band × band rate)

A worked example

A £500,000 home with a 25% deposit needs a £375,000 mortgage (75% loan to value). Over 25 years at 5%, r = 0.05 ÷ 12 and n = 300, so the repayment is £2,192.21 a month and the 300 repayments add up to £657,663.80, of which £282,663.80 is interest. Interest-only would be £375,000 × 0.05 ÷ 12 = £1,562.50 a month.

Questions

What is the difference between repayment and interest-only?

A repayment mortgage clears the loan by the end of the term because every payment also pays off some capital. Interest-only is cheaper each month but leaves the whole loan to repay at the end, so lenders ask how you will do that.

What is loan to value?

Loan to value (LTV) is the mortgage as a share of the property price. A £30,000 deposit on a £300,000 home gives a 90% LTV. Lower LTVs usually get better rates, often at 60%, 75%, 85% and 90%.

How much stamp duty will I pay?

In England and Northern Ireland there is no stamp duty on the first £125,000, then 2% up to £250,000 and 5% up to £925,000, with higher bands above. First-time buyers pay nothing up to £300,000 on homes up to £500,000. Second homes pay 5% more on every band. Scotland and Wales have their own taxes.

Why does my lender's figure differ slightly?

Lenders may add product fees to the loan, charge interest daily, or move to a standard variable rate when a fixed deal ends. This calculator keeps one rate for the whole term, which is the usual way to compare deals.

Should I include council tax?

It is not part of the mortgage, but it is a fixed monthly bill for every home, so adding it gives a truer monthly cost. Find your band and yearly amount on your local council's website.
