What is a percentage?

A percentage is a way of writing a number as a part of 100. The word comes from the Latin per centum, "by the hundred", so 40% means 40 out of every 100, the fraction 40/100, or the decimal 0.4. Writing amounts this way makes them easy to compare: 18 out of 25 and 36 out of 50 look different, but a percentage calculator shows that both are 72%.

This percentage calculator answers the questions people ask most: what is P% of a number, what percent one number is of another, which number a part is a percent of, the percentage difference between two numbers, and the percent increase or decrease from an old value to a new one. It shows the working under every answer, so you can copy the method into an exam paper, a spreadsheet or a shop bill.

Every one of these sums comes from one equation, P% × X = Y, where X is the whole, Y is the part and P is the percent. Know any two and you can find the third.

How to use this percentage calculator

  • P% of X = Y: under "What do you want to find?" pick Result Y, Percent P or Number X, then fill in the two boxes that stay open. Finding Result Y also shows X minus P% and X plus P%, which is the price after a discount or after tax in one go.
  • Questions: choose "What is P% of X?", "Y is what percent of X?" or "Y is P% of what number?" and type the numbers into Y (the part), P (the percent) and X (the whole). The answer is written back as a full sentence.
  • Difference: type Value 1 and Value 2. You get the percentage difference measured against their average, plus the percent increase or decrease from Value 1 to Value 2 and back again.
  • Increase or decrease: pick End value, Percent change or Start value. Then type a Start value, choose Increase or Decrease, and type the percent in By; or type a Start value and an End value to get the percent change; or type the percent and the End value to work back to the start.
  • More options: "Decimal places in the answer" rounds to 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8 or 10 places (6 by default), or All for 14 significant digits.

Answers change as you type, and the steps list each multiplication and division with the real numbers. If a sum has no answer, such as asking what percent a number is of 0, the calculator says why instead of showing an error number.

How to calculate percentage of marks

To find your percentage of marks, add up the marks you obtained, divide by the total marks, and multiply by 100. On the Questions tab, pick "Y is what percent of X?", type your obtained marks as Y and the total as X.

Here is how to calculate the percentage of marks of 6 subjects, each marked out of 100. The obtained marks add up to 498 out of 600, and 498 ÷ 600 × 100 = 83%.

Example: percentage of marks in 6 subjects

When subjects have different totals, add the marks first and divide once. Averaging the subject percentages gives the wrong answer: 45 out of 50 (90%) and 60 out of 100 (60%) average to 75%, but the true result is 105 out of 150, which is 70%.

The same method works for an entry test such as MDCAT, a board result card or a university paper: divide your score by that year's total marks. To find the marks you need for a target, use "What is P% of X?": with a total of 1,200, a target of 80% needs 0.8 × 1,200 = 960 marks. If an admission aggregate combines several results with weights, turn each result into a percentage first, then apply the weights published by the admitting body for that year.

Attendance percentage works the same way: classes attended ÷ classes held × 100. Attending 62 of 80 lectures is 62 ÷ 80 × 100 = 77.5%.

Percentages of money: discounts, tax and salary in PKR

Money questions are percentage questions with a currency attached. A 30% discount on a PKR 4,999 jacket saves 4,999 × 0.30 = PKR 1,499.70, so you pay PKR 3,499.30. On the first tab, keep Result Y selected, type 30 and 4,999, and read "4,999 minus 30%" for the sale price.

Pakistan's standard federal sales tax on goods is 18% under section 3 of the Sales Tax Act, 1990 (FBR's consolidated text updated to 30 June 2026); some goods and services are taxed at other rates, and rates can change in each budget. Adding 18% to a PKR 2,500 price gives 2,500 × 1.18 = PKR 2,950. To find the price before tax from a receipt total, use Increase or decrease, choose Start value and Increase, and type 18 and 2,950: the answer is PKR 2,500.

For a pay rise, a 10% increase on a PKR 85,000 monthly salary is 85,000 × 1.10 = PKR 93,500. If your salary instead moves from PKR 85,000 to PKR 95,000, choose Percent change: the rise is 10,000 ÷ 85,000 × 100 = 11.76%.

Percent increase, decrease and percentage difference

Percent change always has a starting point, and the change is measured against it. Going from 80 to 100 is a 25% increase because 20 is a quarter of 80. Going back from 100 to 80 is only a 20% decrease, because the same 20 is now measured against 100.

Percentage difference has no starting point. It compares the gap with the average of the two values, so the answer is the same in both directions: 80 and 100 differ by 20 ÷ 90 = 22.22%. Use it when you compare two prices, two shops or two measurements, and use percent change when something moved from an old value to a new one.

Because a fall is measured against a bigger number than the rise that follows it, equal percentages do not cancel out. A 10% rise then a 10% fall leaves 99 out of 100. To win back a fall of F%, you need a rise of F ÷ (100 − F) × 100 percent.

Percent or percentage points?

When the thing that changes is itself a percentage, say whether you mean percent or percentage points. If a loan's interest rate drops from 12% to 11%, it fell by 1 percentage point, which is a 1 ÷ 12 = 8.33% decrease in the rate.

The same care helps with exam results and statistics. A pass rate that climbs from 40% to 44% has risen by 4 percentage points, or by 10% of its old value. Both statements are true; they simply answer different questions.

Percentage formulas in Excel and Google Sheets

You do not need a special function in a spreadsheet; you write the same formulas with cell references. With obtained marks in B2 and total marks in C2, =B2/C2 gives a decimal such as 0.83, and the Percent Style button on Excel's Home tab shows it as 83%. The same arithmetic works in Google Sheets.

  • Percent of a number: =A2*15% or =A2*0.15
  • What percent Y is of X: =B2/C2, then format the cell as a percentage
  • Percent change from old (A2) to new (B2): =(B2-A2)/A2
  • Increase by a percent in B2: =A2*(1+B2); decrease: =A2*(1-B2)

A common spreadsheet slip is typing 15 instead of 15% or 0.15 in a cell that is then multiplied, which makes the answer 100 times too big. When a result looks wrong, compare it with this calculator.

Common percentage mistakes

  • Using the wrong base. "What percent of 80 is 20?" divides by 80; "20 is what percent less than 80?" also uses 80, but "80 is what percent more than 20?" divides by 20 and gives 300%.
  • Adding percentages of different wholes. A 10% discount followed by a further 10% is 19% off in total, not 20%, because the second cut applies to the already reduced price.
  • Taking a discount off the final price to find the original. A PKR 850 sale price after 15% off came from 850 ÷ 0.85 = PKR 1,000, not 850 × 1.15 = PKR 977.50.
  • Rounding too early. Keep the full decimal through the sum and round only the final answer; the decimal places option does this for you.
  • Mixing up percentage and percentile. Scoring 90% means 90 out of 100 marks; the 90th percentile means you scored as well as or better than 90% of the people who took the test.

How it works

A percent is a number out of 100, so 15% means 15 hundredths, or 0.15. Every percentage sum is the same small equation, P% of X = Y: pick which of the three you are missing and type the other two. The Questions tab asks the same sums the way people say them, such as "45 is what percent of 360?". Difference compares two values against their average, which is the fair choice when neither one is the starting point. Increase or decrease starts from a value and moves up or down by a percent; it can also work back to the percent or to the start value. Answers update as you type, and the steps show each sum in full.

The formula

  • Y = P ÷ 100 × X (15% of 200 = 0.15 × 200 = 30)
  • P = Y ÷ X × 100 (30 is 15% of 200)
  • X = Y ÷ (P ÷ 100) (30 is 15% of 200)
  • Percentage difference = |a − b| ÷ ((a + b) ÷ 2) × 100
  • Percent change from a to b = (b − a) ÷ |a| × 100
  • Increase: end = start × (1 + P ÷ 100); decrease: end = start × (1 − P ÷ 100)

A worked example

A shop takes 15% off a 200 price. 15% of 200 = 0.15 × 200 = 30, so you save 30 and pay 200 − 30 = 170. Going the other way, a price that rises from 500 to 540 has gone up by 540 ÷ 500 − 1 = 0.08, which is an 8% increase.

Questions

How do I work out a percentage of a number?

Divide the percent by 100 and multiply by the number. 12.5% of 360 is 0.125 × 360 = 45. On the first tab, keep "Result Y" selected and type the percent and the number.

What is the percentage formula?

Percentage = part ÷ whole × 100. Rearranged, the part is whole × percentage ÷ 100 and the whole is part ÷ (percentage ÷ 100). The table in this guide lists the formula for every common question with an example.

How do I calculate the percentage of marks?

Add the marks you obtained in every subject, divide by the total marks, and multiply by 100. For 6 subjects out of 100 each, 498 obtained marks is 498 ÷ 600 × 100 = 83%. Add the marks first rather than averaging subject percentages when the totals differ.

How do I calculate the percentage between two numbers?

It depends on the question. To find what percent one number is of another, divide and multiply by 100 (30 is 25% of 120). For the percent change from one to the other, use the Increase or decrease tab; to compare two numbers with no starting point, use Difference.

How do I calculate percentage increase?

Subtract the old value from the new one, divide by the old value, and multiply by 100. A rent that goes from PKR 40,000 to PKR 46,000 rises by 6,000 ÷ 40,000 × 100 = 15%. Choose Percent change on the Increase or decrease tab to get it with steps.

What is the difference between percentage difference and percentage change?

Percentage change has a starting point: going from 80 to 100 is a 25% increase, measured against 80. Percentage difference has none, so it measures the gap against the average of the two values: 80 and 100 differ by 20 ÷ 90 = 22.2%.

Why is a 10% rise followed by a 10% fall not back to the start?

The fall is taken from the bigger, raised value. 100 plus 10% is 110, and 110 minus 10% is 99. To undo a 10% rise you need a fall of about 9.09%, which the Increase or decrease tab shows if you find the percent from 110 to 100.

How do I find the original price before a discount or a tax was added?

Use Increase or decrease and choose "Start value". A price of 230 after a 15% increase started at 230 ÷ 1.15 = 200; a price of 170 after a 15% discount started at 170 ÷ 0.85 = 200.

How do I calculate attendance percentage?

Divide the classes you attended by the classes held and multiply by 100. Attending 62 of 80 lectures gives 77.5%. To see how many classes a minimum needs, use "What is P% of X?": 75% of 80 classes is 60.

Can I convert CGPA to percentage with this calculator?

Not directly. A CGPA is a grade-point average, not a share of marks, so turning it into a percentage needs the conversion your university uses, which is often printed on the transcript or given by the registrar. Once you have that rule, this calculator can do the arithmetic, and the GPA calculator works out the CGPA itself.

How do I calculate percentage in Excel?

Divide the part by the whole, such as =B2/C2, then apply the Percent Style format so 0.83 shows as 83%. For a percent change use =(new-old)/old. The formulas are the same ones this calculator shows in its steps.

How many decimal places does it show?

Six by default. Under More options you can pick from 0 to 10 decimal places, or All to see 14 significant digits, as in 33.333333333333% for 27 out of 81.
