How it works

A percent is a number out of 100, so 15% means 15 hundredths, or 0.15. Every percentage sum is the same small equation, P% of X = Y: pick which of the three you are missing and type the other two. The Questions tab asks the same sums the way people say them, such as "45 is what percent of 360?". Difference compares two values against their average, which is the fair choice when neither one is the starting point. Increase or decrease starts from a value and moves up or down by a percent; it can also work back to the percent or to the start value. Answers update as you type, and the steps show each sum in full.

The formula

  • Y = P ÷ 100 × X (15% of 200 = 0.15 × 200 = 30)
  • P = Y ÷ X × 100 (30 is 15% of 200)
  • X = Y ÷ (P ÷ 100) (30 is 15% of 200)
  • Percentage difference = |a − b| ÷ ((a + b) ÷ 2) × 100
  • Percent change from a to b = (b − a) ÷ |a| × 100
  • Increase: end = start × (1 + P ÷ 100); decrease: end = start × (1 − P ÷ 100)

A worked example

A shop takes 15% off a 200 price. 15% of 200 = 0.15 × 200 = 30, so you save 30 and pay 200 − 30 = 170. Going the other way, a price that rises from 500 to 540 has gone up by 540 ÷ 500 − 1 = 0.08, which is an 8% increase.

Questions

How do I work out a percentage of a number?

Divide the percent by 100 and multiply by the number. 12.5% of 360 is 0.125 × 360 = 45. On the first tab, keep "Result Y" selected and type the percent and the number.

What is the difference between percentage difference and percentage change?

Percentage change has a starting point: going from 80 to 100 is a 25% increase, measured against 80. Percentage difference has none, so it measures the gap against the average of the two values: 80 and 100 differ by 20 ÷ 90 = 22.2%.

Why is a 10% rise followed by a 10% fall not back to the start?

The fall is taken from the bigger, raised value. 100 plus 10% is 110, and 110 minus 10% is 99. To undo a 10% rise you need a fall of about 9.09%, which the Increase or decrease tab shows if you find the percent from 110 to 100.

How do I find the original price before a discount or a tax was added?

Use Increase or decrease and choose "Start value". A price of 230 after a 15% increase started at 230 ÷ 1.15 = 200; a price of 170 after a 15% discount started at 170 ÷ 0.85 = 200.

How many decimal places does it show?

Six by default. Under More options you can pick from 0 to 10 decimal places, or All to see 14 significant digits, as in 33.333333333333% for 27 out of 81.
