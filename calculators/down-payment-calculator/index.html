How it works

Pick the question you want answered. With your cash and the down payment percentage you want, the calculator finds the price where the down payment plus the closing costs use up exactly your cash. With a price and a percentage it adds the down payment and the closing costs to give the cash you need, and shows the same price with 3.5% to 20% down. With a price and your cash it takes the closing costs out of the cash and turns what is left into a percentage of the price. Each answer also shows the loan (price minus down payment) and its monthly payment.

The formula

  • Price from cash, closing costs as a %: price = cash ÷ (down % + closing %)
  • Price from cash, closing costs as an amount: price = (cash − closing costs) ÷ down %
  • Cash needed = price × down % + closing costs
  • Down payment % = (cash − closing costs) ÷ price
  • Loan = price − down payment; monthly payment = loan × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n), r = rate ÷ 1200, n = years × 12

A worked example

You have $60,000, want to put 10% down and expect $5,000 of closing costs. That leaves $55,000 for the down payment, so the price is $55,000 ÷ 0.10 = $550,000. The loan is $495,000, which at 6.5% over 30 years is about $3,129 a month.

Questions

Do I need 20% down to buy a home?

No. Many conventional loans accept 3% to 5% down and FHA loans 3.5%, while VA and USDA loans can need nothing down. Under 20% you usually pay mortgage insurance, and a smaller down payment means a bigger loan and more interest.

What are closing costs?

Fees paid when you buy: the lender's charges, appraisal, title insurance, recording fees, transfer taxes and prepaid items such as insurance. They are often 2% to 5% of the price and are paid in cash on top of the down payment, unless the seller or lender covers some.

Why does a higher down payment lower my monthly payment?

Everything you put down is money you do not borrow. A smaller loan has a smaller monthly payment, less interest over the years and, from 20% down, usually no PMI.

Should I put all my savings into the down payment?

Usually not. Keep an emergency fund for repairs, moving costs and a few months of bills. A slightly smaller down payment with savings in reserve is often safer than an empty account on moving day.
