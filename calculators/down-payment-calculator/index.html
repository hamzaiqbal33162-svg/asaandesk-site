What is a down payment?

Your down payment is the share of the purchase price you pay from your own money at the start; the rest is borrowed. On a 400,000 home with 20% down, you pay 80,000 and take a 320,000 mortgage. The same idea applies to cars, motorcycles and phones bought on installments.

This down payment calculator answers the three questions buyers ask most: what home price my savings can buy, how much cash a given price needs, and what percentage down my cash works out to. Each answer includes closing costs, the loan amount and the monthly payment, so you see the whole picture before you talk to a lender.

The size of the down payment drives almost everything else: the loan amount, the monthly payment, the interest over the years, whether you pay mortgage insurance and which loan programs you qualify for.

How to use the down payment calculator

  • Pick a tab: What price can I afford?, How much cash do I need? or What % down is my cash?
  • Choose your currency. Dollars are the default; rupees, pounds, Canadian dollars and others are in the list.
  • Enter Cash you have for the purchase (default 100,000), the money set aside for the down payment and closing costs, or the Home price (default 400,000), depending on the tab.
  • Enter the Down payment as a % of the price (default 20%) on the first two tabs.
  • Leave Include closing costs ticked and set Closing costs as a % of the price (default 3%) or as an amount, or untick it to see the down payment alone.
  • Set the Interest rate (default 6.5% a year) and the Loan term (default 30 years) for the monthly payment.

The monthly payment is principal and interest only. Property tax, home insurance, HOA fees and mortgage insurance come on top; use the mortgage calculator to add them.

Minimum down payment by loan type in the US

You do not need 20% down to buy a home in the US. The CFPB says that in most cases you need at least 3% of the price, while many loan types and lenders ask for 5% or more, and special programs exist for veterans and service members, rural residents and some first-time buyers.

For conventional loans, the CFPB explains that you can ask to cancel PMI once the balance is scheduled to reach 80% of the home's original value, and that the servicer must end it automatically at 78%, or at the midpoint of the loan term, if you are current on payments.

Down payment rules in Canada and Pakistan

Canada sets a legal minimum by price. According to the Financial Consumer Agency of Canada (page dated 15 October 2025), you need 5% of a price up to C$500,000; 5% of the first C$500,000 plus 10% of the part above it for prices up to C$1.5 million; and 20% for C$1.5 million or more. Under 20% down you also need mortgage loan insurance, which protects the lender.

In Pakistan, the State Bank of Pakistan's Prudential Regulations for Housing Finance (updated 18 August 2026) cap the loan-to-value ratio at 90:10, so a home loan needs at least 10% down. The tenor can be up to 30 years, and all monthly installments together may not exceed 65% of the borrower's net disposable income. Banks can ask for more than these minimums.

For cars in Pakistan, the State Bank raised the minimum down payment for auto finance to 30% in September 2021, capped total auto finance per person at Rs 3,000,000 across all banks, and kept locally made cars up to 1,000cc and locally made electric vehicles under the older rules. On a Rs 4,500,000 car, 30% is Rs 1,350,000, leaving a Rs 3,150,000 loan; at an example 18% over 3 years that is Rs 113,880 a month.

A large or a small down payment?

A larger down payment means a smaller loan, a lower monthly payment, less interest over the life of the loan and, at 20% on a conventional loan, no PMI. Lenders also see more equity as lower risk, which can help you qualify. In the table above, 20% down saves about 417 a month compared with 3.5%.

A smaller down payment gets you into a home sooner and keeps cash in the bank for repairs, furniture and emergencies. The CFPB suggests keeping three to six months of expenses as an emergency cushion and taking that out of your savings before you decide how much to put down. The cost is a bigger loan, more interest and, under 20%, mortgage insurance.

Where down payment money can come from

  • Savings set aside in a separate account, ideally one that earns interest while you save.
  • Gifts from family. Lenders may ask for a gift letter and records showing where the money came from, so plan ahead.
  • Down payment assistance from state or local housing agencies and some lenders. A HUD-approved housing counselor can point you to local programs.
  • An IRA. Up to 10,000 withdrawn for a qualified first-time home purchase is exempt from the IRS 10% early-withdrawal penalty, though income tax can still apply to traditional IRA money.
  • A second loan (a piggyback loan) for part of the price, which lowers the first mortgage but adds another payment.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Do not forget closing costs. They are paid in cash on top of the down payment, and the calculator's 3% default is only a starting point; your lender's Loan Estimate shows the real figure.
  • Do not spend every rupee or dollar. Keep an emergency fund and money for moving and repairs.
  • Check the payment, not just the price. The price tab can show a high price with a small down payment; make sure the monthly payment fits your income.
  • Compare loan programs. A 3.5% FHA loan and a 5% conventional loan have different mortgage insurance costs.
  • In Pakistan, ask the bank about every cost at transfer, such as taxes and registration, and enter them as closing costs in rupees.

How it works

Pick the question you want answered. On What price can I afford?, enter your cash and the down payment percentage you want: the calculator finds the price where the down payment plus the closing costs use up exactly your cash. On How much cash do I need?, enter a price and a percentage: it adds the down payment and the closing costs to give the cash you need, and shows the same price with 3.5% to 20% down. On What % down is my cash?, enter a price and your cash: it takes the closing costs out of the cash and turns what is left into a percentage of the price. Closing costs can be a % of the price or an amount, or switched off. Each answer also shows the loan (price minus down payment) and its monthly principal and interest payment.

The formula

  • Price from cash, closing costs as a %: price = cash ÷ (down % + closing %)
  • Price from cash, closing costs as an amount: price = (cash − closing costs) ÷ down %
  • Cash needed = price × down % + closing costs
  • Down payment % = (cash − closing costs) ÷ price
  • Loan = price − down payment; monthly payment = loan × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n), r = rate ÷ 1200, n = years × 12

A worked example

You have 60,000, want to put 10% down and expect 5,000 of closing costs. That leaves 55,000 for the down payment, so the price is 55,000 ÷ 0.10 = 550,000. The loan is 495,000, which at 6.5% over 30 years is about 3,129 a month.

Questions

How do I calculate a down payment on a house?

Multiply the price by the down payment percentage: 20% of a 400,000 home is 80,000. Add closing costs to find the cash you need at closing, 92,000 with 3% closing costs. The How much cash do I need? tab does this and shows 3.5% to 20% side by side.

How do I calculate the down payment percentage?

Take the closing costs out of your cash, divide what is left by the price and multiply by 100. With 100,000 of cash, 12,000 of closing costs and a 400,000 price, that is 88,000 ÷ 400,000 = 22.0%.

How much is 20% down on a house?

One fifth of the price: 80,000 on a 400,000 home or 60,000 on a 300,000 home. Remember the closing costs on top, and that 20% is what avoids PMI on a conventional loan, not a legal minimum.

Do I need 20% down to buy a home?

No. The CFPB says most buyers need at least 3%, FHA loans start at 3.5% and VA loans can need nothing down. Under 20% you usually pay mortgage insurance, and a smaller down payment means a bigger loan and more interest.

What is the minimum down payment for a first-time home buyer?

In the US it depends on the loan rather than on being a first-time buyer: about 3% for many conventional loans, 3.5% for FHA and 0% for VA. Some state and local programs help first-time buyers with the down payment or closing costs.

What is the minimum down payment in Canada?

5% of the first C$500,000, 10% of the part from C$500,000 to C$1.5 million, and 20% for homes of C$1.5 million or more. Below 20% down you need mortgage loan insurance. The rule is the same in Ontario, British Columbia, Alberta and the other provinces.

What is the down payment for a house loan in Pakistan?

The State Bank's housing finance rules cap the loan at 90% of the value, so at least 10% down, with a tenor of up to 30 years. Banks can ask for more, so check the bank's own home finance terms.

What is the down payment for a car in Pakistan?

Since September 2021, State Bank rules require at least 30% down on bank car finance, with total auto finance capped at Rs 3,000,000 per person; locally made cars up to 1,000cc and local electric vehicles stayed under the older rules. Ask the bank for its current terms.

What are closing costs?

Fees paid when you buy: the lender's charges, appraisal, title insurance, recording fees, transfer taxes and prepaid items such as insurance. They are paid in cash on top of the down payment, unless the seller or lender covers some. Your Loan Estimate lists them.

Why does a higher down payment lower my monthly payment?

Everything you put down is money you do not borrow. A smaller loan has a smaller monthly payment, less interest over the years and, from 20% down on a conventional loan, usually no PMI.

Should I put all my savings into the down payment?

Usually not. The CFPB suggests keeping three to six months of expenses as an emergency fund. A slightly smaller down payment with savings in reserve is often safer than an empty account on moving day.
