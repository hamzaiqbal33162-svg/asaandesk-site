Mean, median, mode and range in simple words

A mean, median, mode and range calculator sums up a list of numbers in four figures. The first three are averages, also called measures of central tendency: each one picks a single value that stands for the whole list. The range is different; it measures spread, how far the numbers stretch from the smallest to the largest.

The mean is the everyday average: add everything and divide by how many numbers there are. The median is the middle value once the list is in order. The mode is the value that turns up most often. The range is the largest number minus the smallest.

Each answers a slightly different question. The mean asks what everyone would get if the total were shared equally, the median asks where the middle person stands, and the mode asks what is most common. When the three agree, the data are balanced; when they differ, the gap tells you something about the shape of the data.

Formulas and how to calculate each one by hand

  • Mean: x̄ = (x₁ + x₂ + … + xₙ) ÷ n. For 4, 8, 15, 16, 23, 42 the sum is 108 and n = 6, so the mean is 18.
  • Median, odd count: sort the list and take the value in position (n + 1) ÷ 2. For 0, 7, 12, 33, 45, 88, 102 (n = 7) that is the 4th value, 33.
  • Median, even count: take the two values in positions n ÷ 2 and n ÷ 2 + 1 and average them. For 4, 8, 15, 16, 23, 42 that is (15 + 16) ÷ 2 = 15.5.
  • Mode: count how often each value appears and pick the highest count. A list can have one mode, several tied modes, or none when every value is different.
  • Range: largest − smallest. For −3, 4, −1, 0, 6 it is 6 − (−3) = 9; subtracting a negative adds its size.
  • Geometric mean: multiply all n values and take the n-th root. It needs values of 0 or more and suits growth rates and ratios.

Worked examples

School marks: a class of eight scores 72, 85, 64, 85, 90, 58, 77 and 85. The mean is 77, the median 81 and the mode 85, with a range of 32. The median sits above the mean because the two low marks, 58 and 64, pull the mean down more than they move the middle.

Cricket scores: a batter's last seven innings are 45, 0, 12, 88, 102, 7 and 33. The mean is 287 ÷ 7 = 41 and the median is 33; no score repeats, so there is no mode. Add one more innings of 250 and the mean jumps to 67.125 while the median only moves to 39, which shows how one big score stretches the mean.

Growth rates: an investment grows 5%, 10%, −5% and 20% in four years, which are factors of 1.05, 1.10, 0.95 and 1.20. Their ordinary mean is 1.075, but the geometric mean is 1.0712028239014, so the true average growth is about 7.12% a year, not 7.5%. Multiplying 1.0712 four times gives the same end value as the real years.

Mean, median, mode and range in Excel and Google Sheets

  • Mean: =AVERAGE(A1:A8)
  • Median: =MEDIAN(A1:A8)
  • Mode: =MODE.SNGL(A1:A8) returns one mode; =MODE.MULT(A1:A8) returns every tied mode. In Excel both give #N/A when no value repeats. Google Sheets has the same MODE and MODE.MULT functions.
  • Range: =MAX(A1:A8)-MIN(A1:A8), as there is no single range function.
  • Geometric mean: =GEOMEAN(A1:A8); sample standard deviation: =STDEV.S(A1:A8).
  • Check a spreadsheet against this page by pasting the same column here; the numbers should match to the last digit.

Common mistakes and tips

  • Forgetting to sort before finding the median. The middle of 72, 85, 64 as typed is 85, but the true median is 72.
  • Using n ÷ 2 as the median position for an odd count. Use (n + 1) ÷ 2, and average the two middle values for an even count.
  • Calling the most common value a mode when nothing repeats. If every value appears once, the list has no mode, not a mode of every value.
  • Reporting only the mean for skewed data. Give the median too, or both.
  • Leaving out zeros: a score of 0 is data and changes the mean and median.

How it works

Type or paste your numbers, separated by commas, spaces or new lines. The mean, median and mode appear at the top, each with a short line saying how it was found, followed by the range, the geometric mean, the largest and smallest numbers, the sum and the count. The column chart draws your numbers in the order you typed them, so a number far above or below the rest is easy to spot, and the sorted list underneath shows where the middle falls.

The formula

  • Mean = sum of the numbers ÷ how many there are
  • Median = the middle number once the list is sorted; with an even count, halfway between the two middle numbers
  • Mode = the number that appears most often; several numbers can tie, and a list where no number repeats has no mode
  • Range = largest − smallest
  • Geometric mean = the N-th root of all N numbers multiplied together (numbers 0 or more)

A worked example

Eight test marks, 72, 85, 64, 85, 90, 58, 77, 85, add up to 616, so the mean is 616 ÷ 8 = 77. Sorted they read 58, 64, 72, 77, 85, 85, 85, 90; the two middle marks are 77 and 85, so the median is 81. The mark 85 appears three times, so it is the mode, and the range is 90 − 58 = 32.

Questions

How do you calculate the mean, median, mode and range?

Add the numbers and divide by the count for the mean. Sort them and take the middle value for the median. Find the value that appears most for the mode, and subtract the smallest from the largest for the range.

When is the median better than the mean?

When a few numbers are much bigger or smaller than the rest. One very high salary pulls the mean of a small team's pay up a lot, but the median, the middle value, barely moves, so it describes the typical person better.

Can a list have more than one mode?

Yes. If two or more numbers tie for the most appearances, each of them is a mode, and we list them all, smallest first. If no number appears more than once, the list has no mode.

How is the median found for an even count?

Sort the numbers and take the two in the middle; the median is halfway between them. For 1, 2, 3, 4 the middle pair is 2 and 3, so the median is 2.5.

What does the range tell me?

How far apart the smallest and largest numbers are. It is quick to work out, but because it uses only those two numbers, one unusual value can make it large. The standard deviation calculator gives a measure of spread that uses every number.

Is the mean the same as the average?

In everyday speech, yes: the average usually means the arithmetic mean. Strictly, the median and the mode are averages too, so a report should say which one it uses.

How do I find the mean, median and mode from a frequency table?

For the mean, multiply each value by its frequency, add the products and divide by the total frequency. For the median, use the running total of frequencies to find the middle position. The mode is the value with the largest frequency.

What is an outlier and how does it affect these measures?

An outlier is a value far from the rest, like a score of 250 among scores under 110. It pulls the mean and stretches the range a lot, moves the median a little, and leaves the mode alone unless it repeats.

How do I calculate mean, median and mode in Excel?

Use =AVERAGE(range), =MEDIAN(range) and =MODE.SNGL(range), or =MODE.MULT(range) for several modes. For the range there is no single function, so use =MAX(range)-MIN(range).

Can the mean be a number that is not in the list?

Yes. The mean of 4, 8, 15, 16, 23 and 42 is 18, which none of them equals, and the mean of whole numbers is often a decimal. The median of an even count can also fall between two listed values.
