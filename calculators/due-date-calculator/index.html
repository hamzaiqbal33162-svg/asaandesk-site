When is my baby due?

This due date calculator estimates the day your baby is due, called the estimated due date or EDD, from the date you know best: the first day of your last period, an ultrasound scan, the conception date or an IVF embryo transfer. It also tells you how many weeks pregnant you are today, your trimester and the average size of a baby that week, with a 42-week schedule.

A due date marks 40 weeks of pregnancy counted from the first day of the last period. ACOG notes that only about 1 in 20 women give birth on the day itself, and the NHS describes a normal pregnancy as lasting 37 to 42 weeks, so treat the date as the middle of a window rather than an appointment. This is an estimate, not medical advice.

How to use the due date calculator

  • Under "Work it out from", choose Last period, Ultrasound, Conception or IVF transfer.
  • Last period: enter the first day of your last period and your usual cycle length (22 to 44 days, from the first day of one period to the first day of the next).
  • Ultrasound: enter the scan date and the weeks and days the scan report gave.
  • Conception: enter the date you conceived, if you know it from ovulation tests or a single date of sex.
  • IVF transfer: enter the transfer date and choose a day 3, day 5 or day 6 embryo.
  • The estimated due date appears at the top, with today's week, trimester and the schedule below.

How each method works

Every method is turned into the same starting point, the first day of a last period for a 28-day cycle, and the due date is 280 days after it. The table shows what that means for each kind of date.

Adding 280 days is the idea behind Naegele's rule. As a rough check by hand, 280 days later is usually about 9 months and 1 week on from the first day of the last period, give or take a day or two for the lengths of the months.

Which date to trust

A period date assumes ovulation about two weeks after the period started. That works well for regular cycles and a date you are sure of. With irregular cycles or a date you are not sure of, a scan is better: the NHS dating scan at about 10 to 14 weeks is used to work out how far along you are and your due date, and to see whether you are expecting more than one baby.

ACOG's Committee Opinion 700 (2017, reaffirmed 2025) says the due date should be set as soon as the last period date, the first accurate ultrasound or both are known, written in your record, and changed after that only in rare cases. A pregnancy with no ultrasound that confirms or changes the date before 22 weeks counts as less well dated. If your scan and your period give different dates, your midwife or doctor decides which one to use; enter that date here with the Ultrasound option.

Twins, caesarean births and going past the date

For twins the due date is worked out the same way, but ACOG notes that more than half of twins are born preterm, before 37 weeks. If a caesarean is planned, your care team gives you a date for it, which may not be the due date shown here.

If labour has not started, the NHS offers induction from 41 weeks, because the risks for the baby rise after that. If a pregnancy goes beyond 42 weeks and you choose not to be induced, you should be offered closer monitoring.

Tips for getting the most accurate date

  • Use the first day of bleeding as day 1 of your last period, not the day it ended.
  • Measure your cycle from the start of one period to the start of the next, and use your usual length rather than a single odd month.
  • Copy the weeks and days from the scan report exactly, such as 8 weeks 2 days, and the date of that scan.
  • Once your care team gives you a due date, use it everywhere so all your appointments line up.

How it works

Choose the date you know and enter it. From the first day of your last period, the due date is 40 weeks (280 days) later, moved later for cycles longer than 28 days and earlier for shorter ones, because ovulation moves with the cycle. From a scan, the age the scan gave is counted back to its starting point. From a conception date it is 266 days later, and from an IVF transfer the embryo's age is taken into account. Today's date then shows how far along the pregnancy is, the trimester and the average size of a baby that week.

The formula

  • Due date = first day of last period + 280 days + (cycle length − 28) days (Naegele's rule)
  • From an ultrasound: due date = scan date − (7 × weeks + days) + 280 days
  • From a conception date: due date = conception + 266 days
  • From an IVF transfer: due date = transfer date + 266 days − embryo age (day 3: + 263, day 5: + 261, day 6: + 260)
  • Weeks pregnant today = days since (due date − 280 days), in weeks and days

A worked example

The last period started on September 22, 2026 with a 24-day cycle. A 24-day cycle is 4 days shorter than 28, so the due date is September 22 + 280 − 4 days = June 25, 2027. On November 21, 2026 the pregnancy is 9 weeks 1 day along, in week 10.

Questions

How is a due date worked out?

The usual method adds 280 days, or 40 weeks, to the first day of your last period. It assumes a 28-day cycle with ovulation on day 14, so for longer or shorter cycles the date moves by the difference. This is called Naegele's rule.

How do I calculate my due date from my last period?

Add 280 days to the first day of your last period, then add or take away the days your cycle is longer or shorter than 28. A period that began on October 1, 2026 with a 28-day cycle gives a due date of July 8, 2027.

How do I calculate my due date from conception?

Add 266 days, or 38 weeks, to the conception date. Conception on October 5, 2026 gives a due date of June 28, 2027. Choose the Conception option to see it with today's week of pregnancy.

My scan gave a different due date. Which one is right?

A scan in the first trimester measures the baby directly and is usually more accurate than a period date, especially if your cycles are irregular. Your doctor or midwife decides whether to change the date, and after that it is changed only in rare cases. Use the ultrasound option to see the dates from your scan.

How accurate is the due date?

It is an estimate. ACOG says only about 1 in 20 women give birth on the due date itself, and the NHS describes a normal pregnancy as lasting 37 to 42 weeks. Plan for a window of several weeks around the date.

Why does an IVF due date depend on the embryo's age?

A day-5 embryo is already five days past fertilisation when it is transferred, so the pregnancy counts as 2 weeks and 5 days on the transfer day. A day-3 embryo counts as 2 weeks and 3 days, so its due date is two days later than a day-5 transfer on the same date.

What happens if I go past my due date?

In England the NHS offers induction if labour has not started by 41 weeks. If you go beyond 42 weeks and choose not to be induced, you should be offered closer checks on the baby. Talk to your midwife or doctor about the plan for you.

Can I use the due date calculator for twins?

Yes, the due date is found the same way. ACOG notes that more than half of twins are born before 37 weeks, so your care team may plan an earlier birth.
