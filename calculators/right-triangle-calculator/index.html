What a right triangle is

A right triangle (or right-angled triangle) is a triangle with one angle of exactly 90°. The two sides that form the right angle are the legs, and the side opposite it is the hypotenuse, always the longest side. The other two angles always add up to 90°, so knowing one of them gives the other.

This right triangle calculator solves the whole triangle from any two values: two sides, a side and an angle, or a length together with the height, area or perimeter. It returns the missing sides and angles, the area, perimeter, height to the hypotenuse, inradius and circumradius, with a labelled picture and every step.

How to use the right triangle calculator

  • Choose Angles in: Degrees or Radians. In radians you can type a number such as 0.5236 or a part of pi such as pi/6.
  • Type any two of Leg a, Leg b, Hypotenuse c, Angle α (faces a) and Angle β (faces b), and leave the rest empty. The page starts with the legs 3 and 4.
  • If you know the height, area or perimeter instead, open Know the height, area or perimeter? and type one of them with one other value.
  • Do not give two angles only: they fix the shape but not the size.
  • Read the first three unknown values at the top and the rest below, plus the inradius r and circumradius R. The All values table marks which values you gave, and the picture is drawn to scale.
  • Use one unit for all lengths (all cm, all m or all inches); the area comes out in that unit squared.

Right triangle formulas

Everything follows from Pythagoras' theorem and the three trigonometric ratios, remembered as SOH CAH TOA: Sine = Opposite ÷ Hypotenuse, Cosine = Adjacent ÷ Hypotenuse, Tangent = Opposite ÷ Adjacent. For angle α, the opposite side is a and the adjacent leg is b.

Worked examples

Legs 3 and 4 (the default). c = √(9 + 16) = √25 = 5. α = arctan(3 ÷ 4) = 36.87° and β = 90° − 36.87° = 53.13°. Area = 3 × 4 ÷ 2 = 6, perimeter = 12, height to the hypotenuse h = 12 ÷ 5 = 2.4, inradius r = 12 ÷ 12 = 1 and circumradius R = 2.5.

A ladder against a wall. A 6 m ladder makes a 75° angle with the ground. The wall height it reaches is the side opposite 75°: 6 × sin 75° = 5.80 m, and its foot stands 6 × cos 75° = 1.55 m from the wall. Type c = 6 and β = 75°, or α = 15°, to get both.

Feet and inches. A ramp rises 30 inches over a run of 30 ft. Put both in inches first: 30 ft = 360 in. The ramp surface is √(30² + 360²) = 361.25 in, about 30 ft 1.25 in, and its angle is arctan(30 ÷ 360) = 4.76°. Mixing feet and inches in the same calculation is the usual source of wrong answers.

Angles from sides only. A triangle with legs 5 and 12 has c = 13, α = arctan(5 ÷ 12) = 22.62° and β = 67.38°. The angles come from the ratios of the sides, so any triangle with sides 10, 24 and 26 has the same angles.

Special right triangles: 30-60-90 and 45-45-90

Two right triangles have sides in fixed, exact ratios, so one side gives all the others without a calculator. In a 30-60-90 triangle the sides are in the ratio 1 : √3 : 2 (short leg : long leg : hypotenuse); a hypotenuse of 10 gives legs of 5 and 5√3 ≈ 8.66. In a 45-45-90 triangle, which is also isosceles, the ratio is 1 : 1 : √2; legs of 7 give a hypotenuse of 7√2 ≈ 9.90. The calculator shows these exact root forms under the decimals.

Pythagorean triples

A Pythagorean triple is a set of three whole numbers that fit a² + b² = c². The 3-4-5 triple gives a quick check for a square corner on a building site or in a room: mark 3 units along one wall and 4 along the other, and the corner is a true right angle when the diagonal between the marks is exactly 5. Any multiple of a triple also works, such as 6-8-10 or 30-40-50.

The altitude theorem and right triangle congruence

The height h from the right angle to the hypotenuse splits the triangle into two smaller triangles that are similar to the original. That gives the altitude (geometric mean) theorem: if the height cuts the hypotenuse into pieces p and q, then h² = p × q. In the 3-4-5 triangle the pieces are 1.8 and 3.2, and 1.8 × 3.2 = 5.76 = 2.4². Each leg also satisfies a² = c × (its piece): 3² = 5 × 1.8.

For congruence, right triangles have their own shortcut, the hypotenuse-leg (HL) rule: two right triangles with equal hypotenuses and one pair of equal legs are congruent, because Pythagoras then fixes the third side.

Common mistakes

  • Calling a leg the hypotenuse. The hypotenuse is opposite the right angle and is always the longest side; a leg as long as c gives an error.
  • Using the wrong angle. α faces leg a and β faces leg b; sin α = a ÷ c, not b ÷ c.
  • Leaving the calculator in the wrong angle mode. sin 30 is 0.5 in degrees but −0.988 in radians.
  • Adding squares instead of subtracting for a leg: b = √(c² − a²).
  • Mixing units, such as one leg in feet and the other in inches.

How it works

A right triangle has one 90° corner. The two sides that meet there are the legs a and b, the long side across from it is the hypotenuse c, and the angles α and β sit opposite a and b. Type any two values you know and leave the rest empty: two sides, a side and an angle, or a length with the height, the area or the perimeter (open "Know the height, area or perimeter?" for those). Two angles alone are not enough, because they fix the shape but not the size. The answer appears as you type, with exact root forms such as 5√3 where they exist.

The formula

  • Pythagoras: a² + b² = c²
  • sin α = a ÷ c, cos α = b ÷ c, tan α = a ÷ b, and α + β = 90°
  • Area = a × b ÷ 2 = c × h ÷ 2, so the height to the hypotenuse is h = a × b ÷ c
  • Perimeter = a + b + c; inradius r = a × b ÷ (a + b + c); circumradius R = c ÷ 2
  • From c and h: a + b = √(c² + 2ch) and the difference of the legs = √(c² − 2ch)

A worked example

Hypotenuse c = 10 and α = 30°: a = 10 × sin 30° = 5 and b = 10 × cos 30° = 8.66025 (5√3). Then β = 90° − 30° = 60°, the height h = 5 × 8.66025 ÷ 10 = 4.33013, the area = 5 × 8.66025 ÷ 2 = 21.65064 and the perimeter = 23.66025.

Questions

How do I calculate the hypotenuse of a right triangle?

Square both legs, add them and take the square root: c = √(a² + b²). Legs of 6 and 8 give √100 = 10. Type the two legs above and leave c empty.

How do I calculate right triangle angles from side lengths?

Use the inverse ratios: α = arctan(a ÷ b), or arcsin(a ÷ c) when you know the hypotenuse. Then β = 90° − α. Legs 3 and 4 give 36.87° and 53.13°.

How do I find a missing side with one side and an angle?

Use SOH CAH TOA. With the hypotenuse c and angle α, a = c × sin α and b = c × cos α; with leg b and α, a = b × tan α. Type the two values you know and the rest appear.

How do I calculate the area of a right triangle?

Multiply the two legs and halve the result: 3 × 4 ÷ 2 = 6. The legs are the base and the height, because they meet at a right angle.

What is the formula for a 30-60-90 triangle?

The sides are in the ratio 1 : √3 : 2. The short leg is half the hypotenuse, and the long leg is the short leg × √3. A hypotenuse of 10 gives legs 5 and 8.66.

What is the formula for a 45-45-90 triangle?

Both legs are equal and the hypotenuse is a leg × √2. A leg of 7 gives a hypotenuse of 9.90; a hypotenuse of 10 gives legs of 7.07.

Which side is a and which is b?

Either leg can be a. Just keep the angle names in step: α is the angle facing leg a and β the angle facing leg b. The hypotenuse c is always the side across from the right angle, and it is the longest side.

Why are two triangles shown sometimes?

Some pairs, such as the hypotenuse and the height, or the area and the perimeter, do not say which leg is which. The same triangle then fits twice, once with its legs swapped, so both are listed. Their sides are the same, only α and β trade places.

Why do I get an error that a leg must be shorter than c?

The hypotenuse is always the longest side of a right triangle. If a leg is as long as the hypotenuse or longer, no right triangle has those sides.

Can I type angles in radians?

Yes. Switch Angles in to Radians and type a number such as 0.5236 or a part of pi such as pi/6. The results show each angle in radians, degrees and, when it is a neat one, as a part of π.

Can I use feet and inches?

Yes, as long as every length is in the same unit. Change feet to inches (× 12) or write inches as parts of a foot (6 in = 0.5 ft) before typing.
