What slope means

The slope of a line, also called its gradient, measures how steep it is: how much it rises for each step to the right. It is written m and worked out as rise over run, m = (y₂ − y₁) ÷ (x₂ − x₁). A slope of 2 climbs 2 units for every 1 across; a slope of −0.5 falls half a unit for every 1 across; a slope of 0 is flat; and a vertical line has no slope at all, because its run is 0.

This slope calculator takes two points and gives the slope as a decimal and a fraction, the angle of the line, the distance between the points and the equation of the line through them, with both intercepts and the steps. Its second tab works backwards and finds a second point from a starting point, a distance and a slope or an angle.

How to use the slope calculator

  • Two points: type x₁ and y₁ for Point 1 (x₁, y₁) and x₂ and y₂ for Point 2 (x₂, y₂). The page opens with (2, 1) and (6, 4). Decimals and negative numbers are fine and are kept exact.
  • Find the second point: type Point 1, the Distance to the second point (5 to start), and under Direction given as choose Slope m (0.75 to start) or Angle θ (30 degrees, counter-clockwise from the positive x direction).
  • Answers update as you type. Open the steps to see the rise, run, slope, angle, line and distance worked out.

Understanding the results

  • Slope m: rise ÷ run, with the rise and run underneath, and Slope as a fraction when it is not whole (3/4 for the opening example). For a vertical line it says Not defined.
  • Angle θ: the direction from point 1 to point 2, counter-clockwise from the positive x direction, from 0° to 360°, also in radians. Going down to the right gives an angle between 270° and 360° (323.13° for a slope of −0.75).
  • Distance d: the straight-line length between the points, √(ΔX² + ΔY²), with the exact root.
  • Run ΔX and Rise ΔY: the two changes, signs included.
  • Equation of the line: y = mx + b in decimals, and as fractions when that differs, plus the y-intercept (where x = 0) and the x-intercept (where y = 0). Flat and vertical lines say when an intercept is missing or is the whole axis.
  • Find the second point: the Second point going right from point 1 and, for a slope, the other one going left (Or, going the other way), the angle each way or the slope tan θ, the line, and a table of both answers. A picture shows the points and the line on a grid.

The slope formula, with worked examples

With the opening points (2, 1) and (6, 4), the steps read: run ΔX = 6 − 2 = 4; rise ΔY = 4 − 1 = 3; slope m = 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75; angle arctan(0.75) = 36.869897645844°; b = 1 − 0.75 × 2 = −0.5, so y = 0.75x − 0.5; the line crosses the x-axis at 0.5 ÷ 0.75 ≈ 0.667; and the distance is √(4² + 3²) = 5.

A falling line, from (−3, 4) to (5, −2): run 8, rise −6, so m = −6/8 = −3/4 = −0.75. The line is y = −0.75x + 1.75 (as fractions, y = −(3/4)x + 7/4), the distance is 10, and the direction is 323.13°.

Special cases: from (1, 5) to (4, 5) the rise is 0, so m = 0 and the line is y = 5, which never meets the x-axis. From (3, 2) to (3, 7) the run is 0, so the slope is not defined, the line is x = 3 and the angle is 90°.

Working backwards: from (2, 1), a distance of 5 along slope 0.75 lands at (6, 4) going right or (−2, −2) going left. With an angle of 30° instead, the single answer is about (6.3301, 3.5), and the slope is tan 30° ≈ 0.57735.

Slope as a percentage, an angle and a ratio

Outside the maths class, slope is often given as a grade in percent (rise ÷ run × 100), an angle in degrees (arctan of the slope), or a ratio of 1 unit up per so many across. They all describe the same steepness:

Grade and angle are not proportional: a 100% grade is 45°, not 90°, and a vertical wall has an infinite grade. To go from an angle to a slope, take its tangent: 10° is a slope of tan 10° ≈ 0.176, a 17.6% grade.

Slope for ramps, roofs, roads and pipes

  • Ramps: in the United States, the 2010 ADA Standards for Accessible Design limit a ramp run to a slope of 1:12 (8.33%, about 4.76°) and a rise of 30 inches per run. Treat the floor as the x-axis: a ramp that rises 1 ft over 12 ft, from (0, 0) to (12, 1), gives m = 1/12 ≈ 0.0833 and an angle of 4.7636°, and its sloping length is √145 ≈ 12.04 ft.
  • Roof pitch: in feet and inches a roof is often described by its rise per 12 inches of run, so a 6/12 roof is points (0, 0) and (12, 6): slope 0.5 and angle 26.565°. A 4/12 roof is 18.43°, a 9/12 roof 36.87° and a 12/12 roof 45°.
  • Roads and hills: a grade of 8% rises 8 m in every 100 m of horizontal distance; points (0, 0) and (100, 8) give an angle of about 4.57°.
  • Drainage and pipes: a fall of 1% means a drop of 1 cm per metre, so a 20 m drain falls 20 cm. Check the fall required by your local plumbing or building code before you build.

Slope-intercept form, parallel and perpendicular lines

The line through the points is given in slope-intercept form, y = mx + b, where m is the slope and b is the y-intercept (O Level books write it y = mx + c). From a slope and one point you can also use point-slope form, y − y₁ = m(x − x₁). With m = 0.75 and the point (2, 1), that is y − 1 = 0.75(x − 2), which simplifies to the same y = 0.75x − 0.5.

To read the slope from an equation like 3x + 2y = 6, rearrange it to y = −1.5x + 3: the slope is −1.5 and the y-intercept is 3. Parallel lines have equal slopes. Perpendicular lines have slopes that multiply to −1, so a line perpendicular to one of slope 3/4 has slope −4/3, the negative reciprocal. Horizontal and vertical lines are the exception, since a vertical line has no slope.

Slope in physics and statistics

On a graph, the slope is the rate of change of the vertical quantity with respect to the horizontal one, so it carries units. On a distance-time graph the slope is speed: a car at 0 m at 0 s and 120 m at 4 s gives points (0, 0) and (4, 120), a slope of 30, meaning 30 m/s. On a velocity-time graph the slope is acceleration, and on a graph of price against quantity it is the cost per item.

With many data points that do not lie exactly on a line, the slope of the best-fit (least-squares regression) line is used instead. In Excel, =SLOPE(known_y's, known_x's) returns it; for two points it gives the same answer as this calculator.

For school, common mistakes and related calculators

Gradient is part of Coordinate geometry in the Cambridge O Level Mathematics syllabus (4024, exams in 2025 to 2027): finding the gradient of a line and from two points, the length and midpoint of a segment, equations in the forms y = mx + c, ax + by = c and x = k, and parallel and perpendicular lines. The equation, intercepts and distance shown here cover most of those steps.

  • Subtracting in a different order on top and bottom: use (y₂ − y₁) ÷ (x₂ − x₁), with the same point first in both.
  • Putting the run on top: slope is rise over run, y over x.
  • Calling a vertical line's slope 0: it is undefined; a horizontal line has slope 0.
  • Mixing a grade in percent with an angle in degrees: 10% is about 5.71°, not 10°.

How it works

With two points, type x and y for each one. The slope is the rise (how far y changes) divided by the run (how far x changes), so it says how many steps up the line goes for each step to the right. You also get the angle of the direction from the first point to the second, measured counter-clockwise from the positive x direction (0° to 360°), the straight distance between the points, and the equation y = mx + b with both intercepts. The other tab works backwards: from one point, a distance and a slope (or an angle) it finds where the second point is. A slope fits two directions, so you get both answers; an angle fixes one direction, so you get one.

The formula

  • Slope m = (y₂ − y₁) ÷ (x₂ − x₁) = rise ÷ run
  • Angle θ = arctan(ΔY ÷ ΔX), turned into a direction between 0° and 360° (add 180° when the run is negative, 360° when only the rise is negative)
  • Distance d = √(ΔX² + ΔY²) (the Pythagorean theorem)
  • Line: y = mx + b with b = y₁ − m × x₁; it meets the x-axis at x = −b ÷ m
  • From a distance and a slope: ΔX = ± d ÷ √(1 + m²), ΔY = m × ΔX
  • From a distance and an angle: ΔX = d × cos θ, ΔY = d × sin θ, m = tan θ

A worked example

From (−3, 4) to (5, −2): the run is 5 − (−3) = 8 and the rise is −2 − 4 = −6, so m = −6 ÷ 8 = −3/4 = −0.75. The distance is √(8² + 6²) = √100 = 10. Then b = 4 − (−0.75 × −3) = 1.75, so the line is y = −0.75x + 1.75.

Questions

How do I calculate the slope with two points?

Subtract the y values and the x values in the same order and divide: m = (y₂ − y₁) ÷ (x₂ − x₁). For (2, 1) and (6, 4), m = (4 − 1) ÷ (6 − 2) = 3/4 = 0.75. Type both points into the Two points tab to see the steps.

What is the slope formula?

Slope m = rise ÷ run = Δy ÷ Δx = (y₂ − y₁) ÷ (x₂ − x₁). In the equation y = mx + b of a line, the number in front of x is the slope.

How do I calculate slope as a percentage?

Divide the rise by the run and multiply by 100. A path that climbs 3 m over 60 m of horizontal distance has a slope of 0.05, a 5% grade, which is about 2.86°.

How do I convert a slope to degrees?

Take the arctangent of the slope: angle = arctan(m). A slope of 1 is 45°, 0.5 is 26.57° and 0.0833 (a 1:12 ramp) is 4.76°. The calculator shows the angle for every pair of points.

How do I calculate the slope of a ramp?

Divide the height the ramp climbs by its horizontal length. A ramp rising 0.5 m over 6 m has a slope of 0.0833, which is 1:12 or 8.33%. Under the US ADA Standards, 1:12 is the steepest slope allowed for a ramp run.

What does a negative slope mean?

The line goes down as you move to the right. A slope of −2 means y falls by 2 for every 1 step to the right. A slope of 0 is a flat line, and a bigger number (positive or negative) means a steeper line.

Why is the slope of a vertical line not defined?

On a vertical line the run is 0, and the slope would divide the rise by 0, which has no value. Its equation is x = a number instead of y = mx + b, and its angle is 90° going up or 270° going down.

What is slope-intercept form?

It is y = mx + b, where m is the slope and b is where the line crosses the y-axis. The calculator gives the line through your two points in this form, in decimals and as fractions.

How do I calculate slope in Excel?

Put the y values in one range and the x values in another, then use =SLOPE(y_range, x_range). With two points it equals rise over run; with more points it gives the slope of the least-squares best-fit line.

Why do I get two answers for the second point?

A slope gives a line but not which way along it you travel. Moving the same distance to the right or to the left both land on the line, so both points are shown. With an angle the direction is fixed, so there is one answer.

Can I use decimals or negative numbers?

Yes. Decimal points are turned into exact fractions first, so a slope such as 0.6/0.8 comes out exactly as 3/4 = 0.75, and negative coordinates are fine anywhere.
