What the student loan calculator does

A student loan calculator shows what your loan will really cost: the monthly payment, how long it takes to repay, the total interest and what extra payments would save. This one has three tools in one. My payment works out your payment, payoff time, the loan you can afford or the interest rate you are being charged. Pay it off faster compares your current plan with extra monthly, yearly or one-time payments. After graduation projects what you will owe, and pay, once school and the grace period end.

It works for US federal and private student loans, for UK and other loans with a fixed rate and monthly payments, and for education loans in Pakistani rupees or any other currency. It assumes a fixed rate and equal monthly payments, so income-driven plans, which set the payment from your income, are explained separately below.

How to use each tool

  • My payment: choose What do you want to find? (Monthly payment, Time to repay, Loan balance or Interest rate), then fill in the other three of Loan balance, Years left to repay, Interest rate and Monthly payment.
  • Pay it off faster: enter the Loan balance, your Monthly payment now and the Interest rate, then choose a Plan: Pay extra (Extra every month, Extra once a year, One-time extra now), Pay it all off now, or Keep paying as now.
  • After graduation: enter Years until you graduate, New borrowing each year, Loan balance today, the Interest rate, the Repayment length and the Grace period after graduation in months, and say whether you pay the interest while in school.
  • Choose your currency at the top: dollars, pounds, rupees or another one.

The yearly extra is paid with every 12th payment, so the first one is a year from now; the one-time extra goes with your next payment. If a payment does not cover the interest the loan adds each month, the calculator says so instead of showing a payoff time that would never come.

Interest is charged each month at the yearly rate ÷ 12. Federal Student Aid explains that federal loans charge simple daily interest on the principal, which comes to almost the same amount over a month. Loan fees are not included.

Student loan payment examples

Example 1: you owe $38,000 at an assumed 6.39%. Over 15 years the payment is $328.73 a month and the total interest is $21,170.90. Over 10 years it is $429.36 a month, but the interest falls to $13,523.03. A shorter term costs more each month and much less in total.

Example 2, the other questions: paying $450 a month on the same loan clears it in 9 years 5 months with $12,604.49 of interest. A $300 budget for 10 years at 6.39% repays a loan of up to $26,551.24. And if a lender asks $350 a month for 15 years on $38,000, the rate you are paying is 7.395%. A payment below $202.35 would not even cover the first month's interest, so the calculator warns you.

Example 3, in Pakistani rupees: an education loan of Rs 1,500,000 at an assumed 14% over 5 years costs Rs 34,902.38 a month, with Rs 594,142.58 of interest. Rates on bank education loans vary, so use the rate in your own offer.

The law lets you repay federal Direct loans early without penalty (check a private loan contract for its own terms), but make sure your servicer puts extra money towards the principal and does not just move your next due date.

What you will owe after graduation

Interest usually builds up while you study and during the grace period, the months between leaving school and your first payment. Unless you pay it, that interest is added to the loan and then charges interest of its own.

Example: you owe $12,000 today, will borrow $7,500 a year for the 3 years until you graduate, at 6.39%, with a 6-month grace period and a 10-year repayment. You will have borrowed $34,500. If you let the interest build up, the balance is $39,257.30 at graduation and $40,528.39 when payments start, so $6,028.39 of interest has been added and the payment is $457.93 a month; total interest is $20,451.19.

If you pay the interest as it comes instead, you pay $5,499.39 during school and grace, the balance stays at $34,500.00, and the payment after school is $389.81, with $17,776.88 of interest in total. Paying the interest while studying saves $2,674.31 overall and $68.12 a month afterwards.

US federal student loan changes from July 2026

Public Law 119-21, signed on 4 July 2025, changed federal student loans for loans made from 1 July 2026. Graduate and professional students can no longer take out new Grad PLUS loans. Instead, Direct Unsubsidized loans are capped at $20,500 a year and $100,000 in total for graduate students, and $50,000 a year and $200,000 for professional students. Parent PLUS loans are capped at $20,000 a year and $65,000 in total per student, and there is a lifetime limit of $257,500 for all federal loans a student borrows.

New borrowers choose between two plans. The new standard plan has a fixed payment over a length set by what you owe: 10 years under $25,000, 15 years from $25,000, 20 years from $50,000 and 25 years from $100,000. The $38,000 example above falls in the 15-year band, which is why it was used. The other choice is the income-based Repayment Assistance Plan (RAP).

The monthly RAP payment is the base payment ÷ 12, minus $50 for each dependent, and never less than $10. With an AGI of $55,000 and one dependent, the base is 5% × 55,000 = $2,750, so the payment is 2,750 ÷ 12 − 50 = $179.17 a month. If an on-time payment does not cover the month's interest, the unpaid interest is not charged, and any balance left after 360 qualifying payments is cancelled. Because RAP payments follow income, not the balance, this calculator cannot show them; use it for the standard plan and for private loans.

Taxes, UK repayments and other tips

  • US interest deduction: you may deduct the lesser of $2,500 or the interest you actually paid on a qualified student loan, subject to an income limit set each year; you should get Form 1098-E if you paid $600 or more of interest.
  • UK student loans: repayments are a share of income over a threshold, not a fixed payment. GOV.UK lists 9% of income above £26,900 a year for Plan 1, £29,385 for Plan 2, £33,795 for Plan 4 and £25,000 for Plan 5, and 6% above £21,000 for a Postgraduate Loan; these thresholds change, so check before you plan.
  • Several loans: the calculator handles one balance and rate at a time. Run each loan separately and put extra money on the one with the highest rate first.
  • Refinancing or consolidating: compare the total interest of the new loan with what you have now. Moving federal loans to a private lender gives up federal options such as income-based plans.
  • Related tools: the Debt Payoff Calculator for several debts, the College Cost Calculator to plan what to borrow, the Loan Calculator and the Debt-to-Income Ratio Calculator.

How it works

Pick one of three tools. My payment finds the one number you are missing (monthly payment, time to repay, loan balance or interest rate) from the other three. Pay it off faster follows your loan month by month with your current payment, then again with extra payments, and shows how much sooner you finish and the interest you save; it can also show the saving of paying everything off today. After graduation adds up what you will have borrowed by the time you leave school, the interest that builds up in school and in the grace period, and the monthly payment once repayment starts.

The formula

  • Monthly rate r = yearly rate ÷ 100 ÷ 12; n = number of monthly payments
  • Payment P = B × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n); balance B = P × (1 − (1 + r)^−n) ÷ r
  • Time to repay n = −ln(1 − B × r ÷ P) ÷ ln(1 + r), rounded up to whole months; the rate has no formula and is found by search
  • Faster: each month interest = balance × r, then the payment plus any extra lowers the balance; a yearly extra comes with every 12th payment
  • After school (interest not paid): balance at graduation = B₀(1 + r)^m + a((1 + r)^m − 1) ÷ r, with a = yearly borrowing ÷ 12 added each month and m school months; then × (1 + r)^grace months
  • After school (interest paid): the balance stays at what you borrowed and you pay its interest each month until repayment starts

A worked example

A $38,000 loan at 6.39% repaid over 15 years costs $328.73 a month: r = 0.0639 ÷ 12 = 0.005325 and n = 180, so P = 38,000 × 0.005325 ÷ (1 − 1.005325^−180). The 180 payments add up to $59,170.90, so $21,170.90 is interest. Adding $120 a month clears the loan in 9 years 5 months instead of 15 years and saves $8,513.16 of interest.

Questions

How do I calculate my student loan monthly payment?

Use P = B × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n), with r the yearly rate ÷ 12 and n the number of months. $38,000 at 6.39% over 15 years is $328.73 a month; the My payment tool does it for you.

How do I calculate student loan interest per month?

Multiply the balance by the yearly rate and divide by 12. On $38,000 at 6.39% that is about $202.35 in the first month; it falls as the balance goes down.

How much do extra payments save on a student loan?

Often thousands. On $38,000 at 6.39% with a $328.73 payment, $120 extra a month ends the loan 5 years 7 months sooner and saves $8,513.16 of interest.

What is a grace period?

It is the time between leaving school and your first payment. For many US federal loans it is six months. Interest usually keeps building during it unless the loan is subsidized.

Should I pay the interest while I am still in school?

If you can, it keeps the loan from growing: unpaid interest is added to the balance and then charges interest of its own. In our example it saved $2,674.31 over the life of the loan.

What is the RAP student loan formula?

From July 2026, the Repayment Assistance Plan takes 1% to 10% of your adjusted gross income a year, depending on your income band ($120 a year at $10,000 or less), divides it by 12 and subtracts $50 per dependent, with a $10 minimum.

How long is the standard repayment plan for new federal loans?

For loans made from 1 July 2026 it depends on what you owe: 10 years under $25,000, 15 years up to $50,000, 20 years up to $100,000 and 25 years for $100,000 or more.

Can I deduct student loan interest?

In the United States you can deduct up to $2,500 of the interest you paid in the year on a qualified student loan, if your income is under the yearly limit. Your servicer sends Form 1098-E if you paid $600 or more.

Does it include loan fees or income-driven plans?

No. It assumes a fixed rate and equal monthly payments. Origination fees, rate changes and income-driven repayment or forgiveness plans change the real cost.

Can I use it for loans outside the US?

Yes. The maths works for any fixed-rate loan repaid monthly; pick your currency symbol at the top. UK student loans are repaid as a share of income instead, so use it there only to see how interest builds.
