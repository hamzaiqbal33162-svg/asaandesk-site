How it works

Pick one of three tools. My payment finds the one number you are missing (monthly payment, time to repay, loan balance or interest rate) from the other three. Pay it off faster follows your loan month by month with your current payment, then again with extra payments, and shows how much sooner you finish and the interest you save; it can also show the saving of paying everything off today. After graduation adds up what you will have borrowed by the time you leave school, the interest that builds up in school and in the grace period, and the monthly payment once repayment starts.

The formula

  • Monthly rate r = yearly rate ÷ 100 ÷ 12; n = number of monthly payments
  • Payment P = B × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n); balance B = P × (1 − (1 + r)^−n) ÷ r
  • Time to repay n = −ln(1 − B × r ÷ P) ÷ ln(1 + r), rounded up to whole months; the rate has no formula and is found by search
  • Faster: each month interest = balance × r, then the payment plus any extra lowers the balance; a yearly extra comes with every 12th payment
  • After school (interest not paid): balance at graduation = B₀(1 + r)^m + a((1 + r)^m − 1) ÷ r, with a = yearly borrowing ÷ 12 added each month and m school months; then × (1 + r)^grace months
  • After school (interest paid): the balance stays at what you borrowed and you pay its interest each month until repayment starts

A worked example

A $30,000 loan at 6.8% for 10 years costs $345.24 a month: r = 0.068 ÷ 12 ≈ 0.005667 and n = 120, so P = 30,000 × 0.005667 ÷ (1 − 1.005667^−120). The 120 payments add up to $41,428.92, so $11,428.92 is interest. Paying $350 a month instead of $500 shows the other side: $350 takes 9 years 10 months, while $500 ($150 extra) clears it in 6 years 2 months and saves $4,421.28 of interest.

Questions

What is a grace period?

It is the time between leaving school and your first payment. For many US federal loans it is six months. Interest usually keeps building during it unless the loan is subsidized.

Should I pay the interest while I am still in school?

If you can, it keeps the loan from growing: unpaid interest is added to the balance and then charges interest of its own. The After graduation tool shows both choices, with the interest in school shown on its own line.

When are the yearly and one-time extra payments made?

The one-time extra goes with your next payment. The yearly extra goes with every 12th payment, so the first one is a year from now.

Does it include loan fees or income-driven plans?

No. It assumes a fixed rate and equal monthly payments. Origination fees, rate changes and income-driven repayment or forgiveness plans change the real cost.

Can I use it for loans outside the US?

Yes. The maths works for any fixed-rate loan repaid monthly; pick your currency symbol at the top.
