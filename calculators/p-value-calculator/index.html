Steps

  1. P(x < Z) = P(x < 2) = 0.97725, read from the standard normal curve
  2. P(x > Z) = 1 − P(x < Z) = 1 − 0.97725 = 0.02275
  3. P(0 < x < Z) = P(x < Z) − 0.5 = 0.47725
  4. P(x < −Z or x > Z) = 2 × P(x > |Z|) = 2 × 0.02275 = 0.0455
  5. P(−Z < x < Z) = 1 − P(x < −Z or x > Z) = 1 − 0.0455 = 0.9545
  • One-tailed tests use the left or right tail; two-tailed tests use the two tails together.

How it works

Type the z-score from your test (for example a z statistic of 2.1) and the page gives the p-value for each kind of test: left-tailed, right-tailed and two-tailed, plus the areas between the mean and Z and between −Z and Z. Each is drawn as a small bell curve with its area shaded. It also works backwards: empty the z-score box, type one p-value, such as 0.05 in the two-tailed box, and you get the z-score that matches it (the critical value) and the other areas. All areas come from the standard normal distribution.

The formula

  • Left tail: p = Φ(z)
  • Right tail: p = 1 − Φ(z)
  • Two tails: p = 2 × (1 − Φ(|z|))
  • From the mean: |Φ(z) − 0.5|; between −z and z: 2Φ(|z|) − 1
  • Backwards: z = Φ⁻¹(p) for a left-tail p, and z = Φ⁻¹(1 − p ÷ 2) for a two-tailed p

A worked example

A test statistic of z = 2 gives a right-tail p-value of 1 − 0.97725 = 0.02275. The two-tailed p-value is twice that, 0.0455, which is below 0.05, so the result is significant at the 5% level in a two-tailed test.

Questions

Should I use the one-tailed or the two-tailed p-value?

Use two-tailed when your test asks whether there is any difference (higher or lower). Use one tail when you only test one direction that you chose before seeing the data: the right tail for “greater than”, the left tail for “less than”.

What z-score matches p = 0.05?

For a two-tailed test it is 1.96 (2.5% in each tail). For a one-tailed test it is 1.6449. Type 0.05 into the matching box to see it.

Why is my p-value so small it shows powers of ten?

Far from the mean the areas get tiny: a z-score of 6 has a right-tail p-value of about 9.87 × 10^-10. The page keeps those digits instead of rounding them to 0.

Does this work for t-tests or chi-square tests?

No, it uses the normal (z) distribution only. For a t statistic with large samples (over about 30 values per group) the normal p-value is close; for small samples use a t-distribution table or tool.
