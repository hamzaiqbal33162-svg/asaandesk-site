What a debt consolidation calculator shows

A debt consolidation calculator tells you whether swapping several debts for one new loan really saves money. Debt consolidation means taking one loan, using it to pay off credit cards, store cards or other loans, and then making a single monthly payment. It can lower your rate and simplify your bills, but a fee or a longer term can quietly cancel the saving.

This calculator puts both choices on the same footing. It follows your current debts month by month as you keep paying what you pay now, then works out one true yearly rate for that stream of payments and another for the new loan with its fee included. The lower true rate is the cheaper way to borrow, and the totals show what each path costs from start to finish.

How to use this calculator, step by step

  • Choose your currency and set Number of debts to combine (up to 20).
  • For each debt, enter a name if you like, the Balance owed, your Monthly payment and the Interest rate (APR). Use 0% for an interest-free debt.
  • Under New loan amount, keep Enough for the debts and the fee to let the calculator size the loan, or pick An amount I choose and type the offer you have.
  • Enter the New loan interest rate and its length in years and months.
  • Enter the Loan fee (origination fee or points) as a percent of the new loan or as an amount. A fee taken from the payout is what makes the true rate higher than the quoted rate.

How to calculate debt consolidation savings: two examples

Default example: the three debts above total $17,500 and take 49 months at $550 a month, with $9,059.78 of interest. A 4-year loan at 11.99% with a 3% fee must be $18,041.24 to leave $17,500 after the fee. It costs $475.01 a month and $4,759.07 of interest, so you pay $3,759.47 less in total and finish one month sooner.

Now stretch the same loan to 6 years. The payment drops to $352.62 and the true rate is still lower than your debts', but the saving shrinks to $1,171.44 because interest runs for 72 months. A lower payment is not the same as a cheaper loan.

Pakistan example in PKR, with example rates: two credit cards of Rs 120,000 at 36% and Rs 60,000 at 40% and a personal loan of Rs 150,000 at 24%, paid with Rs 16,000 a month, clear in 30 months at a true 31.14%. A 3-year loan at 22% with a 2% fee needs to be Rs 336,734.69, costs Rs 12,860.05 a month and has a true rate of 23.50%. It takes 6 months longer, yet you pay Rs 15,605.62 less in total. Use the rates and fees in your own bank's offer, as they differ widely between lenders.

Ways to consolidate debt and their risks

There is more than one way to combine debts into one payment. The US Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) and the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) point out these trade-offs:

  • Debt consolidation loan: a personal loan from a bank, credit union or other lender. The CFPB notes that a lower monthly payment may come only from paying over a longer time.
  • Balance transfer credit card: a card with a low promotional rate. The promotion usually lasts a limited time, a transfer fee usually applies, and new purchases may be charged interest at once. Enter the transfer fee as the loan fee here; the rate rising after the promotion is not modelled.
  • Home equity loan or line of credit: usually cheaper, but the CFPB calls using one for credit card debt risky, because you could lose your home if you cannot repay.
  • Debt management plan through a credit counselling agency: the FTC says such a plan can take 48 months or more, and a reputable counsellor will not charge for help before giving it.
  • Debt settlement: companies that promise to settle for less. The FTC says they cannot collect fees before they settle a debt, and that settlement is likely to damage your credit report and score.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Compare the true rate, not the advertised rate. A 3% fee on a 4-year loan adds well over a percentage point to the cost each year.
  • Keep the term short. Choose the shortest loan whose payment you can afford; that is where the total saving is.
  • Make sure the loan covers everything. If the payout after the fee is smaller than your debts, the results show how much you must add from your own money.
  • Close the gap that caused the debt. Consolidation fails when the cleared cards fill up again, leaving you with the new loan and new balances.
  • Lenders look at your income. Many check your debt-to-income ratio before approving a consolidation loan, so work it out first.
  • Treat results as estimates: real loans can have different payment dates, insurance or other charges.

How it works

List the debts you want to combine with what you owe, what you pay each month and their interest rates, then describe the new loan: its amount (or let the calculator size it to cover the debts and the fee), rate, length and fee. Your current debts are followed month by month as if you keep paying the same total until they are all clear. Both choices are then turned into one true yearly rate that counts every payment and the fee, so they can be compared fairly. A lower true rate for the new loan means it is the cheaper way to borrow the same money.

The formula

  • New loan payment P = L × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n), with r = rate ÷ 100 ÷ 12 and n = months
  • Loan fee = L × fee% (or the amount); cash you get = L − fee
  • Loan that covers everything = what you owe ÷ (1 − fee%) (or what you owe + the fee amount)
  • True rate of the new loan: the monthly rate i with cash you get = P × (1 − (1 + i)^−n) ÷ i; APR = 12 × i
  • Current debts: same monthly total every month, highest rate first, a cleared debt's payment moving on
  • True rate of the current debts: the monthly rate i with Σ payment of month k ÷ (1 + i)^k = what you owe today; APR = 12 × i
  • Cash flow today = cash you get − what you owe (negative: you add the rest yourself)

A worked example

A credit card of $8,000 at 23.99%, a store card of $3,500 at 26.99% and a personal loan of $6,000 at 14.5%, paid with $550 a month, take 49 months and cost a true 22.07% a year, with $9,059.78 of interest. A loan of $18,041.24 at 11.99% for 4 years with a 3% fee ($541.24) pays out exactly the $17,500 owed and costs $475.01 a month. Counting the fee, its true rate is 13.63%, so consolidating saves money: $4,759.07 of interest and $3,759.47 less paid in total.

Questions

How do I calculate debt consolidation savings?

Add up what you would pay keeping your debts at today's payments, and compare it with every payment on the new loan plus any cash you add to cover the debts. The calculator does both and also turns each into a true yearly rate, so you can see the cost per year as well as in total.

Why compare the true rate instead of the interest rate?

A loan fee is taken from the money you receive, so you pay interest on money you never got. The true rate (APR) counts that fee, which makes a loan with a low rate and a big fee comparable with your current debts.

Can consolidating cost more even with a lower rate?

Yes. A longer loan at a lower rate can still add up to more interest than paying your debts off fast. The results show the total you pay both ways, and a note appears when the two point in different directions.

What is a debt consolidation loan and how does it work?

It is one new loan, often a personal loan, whose money pays off your other debts. You then owe only the new lender and make one fixed payment for a set number of months. It helps when its true rate is lower than your debts' and the term is not much longer.

What if the loan does not cover all my debts?

After the fee, a smaller loan leaves part of the debts unpaid. The results show how much you would add from your own money, and the loan amount that would cover everything.

How are my current debts paid in the comparison?

You keep paying the same total each month. Each debt gets its own payment and, when one is cleared, its payment goes to the debt with the highest rate, until all are paid.

Does it work for a balance transfer card?

Roughly. Enter the transfer fee as the loan fee and the card's rate as the loan rate. A 0% promotion that ends later is not modelled, so treat the result as a guide.

How do I consolidate debt into one payment?

Get offers for a consolidation loan or balance transfer, compare each here against your current debts, and pick the one with the lower true rate and a term you can keep up with. Once the loan pays the debts, avoid using the cleared cards again.

Does debt consolidation hurt your credit?

Applying for a new loan or card leads to a credit check, and the effect depends on your lender and credit bureau. Debt settlement is different: the FTC warns it is likely to damage your credit report and score.

Can I use this debt consolidation calculator in Pakistan?

Yes. Choose Rs as the currency and enter balances, payments and yearly rates from your bank statements and loan offer. Rates, fees and processing charges differ between banks, so enter the ones in the offer you actually have.
