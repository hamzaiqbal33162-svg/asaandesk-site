What the mortgage payoff calculator tells you

A mortgage payoff calculator shows when your home loan will be paid off and how much sooner it ends if you pay more. Every dollar you add to a payment goes straight to the balance, so it stops interest from building on that dollar for the rest of the loan. Over a long mortgage that adds up to years and tens of thousands in interest.

This page compares four plans from where your loan stands today: pay extra (every month, once a year, once now, or a mix), pay every two weeks, pay the whole balance off now, or keep paying as you do. It shows the new payoff date, the interest saved and a schedule of both paths.

How to use the mortgage payoff calculator, step by step

  • Choose your currency and how you want to start. I know the years left: enter the Original loan amount, Original term, Interest rate and Time left in years and months; the calculator works out your payment and today's balance.
  • I know my balance and payment: enter the Balance you still owe and your Monthly payment (principal and interest only) from your latest statement, plus the rate. The calculator works out how long is left.
  • Pick How do you want to pay it off?: Pay extra, Pay every two weeks, Pay it all off now or Keep paying as now.
  • For Pay extra, enter Extra every month. Under More options you can add Extra once a year, paid with every 12th payment from now, and a One-time extra now, paid with your next payment.
  • Read the payoff time at the top, then the comparison table, the ways-to-pay table and the yearly and monthly schedules.

How to calculate a mortgage payoff by hand

Three formulas do the work. The monthly payment is M = L × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n), with r the monthly rate. The balance after k payments is B = L × (1 + r)^k − M × ((1 + r)^k − 1) ÷ r. And the mortgage payoff formula for the months left at a payment M is n = −ln(1 − B × r ÷ M) ÷ ln(1 + r).

For the defaults, r = 0.065 ÷ 12 and M = $2,212.24. After 60 payments, B = $327,638.42. With the $300 extra, the payment is $2,512.24, and the months-left formula gives about 226.9 months, so the loan ends with a smaller 227th payment: 18 years 11 months.

If you only know your balance and payment, the same formula tells you the payoff date. A balance of $250,000 at 6.5% with $1,800 a month gives n = −ln(1 − 250,000 × 0.0054167 ÷ 1,800) ÷ ln(1.0054167) = 258.3 months, so 259 payments, or 21 years 7 months. In Excel or Google Sheets, =NPER(6.5%/12, -1800, 250000) returns the same 258.3. Add the extra to the payment, =NPER(6.5%/12, -2100, 250000), to see the new payoff time of 191.6 months, which the calculator shows as 16 years (192 payments, the last one smaller).

Pay off the mortgage early or invest?

Paying early earns a sure return equal to your mortgage rate, with no market risk. If you can clearly earn more elsewhere after tax, investing may come out ahead over long periods, but returns are not guaranteed. You do not have to choose one or the other: a smaller extra payment still saves interest while the rest is invested.

Before you put spare money into the house, keep an emergency fund, take any employer retirement match and clear dearer debts such as credit cards. Money paid into a mortgage is hard to get back without selling or borrowing again. If your rate has dropped since you borrowed, refinancing to a shorter term may save more; compare it with the Refinance Calculator.

Payoff amount, payoff statement and prepayment penalties

Your payoff amount is not quite the balance on your last statement. Interest builds every day until the money arrives, and there may be fees, so lenders quote a payoff amount good until a certain date. In the US, Regulation Z (12 CFR 1026.36) requires the servicer to send an accurate payoff statement within a reasonable time, and no later than seven business days, after your written request. When you sell a home, the loan is normally paid from the sale proceeds at closing, using this statement.

As a rough guide, interest on the default balance of $327,638.42 at 6.5% is about $58 a day if counted on a 365-day year, so a payoff 15 days after a payment date adds about $875. The exact method is in your loan terms.

Check for a prepayment penalty. The CFPB explains that where one exists it usually applies only if you pay off the whole balance within a set number of years, typically three or five, or pay a large amount at once, while small extra principal payments do not normally trigger it.

In Canada, the Financial Consumer Agency of Canada (FCAC) says the penalty for breaking a mortgage is usually the higher of three months' interest or the interest rate differential (IRD), and most lenders let you prepay a set amount each year without penalty, often as a lump sum or a higher payment; unused room usually does not carry over. In Pakistan, read your bank's key fact statement: BankIslami's statement for its Muskun home financing (May 2026), for example, prices the remaining units 5% higher if you settle within the first 12 months.

Tips, common mistakes and related calculators

  • Tell your lender the extra money is for principal, and check that the next statement shows the balance falling by that amount.
  • Use only principal and interest as your payment; tax and insurance paid through escrow do not lower the loan.
  • Start early. The same extra amount saves far more in the first years of a loan than in the last.
  • Ask your lender before paying a large lump sum, in case a penalty or a yearly prepayment limit applies.
  • Keep some cash. A mortgage paid off early does not help in an emergency if all your savings are locked in the house.
  • Remember that results assume your rate stays the same; for a variable rate, run it again when the rate changes.

How it works

Start from where your loan stands today. If you know the original loan, its term and the time left, the calculator works out your monthly payment and the balance you still owe. If you only know your balance and payment, it works out how long is left. It then follows the loan month by month twice: once as you pay now and once with your plan. Each month the interest is the balance times the monthly rate, and the rest of the payment, plus any extra, lowers the balance. The difference between the two runs is the time and interest you save.

The formula

  • Monthly rate r = yearly rate ÷ 100 ÷ 12
  • Monthly payment M = L × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n) for a loan L over n months
  • Balance after k payments B = L × (1 + r)^k − M × ((1 + r)^k − 1) ÷ r
  • Months left when you know B and M: n = −ln(1 − B × r ÷ M) ÷ ln(1 + r), which needs M > B × r
  • Each month: interest = balance × r; balance falls by M − interest + any extra payment
  • Every two weeks: half of M every two weeks is 13 monthly payments a year, counted as M each month plus half of M with every sixth payment

A worked example

The defaults: a $350,000 loan over 30 years at 6.5% costs $2,212.24 a month. With 25 years left, 60 payments have been made and $327,638.42 is still owed. Paying $300 extra each month clears it in 18 years 11 months instead of 25 years, and the interest still to pay drops from $336,033.00 to $242,341.49, a saving of $93,691.52.

Questions

How do I calculate my mortgage payoff date with extra payments?

Add the extra to your monthly payment and use n = −ln(1 − B × r ÷ M) ÷ ln(1 + r) for the months left, or enter your loan and the extra here. On a $327,638.42 balance at 6.5%, raising the payment from $2,212.24 to $2,512.24 brings the payoff from 25 years to 18 years 11 months.

How do I calculate my mortgage payoff from my current balance?

Choose I know my balance and payment, enter the balance and the principal and interest payment from your statement, and the rate. The calculator works out the time left and then the effect of your plan.

What is a mortgage payoff amount?

The exact sum that clears the loan on a given day: the balance plus interest up to that day and any fees. It is a little more than the balance on your last statement, so ask your lender for a payoff quote before you pay.

What is a mortgage payoff statement or letter?

A written quote from your lender or servicer of the amount needed to repay the loan in full by a set date. In the US the servicer must send it within seven business days of a written request.

Is it better to pay extra every month or once a year?

The same yearly amount saves a little more when it is spread over the months, because each extra payment starts cutting interest sooner. The difference is small, so pick what fits your budget. Try both in the calculator and compare the interest saved.

Why does paying every two weeks finish the loan sooner?

Half your payment every two weeks is 26 half payments a year, the same as 13 monthly payments instead of 12. That extra payment each year goes straight to the balance, which can take years off a long mortgage.

Will my lender charge me for paying early?

Many home loans have no prepayment penalty, but some do, especially in the first few years. Check your loan papers or ask your lender, and tell them that extra money should go to the principal.

Where do I find my balance and monthly payment?

Both are on your latest mortgage statement. Use the principal and interest part of the payment only; tax and insurance paid through an escrow account do not lower the loan.

What is the mortgage payoff formula in Excel?

=NPER(rate/12, -payment, balance) gives the months left, for example =NPER(6.5%/12, -1800, 250000) = 258.3. =PMT gives the payment, and =FV(6.5%/12, 60, -2212.24, 350000) returns about −327,638, the balance still owed after 60 payments; the minus sign marks money you owe.

How do I work out my mortgage payoff when selling my home?

Ask your lender for a payoff statement dated near closing. The sale proceeds pay that amount first, then selling costs, and what is left is yours. Use this calculator's balance figure as a first estimate.

Should I pay off my mortgage or invest the money?

Paying early earns you a sure return equal to your mortgage rate. If you can earn clearly more elsewhere after tax, investing may come out ahead, but keep an emergency fund and clear dearer debts such as credit cards first.
