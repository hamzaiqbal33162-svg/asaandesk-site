What a one rep max is and what this calculator does

Your one rep max (1RM), or one repetition maximum, is the heaviest weight you can lift once with proper technique for a given exercise. A 1 rep max bench press of 100 kg means you can press 100 kg once but not 102.5 kg. It is the standard measure of maximal strength in gyms, sports science and school PE.

Lifting your true max is tiring and carries more risk, so most people estimate it instead. This one rep max calculator takes a set you have already done, such as 100 kg for 5 reps, and estimates your 1RM with three formulas: Epley, Brzycki and Lombardi. It then turns the estimate into a training table: the weight you could lift for 1 to 20 reps, and how many reps you should manage at 50% to 100% of your max.

It works for any lift you load with weight, including the bench press, squat, deadlift, overhead press, leg press and weighted pull-ups or dips. Results are estimates for planning training, not a promise of what you will lift on the day.

How to use the one rep max calculator, step by step

  • Choose metric or U.S. units, or pick kg or lb next to Weight lifted. The default is 100 kg (225 lb in U.S. units).
  • Enter the weight of a recent hard set. For barbell lifts include the bar: a 20 kg bar with 30 kg on each side is 80 kg.
  • Enter the Repetitions you completed with good form, from 1 to 10. Do not count a rep you failed or that needed help from a spotter.
  • Read the estimated one rep max at the top, then all three formulas and their average below it.
  • Optional, under More options: choose which formula fills the tables (Epley by default), and show results in the same unit, in kg or in lb, for example if your gym's plates are in kg but your programme is in pounds.
  • Use the two tables to set your training weights, and the bar chart to see how the weight falls as the reps rise.

One rep max formulas: Epley, Brzycki, Lombardi and others

Every formula assumes that each extra rep you can do means the weight is a little further below your max. They differ in how steeply that relationship runs. Epley (1985) adds one thirtieth of the weight for each rep: 1RM = weight × (1 + reps ÷ 30). Brzycki (1993) uses 1RM = weight × 36 ÷ (37 − reps), which treats each rep below 37 as about 2.8% of the max. Lombardi (1989) uses a power curve: 1RM = weight × reps^0.10.

Epley and Brzycki give the same answer at exactly 10 reps (133.3 kg from 100 kg × 10). Below 10 reps Epley is higher, and above 10 Brzycki climbs faster. Lombardi bends more gently, so it is the highest of the three at 2 to 5 reps and the lowest at high reps.

A single rep needs no formula: if you lifted it once, that is your 1RM. Taken literally, Epley would add 3.3% even to a single (weight × 1.033), so the calculator returns the weight you entered whenever you type 1 rep, for every formula.

Researchers have published many more equations. The table shows four that appear in comparison studies, all applied to the same set of 100 kg for 5 reps. This calculator uses the first three.

Which is the best one rep max formula? No single one wins for every lift and every person. A study of untrained students by LeSuer and colleagues (1997) found that all seven equations it tested predicted the bench press and squat closely but underestimated the deadlift. For most people the spread between formulas, here 112.5 to 119.0 kg, is smaller than the day-to-day change in their own strength.

Up to 10 reps the methods agree within about 3 points, which is why the calculator stops at 10. Beyond that they drift apart, and real people vary even more. A 2024 meta-regression by Nuzzo and colleagues, pooling tests from 7,289 people, found slightly more reps at each percentage than the NSCA chart, and clearly more on the leg press than on the bench press; sex, age and training status mattered little.

Use the chart in both directions. A 5-rep max is about 87% of your 1RM, so 100 kg × 5 points to about 115 kg. And 85% of a 116.7 kg max is 99.2 kg, which you should manage for about 5 or 6 reps.

Worked examples: bench press, squat, deadlift and pull-ups

Bench press in pounds. You press 225 lb for 5 reps. Epley: 225 × (1 + 5 ÷ 30) = 262.5 lb. Brzycki: 225 × 36 ÷ 32 = 253.1 lb. Lombardi: 225 × 5^0.1 = 264.3 lb. The average is 260.0 lb (about 117.9 kg). For a strength block at 85% of the Epley figure, you would work with about 223 lb.

Squat in kilograms. A lifter at a gym in Lahore squats 80 kg (a 20 kg bar plus 30 kg a side) for 8 reps. Epley gives 80 × (1 + 8 ÷ 30) = 101.3 kg, Brzycki 80 × 36 ÷ 29 = 99.3 kg and Lombardi 98.5 kg, so a 1RM of about 100 kg. A plan of 4 sets of 6 at 75% would use 75 kg.

Deadlift. You pull 140 kg for 3 reps: Epley 154.0 kg, Brzycki 148.2 kg, Lombardi 156.3 kg. Because studies show the formulas tend to underestimate the deadlift, your true max may be a little higher than these numbers.

Weighted pull-ups or dips. The weight you move is your body plus any added load. A 75 kg person doing 6 pull-ups with a 10 kg belt moves 85 kg; Epley gives 85 × 1.2 = 102 kg, which suggests a single pull-up with about 27 kg added. Treat this as rough, because the formulas were built on barbell lifts.

Using RPE. If you rate a set by reps in reserve (RIR), add those reps before you use the calculator. A set of 100 kg × 5 at RPE 8, which means about 2 reps left, is close to a 7-rep max: enter 7 reps to get an Epley estimate of 123.3 kg.

Overhead press for a woman. A woman presses 40 kg for 8 reps. Epley gives 40 × (1 + 8 ÷ 30) = 50.7 kg, Brzycki 40 × 36 ÷ 29 = 49.7 kg and Lombardi 49.2 kg. The formulas are the same for women and men, so a 1RM of about 50 kg is a fair planning number; 80% of it, 40 kg, is a weight she can already move for about 8 reps, which matches the table.

Leg press. A set of 200 kg for 10 reps gives 266.7 kg with both Epley and Brzycki and 251.8 kg with Lombardi. People can usually do more reps at a given percentage on the leg press than on the bench press, so a 10-rep set there sits at a higher share of the true max than the formulas assume; the real 1RM is often lower than the estimate. Use a heavier set of 3 to 5 reps on machines for a closer figure.

How to test your one rep max safely

A direct 1RM test is the most accurate method, but it should be done only on lifts you can perform with solid technique, with a spotter or safety bars, and when you are rested. The NSCA protocol, summarised by the U.S. military's Human Performance Resources by CHAMP (HPRC), runs like this:

  • Warm up with a light weight for 5 to 10 reps, then rest 1 minute.
  • Add about 5 to 10% (10 to 20 lb) for upper-body lifts or 10 to 20% (30 to 40 lb) for lower-body lifts and do 3 to 5 reps. Rest 2 minutes.
  • Add the same amount again for a near-maximal set of 2 or 3 reps. Rest 2 to 4 minutes.
  • Add the same amount and attempt one rep.
  • If it goes up with good form, rest 2 to 4 minutes, add weight and try again. If it fails, take off 2.5 to 5% (upper body) or 5 to 10% (lower body) and retry after rest.
  • Record the heaviest single you completed with proper technique: that is your 1RM.

Beginners, people returning from injury and anyone with heart disease, high blood pressure or other health conditions should not test a true max without advice from a doctor or qualified coach. For them a set of 5 to 10 reps entered into the calculator gives a useful estimate with far less strain.

In UK schools, the AQA GCSE PE specification names the one rep max test as the measure of maximal strength. The school test follows the same pattern: a warm-up, gradually heavier single lifts with rest in between, and the heaviest correct lift as the score.

Using your one rep max to plan training

Once you know your 1RM, you can set loads for your goal instead of guessing. The NSCA links heavy loads with strength, moderate loads with muscle growth and lighter loads with endurance:

The American College of Sports Medicine's 2026 position stand, the first full update since 2009, reviewed 137 systematic reviews. Its summary is reassuring for ordinary lifters: regular resistance training of every major muscle group at least twice a week matters more than a perfect plan. For strength it points to heavier loads of about 80% of 1RM for 2 to 3 sets per exercise; for muscle size, about 10 hard sets per muscle group a week. Training to failure, machines versus free weights and complex periodisation did not consistently change results for healthy adults.

Here is how the numbers turn into a simple four-week block for someone with the default 116.7 kg estimate: week 1, 3 sets of 8 at 70% (81.7 kg); week 2, 3 sets of 6 at 75% (87.5 kg); week 3, 4 sets of 5 at 80% (93.3 kg); week 4, 3 sets of 3 at 85% (99.2 kg). Then do a fresh hard set, enter it here and set the next block from the new estimate. This is an example of percentage-based planning, not a prescription; a coach can adjust it to your sport and recovery.

A meta-analysis by Schoenfeld and colleagues (2017) found that light loads (60% of 1RM or less) and heavy loads built similar muscle when sets were taken close to failure, but heavy loads gave the biggest gains in maximal strength. The World Health Organization recommends muscle-strengthening activity on at least 2 days a week for all adults.

Accuracy, limits and tips for raising your 1RM

Estimates are most accurate from heavy sets with few reps. In a study of 70 adults, Reynolds, Gordon and Robergs (2006) found that a 5-rep max predicted the 1RM best, and that accuracy fell at higher rep counts; they advised using no more than 10 reps. Expect an estimate to be within about 10% of your true max, and sometimes further off.

Estimates are less reliable for beginners, whose technique and nerve-muscle coordination improve quickly, for very fatigued sets, and for complex lifts such as the power clean, where form breaks down with fatigue. The formulas do not use age, sex or body weight, so they work the same for women and men, and the 2024 meta-regression found little effect of sex on the reps-to-load relationship; the type of exercise matters more. Re-check your estimate with a fresh hard set every few weeks to keep your training weights current.

  • Train each lift regularly; strength is specific to the movement you practise.
  • Increase the load gradually: the ACSM 2009 position stand suggested adding 2 to 10% once you can do one or two reps more than planned in two sessions in a row.
  • Spend some weeks at 80 to 90% of your max for 2 to 5 reps when your goal is a bigger single.
  • Strengthen the supporting muscles: back and shoulder work for the bench press, glutes and hamstrings for the squat and deadlift.
  • Rest at least 2 to 3 minutes between heavy sets, as ACSM advises for heavy multi-joint lifts, and sleep well and eat enough protein to recover.
  • Use a spotter or safety bars for heavy pressing and squatting, and stop a set when form breaks down.

How it works

Enter a weight you lifted with good form and how many full reps you managed, ideally between 2 and 10. Three well-known formulas turn that set into an estimated one rep max (1RM), the most you could lift once. Pick the formula for the tables under More options: one table gives the weight to use for any number of reps, the other the number of reps you should manage at a given share of your max, which is how most training plans are written.

The formula

  • Epley: 1RM = weight × (1 + reps ÷ 30)
  • Brzycki: 1RM = weight × 36 ÷ (37 − reps)
  • Lombardi: 1RM = weight × reps^0.10
  • Weight for n reps (Epley) = 1RM ÷ (1 + n ÷ 30); reps at p% of 1RM (Epley) = 30 × (100 ÷ p − 1), rounded down
  • Weight for n reps (Brzycki) = 1RM × (37 − n) ÷ 36; (Lombardi) = 1RM ÷ n^0.10
  • One rep is the max itself, so 1 rep gives the weight you entered
  • 1 lb = 0.45359237 kg

A worked example

100 kg for 5 reps: Epley gives 100 × (1 + 5 ÷ 30) = 116.7 kg, Brzycki 100 × 36 ÷ 32 = 112.5 kg and Lombardi 100 × 5^0.1 = 117.5 kg. At 80% of the Epley max (93.3 kg) you should manage about 30 × (1 ÷ 0.8 − 1) = 7.5, so 7 full reps.

Questions

What is a one rep max?

It is the heaviest weight you can lift for one full repetition with good technique in a given exercise. It measures maximal strength and is used to set training weights as a percentage, such as 80% of 1RM.

How do I calculate my one rep max?

Do a hard set of 2 to 10 reps, then use a formula. With Epley, multiply the weight by 1 + reps ÷ 30: 100 kg for 5 reps gives 100 × 1.167 = 116.7 kg. This calculator shows Epley, Brzycki and Lombardi side by side.

How do I calculate my one rep max for bench press?

Use a recent bench set, including the bar, and enter the weight and reps. For 225 lb × 5 the formulas give 253 to 264 lb. The bench press is the lift most formulas were tested on, so estimates there are usually close.

How do I calculate my one rep max for squat and deadlift?

The method is the same. For the squat the estimates are usually close; for the deadlift, studies show most formulas underestimate the true max, so your real single may be a few kilograms higher.

Which formula should I use?

For sets of up to about 10 reps the three agree within a few percent. Epley is the most widely used. Brzycki gives slightly lower, more cautious numbers, and Lombardi slightly higher ones at moderate rep counts.

What is the most accurate way to estimate 1RM?

Use a heavy set of about 3 to 5 reps taken close to failure with good form. A 5-rep max predicted the 1RM best in research, and accuracy falls as the reps go up.

Why only up to 10 reps?

The formulas were built from sets of roughly 2 to 10 reps. With more reps, endurance rather than strength decides when you stop, and the estimate becomes unreliable.

How do I use RPE with a one rep max calculator?

On the reps-in-reserve RPE scale, RPE 10 means no reps left, RPE 9 one rep left and RPE 8 two left. Add the reps left to the reps done and enter that number: 5 reps at RPE 8 becomes 7.

Can I calculate a one rep max for pull-ups or dips?

Yes, roughly. Enter your body weight plus any added weight as the weight lifted, then subtract your body weight from the estimate to see how much you could add for a single rep.

What percentage of my max should I train at?

About 85% or more for maximal strength in sets of 6 reps or fewer, 67 to 85% for muscle growth in sets of 6 to 12, and under 67% for endurance. Beginners do well starting with lighter weights they can lift 8 to 12 times.

Is it safe to test my real one rep max?

A true max attempt needs a good warm-up, perfect form and a spotter or safety bars. The estimate lets you train by percentages without lifting your max at all.

Why do the high-rep rows look odd?

Rows above 10 reps are stretched beyond the data behind the formulas, and the Lombardi formula climbs very steeply at light weights. Use those rows as a rough guide only.
