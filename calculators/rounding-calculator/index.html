What rounding off means in maths

Rounding off replaces a number with a simpler one nearby that is easier to read, write or work with. 1,234.5678 rounded to the nearest hundredth is 1,234.57, to the nearest whole number it is 1,235, and to the nearest thousand it is 1,000. The rounded number is less exact, so you choose the place to round to based on how much precision you need.

This rounding calculator rounds to any number of decimal places, to tens, hundreds, thousands or millions, or to a fraction such as the nearest quarter or sixteenth. It also shows how much the rounding changed the number and what each of nine rounding rules would give.

How to use the rounding calculator

  • Number to round: type any number, as long as you like. E-notation such as 1.5e-3 is fine. The page starts with 1234.5678.
  • Round to: choose Whole number (ones), Tenths, Hundredths, Thousandths, 4, 5 or 6 decimal places, Tens, Hundreds, Thousands, Millions, or a fraction from Halves (1/2) to Sixty-fourths (1/64).
  • For any other place, pick Other: type the decimal places and enter a number; a negative number rounds to the left of the point (−4 rounds to the nearest ten thousand).
  • Under More options, choose the Rounding rule. The default is Nearest, halves away from zero, the rule taught in school.
  • Read the Rounded answer and the Change made by rounding. For fractions you also get the answer As a decimal and In lowest terms. The table lists the same number under every rule.

How to round a number, step by step

Find the digit in the place you are rounding to, then look only at the digit just to its right. If that digit is 5 or more, add 1 to the rounding digit; if it is 4 or less, leave the rounding digit as it is. Then drop every digit after the rounding place (or turn the ones before the point into zeros).

  • Nearest tenth: 3.46 has 4 in the tenths place and 6 after it, so it rounds up to 3.5.
  • Nearest hundredth: 0.8349 has 3 in the hundredths place and 4 after it, so it stays 0.83. The 9 further along does not matter.
  • Nearest thousandth: 2.71828 becomes 2.718, since the next digit is 2.
  • Nearest whole number: 17.5 has 5 after the point, so it rounds up to 18.
  • Nearest ten thousand: 46,812 has 4 in the ten-thousands place and 6 after it, so it becomes 50,000.

Only the one digit after the rounding place decides. Rounding in stages, such as 2.449 to 2.45 and then to 2.5, gives the wrong answer; 2.449 to one decimal place is 2.4.

Rounding rules for exact halves

Rules only differ when a number is exactly half way, such as 2.5 between 2 and 3, or when you ask to always round up or down. The usual school rule sends halves away from zero. Banker's rounding (half to even) sends them to the even neighbour, so over many numbers half go up and half go down and totals stay fair; it is the default in IEEE 754 computer arithmetic.

Rounding money to the nearest cent, rupee or dollar

Money is usually rounded to the smallest unit in use. A 17.5% charge on 349.99 is 61.24825; to the nearest cent that is 61.25, and to the nearest whole rupee or dollar it is 61. Choose Hundredths for cents and Whole number for whole rupees or dollars.

When you round each line of a bill and then add, the total can differ by a cent or a rupee from rounding the exact total once. If two totals disagree, check whether the lines or only the final total were rounded.

Rounding to fractions and significant figures

Fractions. Tape measures and drill bits work in halves, quarters, eighths and sixteenths of an inch. To round 2.3 in to the nearest sixteenth: 2.3 × 16 = 36.8 sixteenths, which rounds to 37, so the answer is 37/16 = 2 5/16 in (2.3125 in). The calculator does this for halves to sixty-fourths and also gives the lowest terms.

Significant figures. Rounding to significant figures counts digits from the first non-zero one, so it suits very large and very small numbers. 0.004567 to 2 significant figures is 0.0046, and 45,678 to 2 significant figures is 46,000. To do it here, choose the place where your last significant digit falls: 4 decimal places for 0.0046, Thousands for 46,000.

Rounding formulas in Excel and Google Sheets

  • =ROUND(A1, 2) rounds to 2 decimal places, halves away from zero, just like the default rule here.
  • =ROUND(A1, 0) gives the nearest whole number, =ROUND(A1, -1) the nearest 10 and =ROUND(A1, -2) the nearest 100.
  • =ROUNDUP(A1, 2) always rounds away from zero and =ROUNDDOWN(A1, 2) always toward zero.
  • =MROUND(A1, 0.25) rounds to the nearest multiple, here the nearest quarter.

Rounding error

Rounding error is the difference between the rounded and the exact value, shown here as the Change made by rounding. For one number it is at most half a step, but errors can add up. Three shares of one third each are 33.33% after rounding, and together they make 99.99%, not 100%. Keep full precision while you calculate and round only the final answer.

How it works

Type a number and choose what to round to: decimal places, tens, hundreds and so on, or a fraction such as the nearest quarter. The usual rule rounds to the nearer neighbour and sends exact halves away from zero (2.5 becomes 3, −2.5 becomes −3). Other rules, such as banker's rounding, are under More options, and the table shows the answer under all nine rules at once. The number is handled as exact decimal digits, so ties such as 1.005 are true ties.

The formula

  • Step q = 10^(−places), or 1/n for a fraction. Count how many steps fit: k = x ÷ q
  • Round k to a whole number by the rule, then the answer = k × q
  • Exact halves: nearest and half away from zero go away from zero; half up goes toward +∞; half down toward −∞; half to even / odd picks the even / odd neighbour; half toward zero goes toward zero
  • Always up (ceiling) and always down (floor) move every value that is not already on a step

A worked example

1234.5678 to hundredths: 1234.5678 ÷ 0.01 = 123456.78 steps, which rounds to 123457, so the answer is 1234.57. To the nearest quarter, 2.3 ÷ 0.25 = 9.2 quarters, which rounds to 9, giving 2 1/4.

Questions

How do I round to the nearest tenth?

Look at the hundredths digit, the second after the point. If it is 5 or more, add 1 to the tenths digit; otherwise keep it. So 6.37 becomes 6.4 and 6.32 becomes 6.3.

How do I round to the nearest hundredth?

Look at the third digit after the point. If it is 5 or more, round the second digit up. 0.8349 becomes 0.83 and 4.276 becomes 4.28.

How do I round to the nearest whole number?

Look at the first digit after the point: 5 or more rounds up, 4 or less rounds down. 17.5 becomes 18 and 17.49 becomes 17.

How do I round money to the nearest cent or rupee?

For cents, round to two decimal places (Hundredths): 61.24825 becomes 61.25. For whole rupees or dollars, round to a whole number: 61.25 becomes 61.

Can this calculator round to significant figures?

Not by count, but you can get the same answer by picking the place of the last significant digit. 45,678 to 2 significant figures is the nearest thousand, 46,000.

What is the rounding formula in Excel?

=ROUND(number, digits). Use 2 for two decimal places, 0 for a whole number and −2 for the nearest hundred. ROUNDUP, ROUNDDOWN and MROUND cover the other cases.

What is banker's rounding?

Half to even: an exact half goes to the even neighbour, so 2.5 becomes 2 and 3.5 becomes 4. Over many numbers the halves go up and down equally often, which keeps totals fair. Choose it under More options.

Why does 2.999 round to 3.00 and not 3?

Rounding to hundredths means the answer has two decimal places, and the zeros show that precision. 3.00 and 3 are the same number.

Why is 2.5 to the nearest quarter shown as 2 2/4?

The answer is given in the steps you chose, quarters here. Its lowest terms, 2 1/2, are shown just below.

How do I round to tens, hundreds or thousands?

Pick Tens, Hundreds, Thousands or Millions in the Round to menu. For other positions choose the last option and type a negative number of places: −2 rounds to the nearest 100.

What is rounding error?

The gap between the rounded and the exact number, shown as Change made by rounding. Rounding many numbers and then adding them can make the errors pile up, so round at the end.
