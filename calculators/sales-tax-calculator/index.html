How it works

Choose what you know. Add tax: type the price and the rate to get the tax and the total. Take tax out: type a total that already includes tax and the rate to get the price before tax. Find the rate: type both prices. For the rate you can type your own or pick a US state, which fills in the state's base rate, and then add your city or county rate on top. Results update as you type.

The formula

  • Tax = price before tax × rate ÷ 100; price with tax = price before tax + tax
  • Price before tax = price with tax ÷ (1 + rate ÷ 100)
  • Rate = (price with tax − price before tax) ÷ price before tax × 100

A worked example

A $250 bill that includes New York City's 8.875% tax: price before tax = 250 ÷ 1.08875 = $229.62, so the tax is $20.38.

Questions

How do I take sales tax out of a total?

Divide the total by 1 plus the rate. At 8%, a $54 total is $54 ÷ 1.08 = $50 before tax. Taking 8% off $54 instead would give $49.68, which is wrong, because the tax was worked out on the lower price.

Why is the rate at the shop higher than the state rate?

Most states let cities, counties and special districts add their own sales tax. The state rate in the list is only the base; add your local rate in the box below it. Your state's tax department has a lookup by address for the exact total.

Which states have no sales tax?

Delaware, Montana, New Hampshire and Oregon have no general sales tax, and Alaska has no state sales tax, though many Alaskan towns charge their own.

Can I use it for VAT or GST?

Yes, the maths is the same for any tax added on top of a price. Pick your currency symbol at the top and type your own rate.
