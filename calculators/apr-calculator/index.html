What is APR, and what does this APR calculator work out?

APR, the annual percentage rate, is the yearly cost of a loan with its fees included. The interest rate only prices the money you borrow; the APR also counts what you pay to get the loan, such as an origination fee, discount points or required mortgage insurance. Because those costs come out of your pocket while you still repay the full loan, the APR is usually higher than the interest rate.

This APR calculator finds that real rate for any loan, from a car loan or personal loan to a mortgage, and shows the payment, the total interest and every cost added up. In the United States, the Truth in Lending Act and its Regulation Z make lenders disclose the APR so that offers can be compared on the same basis; the calculator uses the same idea, the actuarial method, that the regulation describes.

How to use the APR calculator

  • Pick a tab. Any loan covers car loans, personal loans, student loans and business loans; Home loan adds a down payment, points and mortgage insurance.
  • Any loan: enter the Loan amount, the Years and Months, and the Interest rate in % a year. Put cash fees under Fees paid up front, and fees the lender adds to what you owe under Fees added to the loan.
  • Open Compounding and payment frequency if your loan is not monthly. Interest can compound yearly, twice a year, quarterly, monthly, twice a month, every two weeks, weekly, daily or continuously, and payments can be made every day up to once a year.
  • Home loan: enter the Home price, the Down payment as a % of the price, the Loan term in years, the Interest rate, the Lender fees (origination, underwriting and similar), the Discount points as a % of the loan, and Mortgage insurance (PMI) per year if you put down less than 20%.
  • Choose your currency at the top. The result updates as you type, and the payment schedule opens below it.

A home loan. Take a 350,000 home with 10% down, so the loan is 315,000 for 30 years at 6.75%, with 3,500 of lender fees and 1 discount point (3,150). The payment is 2,043.08 and the APR is 6.960%. Add mortgage insurance of 1,260 a year and the APR rises to 7.211%, because the insurance is charged for 97 months, until the balance falls below 280,000 (80% of the price).

Buying points to lower the rate is a trade-off you can test. With 3 points (9,450) for a 6.25% rate instead of 1 point at 6.75%, the payment falls to 1,939.51 and the APR to 6.895%, but you pay 6,300 more at closing. If you expect to sell or refinance within a few years, the lower payment may never pay back the extra points.

What the APR includes, and what it leaves out

Under Regulation Z (12 CFR 1026.4), the finance charge behind the APR includes interest, points, loan and finder's fees, service and transaction charges, appraisal, investigation and credit report fees, and insurance premiums that protect the lender against default, such as mortgage insurance. For loans secured by real estate, some bona fide and reasonable fees are left out: title examination and title insurance, surveys, preparing the loan documents, notary and credit report fees, pre-closing appraisals and inspections, and money placed in escrow for taxes and insurance.

So two mortgages with the same APR can still have different closing costs. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau also warns that an adjustable-rate mortgage's APR does not show the highest rate the loan could reach, that the APR of a home equity line of credit does not include fees the way a closed-end loan's APR does, and that you should not judge a loan by its APR alone.

Where the APR can mislead: short loans, early payoff and variable rates

  • The same fee weighs more on a short loan. A 2,000 fee on a 200,000 loan at 7% gives an APR of 7.680% over 3 years but only 7.100% over 30 years, because it is spread over far fewer payments.
  • The APR assumes you keep the loan to the end. A 300,000 mortgage at 6.5% over 30 years with 6,000 of fees has an APR of 6.695%, but if you sell and repay it after 5 years, the true yearly cost works out to about 6.989%. If you may move or refinance early, favour the loan with the lower up-front fees.
  • A variable-rate APR is only a starting estimate. It is worked out from today's rate and will change when the rate resets, so compare the margin, the reset dates and the caps as well.
  • Disclosed APRs are allowed a small tolerance. Regulation Z treats an APR as accurate if it is within one eighth of a percentage point of the exact figure for a regular loan, or one quarter of a point for an irregular one, so tiny differences between lenders may mean nothing.

APR, APY and effective rates: credit cards, savings and UK loans

In the US, an APR is a nominal rate: the rate per month times 12. It does not include the effect of compounding. The annual percentage yield (APY) on savings does: Regulation DD defines it as the total interest paid over a 365-day period, based on the rate and how often it compounds. That is why banks show APR on loans and APY on deposits, and why an APY looks higher than the same nominal rate. The effective annual rate is (1 + APR ÷ 12)^12 − 1 for monthly compounding.

Credit cards. A card's APR is turned into a daily periodic rate, which the CFPB says is the APR divided by 365 or by 360, depending on the card issuer. At a 24% APR that is about 0.0658% a day. Interest is usually charged only when you do not pay the full balance by the due date, so a card has no single loan APR to calculate here; use the credit card calculator for that.

United Kingdom. A UK APR is an effective annual rate: the Consumer Credit (Total Charge for Credit) Regulations 2010 measure time in years and fractions of a year, so compounding is built in. To compare a UK quote with the result here, use effective rate = (1 + APR here ÷ 1,200)^12 − 1, the same conversion as the table. A UK representative APR in an advert is one the lender expects at least 51% of customers responding to it to get, under FCA rules that were under review in 2026.

APR and mark-up rates on loans in Pakistan

Pakistani banks usually quote a mark-up rate, and many price it as KIBOR (the Karachi Interbank Offered Rate) plus a spread. Allied Bank's car finance page (2026), for example, sets the rate at 1-year KIBOR plus a fixed spread and resets it every year. State Bank of Pakistan rules (BC&CPD Circular No. 2 of 2016) require banks to give a Key Fact Statement before a personal, housing or auto loan is paid out, and that is where to find the fees and charges to enter here.

Example: a Rs 2,000,000 personal loan over 3 years at an assumed 18% mark-up, with a Rs 20,000 processing fee paid up front. Choose Pakistani rupee, enter the loan on the Any loan tab and put 20,000 under Fees paid up front. The installment is Rs 72,304.79 and the real APR is 18.726%. Double the fee to Rs 40,000 and the APR becomes 19.462%. Shown as an effective yearly rate, the first case is about 20.42%. On a KIBOR-linked loan the figure holds only until the next reset.

Tips for comparing loan offers, and related calculators

  • Compare APRs only for loans of the same type and length: a 3-year APR and a 30-year APR are not a fair match.
  • Ask each lender for a written list of fees, and enter all of them; a low rate with high fees can cost more than a higher rate with none.
  • Check how long you will really keep the loan before paying for points.
  • Look at the total in All payments and fees as well as the APR, especially when fees are added to the loan.

How it works

The interest rate a lender quotes leaves out the fees you pay to get the loan. The APR puts them back in: it is the one yearly rate at which your payments are worth exactly the cash you really receive. On the Any loan tab, type the amount, the length in years and months, the rate and the fees (paid up front or added to the loan); under Compounding and payment frequency you can change how often interest compounds and how often you pay. On the Home loan tab, type the price, down payment, term, rate, lender fees, discount points and any mortgage insurance; the insurance is counted for the months you owe more than 80% of the price.

The formula

  • Rate per payment i = (1 + j ÷ m)^(m ÷ p) − 1, with j the yearly rate, m compoundings and p payments a year (continuous: e^(j ÷ p) − 1)
  • Payment P = F × i ÷ (1 − (1 + i)^−n), with F = loan + fees added to it and n payments
  • Find the rate a with P × (1 − (1 + a)^−n) ÷ a = loan − fees paid up front (the cash you get)
  • APR = ((1 + a)^(p ÷ 12) − 1) × 1200, a yearly rate compounded monthly (1200 × a for monthly payments)
  • Home loan: cash you get = loan − lender fees − points; each month's flow = payment + PMI ÷ 12 while the balance is above 80% of the price; APR = 1200 × the monthly rate that fits

A worked example

A 15,000 personal loan for 3 years at 9% has a monthly payment of 477.00. With a 600 fee paid up front you really receive 14,400, so the APR is the monthly rate a that makes 36 payments of 477.00 worth 14,400: a ≈ 0.9843%, an APR of 11.812%. The fee adds almost 2.8 percentage points to the 9% rate.

Questions

How do I calculate APR on a loan?

Work out the payment from the loan amount, rate and term, then find the rate at which those payments are worth exactly the cash you receive after fees. That rate per month times 12 is the APR. The calculator does this search for you and shows it to three decimals.

Why is the APR higher than the interest rate?

Fees and points are paid when the loan starts, so you get less cash than you owe while you still pay interest on the full amount. Spread over the loan, that extra cost works like a higher rate.

Should fees be paid up front or added to the loan?

Paying in cash gives a slightly higher APR, because you receive less money on day one. Adding the fee to the loan gives a slightly lower APR but a higher total cost, because you pay interest on the fee too. Compare the All payments and fees line as well as the APR.

Why does a short loan with the same fee have a higher APR?

The fee is spread over fewer payments, so it adds more per year. The same 2,000 fee raises a 3-year loan's APR far more than a 30-year loan's.

How do I use this as an APR calculator for a car loan?

Use the Any loan tab: enter the amount you finance after your down payment, the term and the rate, and put dealer or lender fees under the fee boxes. For trade-in, sales tax and rebates, work out the amount first with the auto loan calculator.

What is the APR formula?

There is no single closed formula, because the APR is the rate that solves cash received = payment × (1 − (1 + a)^−n) ÷ a. It is found by trial and error, then multiplied by 12 for monthly payments. The interest rate itself only sets the payment.

How do I calculate APR per month?

Divide the APR by 12. A 12% APR is 1% a month, which is the rate charged on the balance each month on a monthly loan. Over a full year that 1% a month compounds to about 12.68%.

What is APR on a credit card?

It is the yearly rate used to charge interest on balances you carry past the due date. The issuer turns it into a daily rate by dividing by 365 or 360. If you pay the full statement balance on time, most cards with a grace period charge no purchase interest.

What is the difference between APR and APY?

APR is a yearly rate without compounding, used for loans. APY includes compounding over a year and is used for savings and deposits, so it is higher than the same nominal rate. A 12% rate compounded monthly is a 12.68% APY.

What are points?

Each discount point is 1% of the loan paid at closing, usually to get a lower interest rate. Here they count as a cost of the loan, both in the APR and in the total.

Is mortgage insurance part of the APR?

Yes. US rules count insurance that protects the lender against default as a finance charge. The calculator includes it for the months your balance is above 80% of the home price.

How is APR different in the UK?

A UK APR is an effective yearly rate that already includes compounding, while a US-style APR is the monthly rate times 12. To compare, convert the result here with (1 + APR ÷ 1,200)^12 − 1.
