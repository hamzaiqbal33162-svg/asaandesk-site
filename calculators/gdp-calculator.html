What GDP is and what this GDP calculator does

Gross domestic product (GDP) is the market value of all final goods and services produced inside a country during a period, usually a quarter or a year. It is the most widely used measure of the size of an economy, and its change from one year to the next is what news reports call economic growth.

This GDP calculator works out GDP from its parts in two ways. The expenditure approach adds up what is spent on final output: GDP = C + I + G + (X − M). The income approach adds up the incomes earned producing it, passing through national income and gross national product (GNP) on the way. Both are standard in economics courses, from class 10 and class 12 to university macroeconomics, and in a complete set of national accounts they give the same total.

"Final" matters. Flour sold to a bakery is an intermediate good, and only the bread the customer buys counts, otherwise the flour would be counted twice. Second-hand sales, shares and other financial assets are not new production either, so they stay out of GDP.

How to use the calculator

  • Pick a tab: Expenditure approach or Income approach.
  • Expenditure approach: type Personal consumption (C), Gross private investment (I), Government spending (G), Exports (X) and Imports (M). Investment may be negative when businesses run down their stocks.
  • Income approach: fill in the five income boxes from Employee compensation down to Net interest, then Indirect business taxes, Depreciation and, last, the Net income of foreigners. Every box except compensation and depreciation accepts a minus sign for losses or net outflows.
  • Choose the currency from the currency menu, and under More options set Amounts are in to units, thousands, millions, billions (the default) or trillions. This is only a label: type the figures exactly as your table gives them.
  • Leave a box empty if you have no figure for it. It is counted as 0 and the result lists which boxes were blank, so you can check nothing was missed.

Understanding the results

On the Expenditure approach tab the headline is Gross domestic product (GDP). Below it you get Net exports (X − M) and the Consumption share of GDP, and the table What makes up GDP shows every part with its share in percent. Imports appear as a negative share, which is how they reduce the total.

On the Income approach tab the headline is GDP again, with National income, Gross national product (GNP) and Net income of foreigners listed under it. The table From income to GDP walks from the five income items to national income, adds indirect business taxes and depreciation to reach GNP, then adds the net income of foreigners. The working under each result writes out the same sums with your numbers.

Worked examples: expenditure and income approach

Expenditure approach. Take an economy with consumption of 21,600, investment of 5,400, government spending of 6,300, exports of 4,200 and imports of 5,100, all in billions. GDP = 21,600 + 5,400 + 6,300 + (4,200 − 5,100) = 32,400 billion. Net exports are −900 billion, a trade deficit, and consumption is 66.7% of GDP, investment 16.7% and government 19.4%.

Income approach. Suppose employee compensation is 9,800, proprietors' income 1,200, rental income 650, corporate profits 2,100 and net interest 450. National income is 14,200. Adding indirect business taxes of 1,250 and depreciation of 2,400 gives GNP of 17,850. Residents earn more abroad than foreigners earn here, so the net income of foreigners is −150 and GDP is 17,850 − 150 = 17,700.

A textbook exercise with a gap. In rupees, with consumption 640, investment left blank, government spending 150, exports 95 and imports 120 (millions), the calculator gives GDP of Rs 765 million and notes that gross investment was counted as 0. If the question does give investment, add it and the total rises by the same amount.

Real GDP, nominal GDP, the GDP deflator and growth rate

Nominal (current-price) GDP is measured at the prices of the period itself. Real GDP removes the effect of price changes so that years can be compared; the US Bureau of Economic Analysis calls these chained-dollar figures. If GDP rises 8% while prices rise 5%, most of the increase is inflation, not extra output.

The GDP deflator links the two: deflator = nominal GDP ÷ real GDP × 100. So real GDP = nominal GDP ÷ deflator × 100. With nominal GDP of 32,400 and a deflator of 120, real GDP is 32,400 ÷ 120 × 100 = 27,000.

The growth rate compares real GDP with the year before: growth = (this year − last year) ÷ last year × 100. If real GDP was 26,200 last year and 27,000 this year, growth is 800 ÷ 26,200 × 100 = 3.05%. GDP per capita divides GDP by the population: an economy producing 32,400 billion dollars with 330 million people has GDP per capita of about $98,182. For comparing living standards between countries, economists often convert at purchasing power parity (PPP), which allows for different price levels.

How GDP is calculated in Pakistan

Pakistan's GDP is compiled by the Pakistan Bureau of Statistics (PBS) under the UN System of National Accounts 2008, with 2015-16 as the base year. PBS uses a combination of the production, income and expenditure approaches, but the production approach (value added in agriculture, industry and services) is the base of the annual estimates. Figures are for the fiscal year from July to June and are approved by the National Accounts Committee, then revised as fuller data arrive.

At its 118th meeting on 19 October 2026, the committee put growth for fiscal year 2025-26 at 3.65% (revised from 3.70%), the size of the economy at US$450.4 billion and per capita income at US$1,896. These are official provisional numbers that PBS may revise again, so check its national accounts page for the latest release before quoting them.

What GDP does not measure, and common mistakes

  • Unpaid work is left out: cooking, cleaning and caring at home, and volunteer work, add no GDP although they have real value. Black-market activity is also excluded, as the IMF notes.
  • GDP is not well-being. It says nothing about how income is shared, about health or about damage to the environment.
  • GDP and GNP differ by income crossing borders. A German-owned factory in the United States adds to US GDP but to German GNP. For countries with many workers abroad, the gap can be large.
  • Do not add transfers such as pensions to G; the money is counted when households spend it.
  • Keep signs right: imports are typed as positive numbers and subtracted by the formula, while a net outflow of income in the income approach is typed with a minus sign.
  • Use one unit throughout. Mixing millions and billions in the same calculation is the most common slip in homework answers.

How it works

GDP is the value of everything produced inside a country in a period, usually a year. It can be measured two ways that should give the same answer. The expenditure approach adds up who buys the output: households (consumption), businesses (investment), the government, and foreigners (exports), then takes away imports because they were produced abroad. The income approach adds up the incomes earned making it: pay, the income of the self-employed, rent, company profits and interest give national income; adding indirect business taxes and depreciation gives gross national product (GNP), and adding the net income of foreigners turns that into GDP. Pick a tab, type the figures from your textbook or statistics office, and choose the currency symbol and, under More options, whether the amounts are in millions, billions or trillions. A box left blank counts as 0 and the result says which ones were blank.

The formula

  • Expenditure approach: GDP = C + I + G + (X − M)
  • National income = compensation of employees + proprietors' income + rental income + corporate profits + net interest
  • GNP = national income + indirect business taxes + depreciation
  • Income approach: GDP = GNP + net income of foreigners (income foreigners earn in the country − income residents earn abroad)

A worked example

With consumption 21,600, investment 5,400, government spending 6,300, exports 4,200 and imports 5,100 (billions), GDP = 21,600 + 5,400 + 6,300 + (4,200 − 5,100) = 32,400 billion. Net exports are −900 because the country buys more from abroad than it sells, and consumption makes up 66.7% of GDP.

Questions

How do I calculate GDP using the expenditure approach?

Add consumption, investment and government spending, then add exports and subtract imports: GDP = C + I + G + (X − M). With 21,600 + 5,400 + 6,300 + (4,200 − 5,100) the answer is 32,400.

How do I calculate GDP using the income approach?

Add employee compensation, proprietors' income, rent, corporate profits and net interest to get national income. Add indirect business taxes and depreciation for GNP, then add the net income of foreigners for GDP.

Why are imports subtracted from GDP?

Consumption, investment and government spending include things made abroad. GDP counts only what is produced inside the country, so the imported part is taken out again, which is why net exports (X − M) appear in the formula.

What is the difference between GDP and GNP?

GDP counts production inside the country, whoever earns the income. GNP counts the income of the country's residents, wherever they earn it. GDP = GNP + income foreigners earn in the country − income residents earn abroad.

How do I calculate GDP per capita?

Divide GDP by the population for the same year, using the same currency and units. GDP of $32,400 billion shared by 330 million people is about $98,182 per person.

How do I calculate the GDP deflator?

Divide nominal GDP by real GDP and multiply by 100. A deflator of 120 means prices are 20% higher than in the base year.

How do I calculate the GDP growth rate?

Subtract last year's real GDP from this year's, divide by last year's and multiply by 100. Use real, not nominal, GDP so that inflation does not look like growth.

What is the GDP of Pakistan?

The Pakistan Bureau of Statistics put the economy at US$450.4 billion for fiscal year 2025-26, with growth of 3.65% and per capita income of US$1,896 (National Accounts Committee, October 2026). These figures are provisional and get revised.

Can net exports or GDP parts be negative?

Yes. Net exports are negative when imports are larger than exports, and investment can be negative when businesses run down their stocks. Profits and the net income of foreigners can be negative too, so those boxes accept a minus sign.

Do government transfers such as pensions count in G?

No. G is what the government spends on goods, services and investment. Pensions and benefits only move money to households, who then spend it, so it shows up in consumption instead.
