How it works

GDP is the value of everything produced inside a country in a period, usually a year. It can be measured two ways that should give the same answer. The expenditure approach adds up who buys the output: households (consumption), businesses (investment), the government, and foreigners (exports), then takes away imports because they were produced abroad. The income approach adds up the incomes earned making it: pay, the income of the self-employed, rent, company profits and interest give national income; adding indirect business taxes and depreciation gives gross national product (GNP), and adding the net income of foreigners turns that into GDP. Pick a tab, type the figures from your textbook or statistics office, and choose the currency symbol and, under More options, whether the amounts are in millions, billions or trillions. A box left blank counts as 0 and the result says which ones were blank.

The formula

  • Expenditure approach: GDP = C + I + G + (X − M)
  • National income = employee compensation + proprietors' income + rental income + corporate profits + net interest
  • GNP = national income + indirect business taxes + depreciation
  • Income approach: GDP = GNP + net income of foreigners (income foreigners earn in the country − income residents earn abroad)

A worked example

With consumption 14,000, investment 3,500, government spending 3,800, exports 2,500 and imports 3,000 (billions), GDP = 14,000 + 3,500 + 3,800 + (2,500 − 3,000) = 20,800 billion. Net exports are −500 because the country buys more from abroad than it sells.

Questions

Why are imports subtracted from GDP?

Consumption, investment and government spending include things made abroad. GDP counts only what is produced inside the country, so the imported part is taken out again, which is why net exports (X − M) appear in the formula.

What is the difference between GDP and GNP?

GDP counts production inside the country, whoever earns the income. GNP counts the income of the country's residents, wherever they earn it. GDP = GNP + income foreigners earn in the country − income residents earn abroad.

Can net exports or GDP parts be negative?

Yes. Net exports are negative when imports are larger than exports, and investment can be negative when businesses run down their stocks. Profits and the net income of foreigners can be negative too, so those boxes accept a minus sign.

Do government transfers such as pensions count in G?

No. G is what the government spends on goods, services and investment. Pensions and benefits only move money to households, who then spend it, so it shows up in consumption instead.
