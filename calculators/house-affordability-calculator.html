How it works

Lenders cap two ratios. The front-end ratio is your monthly housing cost (loan payment, mortgage insurance, property tax, home insurance and HOA) divided by your gross monthly income. The back-end ratio adds your other debt payments. Conventional loans usually stop at 28% and 36%, FHA loans at 31% and 43%, and VA loans look at the back-end 41% only. The calculator takes the lower of the two limits as the most you can spend on housing each month, then finds the home price whose monthly costs add up to exactly that, with your down payment. Mortgage insurance follows the loan type: PMI on a conventional loan with less than 20% down, HUD's annual premium on an FHA loan (its upfront premium is paid at closing), and the one-time VA funding fee, which is added to the loan. In the budget mode you name the monthly amount yourself. Upkeep and closing costs are shown so you see the full cost, but lenders leave them out of the ratios.

The formula

  • Monthly income I = yearly income ÷ 12
  • Housing limit H = the lower of front% × I and back% × I − other debts (VA and your own limit: back-end only)
  • Loan L = price − down payment; payment = L × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n), r = rate ÷ 12, n = years × 12
  • Solve price P: payment + mortgage insurance + (tax + insurance + HOA) ÷ 12 = H; every part is linear in P, so P = (H − fixed costs + payment factor × down) ÷ (payment factor + costs per dollar of price)
  • PMI = 0.5% of the loan a year with under 20% down; FHA annual premium from HUD's table; VA fee = table rate × loan, added to the loan
  • Cash at closing = down payment + closing costs (+ FHA upfront premium 1.75% of the loan)

A worked example

With $120,000 a year ($10,000 a month), no other debts, 20% down, a 30-year loan at 7.282%, property tax 1.5% and insurance 0.5% of the price, the 28% front-end limit allows $2,800 a month. The price that costs exactly that is $392,077: a $313,662 loan at $2,147 a month plus $490 tax and $163 insurance.

Questions

What is the 28/36 rule?

A guide many conventional lenders use: spend no more than 28% of your gross monthly income on housing (payment, insurance, tax and HOA) and no more than 36% on all debts together, housing included. The lower of the two decides what you can borrow.

Why can I afford more with an FHA loan?

FHA allows higher ratios, 31% for housing and 43% for all debts, and needs only 3.5% down. You pay for it with mortgage insurance: 1.75% of the loan upfront and an annual premium, usually 0.55% with under 5% down.

Is the VA funding fee a monthly cost?

No. It is a one-time fee, a share of the loan that depends on your down payment and whether you used a VA loan before. Most borrowers add it to the loan, which raises the monthly payment a little. Here it is added to the loan unless you choose to pay it at closing.

Which number should I trust, the income rule or my own budget?

The income rule shows roughly what a lender may approve. Your own budget shows what feels comfortable after savings and other spending. Try both modes; buying below the lender's maximum leaves room for repairs and surprises.

What else do I need cash for?

Besides the down payment, closing costs of about 2% to 5% of the price, any upfront mortgage insurance, moving costs and a cash reserve. The result shows the cash needed at closing.
