How it works

Type your income before tax and each debt payment you make, choosing for each box whether the amount is per month or per year. Everything is turned into monthly amounts and added up. Your debt-to-income ratio is all your monthly debt payments divided by your monthly income; the front-end ratio counts only housing costs (rent or mortgage, property tax, home insurance and HOA fees). The rating uses the usual lender bands, and the house estimate shows the largest new home payment the common 28% and 36% rules leave room for, and the price it could buy.

The formula

  • Monthly amount = yearly amount ÷ 12 for every box set to "a year"
  • Debt-to-income (back-end) = all monthly debt payments ÷ monthly income × 100
  • Front-end = monthly housing costs ÷ monthly income × 100
  • Rating: 36% or less good, up to 50% mid-range, 50% or more high
  • New home payment = the smaller of 28% of income and 36% of income − your other (non-housing) debt payments
  • Home price P from that payment: (1 − down%) × P × f + tax and insurance% × P ÷ 12 = payment, with f the monthly payment of 1 borrowed over the loan term

A worked example

A $60,000 salary is $5,000 a month. Rent of $1,200, card payments of $200 and a $250 car payment add up to $1,650, so the ratio is 1,650 ÷ 5,000 = 33% (good) and the front-end ratio is 1,200 ÷ 5,000 = 24%. If the rent stopped, a lender could allow the smaller of 28% × 5,000 = $1,400 and 36% × 5,000 − 450 = $1,350 for a new home payment.

Questions

What is a good debt-to-income ratio?

Most lenders like to see 36% or less, with no more than about 28% going to housing. Up to around 43% to 50% you may still qualify for some loans, often at a higher cost. Above 50% most lenders will say no.

What counts as debt here?

Payments you are bound to make: rent or mortgage, property tax and home insurance, HOA fees, minimum card payments, car and student loans, personal loans and support payments. Food, utilities, phone bills and other living costs are not counted.

Should I use income before or after tax?

Before tax. Lenders work out the ratio from your gross monthly income.

What is the difference between front-end and back-end?

Front-end counts only your housing costs. Back-end counts every debt payment, housing included; that is the number usually meant by "your DTI".

How is the home price estimate worked out?

It assumes your current rent or mortgage stops, takes the largest payment the 28% and 36% rules allow, and finds the price whose loan payment plus property tax and insurance fits it. Change the rate, term, down payment and tax and insurance share under House estimate.
