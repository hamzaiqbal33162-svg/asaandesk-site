Month by month

How it works

Enter what you start with, what you add each month (and each year under More options), the yearly interest rate with how often it compounds, and how long the money stays in. The calculator turns the yearly rate into the matching monthly rate and follows the balance month by month: each month's deposit goes in (at the start or the end, as you choose), the month's interest is added, and any tax on that interest is taken out. The final balance, the total interest and the value in today's money come from that month-by-month record.

The formula

  • Monthly rate j = (1 + r ÷ m)^(m ÷ 12) − 1, where r is the yearly rate and m the times it compounds a year (daily = 365.25); continuous: j = e^(r ÷ 12) − 1
  • Each month: balance = balance + deposit (start timing), then interest = balance × j, tax = interest × tax rate, balance = balance + interest − tax (end timing adds the deposit after the interest)
  • A yearly deposit goes in at the start of each year, or at the end of each full year with end timing
  • Interest on the starting amount = start × ((1 + j)^months − 1); the rest of the interest came from the deposits
  • Value in today's money = final balance ÷ (1 + inflation)^years
  • APY = (1 + r ÷ m)^m − 1 (continuous: e^r − 1)

A worked example

$20,000 plus $5,000 at the start of every year, at 5% compounded yearly for 5 years: the monthly rate is 1.05^(1/12) − 1 ≈ 0.4074%, so the first month earns $25,000 × 0.4074% = $101.85. After 5 years the balance is $54,535.20: $45,000 put in and $9,535.20 of interest. With 3% inflation that is worth $54,535.20 ÷ 1.03^5 = $47,042.54 today.

Questions

What is the difference between simple and compound interest?

Simple interest is paid only on the money you put in. Compound interest is also paid on the interest already earned, so the balance grows faster every year. Over 30 years at 6%, $10,000 earns $18,000 of simple interest but about $47,000 of yearly compound interest.

Does it matter how often the interest compounds?

A little. The more often interest is added, the sooner it starts earning interest itself. 5% compounded monthly grows like 5.116% added once a year; daily or continuous compounding adds only a few hundredths of a percent more. The APY line shows this for the rate you entered.

Should deposits go in at the start or the end of the month?

Money that goes in at the start of a month earns that month's interest, so start timing gives a slightly higher balance. Pick the one that matches when your money actually arrives, for example payday at the end of the month.

What does 'in today's money' mean?

Prices rise over time, so the same amount buys less in the future. This figure divides the final balance by the price rise you expect (the inflation rate, 3% a year unless you change it) to show what the balance would buy at today's prices. Set inflation to 0 to hide it.

How is tax on interest handled?

If you enter a tax rate, that share of each month's interest is taken out at once, so only the interest left after tax keeps growing. Accounts where tax is paid once a year come out a little higher than this.
