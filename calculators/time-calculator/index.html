What this time calculator does

This time calculator adds and subtracts time in days, hours, minutes and seconds, the way you would on paper but without the carrying mistakes. Put in 3 hours 45 minutes plus 2 hours 30 minutes and you get 6 hours 15 minutes, along with the same answer as 6.25 hours, 375 minutes and 22,500 seconds.

It has three tabs. Add or subtract times works with two lengths of time. Add to a date and time moves a clock time forward or back and tells you the new date, time and weekday. Type a calculation takes a whole sum in one line, such as a week of work shifts. All three turn every amount into seconds first, do the sum, and then split the answer back into days, hours, minutes and seconds.

How to use each tab

  • Add or subtract times: fill in Days, Hours, Minutes and Seconds for the first time and the second time. Empty boxes count as 0, and decimals such as 1.5 hours are fine. Choose + Add or − Subtract under "Then".
  • Add to a date and time: pick the Start date, type the Start time (like 8:30, 8:30:15 or 20:30) and choose AM, PM or 24-hour. Choose add or subtract, then enter the amount of time in days, hours, minutes and seconds.
  • Type a calculation: write the sum with d for days, h for hours, m for minutes and s for seconds, joined with + or −, for example 2h 30m + 1h 45m − 50m. You can also use w for weeks, whole words such as 2 hours 30 minutes, and h:mm terms such as 1:30 for 1 hour 30 minutes.
  • Read the answer at the top, then the same answer in days, hours, minutes and seconds underneath. The working steps show how every part was turned into seconds, so you can check the sum yourself.

Understanding the results

On the two time tabs the big answer is written in words, such as 1 day 16 hours, and the rows under it give the total in each single unit. Both are the same amount: 1 day 16 hours is 40 hours, 2,400 minutes or 144,000 seconds. Use the words for reading and the single-unit totals for payroll, invoices or a spreadsheet.

If you take away more time than you started with, the answer has one minus sign in front, such as −1 hour 15 minutes, and the table splits it into signed days, hours, minutes and seconds.

On the date tab the answer is a date and clock time with its weekday. The rows show the same time on the other clock (12-hour or 24-hour), the starting moment, the time added or taken away, and how many calendar days apart the two dates are. A small calendar marks both days.

Time does not count in tens. A minute has 60 seconds, an hour 60 minutes and a day 24 hours, which is why 90 minutes is 1 hour 30 minutes and not 0.9 hours. These are the conversions the calculator uses.

Work hours, payroll and Excel

Timesheets and pay slips usually want hours as a decimal, so 7 hours 45 minutes becomes 7.75 hours. Divide the minutes by 60 to convert. A common slip is to write 7 hours 45 minutes as 7.45 hours, which pays 18 minutes less than the time worked.

To total a week of shifts, open Type a calculation and enter something like 8h15m + 7h50m + 8h + 9h10m + 6h45m. The answer shows as 1 day 16 hours, and the In hours row gives 40 hours. At a rate of PKR 850 an hour that week is worth 40 × 850 = PKR 34,000.

For one shift with a lunch break, you can type the clock times as h:mm durations from midnight, as long as the shift starts and ends on the same day: 17:30 − 9:00 − 0:45 gives 7 hours 45 minutes, or 7.75 hours. For a full time card with several days and breaks, the Time Card Calculator is quicker.

Adding time to a date and clock time

The date tab answers questions such as "what time will it be 14 hours 45 minutes after 8:30 PM tonight?" Start on Monday, November 23, 2026 at 8:30 PM, add 14 hours 45 minutes, and the answer is Tuesday, November 24, 2026 at 11:15 AM, which is 11:15 on the 24-hour clock.

It works the same for deadlines. A courier that promises delivery within 72 hours of 3:15 PM on Friday, December 4, 2026 has until Monday, December 7, 2026 at 3:15 PM. Enter 3 days or 72 hours; both give the same moment.

The tab counts plain clock time. It does not know about time zones or daylight saving changes, so a flight from Karachi to London is worked out in the departure city's clock. To change the answer to another city's local time, use the Time Zone Calculator.

AM, PM, noon and midnight

AM and PM come from the Latin ante meridiem and post meridiem, before and after noon. NIST points out that noon itself is neither, so 12 a.m. and 12 p.m. are ambiguous, and it writes "noon" and "midnight" instead when it matters. NIST also notes that a digital clock should switch to p.m. the moment it reaches 12:00 at noon. This calculator follows that convention: 12:00 PM is noon and 12:00 AM is midnight at the start of the day.

If you choose 24-hour, type times from 0:00 to 23:59 and leave AM and PM aside. The result also shows the time on the other clock, which helps when a ticket uses one style and your phone the other.

Typing a calculation: what it understands

  • Units: w (weeks), d (days), h (hours), m (minutes) and s (seconds), such as 1d 2h 3m 4s. m always means minutes, never months.
  • Words: hours, minutes, seconds and the like, such as 2 hours 30 minutes + 1 week.
  • Clock style: 1:30 means 1 hour 30 minutes and 1:30:15 adds 15 seconds.
  • Signs: join the terms with + or −. Multiplying and dividing are not supported, so for 7 hours 30 minutes on each of 5 days, either type the term five times or convert to decimal hours first: 7.5 × 5 = 37.5 hours, or 37 hours 30 minutes.
  • Speed, distance and time: to find a travel time, divide the distance by the speed. 150 km at 60 km/h takes 150 ÷ 60 = 2.5 hours, or 2 hours 30 minutes. The Speed Calculator does this for you.

From sundials to the atomic second

About five thousand years ago the Sumerians split the day into 12 periods, each about two of our hours, and each period into 30 parts of about four minutes. Egyptian obelisks from as early as 3500 BCE worked as giant sundials, and by around 1500 BCE the Egyptians used a portable shadow clock that split daylight into 10 parts plus two twilight hours.

Water clocks measured time at night and on cloudy days. One of the oldest was found in the tomb of the pharaoh Amenhotep I, buried around 1500 BCE, and the Greeks used them from about 325 BCE. Weight-driven mechanical clocks appeared in the towers of Italian cities in the first half of the 14th century, and Peter Henlein of Nuremberg made spring-powered clocks between 1500 and 1510.

Every answer here rests on the second. Since 1967 the second has been defined by the cesium atom: an atomic clock counts 9,192,631,770 cycles of the microwave radiation that cesium atoms absorb and emit, and that interval is one second. Before that, the second was tied to the movement of the Earth.

Because the Earth's spin is slightly irregular and, over long periods, slowing down, the world's official time, Coordinated Universal Time (UTC), has been nudged now and then with a leap second to stay within 0.9 seconds of time by the Earth's rotation. Leap seconds have always been added at the end of June or December, so a clock can read 23:59:60. In 2022 the General Conference on Weights and Measures decided that the allowed gap will be increased in, or before, 2035, which in practice means far fewer leap seconds. This calculator, like everyday clocks, treats every day as exactly 86,400 seconds.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Do not mix decimal hours and minutes: 7.5 hours is 7 hours 30 minutes, not 7 hours 50 minutes.
  • In typed sums, m is minutes. Write a month as days, or use the Date Calculator.
  • Leave a box empty rather than typing a space or a dash; empty boxes count as 0.
  • For trips across time zones or on the night clocks change, work out the time here and then adjust it for the local clock.
  • If a sum goes below zero, the minus sign is on the whole answer: −1 hour 15 minutes means 75 minutes short.

How it works

Pick a tab. To add or subtract two lengths of time, fill in the days, hours, minutes and seconds you have (empty boxes count as 0) and choose add or subtract. To move a date and clock time forward or back, give the start date and time and the amount to add or take away; the result shows the new date, clock time and weekday. To work out a longer sum, type it in one line, such as 1d 2h 30m + 45m − 2h. Every result is turned into seconds first, worked out, and then split back into days, hours, minutes and seconds, so 90 minutes becomes 1 hour 30 minutes and 30 hours becomes 1 day 6 hours.

The formula

  • Seconds = days × 86,400 + hours × 3,600 + minutes × 60 + seconds
  • Result = first time ± second time (in seconds), then split back: days = whole part of S ÷ 86,400, hours = whole part of the rest ÷ 3,600, and so on
  • Total in days = S ÷ 86,400, in hours = S ÷ 3,600, in minutes = S ÷ 60
  • Date and time: new moment = start date and time ± the amount in seconds, on the calendar clock (12 AM is midnight, 12 PM is noon)

A worked example

1 day 5 hours 40 minutes 30 seconds plus 20 hours 30 minutes 45 seconds: the first is 106,230 seconds and the second 73,845 seconds, so the total is 180,675 seconds. That is 2 days (172,800 s), 2 hours (7,200 s), 11 minutes (660 s) and 15 seconds, or 50.1875 hours.

Questions

How do I add hours and minutes together?

Put the first time in the top row and the second in the bottom row, for example 3 hours 45 minutes and 2 hours 30 minutes. The answer, 6 hours 15 minutes, is shown at once, together with the same time in decimal hours (6.25), minutes and seconds.

How do I calculate hours and minutes for work?

Type each day's hours in one line on the Type a calculation tab, such as 8h15m + 7h50m + 8h + 9h10m + 6h45m. The In hours row gives the total as a decimal, here 40 hours, ready to multiply by an hourly rate.

How do I convert minutes to decimal hours?

Divide the minutes by 60. So 15 minutes is 0.25 hours, 45 minutes is 0.75 hours and 50 minutes is about 0.83 hours. The calculator shows every answer in decimal hours as well.

How do I subtract a lunch break from a shift?

On the Type a calculation tab, write the end time minus the start time minus the break, such as 17:30 − 9:00 − 0:45. The answer is 7 hours 45 minutes, or 7.75 hours. This works when the shift starts and ends on the same day.

What happens when the answer is negative?

If you take away more time than you started with, the result is shown with one minus sign in front, such as −1 hour 15 minutes, meaning one hour and fifteen minutes less than nothing. The table splits the same answer into signed days, hours, minutes and seconds.

What can I type in a calculation?

Numbers followed by w (weeks), d (days), h (hours), m (minutes) or s (seconds), such as 1d 2h 3m 4s, joined with + or −. Words such as hours or minutes and clock style terms such as 1:30 (1 hour 30 minutes) work too. m always means minutes, not months.

Can the time calculator multiply or divide time?

No, it adds and subtracts. To multiply, convert to decimal hours first: 7 hours 30 minutes × 5 is 7.5 × 5 = 37.5 hours. To divide, do the same, then turn the decimal part back into minutes by multiplying it by 60.

Is 12 PM noon or midnight?

In this calculator 12:00 PM is noon and 12:00 AM is midnight at the start of the day, the digital-clock convention NIST describes. NIST also calls 12 a.m. and 12 p.m. ambiguous, so write noon or midnight on anything important.

How many seconds are in a day?

A day of 24 hours has 86,400 seconds: 24 × 60 × 60. A week has 604,800 seconds. The calculator uses these values and does not add leap seconds.

How do I calculate a time difference in Excel?

Subtract the earlier time cell from the later one and multiply by 24 to get hours, by 1,440 for minutes or by 86,400 for seconds. Excel's time formats roll over after 24 hours, so use the decimal result for long totals.

Does adding time to a date count daylight saving time?

No. The calculator counts plain clock time: adding 24 hours always lands on the same clock time the next day. On the night clocks change in your area, the real elapsed time differs by an hour.

Can it convert Pakistan time to USA or UK time?

Not on this page, which works in one clock. Work out the time here, then use the Time Zone Calculator to see the same moment in another country's local time.
