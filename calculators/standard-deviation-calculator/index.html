What standard deviation means, in simple words

Standard deviation is the typical distance between the numbers in a list and their mean. A small standard deviation says the numbers sit close together; a large one says they are scattered. This standard deviation calculator works it out for any list you paste, with every step shown.

Two lists can share the same average and still look nothing alike. Marks of 69, 70 and 71 and marks of 40, 70 and 100 both average 70, but the first list barely moves while the second runs across the whole scale. The standard deviation puts that difference into one number, in the same units as your data: marks, kilograms, rupees or seconds.

How to use the standard deviation calculator

  • Numbers: type or paste your data. Commas, spaces, tabs, semicolons and new lines all separate values, so a column copied from Excel or Google Sheets works as it is.
  • Do not use thousands separators inside a number (type 1250, not 1,250), because a comma starts a new value.
  • Your numbers are: choose A sample when the list is only part of the group you care about, or The whole population when it holds every member. A sample is the default because it is the usual case.
  • The answer updates as you type. The steps show the mean, the sum of squared differences, the variance, the standard deviation and the standard error, each with the actual numbers.
  • A sample needs at least two numbers. With one number and The whole population chosen, the standard deviation is 0, because a single value has no spread.

Sample or population: which formula to use

The two formulas differ in one place: the population version divides the sum of squared differences by N, the sample version by N − 1. A sample tends to sit closer to its own mean than to the mean of the whole group, so dividing by N would understate the spread. Using N − 1, known as Bessel's correction, makes up for that.

How to calculate standard deviation by hand

Take the five numbers 4, 8, 6, 5 and 7. Working from the mean, the method is the same for any list:

  • Find the mean: (4 + 8 + 6 + 5 + 7) ÷ 5 = 30 ÷ 5 = 6.
  • Subtract the mean from each number: −2, 2, 0, −1, 1.
  • Square each difference so negatives do not cancel positives: 4, 4, 0, 1, 1.
  • Add the squares: 4 + 4 + 0 + 1 + 1 = 10. This is the sum of squared differences.
  • Divide: 10 ÷ (5 − 1) = 2.5 for a sample, or 10 ÷ 5 = 2 for a population. This is the variance.
  • Take the square root: √2.5 = 1.5811 (sample) or √2 = 1.4142 (population).

Paste 4, 8, 6, 5, 7 into the box to see the same steps with all digits. Squaring is also why the variance is in squared units (marks², PKR²), while the standard deviation is back in the original units and easier to read.

Reading standard deviation with the normal curve

When data follows a roughly bell-shaped (normal) curve, the standard deviation tells you where most values fall. This is the 68–95–99.7 rule.

The height figures are an illustration, not a survey result. The rule only holds for data that is close to normal; incomes, waiting times and many prices are skewed, so check a histogram before relying on it. To see how unusual one value is, the Z-Score Calculator divides its distance from the mean by the standard deviation.

To compare spread between lists with different sizes or units, divide the standard deviation by the mean. This is the coefficient of variation, called the relative standard deviation (RSD) in chemistry labs. Five titration readings of 10.02, 10.05, 9.98, 10.01 and 10.04 mL have a sample SD of 0.0274 mL and an RSD of 0.0274 ÷ 10.02 × 100 = 0.27%.

Worked examples: exam marks in Pakistan and share prices

Exam marks: two sections of Class 9 in a Lahore school both average 70 out of 100. Section A scored 62, 66, 68, 70, 72, 74, 78, 70 and Section B scored 41, 52, 61, 70, 79, 88, 99, 70. As samples, Section A has a standard deviation of 4.90 marks and Section B 18.87 marks. The averages match, but Section B mixes students who are struggling with students near full marks, so it needs more varied teaching. Its coefficient of variation is about 27%, against 7% for Section A.

Share prices: investors use the standard deviation of returns as a measure of volatility. Suppose one share's daily changes over a week are 0.5%, −0.3%, 0.4%, 0.2% and −0.1%, while another's are 3.0%, −2.5%, 1.8%, −1.2% and −0.4%. Both average a 0.14% gain a day, but the sample standard deviation is 0.34% for the first and 2.24% for the second, so the second share's price swings far more. The same idea applies to shares on the Pakistan Stock Exchange or any other market. Past volatility does not predict future returns, and five days is far too short for a real decision.

Standard deviation in Excel, Google Sheets, Casio and TI-84

  • Excel: =STDEV.S(A2:A50) for a sample and =STDEV.P(A2:A50) for a population. The older STDEV and STDEVP do the same. Empty cells and text inside the range are ignored.
  • Google Sheets: =STDEV(range) gives the sample standard deviation and =STDEVP(range) the population one; STDEV.S and STDEV.P are accepted too.
  • TI-83 and TI-84: enter the list, then STAT, CALC, 1-Var Stats. Sx is the sample standard deviation and σx the population one.
  • Casio scientific calculators in statistics (STAT or SD) mode show the same pair: sx is the sample and σx the population standard deviation.
  • Grouped data or a frequency table: either type each value as often as it occurs (5, 5, 5 for a value seen three times), or use class midpoints for ranges such as 10–20. Midpoints give an estimate, because the exact values inside each class are unknown.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing population for survey or test data that is really a sample. If in doubt, use sample.
  • Mixing up standard deviation and standard error: the first describes single values, the second the precision of the mean, and it falls as N grows.
  • Reporting the variance as if it were in the original units. Take the square root first.
  • Typing 1,250 with a thousands comma, which the page reads as two numbers, 1 and 250.
  • Comparing the spread of data in different units, such as kg and cm, without using the coefficient of variation.
  • Reading too much into a standard deviation from very few values, which can change a lot with one more number.

How it works

Type or paste your numbers, separated by commas, spaces or new lines, and choose whether they are a sample from a bigger group or the whole group (the population). The result shows how spread out the numbers are: the standard deviation, the variance (the standard deviation squared), the mean they spread around, and the standard error of the mean, which tells you how far the mean of a sample of this size is likely to be from the true mean. The margin-of-error table turns that standard error into ranges at eight confidence levels, and the frequency table counts how often each number appears.

The formula

  • Mean: x̄ = Σx ÷ N
  • Sample variance: s² = Σ(x − x̄)² ÷ (N − 1); population variance: σ² = Σ(x − μ)² ÷ N
  • Standard deviation = √variance
  • Standard error of the mean = standard deviation ÷ √N
  • Margin of error = z × standard error, with z = 1, 1.645, 1.96, 2.576, 3.291, 3.891, 4.417 and 4.892 for 68.3%, 90%, 95%, 99%, 99.9%, 99.99%, 99.999% and 99.9999% confidence

A worked example

For 10, 12, 23, 23, 16, 23, 21, 16 the sum is 144, so the mean is 144 ÷ 8 = 18. The squared distances from 18 are 64, 36, 25, 25, 4, 25, 9 and 4, which add up to 192. As a sample, the variance is 192 ÷ 7 = 27.428571428571 and the standard deviation is √27.428571428571 = 5.2372293656638. As the whole population it is 192 ÷ 8 = 24, so √24 = 4.8989794855664.

Questions

How do I calculate standard deviation?

Find the mean, subtract it from each number, square the differences and add them up. Divide by N − 1 for a sample or N for a population, then take the square root. The steps on this page show each of those numbers for your data.

What is the standard deviation formula for sample and population?

Sample: s = √(Σ(x − x̄)² ÷ (N − 1)). Population: σ = √(Σ(x − μ)² ÷ N). Only the divisor differs.

Should I choose sample or population?

Choose sample when your numbers are only some of the group you care about, such as 30 customers out of thousands; this is the usual case. Choose population when you have every member, such as the marks of all 25 students in one class.

Why does a sample divide by N − 1?

A sample's numbers sit a little closer to their own mean than to the true mean of the whole group, so dividing by N would make the spread look too small. Dividing by one less makes up for that (this is called Bessel's correction).

What is the difference between standard deviation and variance?

The variance is the average squared distance from the mean, and the standard deviation is its square root. The standard deviation is easier to read because it is in the same units as your data.

What is the difference between standard deviation and standard error?

The standard deviation describes how far single numbers spread from the mean. The standard error is the standard deviation divided by √N and describes how much the mean itself would move from one sample to the next, so it shrinks as you collect more numbers.

What is a good or high standard deviation?

There is no fixed cut-off; it depends on the scale of the data. Compare it with the mean using the coefficient of variation: an SD of 5 is small for values around 1,000 but large for values around 10.

How do I calculate standard deviation in Excel?

Use =STDEV.S(range) for a sample or =STDEV.P(range) for a whole population. You can also copy the column and paste it here to check the result and see the steps.

Can I paste numbers from a spreadsheet?

Yes. Copy a column or a row and paste it into the box: new lines, tabs, spaces, commas and semicolons all work as separators. Leave out thousands separators inside numbers (type 1250, not 1,250), because a comma starts a new number.

How do I read the margin-of-error table?

Each row gives a range around the mean, mean ± z × standard error. At 95% confidence, if you repeated the sampling many times, about 95 out of 100 such ranges would contain the true mean. It assumes the sample means follow a normal curve, which holds well for larger samples.

How do I find the standard deviation of grouped data?

Type each value as many times as it occurs, or use the midpoint of each class repeated by its frequency. With class midpoints the result is an estimate, since the exact values inside each class are not known.
