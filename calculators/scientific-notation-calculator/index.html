What scientific notation is

Scientific notation is a short way to write very large and very small numbers as a number between 1 and 10 times a power of ten: a × 10ⁿ. The speed of light, 299,792,458 m/s, becomes 2.99792458 × 10^8, and 0.0072 becomes 7.2 × 10^-3. The power n says how many places the decimal point has moved: positive for big numbers, negative for numbers below 1. In Cambridge O Level and British textbooks the same form is called standard form.

This scientific notation calculator converts any number into scientific notation, E-notation, engineering notation and back to a plain real number, and it adds, subtracts, multiplies, divides, squares, square-roots and raises numbers in scientific notation. The arithmetic is exact, digit for digit, so even very long answers are not rounded behind your back, and the steps show how the powers of ten combine.

Understanding the results

  • Scientific notation: the answer as a × 10ⁿ with one non-zero digit before the point.
  • As a real number: the same value written out in full with thousands separators, 45,000,000 or 0.000345. Numbers too long to print say how many digits they have.
  • E-notation: the computer form, where e7 means × 10^7, so 4.5e7.
  • Engineering notation: the power of ten is a multiple of 3, so the number part runs from 1 to 999: 45 × 10^6, 345 × 10^-6.
  • In Calculate, X and Y are repeated in scientific notation so you can check what was read, and the steps show the rule used. Notes say when a result was cut, or when a power was worked out with logarithms (to about 15 significant digits) because Y was not a small whole number.

How to convert to and from scientific notation

To scientific notation: move the decimal point until exactly one non-zero digit is in front of it, and count the places moved. Moving left (for big numbers) gives a positive power; moving right (for small numbers) gives a negative one. For 0.000345 the calculator's step reads: move the decimal point 4 places to the right so one digit (not 0) is in front of it; the power of ten is −4, so 0.000345 = 3.45 × 10^-4.

Back to a real number (standard decimal form): move the point the other way. A positive power moves it right, adding zeros as needed (2.5 × 10^8 = 250,000,000); a negative power moves it left (7.2 × 10^-3 = 0.0072).

Multiplying, dividing, adding and subtracting in scientific notation

  • Multiplication: multiply the number parts and add the powers. 2.5 × 10^6 × 4 × 10^-3 = (2.5 × 4) × 10^(6 + (−3)) = 10 × 10^3, then move the point to get 1 × 10^4 = 10,000. Likewise 6.4 × 10^3 × 5 × 10^4 = 32 × 10^7 = 3.2 × 10^8.
  • Division: divide the number parts and subtract the powers. 6 × 10^8 ÷ 1.5 × 10^-3 = (6 ÷ 1.5) × 10^(8 − (−3)) = 4 × 10^11.
  • Addition and subtraction: first write both numbers with the same power of ten, usually the larger one. For 4.2 × 10^5 + 3.1 × 10^3, write the second as 0.031 × 10^5; the sum is 4.231 × 10^5 = 423,100, and the difference is 4.169 × 10^5 = 416,900.
  • Powers: raise the number part and multiply the power. (2 × 10^3)³ = 2³ × 10^9 = 8 × 10^9, and (3 × 10^4)² = 9 × 10^8.
  • Square roots: make the power even first, then halve it. √(9 × 10^10) = 3 × 10^5. For an odd power, borrow one: 1.6 × 10^7 = 16 × 10^6, so its root is 4 × 10^3.
  • After any step, if the number part has drifted outside 1 to 10 (like 10 × 10^3 or 0.25 × 10^5), move the point and adjust the power to match.

A real example: sunlight takes 1.495978707 × 10^11 m ÷ 2.99792458 × 10^8 m/s ≈ 4.99004783 × 10^2 s to reach Earth, about 499 seconds or 8 minutes 19 seconds. The distance is the astronomical unit and the speed is the speed of light, both exact by definition.

Engineering notation and SI prefixes

Engineering notation keeps the power of ten a multiple of 3, so each power matches an SI prefix and can be read straight off as a unit: 345 × 10^-6 m is 345 micrometres, and 45 × 10^6 W is 45 megawatts.

Scientific notation in physics and chemistry

Science uses scientific notation because its numbers span so many powers of ten. Since 2019 the SI has been defined by exact constants that are easiest to read this way: the speed of light c = 2.99792458 × 10^8 m/s, the Planck constant h = 6.62607015 × 10^-34 J s, the elementary charge e = 1.602176634 × 10^-19 C, the Boltzmann constant k = 1.380649 × 10^-23 J/K and the Avogadro constant N_A = 6.02214076 × 10^23 per mole.

Written out, the elementary charge is 0.0000000000000000001602176634 C, which the converter confirms, and the Avogadro constant is 602,214,076,000,000,000,000,000. Typing such numbers in full invites errors; scientific notation also makes the precision visible, since every digit in the number part is significant. Writing 2.50 × 10^3 says the value is known to three significant figures, while 2,500 leaves that unclear.

For school, on calculators and in Excel, and common mistakes

Standard form is section 1.8 of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics syllabus (4024, exams in 2025 to 2027): use A × 10ⁿ with 1 ≤ A < 10 and n a positive or negative whole number, convert numbers into and out of standard form, and calculate with values in standard form. Paper 1 allows no calculator, so the multiply-and-add-the-powers rules above matter.

Scientific calculators and spreadsheets show the same thing in E-notation: 4.5E+07 in Excel and 4.5e7 here both mean 4.5 × 10^7. In Excel, the Scientific number format displays a cell this way, and typing 4.5E7 into a cell enters 45,000,000.

  • Leaving the number part outside 1 to 10: 45 × 10^6 is correct engineering notation but not scientific notation; it should be 4.5 × 10^7.
  • Getting the sign of the power backwards: numbers below 1 have negative powers.
  • Adding the number parts without matching the powers first.
  • Multiplying the powers when you multiply numbers: 10^3 × 10^4 = 10^7, not 10^12.

How it works

Scientific notation writes a number as a × 10ⁿ, with exactly one digit (not 0) before the point: 45,000,000 is 4.5 × 10^7 and 0.000345 is 3.45 × 10^-4. In Convert, type a number in any common form and see it in scientific notation, E-notation (4.5e7, used by computers and spreadsheets), engineering notation (the power is a multiple of 3, matching kilo, mega, micro …) and as a plain real number. In Calculate, give X and Y as a number part and a power of ten and pick the operation. Every digit is worked out exactly; answers that never end (like 1 ÷ 3) are cut, not rounded, after the number of digits you choose.

The formula

  • x = a × 10ⁿ with 1 ≤ |a| < 10, n = the number of places the point moves
  • Engineering: x = b × 10ᵐ with m a multiple of 3 and 1 ≤ |b| < 1000
  • (a × 10ᵐ) × (b × 10ⁿ) = (a × b) × 10^(m+n), then move the point
  • (a × 10ᵐ) ÷ (b × 10ⁿ) = (a ÷ b) × 10^(m−n)
  • Adding or subtracting: first write both with the same power of ten
  • √(a × 10ⁿ) = √a × 10^(n/2) for an even n

A worked example

X = 2.5 × 10^6 and Y = 4 × 10^-3. X × Y = (2.5 × 4) × 10^(6 + (−3)) = 10 × 10^3, and moving the point gives 1 × 10^4 = 10,000. For X + Y with X = 4.2 × 10^5 and Y = 3.1 × 10^3, write Y as 0.031 × 10^5; the sum is 4.231 × 10^5 = 423,100.

Questions

How do I calculate scientific notation?

Move the decimal point until one non-zero digit is in front of it, then write × 10 to the number of places you moved: positive if you moved left, negative if you moved right. 52,000 becomes 5.2 × 10^4 and 0.0061 becomes 6.1 × 10^-3. Type the number into Convert to check.

How do I multiply numbers in scientific notation?

Multiply the number parts, add the powers of ten, then tidy up. (3 × 10^4) × (5 × 10^2) = 15 × 10^6 = 1.5 × 10^7. Choose X × Y in Calculate and the steps show both stages.

How do I divide numbers in scientific notation?

Divide the number parts and subtract the power of the bottom number from the top one. (8 × 10^6) ÷ (2 × 10^2) = 4 × 10^4. If the number part comes out below 1, move the point right and lower the power by one.

How do I add numbers in scientific notation?

Rewrite one of them so both have the same power of ten, add the number parts, then tidy up. 5.0 × 10^4 + 3.0 × 10^3 = 5.0 × 10^4 + 0.3 × 10^4 = 5.3 × 10^4.

How do I convert scientific notation to a real number?

Move the point right by the power if it is positive, adding zeros as needed, or left if it is negative. 3.6 × 10^5 = 360,000 and 3.6 × 10^-5 = 0.000036. The converter shows the real number for any input.

Is standard form the same as scientific notation?

In Cambridge O Level and British textbooks, yes: standard form means A × 10ⁿ with 1 ≤ A < 10. In some American textbooks, "standard form" instead means a number written out in full, like 360,000, so check which one your question means.

What is the difference between scientific and engineering notation?

Scientific notation keeps one digit before the point (3.45 × 10^-4). Engineering notation lets the number part be from 1 to 999 so that the power of ten is a multiple of 3 (345 × 10^-6), which matches unit prefixes: 345 × 10^-6 metres is 345 micrometres.

What does 4.5e7 mean?

It is E-notation, the way calculators, spreadsheets and programming languages write scientific notation: e7 means × 10^7, so 4.5e7 = 4.5 × 10^7 = 45,000,000. A negative power works the same way: 3.45e-4 = 0.000345.

Why is my answer cut off?

Some answers never end, such as 1 ÷ 3 = 0.333… or the square root of 2. They are cut (not rounded) after the number of digits after the point you choose under More options (20 at first). Answers smaller than 1 keep that many digits after their leading zeros, so very small results never turn into 0.

Can I type a number like 3.5 × 10^-12?

Yes. The converter understands 3.5x10^-12, 3.5*10^-12, 3.5 × 10^(-12), 3.5e-12 and 3.5E-12, and also plain numbers with or without thousands separators.
