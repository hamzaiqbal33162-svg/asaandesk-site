How it works

Pick what you want to work out, then fill in the other two. Pace is the time you need for one mile or one kilometre; speed is the same thing turned around (distance per hour, minute or second). From your pace the calculator lists the time for common race distances and the time at every whole kilometre and mile. The Checkpoint splits tab takes the total distance and total time at up to 12 points along a run and shows how fast you went between them. The converter turns any pace or speed into the other units, and the Finish time tab assumes you keep your pace so far to the end.

The formula

  • Pace (seconds per unit) = time in seconds ÷ distance
  • Time = distance × pace; distance = time ÷ pace (or speed × time)
  • Speed = distance ÷ time, e.g. miles per hour = miles ÷ hours
  • 1 mile = 1.609344 km, 1 yard = 0.9144 m; marathon = 42.195 km, half marathon = 21.0975 km
  • Pace of one part of a run = (time at this checkpoint − time at the last one) ÷ (distance here − distance at the last one)
  • Finish time = time so far × full distance ÷ distance so far

A worked example

5 miles in 50:25 is 3,025 seconds ÷ 5 = 605 seconds, or 10:05 per mile. One mile is 1.609344 km, so the same pace is 605 ÷ 1.609344 = 375.93 seconds, or 6:15.93 per km, and a 10K at that pace takes 10 × 375.93 = 3,759 seconds = 1:02:39.

Sources

  • NIST Special Publication 811: Guide for the Use of the International System of Units (exact mile and yard)
  • World Athletics: Competition and Technical Rules (road race distances)

Questions

What is the difference between pace and speed?

Pace is time per distance, like 5:30 per km. Speed is distance per time, like 10.9 km/h. They describe the same effort: a faster pace is a smaller number, a faster speed is a bigger one.

How do I type a time?

Use the hours, minutes and seconds boxes. Leave hours at 0 for anything under an hour. Decimals are fine in the seconds box.

How do I find the pace for each lap or section?

Open Checkpoint splits and enter the total distance and total time at each point, the way a watch shows them. The table shows the pace between points and your average so far.

Is the finish time prediction reliable?

It assumes you hold your current pace to the end. Most people slow down in the second half of a long race, so treat it as the best case.
