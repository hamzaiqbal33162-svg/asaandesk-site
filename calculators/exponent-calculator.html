What an exponent is

An exponent calculator works out powers: a base number multiplied by itself a set number of times. In 3⁴ the base is 3 and the exponent (also called the index or power) is 4, so 3⁴ = 3 × 3 × 3 × 3 = 81. The whole expression is read "3 to the power 4" or "3 to the fourth".

Two powers have their own names. x² is "x squared", because a square with side x has area x², and x³ is "x cubed", the volume of a cube with side x. On a keyboard or in a text message, the exponent symbol is the caret: 3^4 means 3⁴.

Exponents are a shorthand for very large and very small numbers. Instead of writing 1,000,000 you write 10⁶, and 0.000001 becomes 10⁻⁶. Scientists, engineers, bankers and programmers use them daily, from compound interest to computer memory sizes.

How to use this exponent calculator with steps

  • What do you want to find? Pick The result (work out xⁿ), The base (solve xⁿ = y for x) or The exponent (solve xⁿ = y for n). The box for the unknown disappears.
  • Base x: a whole number, a decimal, a negative number, a fraction such as 1/2, or the letter e (about 2.718281828459). It starts at 2.
  • Exponent n: any number, including negatives and fractions such as 1/3 or 2/3, which take a root. It starts at 10.
  • Result y: shown only when you solve for the base or the exponent. It starts at 1024, so the defaults read 2¹⁰ = 1024.
  • Read the answer, the equation, and the steps: repeated multiplication for small whole powers, 1 over a power for negative ones, root-then-power for fractions, and logarithms when solving for the exponent.

Understanding the results

The headline gives the unknown: Result y, Base x or Exponent n. Below it, the Equation row writes the full statement, such as 2¹⁰ = 1024 or 7³ = 343, so you can copy it into your work.

Whole-number powers are exact. When the answer has more than 15 digits, an Every digit row lists all of them (up to 400 digits), and beyond that a Number of digits row tells you how long the number is. A decimal answer that is an exact fraction, such as 2⁻³, also gets an As a fraction row (1/8).

Other powers keep 14 significant digits and switch to the × 10ⁿ form for very large or tiny values. When a negative base has no real answer, as with (−4)^(1/2), the page shows the principal complex value (2i) and a warning instead of an error.

Worked examples

Whole power: 2¹⁰ = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 1,024. This is why a kibibyte is 1,024 bytes rather than 1,000.

Negative exponent: 2⁻³. First 2³ = 8, then the negative sign flips it to 1 ÷ 8 = 0.125, which the page also gives as the fraction 1/8.

Fraction exponent: 16^0.75. The decimal 0.75 is 3/4, so take the 4th root of 16 (which is 2) and cube it: 2³ = 8. With a negative base and an odd root the answer stays real: (−8)^(1/3) = −2, because −2 × −2 × −2 = −8.

Decimal base and exponent: 1.1^(−2.5). The exponent is −5/2, the square root of 1.1 is 1.0488088481702, and 1.0488088481702⁻⁵ = 0.78798561094677.

Solve for the base: x³ = 343. Undo the cube with a cube root, x = ∛343 = 7, and check 7³ = 343. Solve for the exponent: 2ⁿ = 1,000 gives n = ln 1000 ÷ ln 2 = 6.9077552789821 ÷ 0.69314718055995 = 9.9657842846621, just under 10 because 2¹⁰ is 1,024.

Money in PKR: PKR 100,000 growing 10% a year for 5 years becomes 100,000 × 1.1⁵. The calculator gives 1.1⁵ = 1.61051, so the amount reaches PKR 161,051. The same power sits inside every compound interest formula.

Exponents on a scientific calculator, in Excel and in Python

On most scientific calculators the power key is marked xʸ, yˣ or ^. Type the base, press the key, type the exponent and press equals. For roots use the x√y key or type the root as a fraction exponent in brackets, for example 8 ^ (1 ÷ 3).

In Excel, type =2^10 or =POWER(2,10); both return 1024, and =POWER(4,5/4) handles fraction exponents. Watch the minus sign: Excel applies negation before exponentiation, so =-2^2 gives 4, not −4. Write =-(2^2) when you mean the negative of a square.

In Python the power operator is **. It binds tighter than a minus on its left, so -1**2 is −1, and chains run right to left, so 2**3**2 is 2**9 = 512. A negative number raised to a fraction, such as (-8)**(1/3), returns a complex number rather than the real cube root −2 that this page gives.

How to calculate exponents without a calculator

  • Square repeatedly: 3⁸ = ((3²)²)² = (9²)² = 81² = 6,561, three multiplications instead of seven.
  • Split the exponent: 2¹³ = 2¹⁰ × 2³ = 1,024 × 8 = 8,192.
  • Turn negatives into fractions early: 5⁻² = 1/25 = 0.04.
  • Simplify a fraction exponent first: 27^(2/3) means the cube root (3) squared, which is 9, much easier than squaring 27 first.
  • Estimate with powers of 10: 2¹⁰ is about 10³, so 2³⁰ is about 10⁹, a billion (the exact value is 1,073,741,824).

Common mistakes with powers

  • Multiplying the base by the exponent: 2³ is 8, not 6.
  • Dropping brackets on negatives: (−3)² = 9 but −3² = −9, because the square applies before the minus in standard notation.
  • Spreading a power over a sum: (a + b)² is a² + 2ab + b², not a² + b². With a = 3 and b = 4 that is 49, not 25.
  • Adding exponents for different bases: 2³ × 3² is 8 × 9 = 72, not 6⁵.
  • Thinking a negative exponent makes a negative answer: 2⁻³ = 0.125, still positive.
  • Related tools: Log Calculator to undo a power, Root Calculator for square and cube roots, and Big Number Calculator for huge exact arithmetic.

How it works

An exponent says how many times to multiply a number by itself: 2⁵ = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 32. Choose what you want to find and fill in the other two values. You can type whole numbers, decimals, fractions such as 1/3, or the letter e. A negative exponent means 1 divided by the power, and a fraction exponent takes a root: 8^(2/3) is the cube root of 8, squared, which is 4. Whole powers are worked out exactly, so even 2^1000 shows every digit.

The formula

  • x^n = x × x × … × x (n copies) for a whole n
  • x^(−n) = 1 ÷ x^n
  • x^(p/q) = (q-th root of x)^p; the cube root (or any odd root) of a negative number is negative
  • Any other exponent: x^n = e^(n × ln x), for x > 0
  • Solve for the base: x = y^(1/n); solve for the exponent: n = ln(y) ÷ ln(x)

A worked example

1.1^−2.5: the exponent −2.5 is −5/2, so take the square root of 1.1 (1.0488088481702) and raise it to the power −5. That is 1 ÷ 1.0488088481702⁵ = 1 ÷ 1.2690587062858 = 0.78798561094677.

Questions

How do you calculate exponents?

Multiply the base by itself as many times as the exponent says: 4³ = 4 × 4 × 4 = 64. For a negative exponent work out the positive power and take 1 divided by it. For a fraction exponent take the root shown by the bottom number, then the power shown by the top.

What does a negative exponent mean?

It means 1 divided by the positive power. So 2⁻³ = 1 ÷ 2³ = 1 ÷ 8 = 0.125. The bigger the negative exponent, the closer the answer gets to 0.

What does a fraction exponent mean?

The bottom of the fraction is a root and the top is a power. 27^(1/3) is the cube root of 27, which is 3, and 27^(2/3) is that cube root squared, 9. Decimals work the same way: 2.5 is 5/2.

Can this exponent calculator handle fractions?

Yes, in both boxes. Type 1/2 as the base to get powers of one half, or 2/3 as the exponent to take a cube root and then square it. Exact fraction answers are also shown as fractions.

Why is (−8)^(1/3) a real number but (−4)^(1/2) is not?

An odd root of a negative number is real: −2 × −2 × −2 = −8, so the cube root of −8 is −2. An even root of a negative number cannot be real, because any real number squared is positive. For those, the calculator shows the complex answer, such as 2i.

What is 0 to the power 0?

It is taken as 1, which keeps the power rules and many formulas simple. Some books call it undefined, and the result shows a note about that.

How do I find the exponent if I know the base and the answer?

Choose The exponent, type the base and the result, and the page uses logarithms: n = ln(y) ÷ ln(x). For 2ⁿ = 1,000 that gives about 9.9658. Our log calculator does the same with any base.

How do I type exponents in Excel?

Use the caret, =5^2, or the POWER function, =POWER(5,2); both give 25. Put negative bases in brackets and remember that Excel treats =-2^2 as (−2)², which is 4.

What is exponential growth?

It is growth by the same percentage each period, so the amount is multiplied by a fixed factor again and again: amount = start × (1 + rate)ᵗ. At 10% a year the factor is 1.1, and after 5 years 1.1⁵ = 1.61051, a 61% rise rather than 50%.

How big can the numbers be?

Whole powers are exact with any number of digits, and the result shows every digit up to 400 digits (and the digit count beyond that). Other powers keep 14 significant digits and switch to the × 10ⁿ form for very big or very small results.
