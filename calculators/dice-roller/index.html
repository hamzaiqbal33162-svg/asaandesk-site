An online dice roller for any game

This dice roller throws fair virtual dice in your browser: one die or up to 10,000 at once. Standard six-sided dice show as dice pictures with dots for up to 12 dice, so a 1-6 roll for ludo or snakes and ladders looks like the real thing.

Role-playing gamers can pick a d4, d8, d10, d12, d20 or d100, and anyone can type their own number of sides, from 2 (a coin) to 1,000,000. With two or more dice the roller also adds them up, multiplies them, averages them and counts how often each face came up.

How to roll dice online here

  • Set Number of dice: 1 for a single die, 2 for Monopoly, or any number up to 10,000.
  • Pick the Kind of dice: 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 20 or 100 sides. For anything else choose "Other number of sides" and fill in Sides on each die (2 to 1,000,000).
  • Press Roll. Changing any box also rolls again, so a new throw is always one tap away.
  • Under More options, tick "Sort the rolls, smallest first" when you want the results in order, for example to drop the lowest of several dice.
  • Use the Copy button on the result to copy every roll, even when only the first ones fit on screen.

How to calculate dice probability

On one fair die with s sides each face has a chance of 1 in s: 1/6, or about 16.67%, on a six-sided die and 1/20, or 5%, on a d20. For two or more dice, count the ways a result can happen and divide by all the ways the dice can land. Two six-sided dice can land 6 × 6 = 36 ways, and the table shows how many of them give each total.

For the chance of at least one particular face, work out the chance of missing it every time and take that away from 1. The chance of at least one six in n rolls is 1 − (5/6)^n: 30.56% in two rolls, 42.13% in three, 51.77% in four and 83.85% in ten.

Rolling doubles with two dice has a chance of 6 in 36, or 1 in 6, and three of the same in a row 1 in 216 (about 0.46%). Rolling two d20 and keeping the higher one, as some games allow, gives a 20 with a chance of 1 − (19/20)^2 = 9.75%, almost double the 5% of a single roll.

How to calculate the dice average

The average roll of a fair die with s sides is (s + 1) ÷ 2, because the faces 1 to s are spread evenly around their middle. A d6 averages 3.5, a d20 10.5 and a d100 50.5. For several dice, multiply: N dice with s sides average N × (s + 1) ÷ 2, so 3d6 averages 10.5, 4d6 averages 14 and 8d6 averages 28.

The average tells you what to expect over many rolls, not on the next one. One test roll of four six-sided dice here came up 4, 2, 3 and 4, a total of 13, just under the average of 14. Games written in dice notation use the same idea: NdS means N dice with S sides, added together, so 3d6 is three six-sided dice.

Dice for ludo, snakes and ladders, Monopoly and D&D

Ludo and snakes and ladders use one six-sided die, so set Number of dice to 1. In common ludo rules a token needs a six to leave its yard, a six earns a bonus roll, and a third six in a row ends the turn without a move. The chance of a six within three rolls is 42.13%, and of three sixes in a row 1 in 216. Some snakes and ladders players use two dice for a shorter game.

Monopoly uses two six-sided dice, so set 2. Doubles give another turn, and three doubles in a row send the player to jail; that happens on only 1 in 216 sets of three rolls.

For Dungeons & Dragons (5e) and other role-playing games, pick a d20 for checks and attacks and the listed dice for damage. Roll several dice together for a damage roll such as 8d6, then read the Total. A real d10 is often numbered 0 to 9; this roller gives 1 to 10, and its d100 gives 1 to 100, the same as two percentile dice read with 00 as 100.

Is an online dice roller really random?

Each face here is picked with your browser's Web Crypto random number source, the one used for passwords and keys. A plain "random number mod 6" would make low faces slightly more likely, so the roller throws away any value that would cause that and draws again (rejection sampling). Every face of every die then has exactly the same chance, and the rolls never leave your device.

Physical dice are only as fair as their making, which is why casinos use precision dice, usually transparent, with sharp edges and flush pips. Opposite faces of a standard die add up to 7 (1 and 6, 2 and 5, 3 and 4).

You can test any dice with a chi-square check. Roll many times, then for each face take (count − expected)² ÷ expected and add the results. In one run of 6,000 six-sided dice here the faces came up 981, 1,036, 1,028, 977, 979 and 999 times against 1,000 expected, which gives 3.41. For six faces (5 degrees of freedom) only a value above 11.07 would suggest an unfair die at the usual 5% level.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Past rolls do not change the next one. After five rolls without a six, the chance of a six is still 1 in 6; thinking a number is "due" is the gambler's fallacy.
  • Small samples look uneven. Twelve rolls of one die can easily show a face three times and another not at all; the face counts even out only over hundreds or thousands of rolls.
  • Pick the right die for the game. A d10 is not two d6, and 2d6 (totals 2 to 12, peaking at 7) is not the same as one d12 (each total from 1 to 12 equally likely).
  • For huge rolls the product grows very fast: 6,000 dice gave a product with 2,853 digits, so it is shown in powers of ten.

How it works

Choose how many dice to roll and what kind: standard six-sided dice (shown as dice pictures for up to 12), the d4, d8, d10, d12, d20 and d100 used in board and role-playing games, or any number of sides from 2 to 1,000,000. Press Roll for a new throw; changing a box rolls again too. With two or more dice you also get the total, the product of all the faces, the average, the highest and lowest roll, and a count of how often each face came up, with a chart for 20 or more dice. Every face is picked with your browser's secure random number source, and any random value that would favour some faces is thrown away and drawn again, so each face has exactly the same chance, just like a perfectly fair die. Nothing you roll leaves your device.

The formula

  • One die with s sides: face = 1 + r, where r is a fair random whole number from 0 to s − 1
  • Fair r: take just enough random bits for s − 1 and draw again whenever they give s or more (no remainder trick, so no bias)
  • Total = sum of the faces; average = total ÷ number of dice; a fair die averages (s + 1) ÷ 2
  • Product = all faces multiplied; shown exactly up to 15 digits, then in powers of ten

A worked example

Rolling two six-sided dice might give 1 and 4: a total of 5, a product of 4 and an average of 2.5. Over many rolls a total of 7 comes up most often (6 of the 36 ways), and 2 or 12 least often (1 way each).

Questions

Is this dice roller fair?

Yes. Each roll uses the secure random number source built into your browser, and random values that would make some faces more likely are thrown away and drawn again. Every face of every die has exactly the same chance.

Can I roll a 1-6 dice with dots online?

Yes. Keep the Kind of dice on 6 sides and the result shows dice pictures with dots for up to 12 dice, next to the numbers. With more than 12 dice you get the numbers, the face counts and a chart instead.

How do I roll a d20 or another kind of die?

Pick it from the Kind of dice menu: 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 20 or 100 sides. For anything else choose Other number of sides and type any number from 2 to 1,000,000.

Which dice roller setting should I use for ludo?

Set Number of dice to 1 and keep six sides, then press Roll for each turn. Snakes and ladders works the same way, and Monopoly needs 2 dice so you can see doubles.

How do you calculate dice probability?

Divide the number of ways a result can happen by the number of ways the dice can land. Two six-sided dice can land 36 ways and 6 of them make 7, so a total of 7 has a chance of 6/36, about 16.67%. For "at least one" questions, take the chance of missing every time away from 1.

How do you calculate the dice average for D&D?

A die with s sides averages (s + 1) ÷ 2, and several dice add up: N × (s + 1) ÷ 2. So 2d6 averages 7, 8d6 averages 28 and a d20 averages 10.5.

How many dice can I roll at once?

Up to 10,000. The first rolls are shown at the top, the full list is in a table with a CSV download, and the count of each face shows how evenly they came up.

Why does the same face sometimes come up several times in a row?

Every roll is independent, so repeats happen naturally: with one six-sided die the chance of the same face twice in a row is 1 in 6. Over thousands of rolls each face still comes up about equally often.

What is the Dice coefficient?

It is something else that only shares the name: the Dice-Sørensen coefficient, 2|X ∩ Y| ÷ (|X| + |Y|), measures how much two sets overlap and is used to score image segmentation. It is named after Lee R. Dice (1945) and Thorvald Sørensen (1948) and has nothing to do with rolling dice.
