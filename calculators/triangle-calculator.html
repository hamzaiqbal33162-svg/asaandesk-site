What the triangle calculator solves

A triangle has six main measurements: three sides and three angles. Give this triangle calculator any three of them, with at least one side, and it finds the other three, then the area, perimeter, heights, medians, inradius and circumradius, the corner coordinates and a picture drawn to scale. Every step is written out with the law of cosines or the law of sines, so you can follow or copy the working.

Two facts hold for every triangle. The three angles add up to exactly 180°, and any two sides together are longer than the third side (the triangle inequality). Sides of 2, 3 and 6 cannot make a triangle, because 2 + 3 is less than 6, and the calculator tells you so.

How to use the triangle calculator

  • Choose Angles in: Degrees or Radians. In radians you can type 1.0472 or a part of pi such as pi/3 or 2pi/5.
  • Type three values among Side a, Side b, Side c, Angle A, Angle B and Angle C, and leave the others empty. Angle A faces side a, B faces b and C faces c. The page starts with the sides 5, 6 and 7.
  • Include at least one side: three angles fix the shape but not the size.
  • If you fill in more than three, the first three in the order a, b, c, A, B, C are used, and you are told which were left out.
  • Use one unit for every side. All lengths in the answer are in that unit, and the area is in that unit squared.

Worked examples for each case

SSS, sides 5, 6 and 7. The largest angle faces the longest side: C = arccos((5² + 6² − 7²) ÷ (2 × 5 × 6)) = arccos(12 ÷ 60) = arccos(0.2) ≈ 78.46°. The others follow the same way: A ≈ 44.42° and B ≈ 57.12°.

SAS, sides a = 8 and b = 11 with C = 40° between them. c² = 8² + 11² − 2 × 8 × 11 × cos 40° = 185 − 134.82 = 50.18, so c ≈ 7.0835. Then A ≈ 46.55° and B ≈ 93.45°, an obtuse triangle. Area = ½ × 8 × 11 × sin 40° ≈ 28.28.

ASA, angles A = 50° and B = 60° with c = 12 between them. C = 180° − 50° − 60° = 70°. By the law of sines, a = 12 × sin 50° ÷ sin 70° ≈ 9.7825 and b = 12 × sin 60° ÷ sin 70° ≈ 11.0593.

SSA, the ambiguous case: a = 7, b = 10 and A = 40°. sin B = 10 × sin 40° ÷ 7 ≈ 0.9183, and two angles have that sine: B ≈ 66.67° or B ≈ 113.33°. Both work, giving C ≈ 73.33° with c ≈ 10.43, or C ≈ 26.67° with c ≈ 4.89. The calculator shows both triangles side by side. If sin B came out above 1, side a would be too short to reach and no triangle would exist.

To find a height from the area, turn the first formula round: height = 2 × area ÷ base. For the 5-6-7 triangle, the height onto side 5 is 2 × 14.697 ÷ 5 = 5.879.

For land, measure the three sides in feet and type them in; the area comes out in square feet from Heron's formula. A corner plot with sides of 45 ft, 52 ft and 60 ft has s = 78.5 and an area of about 1,135.44 ft², which is about 105.49 m², 5.05 marla of 225 ft² or 4.17 marla of 272.25 ft². A plot with sides 60, 80 and 100 ft is a right triangle (60² + 80² = 100²), so its area is simply ½ × 60 × 80 = 2,400 ft², or 10.67 marla of 225 ft².

Mixed feet and inches must be turned into one unit first: 45 ft 6 in is 45.5 ft. The Area Calculator converts square feet into acres, kanal and marla for you.

Types of triangles

By sides, a triangle is equilateral (all three equal), isosceles (two equal) or scalene (none equal). By angles, it is acute (all under 90°), right (one exactly 90°) or obtuse (one over 90°). You can tell the angle type from the sides alone: square the longest side and compare it with the sum of the squares of the other two.

Triangle facts and theorems

  • Angle sum: A + B + C = 180°. So a triangle can have at most one angle of 90° or more.
  • Exterior angle: an outside angle equals the sum of the two inside angles that are not next to it.
  • Triangle inequality: each side is shorter than the other two added together, and longer than their difference.
  • The longest side is always opposite the largest angle, and the shortest side opposite the smallest.
  • The three medians meet at the centroid, which cuts each median in the ratio 2 : 1; it is the balance point of a cut-out triangle.
  • The incenter (where the angle bisectors meet) is the center of the inscribed circle; the circumcenter (where the perpendicular bisectors meet) is the center of the circle through all three corners. In a right triangle the circumcenter is the middle of the hypotenuse.
  • Law of sines: each side divided by the sine of its opposite angle gives the same number, the diameter 2R of the circumscribed circle.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Keep each angle opposite its own side: angle A faces side a. A mix-up gives a different triangle.
  • Check the angle mode: sin 40 means 40° in degree mode but 40 radians in radian mode.
  • With two sides and an angle that is not between them, expect zero, one or two answers, and check which triangle fits your drawing.
  • Keep full precision until the end; rounding an angle early can shift later sides in the third digit.
  • For woodworking, roofing or concrete jobs, work in one unit (all inches or all millimeters) and round only the final cut length.

How it works

Type any three of the six values: the sides a, b, c and the angles A, B, C, where each angle sits opposite the side with the same letter. At least one of the three must be a side, because angles alone fix the shape but not the size. Leave the rest empty. The calculator works out which case you gave (three sides, two sides and an angle, or two angles and a side), solves the missing values with the law of cosines or the law of sines, and then finds the area, perimeter, heights, medians and the two special circles. When two sides and an angle that is not between them could make two different triangles, both are shown. Angles can be typed in degrees or in radians, and in radians you can write a part of pi such as pi/3.

The formula

  • Law of cosines: c² = a² + b² − 2ab·cos C (and the same for a and b)
  • Law of sines: a ÷ sin A = b ÷ sin B = c ÷ sin C = 2R
  • Angle sum: A + B + C = 180° (π radians)
  • Area = ½·a·b·sin C, perimeter p = a + b + c, semiperimeter s = p ÷ 2
  • Height to side a: ha = 2 × Area ÷ a; median to side a: ma = ½√(2b² + 2c² − a²)
  • Inradius r = Area ÷ s; circumradius R = a ÷ (2·sin A) = abc ÷ (4 × Area)
  • Two sides and a non-included angle: sin B = b·sin A ÷ a gives 0, 1 or 2 values of B between 0° and 180°

A worked example

Sides a = 5, b = 6 and c = 7: angle A = arccos((6² + 7² − 5²) ÷ (2 × 6 × 7)) = arccos(60 ÷ 84) ≈ 44.415°. In the same way B ≈ 57.122° and C ≈ 78.463°, which add up to 180°. The area is ½ × 5 × 6 × sin 78.463° ≈ 14.69694, the perimeter is 18 and the inradius is 14.69694 ÷ 9 ≈ 1.63299.

Questions

How do I calculate the area of a triangle with 3 sides?

Use Heron's formula: s = (a + b + c) ÷ 2, then Area = √(s(s − a)(s − b)(s − c)). For sides 5, 6 and 7, s = 9 and the area is √(9 × 4 × 3 × 2) = √216 ≈ 14.70. Type the three sides above and the area appears.

How do I calculate triangle angles from the sides?

Use the law of cosines turned round: A = arccos((b² + c² − a²) ÷ 2bc), and the same for B and C. For 5, 6 and 7 the angles are about 44.42°, 57.12° and 78.46°.

How do I find the height of a triangle?

Divide twice the area by the base: h = 2 × Area ÷ base. The calculator gives all three heights (ha, hb, hc) in the All values table.

What is the triangle inequality?

Any two sides of a triangle added together must be longer than the third side. Sides 3, 4 and 8 fail (3 + 4 < 8), so no triangle can be made from them.

How do I calculate a triangular plot area in square feet?

Measure all three sides in feet and enter them; the area is given in square feet. Divide by 225 (or the marla size in your papers) to get marla. A 45, 52 and 60 ft plot is about 1,135 ft².

How do I know if a triangle is acute, right or obtuse?

Square the longest side and compare it with the sum of the squares of the other two. Smaller means acute, equal means right, larger means obtuse. The Type row tells you directly.

Why can two different triangles come out?

If you give two sides and an angle that is not between them, the side opposite the angle can swing to two places and still touch the base. Then two triangles fit, one with an acute and one with an obtuse angle, and both are shown. If that side is too short to reach, no triangle fits and you get a message instead.

Why do I need at least one side?

Three angles only tell you the shape. A small triangle and a big one can have exactly the same angles, so one length is needed to fix the size.

What happens if I fill in more than three values?

The calculator uses the first three it finds (in the order a, b, c, A, B, C), says which values it left out, and warns you if one of them does not fit the triangle.

Which unit are the sides in?

Any one unit you like. If you type the sides in centimeters, every length in the answer is in centimeters and the area is in square centimeters. Just use the same unit for all the sides.

How do I type an angle in radians?

Switch Angles in to Radians, then type a number such as 1.0472, or a part of pi such as pi/3, 2pi/5 or 3π/4. The results show each angle in radians, in degrees and as a part of π when it is a neat one.
