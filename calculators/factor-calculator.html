What a factor is in math

A factor of a whole number is a whole number that divides it exactly, with no remainder. 4 is a factor of 100 because 100 ÷ 4 = 25, but 3 is not, because 100 ÷ 3 leaves 1 over. This factor calculator lists every factor of the number you type, pairs them up, and breaks the number into primes with a factor tree and the steps.

Factors and multiples are two sides of the same fact. Since 4 × 25 = 100, both 4 and 25 are factors of 100, and 100 is a multiple of each of them. Every number has 1 and itself as factors; a number above 1 with no other factors is a prime.

How to use the factor calculator

  • Type a positive whole number in the Whole number box, from 1 up to 1,000,000,000,000,000 (10¹⁵). The page opens with 72 as an example.
  • Read the three headline results: Factors of the number (in order), Number of factors and Sum of factors.
  • Below them: Factor pairs, Type of number (prime, composite, or neither for 1), Prime factorization and, when a prime repeats, the same with powers.
  • Open the steps to see each division up to the square root, then the factor tree that splits off the smallest prime each time.
  • Long lists (more than 120 factors) are shortened in the headline and given in full in the Factor pairs table.

How to find the factors of a number by hand, with steps

Factors come in pairs that multiply to the number, and the smaller one of each pair is never bigger than the square root. So you only need to test the numbers from 1 up to √n. Take 84: √84 is about 9.17, so test 1 to 9.

  • 84 ÷ 1 = 84, so (1, 84) is a pair.
  • 84 ÷ 2 = 42, so (2, 42).
  • 84 ÷ 3 = 28, so (3, 28).
  • 84 ÷ 4 = 21, so (4, 21).
  • 84 ÷ 5 leaves 4 over, so 5 is not a factor.
  • 84 ÷ 6 = 14, so (6, 14).
  • 84 ÷ 7 = 12, so (7, 12).
  • 8 and 9 do not divide 84, and the next test, 10, is past the square root, so you can stop.

Reading the small factors up and the large ones back down gives all 12 factors of 84: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 12, 14, 21, 28, 42, 84. Divisibility rules make the testing faster: a number divides by 2 if it ends in an even digit, by 3 if its digits add up to a multiple of 3 (8 + 4 = 12), by 4 if its last two digits form a multiple of 4, by 5 if it ends in 0 or 5, by 6 if it passes the rules for 2 and 3, and by 9 if its digits add up to a multiple of 9.

Factors of common numbers

These come up again and again in school exercises and in everyday sharing out. Numbers like 60, 120 and 360 have so many factors that they split evenly in many ways: an hour of 60 minutes divides into halves, thirds, quarters, fifths, sixths, tenths and twelfths without fractions of a minute.

The factor count and sum formulas

Once a number is written in prime powers, you can count its factors without listing them. For 360 = 2³ × 3² × 5, every factor uses 2 zero to three times, 3 zero to two times and 5 zero or one time, so there are (3 + 1) × (2 + 1) × (1 + 1) = 24 factors. The sum works the same way: (1 + 2 + 4 + 8) × (1 + 3 + 9) × (1 + 5) = 15 × 13 × 6 = 1,170.

The sum of factors also shows a famous kind of number. A perfect number equals the sum of its factors other than itself, so its full Sum of factors is exactly twice the number. 28 is one: 1 + 2 + 4 + 7 + 14 = 28, and the calculator shows a sum of 56. Try 6, 496 and 8,128 as well.

Factors, factorials and factoring polynomials

These three are often mixed up in searches. Factors of a number are its exact divisors (the factors of 6 are 1, 2, 3 and 6). A factorial, written n!, is the product 1 × 2 × … × n, so 5! = 120 and 100! has 158 digits; use the Big Number Calculator for those. Factoring a polynomial means writing it as a product of simpler ones, such as x² + 5x + 6 = (x + 2)(x + 3); the Quadratic Formula Calculator finds the roots that give those brackets.

Factoring whole numbers also matters outside school. RSA, a public-key encryption method widely used on the internet, relies on the fact that no fast method is known for splitting the product of two very large primes back into those primes. Numbers up to 10¹⁵ like the ones here take a moment; numbers with hundreds of digits are out of reach.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving out 1 and the number itself: both are always factors.
  • Stopping too early: keep testing up to the square root, or you miss pairs.
  • Counting a square root twice: for 36, the pair (6, 6) gives one factor, 6.
  • Mixing up factors and multiples: the factors of 12 are at most 12, while its multiples (12, 24, 36 …) go on for ever.
  • Calling 1 a prime: it has only one factor, so it is neither prime nor composite.

How it works

Type a positive whole number. Its factors are all the whole numbers that divide it with nothing left over. They come in pairs that multiply to the number, so it is enough to test the numbers up to its square root: each exact division gives one small and one large factor. The prime factors are found by splitting off the smallest prime again and again, which is what the factor tree shows.

The formula

  • d is a factor of n when n ÷ d leaves no remainder; then n ÷ d is a factor too, so (d, n ÷ d) is a factor pair
  • Only d from 1 up to √n need testing
  • If n = p₁^a × p₂^b × …, the number of factors is (a + 1) × (b + 1) × …
  • Sum of factors = (1 + p₁ + … + p₁^a) × (1 + p₂ + … + p₂^b) × …

A worked example

Factors of 72: its square root is about 8.49, and 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 8 divide 72 exactly, giving the pairs (1, 72), (2, 36), (3, 24), (4, 18), (6, 12) and (8, 9). So 72 has 12 factors. In primes, 72 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 = 2³ × 3², and (3 + 1) × (2 + 1) = 12 confirms the count.

Questions

How do I calculate the factors of a number?

Divide the number by 1, 2, 3 and so on up to its square root. Each division with no remainder gives two factors, the divisor and the answer. For 84 that gives 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7 and their partners 84, 42, 28, 21, 14, 12.

What are the factors of 100?

1, 2, 4, 5, 10, 20, 25, 50 and 100, nine factors in all. They pair up as (1, 100), (2, 50), (4, 25), (5, 20) and (10, 10). In primes, 100 = 2² × 5².

What is a factor pair?

Two whole numbers that multiply to give the number. The factor pairs of 24 are (1, 24), (2, 12), (3, 8) and (4, 6), which are also the ways to arrange 24 chairs in equal rows.

What is the difference between factors and prime factors?

Factors are all the numbers that divide exactly: 12 has 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 12. Prime factors are only the primes that multiply together to make it: 12 = 2 × 2 × 3.

What is the difference between a factor and a multiple?

A factor divides into the number; a multiple is the number times a whole number. 6 is a factor of 30 and 30 is a multiple of 6. A number has a limited list of factors but endless multiples.

Why does a perfect square have an odd number of factors?

Factors come in pairs, but in a perfect square one pair is the square root times itself, and it is counted only once. So 36 has the pairs (1, 36), (2, 18), (3, 12), (4, 9) plus 6, nine factors.

Is a factor the same as a factorial?

No. A factor divides a number exactly; a factorial multiplies all whole numbers from 1 up to n, so 5! = 120. Use the Big Number Calculator for large factorials.

Can this calculator factor a quadratic or a polynomial?

No, it factors whole numbers. For a quadratic such as x² − 5x + 6, find its roots (2 and 3) with the Quadratic Formula Calculator; they give the factors (x − 2)(x − 3).

Is 1 a prime number?

No. A prime has exactly two factors, 1 and itself. The number 1 has only one factor, so it is neither prime nor composite.

How do I know a number is prime?

A number above 1 is prime when its only factors are 1 and itself. The result says so, as for 97, and the factor list then holds just those two numbers.

How large can the number be?

Up to 1,000,000,000,000,000 (10 to the power 15). Very long factor lists are shown in the factor pairs table, which you can download as a CSV file.
