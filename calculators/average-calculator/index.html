What the average calculator works out

An average calculator turns a list of numbers into one typical value. In everyday maths the average means the arithmetic mean: add every number, then divide the total by how many numbers you added. Five marks of 78, 85, 69, 91 and 72 add up to 395, and 395 ÷ 5 = 79, so the average mark is 79.

The same page also gives the median, the geometric mean, the largest and smallest numbers, the range, the sum and the count, because one figure rarely tells the whole story. A second tab works out a weighted average, where some numbers count more than others.

How to use this calculator, step by step

  • Average tab: type or paste your list into the Numbers box. Commas, spaces, tabs and new lines all separate numbers, so a column copied from a spreadsheet works as it is.
  • Leave out thousands separators inside a number (type 25000, not 25,000), because a comma starts a new number.
  • Read the average at the top. The working underneath shows the sum and then sum ÷ count.
  • Weighted average tab: type the values in the first box (for example marks or prices) and the weight of each one in the second box, in the same order. Weights can be credit hours, quantities, percent shares or hours of travel.
  • The two lists must be the same length. Weights cannot be negative, and at least one must be above 0.

Mean, median or geometric mean: which average to use

The mean uses every value, which makes it sensitive to one very large or very small number. Take five monthly salaries in PKR: 40,000, 45,000, 50,000, 55,000 and 400,000. The mean is 590,000 ÷ 5 = 118,000, which none of the first four people earn, while the median is 50,000.

Picking the right kind of average
SituationBest averageWhy
Marks, heights, daily sales without extreme valuesMeanUses every value equally
Incomes, house prices, anything with a few extreme valuesMedianNot pulled by outliers
Growth rates, returns, ratios that multiplyGeometric meanMatches compounding
Parts that count differently (credits, quantities)Weighted averageEach value counts by its weight

For growth, enter growth factors rather than percents. Growth of 10% and then 50% is 1.10 and 1.50; their geometric mean is √1.65 = 1.2845, an average of about 28.45% a year, not the 30% that a plain average of the two percents suggests. For the most common value (the mode), use the Mean, Median, Mode and Range Calculator.

Average marks and average percentage for exams

When every paper has the same total, the average percentage is simply the average of the marks. A student with 78, 85, 69, 91 and 72 out of 100 in five subjects has an average of 79, which is 79%.

When the totals differ, averaging the percents gives the wrong answer. Suppose a Matric student scores 45 out of 50 (90%) in one test and 60 out of 100 (60%) in another. The plain average of the percents is 75%, but the student earned 105 marks out of 150, which is 70%. You get the same 70 on the Weighted average tab by entering 90, 60 as the numbers and 50, 100 as the weights.

Grade averages work the same way. A GPA is a weighted average of grade points by credit hours: 4.0 over 3 credits, 3.3 over 4 credits and 2.7 over 2 credits give (12 + 13.2 + 5.4) ÷ 9 = 3.4. The GPA Calculator adds letter grades and semesters.

Average share price on PSX and other weighted averages in PKR

Investors on the Pakistan Stock Exchange often need the average cost of shares bought at different prices. This is a weighted average with the quantity as the weight. Buying 100 shares at PKR 250 and then 300 shares at PKR 210 costs 25,000 + 63,000 = PKR 88,000 for 400 shares, so the average cost is PKR 220 a share.

Enter 250, 210 as the numbers and 100, 300 as the weights to see it. The plain average of the two prices, PKR 230, is too high because more shares were bought at the lower price. Brokerage, taxes and other charges are not included, so add them to the cost if you want your true break-even price.

Average speed, average time and fuel average

Average speed is total distance ÷ total time, not the average of the speeds. Driving 60 km at 60 km/h takes 1 hour and the next 60 km at 40 km/h takes 1.5 hours, so the trip averages 120 ÷ 2.5 = 48 km/h, not 50. On the Weighted average tab, enter the speeds 60, 40 with the hours 1, 1.5 as weights. The Speed Calculator solves distance, speed and time directly.

To average times such as lap times, change them into one unit first. 1:30, 1:45 and 2:00 minutes are 90, 105 and 120 seconds; their average is 315 ÷ 3 = 105 seconds, or 1:45.

In Pakistan, a car's or motorbike's "average" usually means fuel economy in kilometres per litre. Divide the distance by the litres used: 450 km on 30 litres is 15 km per litre. Over several fill-ups, divide the total distance by the total litres. 420 km on 28 litres and 300 km on 25 litres give 720 ÷ 53 = 13.58 km per litre, not the 13.5 you get by averaging 15 and 12. The Gas Mileage Calculator does this for you.

The average formula in Excel and Google Sheets

In Excel, =AVERAGE(A1:A10) gives the mean of a range. Microsoft notes that empty cells and text inside a range are skipped, but cells that contain 0 are counted, so a 0 lowers the average while a blank does not. Google Sheets uses the same AVERAGE formula.

For a weighted average with values in A1:A3 and weights in B1:B3, use =SUMPRODUCT(A1:A3, B1:B3) / SUM(B1:B3). SUMPRODUCT multiplies each pair and adds the products, which is the top line of the weighted average formula. You can paste either column straight into this page to check a spreadsheet.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Averaging percentages that come from different totals: add up the parts and the totals instead, or use the weights.
  • Leaving zeros out, or typing a 0 for a missing value: a real 0 belongs in the list, a missing value does not.
  • Using the mean for incomes or prices with a few extreme values, where the median describes the typical case better.
  • Averaging speeds or km-per-litre figures directly instead of dividing total distance by total time or fuel.
  • Mixing units, such as minutes and seconds, or kilograms and grams, in one list.

How it works

Type or paste your numbers, separated by commas, spaces or new lines, and the average appears at once with the working: the sum of the numbers divided by how many there are. The median, geometric mean, largest and smallest numbers, range, sum and count are listed under it. When some numbers should count more than others, such as exam parts worth different shares of a grade or items bought in different quantities, open the Weighted average tab and give each number its weight in the same order.

The formula

  • Average = (x₁ + x₂ + ... + x_N) ÷ N
  • Weighted average = (x₁ × w₁ + x₂ × w₂ + ... + x_N × w_N) ÷ (w₁ + w₂ + ... + w_N)
  • Median = the middle number of the sorted list (halfway between the two middle numbers for an even count)
  • Geometric mean = the N-th root of all N numbers multiplied together (numbers 0 or more)
  • Range = largest − smallest

A worked example

The average of 10, 2, 38, 23, 38, 23 and 21 is their sum, 155, divided by the count, 7: 155 ÷ 7 = 22.142857142857. As a weighted example, marks of 80, 90 and 75 that count for 20%, 30% and 50% of a grade give (80 × 20 + 90 × 30 + 75 × 50) ÷ (20 + 30 + 50) = 8,050 ÷ 100 = 80.5, a little lower than the plain average of 81.67 because the lowest mark counts the most.

Questions

How do I calculate the average of numbers?

Add all the numbers, then divide the total by how many there are. For 12, 15 and 18 the sum is 45 and the count is 3, so the average is 45 ÷ 3 = 15. Paste the list into the Numbers box and the page shows the same two steps.

What is the average formula in maths?

Average = sum of the values ÷ number of values. With weights it becomes Σ(value × weight) ÷ Σweight. Both formulas are listed under How it works on this page.

How do I calculate average percentage?

If every percentage comes from the same total, average them like any other numbers. If the totals differ, add the marks earned and divide by the total marks, or enter the percents as numbers and the totals as weights on the Weighted average tab.

Is the average the same as the mean?

In everyday use, yes: the average is the arithmetic mean, the sum divided by the count. The median and the mode are other kinds of "middle" value, and the geometric mean is the right average for growth rates and ratios.

When should I use a weighted average?

When the numbers do not count equally: exam parts worth different shares of a grade, grade points earned over different credit hours, or prices paid for different quantities. Each number's weight says how much it counts.

Do the weights need to add up to 100?

No. The weighted average divides by the total of the weights, so 20, 30, 50 and 2, 3, 5 give the same answer. Weights must not be negative, and at least one must be above 0.

How do I calculate the average price of shares I bought at different prices?

Use the Weighted average tab with the prices as numbers and the number of shares as weights. 100 shares at PKR 250 and 300 at PKR 210 average PKR 220 a share. Add brokerage and taxes separately if you want your full cost.

How do I calculate average speed?

Divide the total distance by the total time. Averaging the speeds only works when each speed was held for the same amount of time; otherwise weight each speed by the hours spent at it.

How do I calculate the average attendance of students?

Add the number of students present on each day and divide by the number of days. 38, 40, 35, 39 and 37 present over five days average 189 ÷ 5 = 37.8 students; in a class of 40 that is 94.5% attendance.

What is the formula for average in Excel?

=AVERAGE(A1:A10) averages a range; blank cells and text are skipped but zeros count. For a weighted average use =SUMPRODUCT(values, weights) / SUM(weights).

Can I average negative numbers or decimals?

Yes, the average works with any numbers. Only the geometric mean needs every number to be 0 or more; with a negative number it is shown as not defined.
