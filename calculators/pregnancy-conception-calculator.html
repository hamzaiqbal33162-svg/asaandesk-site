What the pregnancy conception calculator works out

A pregnancy conception calculator answers the question 'when did I get pregnant?'. It counts back from your due date, your last period or an ultrasound scan to the most likely date of conception, then gives the range of days around it when conception and the sex that led to it most probably and possibly happened. A calendar marks those days.

In everyday speech conception means the moment egg and sperm meet (fertilisation), which happens around ovulation. Medically, a pregnancy is established a week or so later, when the fertilised egg implants in the wall of the uterus. This calculator dates fertilisation. Every result is an estimate from average cycles, not a medical record; your midwife or doctor dates a pregnancy from the scan and your history.

How to use this calculator, step by step

  • Under Work it out from, choose Due date, Last period or Ultrasound.
  • Due date: enter the estimated due date from your doctor, midwife or scan report.
  • Last period: enter the first day of your last period and your usual cycle length, counted from the first day of one period to the first day of the next. Cycles from 22 to 44 days are supported; 28 is the default.
  • Ultrasound: enter the date of the scan and the weeks and days written on the report, for example 12 weeks and 3 days.
  • Read the most likely conception date at the top. With a period or scan you also get the estimated due date.
  • Check the four ranges below it and the calendar, which colours the earlier days of sex, the possible and most probable conception days, and the most likely date.

How the conception date is worked out

Doctors count a pregnancy from the first day of the last menstrual period (LMP), not from conception. With a regular 28-day cycle, ovulation and conception happen about 14 days after that day, and the due date is set 280 days (40 weeks) after the LMP. Conception to due date is therefore 266 days, or 38 weeks.

Cycle length changes the picture because the second half of the cycle, from ovulation to the next period, is fairly steady at about two weeks; a longer cycle means later ovulation. So the calculator moves conception later by one day for every day your cycle is longer than 28 days, and earlier for a shorter cycle. With a 32-day cycle and an LMP of August 10, 2026, conception falls on August 28 rather than August 24, and the due date on May 21, 2027.

From a scan the calculator subtracts the weeks and days on the report from the scan date to find an equivalent LMP, then adds 14 days. A scan on November 8, 2026 that says 12 weeks and 3 days gives an equivalent LMP of August 13 and conception around August 27, 2026.

The fertile window: why the ranges look the way they do

The classic study of this timing followed 221 women trying to conceive and measured ovulation with daily hormone tests (Wilcox, Weinberg and Baird, New England Journal of Medicine, 1995). Pregnancies came only from sex during a six-day window that ended on the day of ovulation. The chance was about 10% for sex five days before ovulation and rose to about 33% on the day of ovulation itself.

That pattern follows from biology: sperm can survive in the female reproductive tract for up to about five days, while the egg lives only about 12 to 24 hours after release. So the sex that led to a pregnancy can have happened several days before conception, but hardly ever more than a day after ovulation. The same study found that the timing of sex made no difference to whether the baby was a boy or a girl.

After fertilisation the embryo travels to the uterus. In a follow-up study, Wilcox and colleagues (1999) found that most successful pregnancies implanted 8 to 10 days after ovulation. A home pregnancy test turns positive only some days after implantation, which is why the NHS says most tests can be used from the first day of a missed period, or at least 21 days after unprotected sex if you do not know when your period is due.

Pregnancy weeks from conception

Because pregnancy weeks start at the last period, you are already counted as about 2 weeks pregnant on the day you conceive. To calculate pregnancy weeks from a known conception date, add 2 weeks to the time since conception.

For pregnancy months from conception, divide the weeks since conception plus 2 by about 4.3; at 18 weeks after conception you are 20 weeks pregnant, in your fifth month.

How accurate is a conception date?

Not very, on its own. Ovulation can move by several days from one cycle to the next, even in women with regular cycles. Natural pregnancy length also varies: a study that measured ovulation directly found a median of 268 days from ovulation to birth, with a spread of about five weeks between women (Jukic and colleagues, 2013).

The most reliable dating comes from an ultrasound measurement of the baby in the first trimester, up to 13 weeks and 6 days, according to the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG). If that scan and the last period disagree by more than about a week, doctors usually go by the scan, and later scans are used to redate only when the gap is larger (more than 10 days at 16 to 21 weeks, more than 14 days at 22 to 27 weeks). For pregnancies from IVF, the transfer date and embryo age give the exact timing.

Dates alone cannot tell which of two partners is the father when the possible days overlap. Only a paternity DNA test can answer that.

Tips for using your result

  • Use the due date from your scan if you have one; it is usually more accurate than counting from a remembered period.
  • If your cycles are irregular, the last-period method can be off by a week or more; choose Ultrasound if you have had a scan.
  • Read the ranges rather than the single date, especially if you are trying to match the date to a particular day.
  • Do not use a conception date to work out your baby's sex; timing has no effect on it.
  • Bring the scan report to your antenatal visits in Pakistan or anywhere else, so your doctor can confirm the dates and plan tests such as the anomaly scan at the right week.

How it works

Enter your due date, the first day of your last period with your cycle length, or the date and result of an ultrasound. The calculator counts back to the most likely day of conception, about two weeks after the start of the last period (later for long cycles, earlier for short ones). Because ovulation and conception vary by a few days, it also gives the most probable and the wider possible conception days, and the days on which sex could have led to the pregnancy, since sperm can survive for several days.

The formula

  • From a due date: conception C = due date − 266 days
  • From a last period: C = first day of last period + 14 days + (cycle length − 28) days
  • From an ultrasound: C = scan date − (7 × weeks + days) + 14 days
  • Estimated due date = C + 266 days (= first day of an equivalent 28-day-cycle period + 280 days)
  • Most probable conception days: C − 2 to C + 2; possible conception days: C − 3 to C + 7
  • Sex most likely to have led to it: C − 5 to C + 2; possible days of that sex: C − 8 to C + 7

A worked example

With a due date of March 21, 2027, conception most likely happened 266 days earlier, on June 28, 2026. The most probable conception days are June 26 to 30, and the sex that led to the pregnancy most probably happened between June 23 and June 30.

Questions

How do I calculate my conception date from my due date?

Count back 266 days, or 38 weeks, from the due date. A due date of April 21, 2027 points to conception around July 29, 2026, give or take a few days. The calculator also shows the most probable and possible days around it.

How do I work out the conception date from my last period?

Add 14 days to the first day of your last period if your cycle is 28 days. For longer or shorter cycles add or subtract the difference: with a 32-day cycle, add 18 days.

Can this tell me the exact day I got pregnant?

No. It gives the most likely day and a range around it. Ovulation can move by several days from cycle to cycle, and dating by the last period or a scan has its own error, so a single exact day cannot be known from dates alone.

Why is the range for sex wider than for conception?

Sperm can survive in the body for up to about five days, so sex several days before ovulation can still lead to conception. The egg lives only about a day after it is released, so the window after ovulation is short.

Which starting date gives the best answer?

A scan in the first trimester usually gives the most reliable dating. The last period works well when cycles are regular and the date is certain. A due date from your doctor is often already based on a scan.

How do I calculate pregnancy weeks from conception?

Add two weeks to the time since conception. Ten weeks after conception you are 12 weeks pregnant, because pregnancy is counted from the last period, about two weeks before conception.

Is conception the same as the start of pregnancy?

Not in the way weeks are counted. Pregnancy weeks start from the first day of the last period, about two weeks before conception, so at conception you are already counted as about two weeks pregnant.

Can the conception date tell me if it is a boy or a girl?

No. In the main study of timing and conception, the day of sex relative to ovulation made no difference to the baby's sex. A scan later in pregnancy or a blood test can show the sex.

Can a conception calculator prove who the father is?

No. The possible days span more than two weeks, and ovulation can vary, so dates cannot settle paternity. A DNA paternity test is the only reliable way.
