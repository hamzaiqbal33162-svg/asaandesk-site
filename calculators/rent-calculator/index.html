How much rent can I afford? What the rent calculator shows

A rent calculator turns your income into a rent budget. Enter what you earn before tax and what you already pay on debts each month, and it shows the most rent you can afford, a comfortable rent that leaves more room for saving, and the level many landlords look for when they check an application.

The limits come from simple rules of thumb used by lenders, landlords and housing programmes. They work in any currency, so the same page answers how much rent you can afford on a US salary in dollars or on a household income in Pakistani rupees.

How to use the rent calculator

  • Income before tax: your whole household income before tax, for everyone who will share the rent and the bills.
  • Income period: choose Yearly or Monthly to match the figure you typed.
  • Monthly debt payments: car, student and personal loan installments and the minimum payment on each card. Do not include your current rent, food or utility bills.
  • Currency: choose US dollar, Pakistani rupee, pound or another currency at the top of the calculator.

The results update as you type. If your debts already use up the limits, the calculator warns you and shows how much of your income they take.

The 28/36 rule, the 30% rule and the three-times-rent rule

The 28/36 rule says that housing should take no more than 28% of income before tax, and housing plus all other debt payments no more than 36%. It began as a mortgage lending guide and is a common way to size rent too. Both limits use your debt-to-income ratio, which the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau defines as all your monthly debt payments divided by your gross (pre-tax) monthly income.

The 30% rule comes from US housing policy. In the Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8) programme, HUD says the tenant's share of the rent and utilities is usually 30% of adjusted monthly income, and it can go as high as 40% when you choose a more expensive unit. The calculator's 28% and 36% limits sit either side of that 30% line.

The three-times-rent rule is a landlord's quick test: your income should be about three times the monthly rent, which means rent at a third (33.3%) of income. It is the most generous of the three, so passing a landlord's check does not mean the rent is comfortable.

Worked rent examples, in dollars and rupees

Example 1: you earn $72,000 a year and pay $450 a month on a car loan. Your income is $6,000 a month and the car takes 7.5% of it. The most rent you can afford is $1,710 a month, a comfortable rent is $1,230, and a landlord's one-third check would allow up to $2,000. Paying off the car would raise both limits by $450.

Example 2, in Pakistan: a household earns Rs 250,000 a month before tax and pays a Rs 30,000 car installment, 12% of income. The most rent is Rs 60,000 a month (Rs 720,000 a year), a comfortable rent is Rs 40,000, and a third of income is Rs 83,333. Without the installment, the same income would allow Rs 70,000 comfortably and Rs 90,000 at most.

Example 3, debts that crowd out rent: on $4,000 a month with $1,200 of debt payments (30% of income), the comfortable rent is $0 and the most you can afford is only $240. With $1,500 of debt payments (37.5%), even the 36% limit is used up, and the calculator warns that any rent will be hard to meet until some debt is paid down.

Working backwards: to rent a $2,000 apartment comfortably at 28% with no debts, you need about $85,714 a year before tax; to pass a landlord's three-times check you need $72,000.

Rent and tax in Pakistan: withholding on rent

If you rent to a company, a government office, a non-profit, a private school, a hospital or clinic, a boutique or beauty parlour, or an individual paying Rs 1.5 million or more in rent a year, the tenant must deduct income tax from the rent under section 155 of the Income Tax Ordinance, 2001. The rates for individuals and associations of persons, as amended up to 30 June 2026, depend on the gross rent for the year; a company landlord has 15% deducted from the gross rent.

Example: a company pays you Rs 150,000 a month, Rs 1,800,000 a year. The deduction is Rs 15,000 plus 10% of Rs 1,200,000, which is Rs 135,000 for the year. When you work out your own taxable property income, the Ordinance allows deductions such as a repairs allowance of one-fifth of the rent, insurance on the building and local property taxes. Tax rules change with each Finance Act, so check the current text on the FBR website.

Landlords also look at rental yield: the yearly rent as a percent of the property's value. Rs 1,800,000 of rent a year on a Rs 45,000,000 house is a gross yield of 4%. The Rental Property Calculator works out the yield and return after costs.

Sharing rent, rent increases and rent per square foot

  • Splitting rent with roommates: an equal split is simplest; splitting by income share or by room size is fairer when incomes or rooms differ. If one person earns 60% of the household income, their share of a $2,200 rent is $1,320.
  • Checking a rent increase: the increase is (new rent − old rent) ÷ old rent × 100. A rise from $1,850 to $1,980 is a 7.03% increase; the Percentage Calculator works it out.
  • Comparing places: divide the monthly rent by the floor area to get rent per square foot or square metre, so a large and a small apartment can be compared fairly.
  • Adding running costs: utilities, internet, renter's insurance, maintenance charges and transport all come on top of rent, so leave room for them inside your limit.

Tips for finding rent you can afford, and related tools

  • Aim for the comfortable figure, not the maximum, especially if your income varies or you have no emergency savings.
  • Pay down a small loan or card before you move; every payment you clear raises the rent you can afford by the same amount.
  • Budget for the move-in costs too: deposit, advance rent, agent fees and moving costs can add up to several months of rent.
  • Get the lease terms in writing, including when and by how much the rent can rise, and record the condition of the home with photos when you move in.

How it works

Enter your income before tax, per year or per month, and what you pay each month on loans and cards. Lenders and money advisers often use two limits: all your debt payments plus housing should stay under about 36% of your income before tax, and under 28% if you want room to breathe. The calculator takes your debt payments away from both limits, and what is left is the rent you can afford. It also shows a third of your income, a limit many landlords use when they check tenants.

The formula

  • Monthly income I = yearly income ÷ 12 (or the monthly income you typed)
  • Most rent you can afford = 36% × I − monthly debt payments
  • Comfortable rent = 28% × I − monthly debt payments
  • Landlord check = I ÷ 3

A worked example

With $72,000 a year and $450 a month of car loan payments, I = $6,000 a month. The most rent is 0.36 × 6,000 − 450 = $1,710, a comfortable rent is 0.28 × 6,000 − 450 = $1,230, and a third of income is $2,000.

Questions

How much rent can I afford based on my income?

A comfortable rent is about 28% of your monthly income before tax minus your debt payments, and the most is about 36% minus debts. On $60,000 a year with no debts that is $1,400 to $1,800 a month.

How do I calculate rent based on salary?

Divide your yearly salary by 12 to get monthly income, then take 28% (comfortable) or 36% (maximum) of it and subtract your monthly debt payments. Landlords often check that the rent is no more than a third of monthly income.

Should I use my income before or after tax?

The 28% and 36% limits are set against income before tax, so that is what the calculator expects. If you only know your take-home pay, enter it anyway: the result will simply be lower and safer.

What is the 3 times rent rule?

Many landlords want your income before tax to be at least three times the monthly rent. For a $2,000 rent that is $6,000 a month, or $72,000 a year. The calculator shows this as the landlord check.

Is spending 30% of income on rent too much?

Thirty percent is a common upper guide, and it is the usual tenant share in the US voucher programme. Whether it is too much depends on your other costs: with large debts, childcare or an irregular income, aim nearer the 28% comfortable figure or below it.

Which debts count?

Count the payments you must make every month: car loans, student loans, personal loans and the minimum on each card. Do not count your current rent, food or utility bills.

How much tax is deducted from rent in Pakistan?

When the tenant is a prescribed person, such as a company, tax is deducted under section 155: nil up to Rs 300,000 of rent a year, then 5%, 10% and 25% in bands for individual landlords, and 15% for company landlords, as amended up to 30 June 2026.

How do I calculate a rent increase percentage?

Subtract the old rent from the new one, divide by the old rent and multiply by 100. Going from $1,850 to $1,980 is a 7.03% increase.

How do I calculate rental yield?

Divide the yearly rent by the property's price and multiply by 100. Rs 1,800,000 a year on a Rs 45,000,000 property is a gross yield of 4%; take off costs such as repairs and taxes for the net yield.

What if the result is zero?

Then your debt payments already use up the limit, and any rent would stretch your budget. Paying off a card or a small loan first, or sharing the rent, frees up room.

Does it work in other currencies?

Yes. Pick your currency symbol at the top of the calculator. The percentages are the same in any country; the symbol only changes how the amounts are shown.
