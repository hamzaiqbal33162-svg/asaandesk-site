What the margin calculator works out

A margin calculator answers three different questions that all use the word margin. The first tab finds your profit margin: how much of each sale you keep after paying for what you sold. The second tab finds the cash you need to buy shares on margin, with the broker lending the rest. The third tab finds the margin a forex or gold trade needs at a given leverage.

Most people need the first tab. Profit margin is profit divided by the selling price. If you buy something for 120 and sell it for 160, you make 40, and 40 ÷ 160 = 25%. That 25% is the margin; the same 40 divided by the cost, 40 ÷ 120 = 33.33%, is the markup.

How to use the margin calculator, step by step

  • Choose your currency.
  • Profit margin tab: in I know the, pick the two values you have: Cost and revenue, Cost and margin, Cost and profit, Revenue and margin, Revenue and profit, or Margin and profit. Only those two boxes appear.
  • Enter them. Revenue is the selling price; the margin is a percent of that price. Type a loss with a minus sign.
  • Stock margin tab: enter the Share price, the Number of shares and the Margin requirement, the share of the price you pay yourself (50% by default).
  • Currency margin tab: enter the Exchange rate (your currency for one unit of the currency you trade), the Units of currency traded and the Leverage, from 1:1 to 500:1.

Margin formula vs markup formula

The margin formula is (selling price − cost) ÷ selling price × 100. The markup formula is (selling price − cost) ÷ cost × 100. Because the price is always bigger than the cost when you make a profit, the markup percent is always bigger than the margin percent for the same sale. Mixing them up is the most common pricing mistake: adding a 25% markup to a cost gives only a 20% margin.

To set a price for a target margin, divide the cost by (1 − margin). A product that costs Rs 2,400 needs a price of 2,400 ÷ 0.80 = Rs 3,000 for a 20% margin. Working the other way, if a shop sells an item for Rs 1,500 and wants a 35% margin, the most it can pay for it is 1,500 × 0.65 = Rs 975.

Buying stock on margin and margin calls

Buying on margin means borrowing part of the price from your broker. In the US, Regulation T lets a firm lend up to 50% of the price of an eligible stock, and FINRA rules require at least $2,000 in the account before you trade on margin. With the defaults, 100 shares at $50 cost $5,000: you put in $2,500 and borrow $2,500.

After the purchase, FINRA requires your own share (equity) to stay at least 25% of the market value, and many brokers set a higher house requirement of 30% or 40%. If the price falls far enough, you get a margin call to add cash or securities. FINRA notes that firms do not have to call you first; they may sell your shares to cover the loan.

You can estimate the price that triggers a call: loan ÷ (shares × (1 − maintenance requirement)). For the default trade that is 2,500 ÷ (100 × 0.75) = $33.33 at 25%, or $35.71 at a 30% house rule. A drop of about a third wipes out most of your own money, which is why margin magnifies losses as well as gains, and the loan charges interest the whole time.

Forex and gold margin: how leverage works

In currency trading, the margin is the deposit a broker holds while your trade is open: position value ÷ leverage. A 10,000-unit EUR/USD trade at 1.10 is worth $11,000. At 50:1 it needs $220 (2%), at 30:1 $366.67 (3.33%) and at 20:1 $550 (5%).

Regulators cap leverage for retail traders. In the US, CFTC rules (17 CFR 5.9) require a deposit of at least 2% for major currency pairs and 5% for others, which is 50:1 and 20:1. In the UK, the FCA's permanent rules for CFDs allow at most 30:1 on major currency pairs, 20:1 on other pairs and gold, 10:1 on other commodities, 5:1 on shares and 2:1 on crypto assets. Brokers can ask for more.

For gold quoted as XAU/USD, enter the ounces as the units and the gold price as the exchange rate. At an example price of $2,500 an ounce, 10 ounces are worth $25,000 and need $1,250 at 20:1. Check the contract size with your broker, since a lot can mean different amounts.

Other margin terms people search for

  • Margin of error: the plus-or-minus range around a survey or sample result. Work it out with the Confidence Interval Calculator.
  • Marginal tax rate: the tax rate on your last unit of income, not your average rate. See the Income Tax Calculator.
  • Marginal cost and marginal revenue: the extra cost of making one more unit and the extra revenue from selling it. Profit is highest where the two meet.
  • Margin of safety: how far sales can fall before a business stops making a profit, (actual sales − break-even sales) ÷ actual sales.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Decide whether a target is a margin or a markup before you price anything; a supplier's 30% markup is not your 30% margin.
  • Include every cost of the item, such as delivery and packaging, or the margin looks better than it is.
  • Discounts hit margin hard. Taking 10% off a price of 100 with a 25% margin cuts the profit from 25 to 15, a margin of only 16.67%, so 40% of the profit is gone.
  • On a margin loan, count the interest and know your call price before you buy.
  • With leverage, a 1% move against a 50:1 position loses half the deposit. Size trades by what you can afford to lose, not by the margin needed.

How it works

Profit is the selling price (revenue) minus the cost. The margin is that profit as a share of the selling price, and the markup is the same profit as a share of the cost, so the two always describe the same sale from different ends. Pick which two values you know and the calculator works out the rest. The stock tool multiplies the price by the number of shares and by the share your broker asks you to pay yourself. The currency tool divides the value of the position by the leverage.

The formula

  • Profit = revenue − cost
  • Margin = profit ÷ revenue × 100; markup = profit ÷ cost × 100
  • From cost and margin m: revenue = cost ÷ (1 − m); from revenue and margin: cost = revenue × (1 − m)
  • Markup from margin = m ÷ (1 − m); margin from markup k = k ÷ (1 + k)
  • Stock margin: cash needed = share price × shares × margin requirement
  • Currency margin: money needed = units × exchange rate ÷ leverage

A worked example

A shop buys a product for Rs 2,400 and wants a 20% margin. The price is 2,400 ÷ (1 − 0.20) = Rs 3,000, the profit is Rs 600, and that is 20% of the price but a 25% markup on the cost.

Questions

How do I calculate margin percentage?

Subtract the cost from the selling price to get the profit, divide the profit by the selling price and multiply by 100. Selling for 160 what cost 120 gives 40 ÷ 160 × 100 = 25%.

What is the difference between margin and markup?

Both use the same profit. Margin divides it by the selling price, markup by the cost. Selling for $160 what cost $120 is a 25% margin and a 33.33% markup.

What is the margin formula from cost?

To reach a target margin from a cost, the selling price is cost ÷ (1 − margin). A cost of Rs 2,400 and a 20% margin give a price of Rs 3,000.

What is the margin formula in Excel?

With the cost in A2 and the price in B2, type =(B2-A2)/B2 and format the cell as a percent. For the price that gives a margin in C2, use =A2/(1-C2).

Why can a margin not reach 100%?

A 100% margin would mean the cost is zero. From a real cost, every price gives a margin below 100%, however high the price goes, so the calculator asks for a margin below 100% in that case.

Can I enter a loss?

Yes. Type the profit with a minus sign, or enter a cost higher than the revenue. The margin and markup then come out negative.

What does buying stock on margin mean?

You pay part of the price and borrow the rest from your broker. In the US you must pay at least 50% when you buy, and keep at least 25% of the value (often more, by broker rules) as the price changes.

What is a margin call?

A demand from your broker to add cash or securities when your own share of a margin account falls below the maintenance requirement. If you do not, or even before asking, the broker can sell your holdings to cover the loan.

How do I calculate margin in forex?

Multiply the units by the exchange rate to get the position value, then divide by the leverage. 10,000 units of EUR/USD at 1.10 with 30:1 leverage need 11,000 ÷ 30 = $366.67.

How does leverage change the currency margin?

Leverage of 50:1 means you put up 1/50 of the position, or 2%. Higher leverage needs less money up front but makes every price move count for more, both ways.

How do I work out the margin for gold (XAU/USD)?

Enter the ounces as the units, the gold price per ounce as the exchange rate and your leverage. Ten ounces at an example price of $2,500 at 20:1 need $1,250.
