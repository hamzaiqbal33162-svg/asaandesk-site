What the dew point is, in simple terms

A dew point calculator finds the temperature at which air becomes saturated with water vapour. Cool air down to its dew point and it cannot hold all its moisture any more, so water starts to condense: dew on grass, fog in a valley, drops on a cold glass or a steamed-up window. If the dew point is below freezing, the moisture can turn to frost instead.

The dew point is a direct measure of how much water is in the air. Relative humidity is not: it is the share of the most the air could hold at its current temperature, so it rises at night and falls in the afternoon even when the moisture stays the same. That is why weather forecasters and pilots watch the dew point, and why a 60% humidity reading means something very different on a cold morning and a hot afternoon.

Air can never have a dew point above its own temperature. At 100% relative humidity the two are equal, and the gap between them (the dew point depression, T − Td) shrinks as the air gets closer to saturation.

Understanding the results

The headline is the value you asked for, in the unit of the box you started from, with the other two temperature units beneath it. Water vapour pressure (e) is the part of the air pressure that comes from water vapour; Saturation vapour pressure (es) is the most it could be at the air temperature. Their ratio is the relative humidity.

Absolute humidity is the mass of water vapour in each cubic meter of air, in grams. Moisture by volume (in parts per million and percent) is the share of air molecules that are water, and Moisture by weight is the share of the air's mass. Below 0°C the page notes that the answer is the dew point over liquid water; the frost point, where ice forms directly, is a little higher.

People used to humid climates tolerate higher dew points, and very low dew points bring their own problems such as dry skin and static. The table is a rule of thumb, not a health limit.

Worked examples in °C and °F

Humid heat. At 34°C and 70% relative humidity the dew point is 27.7°C (81.9°F) and the air holds 26.29 g of water per cubic meter. That is far above the oppressive line in the table, which is why such an afternoon feels heavy even in the shade.

Dry heat in Fahrenheit. At 86°F and 40% humidity the dew point is 58.9°F (14.9°C). The air is hot, but sweat still evaporates well because the dew point is in the comfortable-to-sticky band.

Finding humidity. A room at 22°C with a dew point of 9°C has a relative humidity of 43.42%: the vapour pressure at the dew point (1,148 Pa) divided by the saturation pressure at 22°C (2,644 Pa).

Finding the air temperature. If the dew point is 18°C and the humidity reads 80%, the air must be 21.6°C (70.9°F). Weather stations and hygrometers that report only two of the three values can be checked this way.

Winter. Outside air at −5°C and 80% humidity has a dew point of −7.9°C (17.8°F) and only 2.73 g/m³ of water. Heat that air indoors to 21°C without adding moisture and its relative humidity falls sharply, which is why heated rooms feel dry in winter.

Condensation, mould, walls and painting

Any surface colder than the dew point of the air touching it will get wet. Indoor air at 21°C and 55% humidity has a dew point of 11.6°C (52.9°F), so a single-glazed window, a cold water pipe or an outside wall corner below 11.6°C collects water. Repeated wetting is what lets mould grow on window frames and in wardrobe corners.

There are two ways out: keep the surface warmer (insulation, double glazing, moving furniture away from cold walls) or lower the dew point of the room (ventilation, extractor fans, a dehumidifier). Lowering the humidity in that room to 45% would bring the dew point down to 8.6°C.

Builders checking a wall assembly compare the temperature inside each layer of the wall with the dew point of the indoor air; where a layer is colder than the dew point, vapour can condense inside the wall. This page gives the dew point; the temperatures through the layers come from a separate heat-flow calculation. Paint and coating makers likewise set a minimum gap between the surface temperature and the dew point, so check the product data sheet before you paint on a cool, damp morning.

The dew point formula, Excel and a quick rule of thumb

This calculator uses the Buck (1996) equation for the saturation vapour pressure over water and solves it backwards exactly, so all three tabs agree. Its coefficients are fitted for good accuracy from −80°C to 50°C; outside −40°C to 50°C the page adds a warning.

For a spreadsheet, the Magnus approximation is the usual choice. With T in °C in cell A1 and RH in % in B1, γ = LN(B1/100) + 17.625*A1/(243.04+A1) and the dew point is 243.04*γ/(17.625−γ). As one Excel formula: =243.04*(LN(B1/100)+17.625*A1/(243.04+A1))/(17.625-LN(B1/100)-17.625*A1/(243.04+A1)). For 34°C and 70% it gives 27.75°C, within 0.1°C of the Buck answer here.

Without a calculator, a rule published by Mark Lawrence in 2005 works when the humidity is above 50%: Td ≈ T − (100 − RH) ÷ 5 in °C. For 34°C and 70% that gives 34 − 6 = 28°C. Put another way, relative humidity drops about 5 percentage points for every 1°C between the air temperature and the dew point.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Do not compare relative humidity readings taken at different temperatures; compare dew points instead.
  • Wet-bulb and dry-bulb readings from a sling psychrometer are not entered directly here; convert them to relative humidity first with the psychrometer's table.
  • The air pressure box here does not give the pressure dew point of compressed air. Compressing air raises its vapour pressure and so its dew point; use the compressor or dryer maker's method for that.
  • Use the indoor dew point, not the outdoor one, when checking for condensation inside a building.
  • Dew points below 0°C here are over liquid water; for frost on a car or a freezer coil, the frost point is a little higher.

How it works

Pick what you want to find and enter the other two values, each temperature with its own unit. Warm air can hold more water vapour than cold air. The saturation vapour pressure is the most vapour the air can hold at its temperature; the relative humidity says how much of that it holds now. Cool the air down and at some point it is full: that temperature is the dew point, where dew, fog or condensation on a cold glass appear. The calculator uses one vapour pressure formula and solves it exactly backwards, so the three tabs always agree. It also gives the absolute humidity (grams of water in a cubic meter of air) and the moisture by volume and by weight.

The formula

  • Saturation vapour pressure over water (Buck 1996): es(T) = 611.21 × e^((18.678 − T/234.5) × T/(257.14 + T)) Pa, T in °C
  • Vapour pressure: e = RH/100 × es(T)
  • Dew point Td: es(Td) = e (solved exactly); relative humidity: RH = 100 × es(Td)/es(T)
  • Absolute humidity = e ÷ (461.5 × T in kelvin) kg/m³ (× 1,000 for g/m³)
  • Moisture by volume = e ÷ air pressure × 10⁶ ppm; by weight = by volume × 18.015/28.97

A worked example

A muggy afternoon of 34°C with 70% relative humidity: es = 5,323 Pa, so e = 0.70 × 5,323 = 3,726 Pa. The temperature whose saturation pressure is 3,726 Pa is 27.7°C (81.9°F), so any surface cooler than 27.7°C gets wet. Each cubic meter of that air holds 3,726 ÷ (461.5 × 307.15) = 26.29 g of water vapour.

Questions

How do I calculate dew point from temperature and relative humidity?

Find the saturation vapour pressure at the air temperature, multiply it by the relative humidity to get the actual vapour pressure, then find the temperature whose saturation pressure equals that value. The Find dew point tab does all three steps; 34°C at 70% gives 27.7°C.

What is the difference between dew point and humidity?

Relative humidity is a percentage that changes with temperature even when the amount of water in the air stays the same. The dew point measures the water itself: the higher it is, the more moisture there is. That is why the dew point is a better guide to how muggy it feels.

What dew point feels comfortable?

The US National Weather Service puts it this way: a dew point of 55°F (about 13°C) or lower feels dry and comfortable, 55 to 65°F (13 to 18°C) becomes sticky with muggy evenings, and 65°F (18°C) or higher feels oppressive.

How do I calculate dew point in Fahrenheit?

Set the air temperature unit to °F and type the temperature and humidity; the dew point comes back in °F with °C and K below it. 86°F at 40% gives a dew point of 58.9°F.

Can the dew point be higher than the air temperature?

No. At 100% humidity the dew point equals the air temperature; the air cannot hold more vapour than that, and the extra turns into droplets as fog, cloud or dew. The calculator refuses a dew point above the air temperature.

What is dew point depression?

It is the air temperature minus the dew point. A small gap means the air is close to saturation and fog or dew is likely; a large gap means dry air. For example, 22°C air with a 9°C dew point has a depression of 13°C and a humidity of 43.42%.

Is there a simple dew point formula?

When the humidity is above 50%, Td ≈ T − (100 − RH) ÷ 5 in °C is usually within about 1°C. For an exact answer, or for dry air, use the calculator.

How do I use the dew point to stop condensation and mould?

Any surface colder than the dew point gets wet: windows, cold water pipes, walls in a cool room. Keep surfaces warmer than the dew point, or lower the dew point by ventilating or using a dehumidifier.

Does air pressure change the dew point?

In this calculator the pressure box changes only the moisture by volume and by weight, because the dew point is worked out from the vapour pressure. In compressed air systems, where the whole gas is squeezed, the dew point does rise, and that pressure dew point needs a different method.

What happens when the dew point is below freezing?

Moisture can then form frost instead of dew. The figure shown is the dew point over liquid water; the frost point for the same air is slightly higher, because ice forms more readily than supercooled water.
