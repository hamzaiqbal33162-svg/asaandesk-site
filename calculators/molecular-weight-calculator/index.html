How it works

Type the formula of a molecule or salt, or pick a common one. Write element symbols with a capital first letter and a small second letter (Na, Cl, Fe), put the number of atoms after each symbol, and use brackets for groups that repeat, such as Ca(OH)2 or Al2(SO4)3. For a hydrate, join the parts with a dot: CuSO4·5H2O (a full stop or * works too). The calculator counts every element, multiplies each count by its standard atomic weight and adds them up. The table shows how much each element adds and its share of the mass and of the atoms.

The formula

  • Molar mass = sum over the elements of (count × standard atomic weight)
  • Brackets: every count inside (…)ₙ, […]ₙ or {…}ₙ is multiplied by n
  • Hydrates: CuSO4·5H2O = CuSO4 + 5 × H2O
  • Share of mass of an element = count × atomic weight ÷ molar mass × 100%
  • Mass of one molecule = molar mass ÷ Avogadro constant (6.02214076 × 10²³ per mole)

A worked example

Copper(II) sulfate pentahydrate, CuSO4·5H2O: Cu 1 × 63.546 + S 1 × 32.06 + O 9 × 15.999 + H 10 × 1.0080 = 63.546 + 32.06 + 143.991 + 10.08 = 249.677 g/mol. The five water molecules add 5 × 18.015 = 90.075 g/mol, which is 36.08% of the mass.

Questions

Are molecular weight and molar mass the same?

For everyday chemistry, yes. The molar mass is the mass of one mole in grams (g/mol); the molecular weight is the same number without a unit, or in daltons for a single molecule. Water is 18.015 g/mol, and one water molecule weighs 18.015 Da.

Why does the formula have to be case-sensitive?

Because capital letters start a new element. Co is cobalt, but CO is carbon and oxygen (carbon monoxide). Likewise Sn is tin and SN would be sulfur and nitrogen.

Which atomic weights are used?

The abridged standard atomic weights published by IUPAC's Commission on Isotopic Abundances and Atomic Weights in 2024, to five significant figures. Elements with no stable isotope, such as technetium, have no standard atomic weight, so the mass number of their longest-lived isotope is used and shown in brackets.

How do I enter a hydrate or a complex?

Join the parts of a hydrate with a dot, a full stop or a star: CuSO4·5H2O, CuSO4.5H2O or CuSO4*5H2O all work. Square or curly brackets can hold round ones, as in [Co(NH3)6]Cl3. Leave out charges: type the neutral salt.
