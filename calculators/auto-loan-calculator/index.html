What an auto loan calculator shows

An auto loan calculator works out your monthly car payment from the car price, the down payment, the loan term and the interest rate. This one also counts what the payment leaves out: sales tax under your state's rules, title and registration fees, a cash rebate, a trade-in and any amount still owed on it. You see the loan amount, the cash you need at the start, the total interest and the total cost of the car.

It also works the other way. On From a monthly budget you enter the payment you can afford, and it finds the most expensive car that payment buys once tax, fees and your trade-in are included.

The maths is the standard one for fixed-rate installment loans, so it works for new and used cars, dealer and bank loans, and terms from 1 to 120 months, including 84 and 96-month loans. The figures are in US dollars, but the payment formula is the same in any currency.

How to use the auto loan calculator, step by step

  • Choose From the car price (you know the price) or From a monthly budget (you know the payment you can afford).
  • Enter the Car price or the Monthly payment you can afford, then the Down payment.
  • Enter the Loan term in months (60 months is 5 years, 72 is 6, 84 is 7) and the Interest rate (APR) as a yearly percentage.
  • Open Trade-in and rebate to add a Cash rebate or incentive, your Trade-in value and the amount Still owed on the trade-in.
  • Open Sales tax and fees and choose your State. The state sets the rules for the trade-in and the rebate but not the rate, so type the Sales tax rate yourself: your state plus local rate for vehicles.
  • Enter Title, registration and other fees, and tick Add the sales tax and fees to the loan if you will finance them instead of paying them upfront.

The auto loan formula, and how to calculate it in Excel

The car loan formula is M = L × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n), where M is the monthly payment, L the loan amount, r the monthly rate (APR ÷ 12, as a decimal) and n the number of months. For $30,000 at 6.5% over 60 months, r = 0.065 ÷ 12 = 0.0054167, and M = $586.98. At 0% APR the payment is simply the loan divided by the months: $500 for $30,000 over 60 months.

To calculate auto loan interest for any month, multiply the balance by the monthly rate: $30,000 × 0.0054167 = $162.50 in month 1. The rest of the payment reduces the balance, so the interest falls a little every month. Total interest is all the payments minus the loan: 60 × $586.98 − $30,000 comes to about $5,219, or $5,219.07 when the payment is not rounded to cents.

In Excel or Google Sheets, use =PMT(6.5%/12, 60, −30000), which returns 586.98. The PMT function takes the rate per period, the number of payments and the present value, so divide a yearly APR by 12 and count the term in months. Enter the loan as a negative number to get a positive payment.

For a payoff figure, read the balance after your latest payment in the Monthly schedule; a lender's payoff quote may add the interest that has built up since that payment. Loan-to-value (LTV) is the loan divided by the car's value: borrowing $30,000 on a $35,000 car is an LTV of about 86%, and rolling in negative equity can push it above 100%. To see what extra payments save, use the Amortization Calculator.

The Federal Reserve's consumer credit release of 6 November 2026 puts the average commercial bank rate on new car loans at 7.54% for 60 months and 7.17% for 72 months in the third quarter of 2026 (preliminary), against 8.16% and 8.29% for 2024. Your own rate depends on your credit history, the lender and the term, so use the rate you are actually quoted.

Sales tax on a car: state rules for trade-ins and rebates

Most states charge sales tax on the price minus your trade-in. Eight places tax the full price instead: in the West, California, Hawaii and Montana; in the Midwest, Michigan; and in the South, Kentucky, Maryland, Virginia and Washington, DC.

A maker's cash rebate lowers the taxed price in 21 states. In the West these are Alaska, Arizona, Montana, Oregon, Utah and Wyoming; in the Midwest, Iowa, Kansas, Minnesota, Missouri and Nebraska; in the South, Delaware, Kentucky, Louisiana, Oklahoma and Texas; and in the Northeast, Massachusetts, New Hampshire, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island and Vermont. Everywhere else, tax is charged on the price before the rebate. These are the rules the calculator applies, as researched in November 2026; they can change, so confirm a large purchase with your state's revenue department.

Is car loan interest tax deductible? The 2025 to 2028 rule

From 2025 through 2028, US taxpayers can deduct up to $10,000 a year of interest on a qualifying car loan, whether or not they itemize, under the 2025 tax law known as the One, Big, Beautiful Bill. The deduction phases out when modified adjusted gross income is over $100,000, or $200,000 for joint filers.

The loan must have started after 31 December 2024 and be secured by the vehicle, and the vehicle must be new, for personal use, under 14,000 pounds and finally assembled in the United States: a car, minivan, van, SUV, pickup or motorcycle. Used cars and lease payments do not qualify, and you must put the vehicle identification number (VIN) on your return.

The Yearly schedule shows the interest you pay each year, which is the figure the deduction is based on. In the default example, year 1 interest is $1,795.47. The tax saving depends on your own tax rate, so treat it as a bonus, not as part of the payment you can afford.

Dealer financing, rebates, negative equity and add-ons

  • Get preapproved first. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) suggests starting with a preapproval from a bank, credit union or other lender, which gives you a rate, a term and a maximum amount to compare with the dealer's offer.
  • Dealer-arranged rates can include a markup: the CFPB explains that the dealer's rate is the lender's buy rate plus extra interest that pays the dealer for handling the financing. It also notes that negotiating can save hundreds or even thousands of dollars over the life of a loan.
  • Cash rebate or low rate: some deals let you choose either a cash rebate or a low promotional rate. Compare both with the Cash Back or Low Interest Calculator.
  • Negative equity: when you owe more on your trade-in than it is worth, a dealer may roll the balance into the new loan, which the CFPB warns makes the new loan more expensive. The calculator adds that gap to the loan and says so under the result. With a $10,000 trade-in and $14,000 still owed, the default loan grows to $34,000 and the payment to $665.25.
  • Tax and fees in the loan: financing them in the default example raises the payment from $586.98 to $654.49 and adds $600.19 of interest.
  • GAP insurance covers the difference between the loan balance and the car's value if the car is stolen or written off. The CFPB notes it is generally optional, does not have to be bought from the lender, and, if bought from the dealer, is added to the amount financed, so you pay interest on it.

Car financing in Pakistan: State Bank of Pakistan rules

In Pakistan, bank car loans follow the State Bank of Pakistan's Prudential Regulations for Consumer Financing. Under SBP circulars of September 2021 and May 2022, the minimum down payment is 30% of the vehicle's value, the total auto finance one person can have from all banks and DFIs together is capped at Rs 3,000,000, and imported vehicles, new or used, cannot be financed by banks. The longest term is 3 years for vehicles above 1,000cc and 5 years for vehicles up to 1,000cc, and the 2021 rules also cap total loan payments at 40% of net disposable income. We found no later change as of November 2026; ask your bank for its current terms.

Example: a 1,300cc car priced at Rs 5,500,000. A 30% down payment is Rs 1,650,000, but that leaves Rs 3,850,000, above the Rs 3,000,000 cap, so you would need at least Rs 2,500,000 down. Over the maximum 36 months at an assumed 14% a year, the payment on Rs 3,000,000 is Rs 102,533 a month with Rs 691,184 of interest. Under the 40% limit, that payment needs a net monthly income of at least about Rs 256,333.

A car of up to 1,000cc priced at Rs 3,000,000 with 30% down (Rs 900,000) leaves a loan of Rs 2,100,000. At the same assumed 14%, it costs Rs 48,863 a month over 60 months (Rs 831,800 of interest) or Rs 71,773 a month over 36 months (Rs 483,829 of interest).

To run these numbers here, ignore the dollar sign: leave State as Not chosen, set the sales tax rate to 0 unless you want to include a tax, and put registration and other costs in the fees. Banks set their own car loan rates; the SBP policy rate (12% from 27 October 2026) is a useful benchmark, and you should ask for the rate and the full payment schedule in writing. Islamic banks offer car finance through their own contracts, so compare the total amount you will pay rather than the rate alone.

Tips, common mistakes and related calculators

  • Negotiate the car price first, then the loan; a low payment can hide a long term or a high rate.
  • Compare total cost, not the monthly payment: the 96-month loan above costs $8,552.35 in interest against $3,100.92 over 36 months.
  • Type your real sales tax rate; choosing a state changes the trade-in and rebate rules only.
  • Check what you still owe on your trade-in before you shop, so negative equity does not surprise you at the dealer.
  • Pay tax and fees upfront if you can, so you do not pay interest on them for years.
  • Read the contract before signing and make sure the numbers match the deal, as the CFPB advises.

How it works

On From the car price, enter the price, your down payment, the loan term in months and the APR to get the monthly payment. On From a monthly budget, enter the payment you can afford instead and the calculator works back to the most expensive car it pays for. Under More options add a cash rebate, a trade-in and what you still owe on it, your state, the sales tax rate and the title and registration fees, and choose whether tax and fees are paid upfront or added to the loan. The state decides the tax rules: in most states the trade-in lowers the taxed price, and in some a maker's cash rebate is not taxed.

The formula

  • Taxed price = price − trade-in (if the state gives trade-in credit) − rebate (if the state does not tax rebates); sales tax = taxed price × rate
  • Loan = price − rebate − down payment − (trade-in − still owed) + (tax + fees, if added to the loan)
  • Paid upfront = down payment + (tax + fees, if not added to the loan)
  • Monthly rate r = APR ÷ 100 ÷ 12; monthly payment M = loan × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n); at 0% M = loan ÷ n
  • From a budget: loan = M × (1 − (1 + r)^−n) ÷ r, then the price whose loan is exactly that
  • Total cost = price − rebate + sales tax + fees + total interest

A worked example

A $35,000 car with $5,000 down over 60 months at 6.5% APR: the loan is $30,000, r = 0.065 ÷ 12 and M = 30,000 × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−60) = $586.98 a month. With 7% sales tax ($2,450) and $1,000 of fees paid upfront you pay $8,450 at the start, and the car costs $43,669.07 in all, including $5,219.07 of interest.

Questions

How do I calculate an auto loan payment?

Take the loan amount (price minus down payment, rebate and trade-in equity), the monthly rate (APR ÷ 12) and the number of months, and use M = L × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n). For $30,000 at 6.5% over 60 months that is $586.98 a month.

How do I calculate an auto loan payment in Excel?

Use =PMT(APR/12, months, −loan). =PMT(6.5%/12, 60, −30000) returns 586.98, the same as the calculator's default result.

How is auto loan interest calculated?

Each month the lender charges the balance times the monthly rate, and the rest of your payment lowers the balance. On $30,000 at 6.5%, month 1 interest is $162.50; the Monthly schedule shows every month after that.

What is the auto loan interest rate right now?

The Federal Reserve's latest figures put the average commercial bank rate on new car loans at 7.54% for 60 months and 7.17% for 72 months in the third quarter of 2026 (preliminary). Your own rate depends on your credit, the lender and the term.

Does my trade-in lower the sales tax?

In most states, yes: tax is charged on the price minus the trade-in value. Eight places tax the full price instead: California, Hawaii, Montana and Michigan, plus Kentucky, Maryland, Virginia and Washington, DC. Ohio gives the credit only on new cars. Choose your state and the result shows which rule was used.

Is a cash rebate taxed?

It depends on the state. In states such as Texas, Minnesota and Pennsylvania the rebate lowers the taxed price; in Florida, California and many others tax is charged on the price before the rebate. Rules change, so check with your state's revenue department for a big purchase.

Can I use it for an 84-month or 96-month car loan?

Yes, the term goes up to 120 months. On $30,000 at 6.5%, 84 months costs $445.48 a month and 96 months $401.59, but the interest rises to $7,420.58 and $8,552.35.

Does it work for a used car?

Yes. Enter the used car's price and the APR you are offered; the loan maths is the same. Check your state's tax rules for used cars, since some, such as Ohio, treat trade-ins differently.

What if I owe more on my trade-in than it is worth?

That gap, called negative equity, does not disappear: the dealer pays off your old loan and adds the difference to the new one. The calculator includes it in the loan amount and says so under the result.

Should I add the sales tax and fees to the loan?

Adding them means less cash at the start, but you then pay interest on them for the whole loan. In the default example that is $600.19 of extra interest, so paying upfront costs less if you have the cash.

What are the car loan rules in Pakistan?

Under State Bank of Pakistan rules, banks need at least a 30% down payment, total car finance per person is capped at Rs 3,000,000, and terms are at most 3 years for cars above 1,000cc and 5 years up to 1,000cc. Imported vehicles cannot be financed by banks.

Is car loan interest tax deductible in the US?

For 2025 to 2028, up to $10,000 a year of interest on a loan for a new, US-assembled vehicle for personal use can be deducted, with a phase-out above $100,000 of modified adjusted gross income ($200,000 joint). Used cars and leases do not qualify.
