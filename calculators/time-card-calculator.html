What the time card calculator does

This time card calculator adds up a week of work: for each day you type the start time, the end time and the unpaid break, and it gives the hours worked per day and for the week, in hours and minutes and in decimal hours. Turn on pay and it multiplies by your hourly rate, with overtime worked out by the rule you choose.

It handles lunch breaks, night shifts that end the next morning, rounding to the nearest 5, 10 or 15 minutes, a weekly or daily overtime limit and any currency, so it works as a timesheet for an employee, a freelancer or a small payroll.

How to fill in the time card

  • Monday to Friday are open; Saturday and Sunday are under their own heading. Leave any day off empty.
  • Start and End: type times like 8:30 AM, 8:30, 830 or 17:30. An end time without AM or PM is the first such time after the start, so 9:00 to 5:30 means 5:30 PM. An end earlier than the start ends the next day.
  • Break: the unpaid break as h:mm (0:30, 1:00) or minutes (30, 45). Forms like 1h 15m also work.
  • Work out the pay: type the pay per hour, pick your currency, and choose the overtime rule with its limit and multiplier (1.5 is time and a half, 2 is double time).
  • More options: round each day's hours, list days with no times, rename the days, and add a report title and notes for printing.

How to calculate time card hours manually

For each day, take the start time from the end time and subtract the break. Work in 24-hour time to avoid AM and PM slips: 9:00 AM to 7:00 PM is 9:00 to 19:00, which is 10 hours, and less a 45-minute lunch leaves 9 hours 15 minutes. For a night shift, add 24 hours to the end time: 10:00 PM to 6:30 AM is 22:00 to 30:30, which is 8 hours 30 minutes before the break.

Add up the days in hours and minutes, carrying every 60 minutes into an hour. Then turn the minutes into hundredths of an hour for pay by dividing by 60: 45 minutes is 0.75, 15 minutes 0.25 and 20 minutes 0.33. A 7 h 45 min day is 7.75 hours, and at $18.50 an hour it pays 7.75 × 18.50 = $143.38.

Finally split the week into regular and overtime hours under your rule, multiply each by its rate and add them. This is exactly what the calculator does, rounding money to the cent on each line as a pay slip does.

Overtime rules: weekly, daily and California

Under the US Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA), covered non-exempt employees must be paid at least one and a half times their regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek. The Department of Labor defines the workweek as a fixed, regularly recurring period of 168 hours, seven consecutive 24-hour periods, that need not start on Monday. Hours may not be averaged over two or more weeks, and the overtime requirement cannot be waived by agreement.

Some states add daily rules. California's Labor Commissioner says hours over 8 in a workday, up to 12, are paid at 1.5 times the regular rate and hours over 12 at double time, with overtime after 40 hours in the week as well and special rules for the seventh consecutive day. This calculator applies one rule at a time: for the week above, the daily limit of 8 hours gives 1 h 45 min of overtime and $848.71, while the weekly rule gives $878.75. Where both rules apply, ask your employer or the state labor office how they are combined for your pay.

The other options cover jobs without overtime: every hour at the base rate, or pay up to a daily or weekly cap. At a rate of Rs 650 an hour with no overtime, the default week of 41 h 30 min comes to Rs 26,975.00. Overtime rules outside the US, including in Pakistan, are set by local labour laws and contracts, so set the limits to match yours.

Rounding time card hours to the quarter hour

US rules allow employers to round recorded starting and stopping times to the nearest 5 minutes, or the nearest tenth or quarter of an hour, as long as over time the rounding does not leave employees unpaid for time they actually worked (29 CFR 785.48). Rounding is optional; choose it only if your employer rounds.

The calculator rounds each day's total to the nearest step. With 15-minute rounding, a day from 8:52 AM to 5:29 PM with a 1-hour break is 7 h 37 min and becomes 7 h 30 min, while 8:58 AM to 5:07 PM with a 30-minute break is 7 h 39 min and becomes 7 h 45 min. The two days total 15 h 16 min, or 15 h 15 min rounded, which pays $305.00 at $20 an hour.

Night shifts, split shifts and two-week pay periods

  • Night shift: type the clock times as they are, such as 10:00 PM to 6:30 AM. The day is marked "next day" and counted on the day the shift started; four such shifts with a 30-minute break are 32 hours, or $704.00 at $22 an hour.
  • Multiple in and out on one day: for a split shift such as 8:00 AM to 1:00 PM and 2:00 PM to 6:00 PM, type 8:00 AM to 6:00 PM with a break of 1:00, the time between the two parts.
  • Two weeks or a month: fill in one week, note the totals, then the next. Under the FLSA overtime is counted week by week, so 50 hours one week and 30 the next still earns 10 hours of overtime in the first week.
  • Lunch breaks: short rest breaks of usually 20 minutes or less count as hours worked under the FLSA, while a bona fide meal period of typically 30 minutes or more, with no duties, generally does not, so type only the unpaid break.

Time card formulas in Excel and Google Sheets

Spreadsheets store times as fractions of a day, so with the start in A2, the end in B2 and the break (as a time, such as 0:45) in C2, =(B2-A2-C2)*24 gives the day's decimal hours. For shifts that pass midnight use =(MOD(B2-A2,1)-C2)*24. Add the days with =SUM, and format a total of times as [h]:mm so it can show more than 24 hours.

Weekly overtime then takes two more cells: regular hours =MIN(total,40) and overtime hours =MAX(total-40,0), each multiplied by its rate. Google Sheets uses the same formulas.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Check AM and PM on every day; a single slip changes a day by 12 hours.
  • Keep the minimum wage in mind: the US federal minimum wage is $7.25 an hour (since July 24, 2009), and many states set a higher one; employers must follow both federal and state law.
  • Record times every day rather than at the end of the week, and keep the downloaded file or link as your record.
  • Do not mix decimal hours with hours and minutes: 7.75 hours plus 7 h 45 min is 15.5 hours, but writing the second day as 7.45 gives a wrong 15.20.

How it works

For each day you worked, type when you started and finished and how long your unpaid break was; leave days off empty (Saturday and Sunday are under their own heading). Times can be typed as 8:30 AM, 8:30, 830 or 17:30, and an end time without AM or PM is read as the first such time after the start, so 9:00 to 5:30 means 5:30 PM. If the end is earlier than the start, the shift ends the next day. Each day's hours are the end minus the start minus the break; they can be rounded to the nearest 5, 10, 15 or 30 minutes or the hour. With pay turned on, pick how overtime works: by default hours over 40 in the week are paid at 1.5 times the base rate, the US federal rule, or pick a daily limit, no overtime, or a cap on paid hours.

The formula

  • Day hours = end time − start time − break (plus 24 hours when the end is earlier than the start)
  • Rounded day hours = nearest multiple of the rounding step (7 h 40 min → 7 h 45 min to the quarter hour)
  • Weekly overtime: regular = smaller of total hours and the weekly limit; overtime = total − regular; pay = regular × rate + overtime × rate × overtime multiplier
  • Daily overtime: the same split for each day with the daily limit, each day's pay rounded to the cent, then added up
  • Capped pay without overtime: paid hours = smaller of the hours and the limit (per day or per week) × rate

A worked example

Five days of 8 h, 8 h 15 min, 7 h 40 min, 9 h and 11 h 30 min make 44 h 25 min. At $35 an hour with overtime after 40 hours a week: 40 h × $35 = $1,400.00 base pay, and 4 h 25 min (4.4167 h) × $35 × 1.5 = $231.88 overtime, so the week pays $1,631.88.

Questions

How do I calculate my time card hours?

For each day, take the start time from the end time and subtract the unpaid break, then add up the days. Type the times here and the calculator gives each day's hours and the weekly total, in hours and minutes and in decimal hours.

How do I calculate time card hours with lunch?

Type the lunch in the Break box for that day, such as 0:30 or 45. A day from 9:00 AM to 5:30 PM with a 45-minute lunch is 7 h 45 min, or 7.75 hours.

How do I calculate time cards in hundredths?

Divide the minutes by 60 and add them to the whole hours: 7 h 45 min is 7.75 hours and 8 h 20 min is 8.33 hours. The calculator shows these decimal hours for each day and the week.

How is overtime worked out?

With the default rule, every hour over 40 in the week is paid at 1.5 times the base rate. That is the US federal rule for most hourly workers. Some places count overtime by the day instead (for example after 8 hours a day); pick the daily limit for that. You can change the limits and the overtime multiplier, for example to 2 for double time.

Can I use it for California overtime?

Choose the daily limit of 8 hours for California's daily overtime, and compare with the weekly rule, since California also pays overtime after 40 hours a week and double time after 12 hours a day. The calculator applies one rule at a time, so check how your employer combines them.

How do I enter a night shift?

Type the times as they appear on the clock, such as 10:00 PM to 6:30 AM. Because the end is earlier than the start, the shift is counted as ending the next morning, and the table marks it as next day.

How do I type the break?

As hours and minutes, such as 0:30 or 1:00, or as plain minutes, such as 30 or 45. Forms like 45m or 1h 15m work too. Leave it empty or type 0 when there was no unpaid break.

Should I round the hours?

Only if your employer does. US rules allow rounding to the nearest 5 minutes, or to the nearest tenth or quarter of an hour, as long as it does not short-change workers over time. The rounding here is to the nearest step, so 7 h 40 min becomes 7 h 45 min and 7 h 37 min becomes 7 h 30 min at 15 minutes.

What is the time card formula in Excel?

With the start in A2, the end in B2 and the break in C2, use =(B2-A2-C2)*24 for decimal hours, or =(MOD(B2-A2,1)-C2)*24 for a shift past midnight. Format weekly totals as [h]:mm.

Can I keep or print my time card?

The page address holds everything you typed, so you can bookmark it or send the link. The table has a download button for a spreadsheet file, and you can add a report title and notes under Day names and report before printing the page.
