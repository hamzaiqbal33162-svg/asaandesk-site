What the quadratic formula is

A quadratic formula calculator solves any equation of the form ax² + bx + c = 0, where a is not zero. Such an equation is called quadratic because its highest power is the square (quadratus is Latin for square). Its solutions, also called roots or zeros, are the x values that make the left side equal 0.

The quadratic formula, x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) ÷ 2a, gives those roots for every quadratic, whether or not it factorises neatly. The ± sign is what produces two answers: one with plus and one with minus.

On a graph, y = ax² + bx + c is a U-shaped curve called a parabola. The real roots are the points where it crosses the x-axis. A parabola can cross twice, touch once, or miss the axis completely, and the formula tells you which.

How to use the quadratic formula calculator with steps

  • First rearrange your equation so that everything is on one side and 0 is on the other. For example x² = 5x − 6 becomes x² − 5x + 6 = 0.
  • a (with x²): the number in front of x². It starts at 2. If there is no number, a is 1; if the term is −x², a is −1.
  • b (with x): the number in front of x, with its sign. It starts at 5. If there is no x term, type 0.
  • c (the number alone): the constant, with its sign. It starts at −3.
  • Decimals, negative numbers and fractions such as 3/4 work in every box. Read the roots in the headline, the steps below, and the graph of the curve.
  • If a is 0 the page solves the straight line bx + c = 0 instead, so 4x − 10 = 0 gives x = 2.5.

When the discriminant is a perfect square (1, 4, 9, 25, 49 …) and a, b and c are whole numbers, the roots are fractions and the equation can also be solved by factoring. That makes D a quick test of whether factoring is worth trying.

Worked examples step by step

Example 1, the default 2x² + 5x − 3 = 0: a = 2, b = 5, c = −3. The discriminant is 5² − 4 × 2 × (−3) = 25 + 24 = 49, and √49 = 7. So x = (−5 ± 7) ÷ 4, which gives (−5 + 7) ÷ 4 = 1/2 or (−5 − 7) ÷ 4 = −3. Check: 2(0.5)² + 5(0.5) − 3 = 0.5 + 2.5 − 3 = 0.

Example 2, an irrational answer, x² − 2x − 1 = 0: D = (−2)² − 4 × 1 × (−1) = 8. Since 8 = 4 × 2, √8 = 2√2, and x = (2 ± 2√2) ÷ 2 = 1 ± √2. In decimals that is 2.4142135623731 or −0.4142135623731.

Example 3, a plot of land: a rectangular plot in Lahore is 5 m longer than it is wide and covers 300 m². With width w, w(w + 5) = 300, so w² + 5w − 300 = 0. D = 25 + 1,200 = 1,225 and √1,225 = 35, so w = (−5 ± 35) ÷ 2 = 15 or −20. A width cannot be negative, so the plot is 15 m by 20 m.

Example 4, fractions: (3/4)x² − 1.5x − 6 = 0 gives D = 2.25 + 18 = 20.25 and √20.25 = 4.5, so x = (1.5 ± 4.5) ÷ 1.5 = 4 or −2. The page keeps the fractions exact the whole way.

Where the formula comes from: completing the square

  • Start with ax² + bx + c = 0 and divide every term by a: x² + (b/a)x + c/a = 0.
  • Move the constant across: x² + (b/a)x = −c/a.
  • Add (b/2a)² to both sides so the left side becomes a perfect square: (x + b/2a)² = b²/4a² − c/a.
  • Write the right side over one denominator: (x + b/2a)² = (b² − 4ac) ÷ 4a².
  • Take the square root of both sides, remembering ±: x + b/2a = ± √(b² − 4ac) ÷ 2a.
  • Subtract b/2a: x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) ÷ 2a, the quadratic formula.

The same steps explain the vertex. The squared bracket is smallest when x = −b ÷ 2a, which is the axis of symmetry, and the height there is −D ÷ 4a.

What the quadratic formula is used for in real life

Throws and falls: the height of a thrown object is close to h = −½gt² + vt + h₀, with standard gravity g = 9.80665 m/s², so ½g is about 4.9. A ball thrown upward at 20 m/s from 1.5 m gives −4.9t² + 20t + 1.5 = 0. The positive root says it lands after about 4.155 seconds, and the vertex says it peaks at about 21.91 m after 2.04 seconds.

Area and design: any problem where a length is multiplied by a related length, like the plot above, a picture frame of even width or a box made from folded card, ends in a quadratic.

Money: when price changes affect how many items sell, revenue = price × quantity becomes a quadratic in the price, and its vertex gives the price with the most revenue.

Common mistakes and tips

  • Not setting the equation to 0 first. The formula needs ax² + bx + c = 0.
  • Losing the sign of b. With b = −7, −b is +7, and b² is 49, not −49. Brackets help: (−7)².
  • Dividing only the square root by 2a. The whole top line, −b ± √D, is divided by 2a.
  • Forgetting the second root. The ± always means two answers unless D = 0.
  • Rejecting answers without thinking. A negative length is not possible, but a negative time can mean "before the clock started".

How it works

Type the three numbers of your equation ax² + bx + c = 0: a goes with x², b goes with x, and c stands alone. Whole numbers, decimals and fractions such as 3/4 all work. The answer updates as you type. The steps follow the quadratic formula the way a teacher would write it: put the numbers in, work out the discriminant b² − 4ac, take its square root (simplified, so √8 becomes 2√2), then split the fraction into two answers. The fractions stay exact until the very last line, where the decimals appear. If a is 0 the equation is a straight line, and it is solved as one.

The formula

  • x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) ÷ (2a)
  • Discriminant D = b² − 4ac: D > 0 gives two real roots, D = 0 one repeated root, D < 0 two complex roots
  • For D < 0: x = −b ÷ (2a) ± i × √(−D) ÷ (2a), where i = √−1
  • Vertex of the curve: x = −b ÷ (2a), y = −D ÷ (4a)
  • Sum of the roots = −b ÷ a, product of the roots = c ÷ a
  • When a = 0: bx + c = 0, so x = −c ÷ b

A worked example

2x² − 7x + 3 = 0: here a = 2, b = −7 and c = 3. The discriminant is (−7)² − 4 × 2 × 3 = 49 − 24 = 25, and √25 = 5. So x = (7 ± 5) ÷ 4, which gives x = 12 ÷ 4 = 3 or x = 2 ÷ 4 = 0.5.

Questions

What is the quadratic formula?

It is x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) ÷ 2a, the solution of ax² + bx + c = 0. Put in a, b and c from your equation, work out the part under the square root, then do the plus and the minus separately to get the two roots.

How do you solve a quadratic equation step by step?

Rearrange it to ax² + bx + c = 0, read off a, b and c, and work out the discriminant b² − 4ac. Take its square root, then use x = (−b ± √D) ÷ 2a for the two roots. Check each one by putting it back into the equation.

What does the discriminant tell me?

The discriminant b² − 4ac decides the kind of answer before you finish. Positive means two different real roots, zero means one root that counts twice, and negative means the curve never touches the x-axis, so the two roots are complex numbers.

What does i mean in a complex answer?

i stands for the square root of −1. A root such as −1 ± 2i has a real part (−1) and an imaginary part (2). Complex roots always come in pairs like this, one with + and one with −.

Why do I see a square root in the exact answer?

When b² − 4ac is not a perfect square, the roots are irrational: their decimals never end or repeat. The exact form, for example 1 ± √2, keeps the square root so nothing is lost; the decimals are rounded to 14 significant digits.

How do I find the roots of a quadratic equation by factoring?

Look for two numbers that multiply to a × c and add to b. For x² − 5x + 6 they are −2 and −3, so (x − 2)(x − 3) = 0 and x = 2 or 3. If no such whole numbers exist, use the formula.

How do I find the vertex of a parabola?

The x of the vertex is −b ÷ 2a. Put it back into the equation for y, or use y = −(b² − 4ac) ÷ 4a. For 2x² + 5x − 3 the vertex is (−1.25, −6.125), and the page shows it for every equation.

Can a be 0?

Yes. Then there is no x² term and the equation bx + c = 0 is a straight line with one root, x = −c ÷ b. If b is 0 as well, there is either no solution or every number works, and the calculator says which.

Can I type fractions or decimals?

Yes. Type 3/4, −1.5 or 2 1/2 in any box. The working keeps exact fractions, so 3/4x² − 1.5x − 6 = 0 gives exactly 4 and −2.

What are the sum and product of the roots?

For ax² + bx + c = 0 the roots always add up to −b ÷ a and multiply to c ÷ a. They make a fast check: for x² − 5x + 6 the roots 2 and 3 add to 5 and multiply to 6.
