Steps

  1. Match your equation to ax² + bx + c = 0: a = 2, b = 5, c = -3.
  2. The quadratic formula: x = −b ± √(b² − 4ac)2a
  3. Put the numbers in: x = −5 ± √(5² − 4 × 2 × (-3))2 × 2
  4. Work out the discriminant: D = b² − 4ac = 25 − (-24) = 49
  5. D is positive, so there are two different real roots: x = -5 ± √494
  6. √49 = 7 exactly, so both roots are rational numbers.
  7. Split it into two parts: x = -54 ± 74
  8. So x = -54 + 74 = 12 = 0.5 or x = -54 − 74 = -3
  • The curve opens upward because a is positive; the roots are where it crosses the x-axis.

How it works

Type the three numbers of your equation ax² + bx + c = 0: a goes with x², b goes with x, and c stands alone. Whole numbers, decimals and fractions such as 3/4 all work. The answer updates as you type. The steps follow the quadratic formula the way a teacher would write it: put the numbers in, work out the discriminant b² − 4ac, take its square root (simplified, so √8 becomes 2√2), then split the fraction into two answers. The fractions stay exact until the very last line, where the decimals appear. If a is 0 the equation is a straight line, and it is solved as one.

The formula

  • x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) ÷ (2a)
  • Discriminant D = b² − 4ac: D > 0 gives two real roots, D = 0 one repeated root, D < 0 two complex roots
  • For D < 0: x = −b ÷ (2a) ± i × √(−D) ÷ (2a), where i = √−1
  • Vertex of the curve: x = −b ÷ (2a), y = −D ÷ (4a)
  • Sum of the roots = −b ÷ a, product of the roots = c ÷ a
  • When a = 0: bx + c = 0, so x = −c ÷ b

A worked example

2x² − 7x + 3 = 0: here a = 2, b = −7 and c = 3. The discriminant is (−7)² − 4 × 2 × 3 = 49 − 24 = 25, and √25 = 5. So x = (7 ± 5) ÷ 4, which gives x = 12 ÷ 4 = 3 or x = 2 ÷ 4 = 0.5.

Questions

What does the discriminant tell me?

The discriminant b² − 4ac decides the kind of answer before you finish. Positive means two different real roots, zero means one root that counts twice, and negative means the curve never touches the x-axis, so the two roots are complex numbers.

What does i mean in a complex answer?

i stands for the square root of −1. A root such as −1 ± 2i has a real part (−1) and an imaginary part (2). Complex roots always come in pairs like this, one with + and one with −.

Why do I see a square root in the exact answer?

When b² − 4ac is not a perfect square, the roots are irrational: their decimals never end or repeat. The exact form, for example 1 ± √2, keeps the square root so nothing is lost; the decimals are rounded to 14 significant digits.

Can a be 0?

Yes. Then there is no x² term and the equation bx + c = 0 is a straight line with one root, x = −c ÷ b. If b is 0 as well, there is either no solution or every number works, and the calculator says which.

Can I type fractions or decimals?

Yes. Type 3/4, −1.5 or 2 1/2 in any box. The working keeps exact fractions, so 3/4x² − 1.5x − 6 = 0 gives exactly 4 and −2.
