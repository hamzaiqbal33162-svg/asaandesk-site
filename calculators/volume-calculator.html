How it works

Pick the shape, choose the unit and type its sizes; the volume appears as you type, with the same amount in liters and US gallons, a labelled picture and the formula worked out with your numbers. When the answer is a neat multiple of π it is shown that way too (for example 15π). If your sizes come in different units, such as a radius in feet and a height in inches, open Units and give each box its own unit: the volume is then worked out once in each unit you used, every length converted first, so you can check it either way. For a spherical cap, fill in any two of base radius, ball radius and cap height and the third is found for you.

The formula

  • Sphere: V = 4/3 × π × r³
  • Cone: V = 1/3 × π × r² × h
  • Cube: V = a³; rectangular box: V = l × w × h
  • Cylinder: V = π × r² × h; tube (hollow cylinder): V = π × (d1² − d2²) × l ÷ 4
  • Capsule: V = π × r² × h + 4/3 × π × r³ (a cylinder with a half sphere at each end)
  • Spherical cap: V = 1/3 × π × h² × (3R − h), with r = √(2Rh − h²) and R = (h² + r²) ÷ 2h
  • Conical frustum: V = 1/3 × π × h × (r² + rR + R²)
  • Ellipsoid: V = 4/3 × π × a × b × c; square pyramid: V = 1/3 × a² × h
  • Units: 1 in = 2.54 cm exactly, 1 ft = 12 in, 1 L = 1,000 cm³, 1 US gallon = 231 in³ = 3.785411784 L

A worked example

A cone with a base radius of 3 m and a height of 5 m: V = 1/3 × π × 3² × 5 = 15π, about 47.12 cubic meters, which is 47,124 liters.

Questions

How do I find how much water a tank holds?

Choose Cylinder for a round tank (radius and height) or Box for a rectangular one (length, width, height), and read the volume in liters or US gallons straight under the main answer. Use the inside measurements of the tank.

My sizes are in different units. What should I do?

Open Units and switch on a different unit for each length, then pick the unit next to every box. The volume is given in each unit you used, so a radius of 2 ft with a height of 6 in shows the answer in cubic feet and in cubic inches.

Why is the answer shown with π?

Round shapes have π in their formula. When the number in front of π is short, such as 15π, it is shown as well, because that is the exact answer; the long decimal next to it is the same value rounded to 14 significant digits.

What if I mix up the inner and outer diameter of a tube?

The calculator notices that the inner diameter is the larger one, swaps the two and tells you, so the volume of the tube wall is still right.

Which two values does the spherical cap need?

Any two of the base radius, the ball radius and the cap height. With a base radius and a ball radius two caps are possible (a shallow one and a deep one); the shallow one is used and the height of the deep one is given in a note.
