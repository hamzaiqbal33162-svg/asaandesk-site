GPA and CGPA: what the numbers mean

This GPA and CGPA calculator turns your course grades into a grade point average. Your GPA, sometimes called SGPA, is the average for one semester. Your CGPA, the cumulative grade point average, is the same kind of average taken over every semester so far.

Both are weighted by credit hours. Each grade is worth a number of grade points, the points are multiplied by the credit hours of the course to give quality points, and the total quality points are divided by the total credit hours. Pakistan's Higher Education Commission (HEC) uses exactly this formula in its semester guidelines, and so do US colleges, so an A in a 4-credit course lifts your average more than an A in a 2-credit one.

How to use the GPA calculator

  • Under Grades, choose how your result card shows them: letters on the Pakistan 4.0 scale, letters on the US 4.0 scale, grade points you type in (up to 10, for other scales), or percentage marks out of 100.
  • Set the Number of courses (up to 20), then enter each course's Credit hours and Grade. The course name is optional, and empty rows are skipped. P (pass) and NP (no pass) are listed but left out of the GPA.
  • For your new CGPA, tick "Add my earlier CGPA to get the new CGPA" and enter your CGPA so far and the credit hours it covers. Both are printed on most transcripts.
  • Under More options you can split the courses into semesters to see each semester's GPA and the CGPA after it, count A+ as 4.0 on the US scale, or choose 2 or 3 decimal places.
  • On the Target CGPA tab, enter your CGPA now, the credit hours done, the CGPA you want and the credit hours still to take. Change "Highest GPA on your scale" if your top grade is worth 4.3 or 10.

Understanding the results

The headline is your GPA for the courses entered, or your new CGPA when you added an earlier one. Under it you see the credit hours counted, the quality points, the total credit hours and any pass/fail credit hours that were left out. The course table lists each course's quality points, so you can match it line by line with your result card.

With semesters switched on, a second table and a chart show each semester's GPA next to the CGPA after it. The Target CGPA tab gives the average GPA you need in the credit hours left, the best and worst CGPA you could still end with, and a table of the CGPA you would reach with a range of future GPAs.

Grading scales: Pakistan (HEC) and the US

Letter grades are not worth the same everywhere. The Pakistan option uses fixed points that many Pakistani universities print, such as those of FAST National University: A 4.00, A- 3.67, B+ 3.33 and so on, with no D-. HEC's 2019 guidelines give a range of grade points and a percentage band for each letter, shown in the table. The US option uses the common scale with plus and minus grades from A+ 4.3 down to D- 0.7, the one Stanford University uses. Some US schools stop A+ at 4.0 or do not use plus and minus grades at all; the A+ option under More options covers the first case.

Worked examples

Pakistan semester GPA and new CGPA. Three 3-credit courses graded A, B+ and B give 3 × 4.00 + 3 × 3.33 + 3 × 3.00 = 30.99 quality points over 9 credit hours, a semester GPA of 3.44. With an earlier CGPA of 3.20 over 30 credit hours, the earlier quality points are 96, so the new CGPA is (96 + 30.99) ÷ 39 = 3.26.

US college GPA. An A in a 4-credit course, B+ and A- in two 3-credit courses and a C in a 2-credit course give 16 + 9.9 + 11.1 + 4 = 41 quality points over 12 credits, a GPA of 3.417.

Planning a target CGPA. A student with a CGPA of 2.80 after 60 credit hours wants to graduate with 3.00 after 70 more. The calculator shows an average of 3.171 is needed in those 70 credit hours; a straight 4.0 would lift the CGPA to 3.446 at best. A student with 2.50 after 100 credit hours who wants 3.50 with only 30 left would need 6.833, so the calculator says it is not possible and that 2.846 is the most those 30 credit hours can give.

CGPA to percentage in Pakistan

Some job and admission forms ask for a percentage as well as a CGPA. HEC's guidelines pair each band of grade points with a band of marks, as in the table above, and the earlier draft guidelines added that a student is given the lowest percentage of the band their CGPA falls in. On that reading, a CGPA of 3.00 corresponds to 71%, the bottom of the B band.

Some universities publish their own conversion formula, and the one printed on your transcript or degree is the one that counts. To average percentage marks by credit hours instead of grades, choose "Percentages (marks out of 100)" under Grades.

Failed, repeated and withdrawn courses

HEC's 2019 guidelines set some common rules. A W grade for withdrawing from a course has no effect on the CGPA, but withdrawing after the sixth week gives an F that counts. An F must be repeated, and the earlier F stays on the transcript. An undergraduate may repeat a course graded below C; both grades are recorded, but only the better one is used in the CGPA.

This calculator does not apply repeat rules for you. Enter only the grades that count in your CGPA, leave out W courses, and enter an F as F so its credit hours pull the average down as they would on your transcript.

Weighted and unweighted GPA in US high schools

Some US high schools give extra points for AP and honors classes, a weighted GPA, so a top grade can be worth more than 4.0. College Board notes that colleges may adjust or remove that weighting to compare applicants fairly, and that schools differ in their cutoffs and use of plus and minus grades. For a weighted transcript, choose "Grade points I type in" and enter the points your school gives each class.

Tips for raising your CGPA

  • Courses with more credit hours move your CGPA most, so put your effort where the credits are.
  • The more credit hours you have done, the slower your CGPA moves. Use the Target CGPA tab early, while a change is still possible.
  • Check whether your university lets you repeat low grades and counts only the better one; that can lift a CGPA faster than new courses.
  • Your earlier CGPA is rounded on the transcript, so a new CGPA worked out from it can differ from the official one by about 0.01.

How it works

Pick how your grades are written: letters on the Pakistan 4.0 scale (A 4.00, A- 3.67, B+ 3.33 ...), letters on the US 4.0 scale (A+ 4.3, A- 3.7, B+ 3.3 ...), grade points you type from your result card, or percentage marks. Then enter the credit hours and grade of each course; empty rows are skipped. Each course gives quality points (credit hours × grade points), and your GPA is all quality points divided by all credit hours, so a 4-credit course counts more than a 2-credit one. Tick the earlier CGPA box and enter your CGPA so far and the credit hours it covers to get your new CGPA. Pass/fail courses (P, NP) are listed but not counted. Under More options you can split the courses into semesters to see each semester's GPA and the CGPA after it, cap A+ at 4.0, or choose the decimal places. The Target CGPA tab works out the GPA you need in your remaining credit hours to reach the CGPA you want, and says plainly when it is out of reach.

The formula

  • Quality points of a course = credit hours × grade points
  • GPA = sum of quality points ÷ sum of credit hours (P and NP courses left out)
  • New CGPA = (earlier CGPA × earlier credit hours + quality points now) ÷ (earlier credit hours + credit hours now)
  • GPA needed = (target CGPA × all credit hours − CGPA now × credit hours done) ÷ credit hours still to take

A worked example

On the Pakistan scale, three 3-credit courses graded A, B+ and B give 3 × 4.00 + 3 × 3.33 + 3 × 3.00 = 30.99 quality points over 9 credit hours, a GPA of 3.44. With an earlier CGPA of 3.20 over 30 credit hours, the new CGPA is (96 + 30.99) ÷ 39 = 3.26.

Questions

What is the difference between GPA, SGPA and CGPA?

GPA and SGPA usually mean the grade point average of one semester. CGPA, the cumulative GPA, averages every course you have taken so far, weighted by credit hours. HEC's guidelines ask for both the semester GPA and the CGPA on every transcript.

How is CGPA calculated from my earlier semesters?

Multiply your earlier CGPA by the credit hours it covers to get your earlier quality points, add this semester's quality points, and divide by all the credit hours together. Tick the earlier CGPA box and the calculator does it for you.

Which scale should I choose, Pakistan or US?

Many Pakistani universities give A 4.00, A- 3.67, B+ 3.33 and so on, with no D- (the Pakistan option); some use 3.66 and 2.66 instead. Many US schools give A+ 4.3, A- 3.7, B+ 3.3 down to D- 0.7. If your university uses other points, choose grade points and type them from your transcript.

How do I convert CGPA to percentage?

Use the rule your university prints on its transcript, because it is the one that counts. HEC's guidelines pair grade-point bands with mark bands, and its draft guidelines give a CGPA the lowest percentage of its band, so 3.00 corresponds to 71%.

What is a 3.0 GPA out of 4?

On a 4.0 scale a 3.0 is a B average. On HEC's 2019 table a B covers 2.67 to 3.00 grade points and 71 to 74 percent; on the US scale a B is worth exactly 3.0.

Does a course with more credit hours count more?

Yes. Each grade is weighted by its credit hours, so an A in a 4-credit course lifts your GPA more than an A in a 2-credit course.

What GPA do I need to raise my CGPA?

Open the Target CGPA tab, enter your CGPA now, the credit hours done, the CGPA you want and the credit hours you still have. You get the average GPA you need; if it is above the top of your scale, the target cannot be reached in those credit hours.

How are failed and repeated courses counted?

Under HEC's 2019 guidelines an F counts in the GPA and must be repeated, and when an undergraduate repeats a course graded below C only the better grade is used in the CGPA. Enter the grades that count on your transcript; this calculator does not apply repeat rules itself.

Does a W (withdrawn) grade affect my CGPA?

Under HEC's guidelines a W has no effect on the CGPA, but withdrawing after the sixth week gives an F that does count. Leave W courses out of the calculator.

Why can my result differ slightly from my transcript?

Your earlier CGPA is already rounded, so a new CGPA built on it can be off by about 0.01. Universities also differ on repeated courses and failed grades, which this calculator does not apply.
