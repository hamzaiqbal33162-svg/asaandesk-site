How it works

Enter the first day of your last period, how many days your period usually lasts and how long your cycle usually is. Each new period is expected one cycle length after the one before, so the calculator counts forward in steps of your cycle to find the next period after today and the ones after it. Ovulation usually happens about 14 days before a period starts, so the five days around that point are marked as likely ovulation days. The calendar shows three months at a time; use Earlier and Later to page through.

The formula

  • Period k starts on: first day of last period + k × cycle length
  • Period days: that start date and the following (period length − 1) days
  • Likely ovulation days: start + (cycle length − 16) to start + (cycle length − 12) days
  • Next period = the first expected start after today; cycle day today = days since the last expected start + 1

A worked example

A period that started on November 1, 2026, lasts 5 days and comes every 28 days. The next periods start on November 29, December 27 and January 24. Ovulation days for the first cycle are November 1 + 12 to November 1 + 16, that is November 13 to 17. On November 21 you are on day 21 of the cycle.

Questions

What is a normal cycle length?

Cycles are counted from the first day of one period to the first day of the next. Anything from about 21 to 35 days is common in adults, and periods usually last 2 to 7 days. A cycle that changes by a few days from month to month is normal.

How do I find my cycle length?

Write down the first day of each period for a few months and count the days between them. Use the average here. A period tracking app or a simple calendar both work.

My period is late. What should I do?

A period a few days late is common, and stress, illness, travel or weight changes can shift it. If you could be pregnant, take a home pregnancy test. If periods are often very irregular, stop for three months or more, or come with heavy bleeding or pain, see a doctor.

Why do the earlier months show periods too?

They show where your periods would have fallen if your cycle stayed the same length. Comparing them with the dates you remember is a quick way to check that the cycle length you entered is right.
