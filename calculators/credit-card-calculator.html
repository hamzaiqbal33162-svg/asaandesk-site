The minimum is worked out again every month (interest + 1% of what you owe, at least $15.00), so it shrinks with the balance and takes far longer. The other plans keep today's amount.

How it works

Type what you owe on the card and its yearly interest rate (APR), then choose how you want to pay. With a set amount each month, the calculator counts the months until the balance reaches zero and adds up the interest. With a set time, it finds the one monthly payment that clears the card by then. A quick pick can fill the payment for you (interest plus 1% of the balance, or 2% to 5% of it), and a table shows how each of those plans, and paying only the shrinking minimum, would turn out.

The formula

  • Monthly rate r = APR ÷ 100 ÷ 12
  • Months to pay off n = −ln(1 − B × r ÷ P) ÷ ln(1 + r), shown rounded up to whole months (B = balance, P = payment)
  • Payment for a set time of n months: P = B × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n)
  • Total interest = P × n − B
  • The payment must be more than the first month's interest, B × r, or the balance never goes down

A worked example

A $8,000 balance at 18% has a monthly rate of 1.5%, so the first month adds $120 of interest. Paying $200 a month gives n = −ln(1 − 8,000 × 0.015 ÷ 200) ÷ ln(1.015) ≈ 61.5, so the card is clear in 62 months (5 years 2 months), and the interest is about 200 × 61.54 − 8,000 = $4,308.61.

Questions

Why does paying only the minimum take so long?

The minimum is usually the month's interest plus about 1% of what you owe. As the balance falls the minimum falls with it, so most of each payment keeps going to interest. Keeping your payment at today's amount, even when the bill asks for less, cuts years off the debt.

What if my payment is smaller than the interest?

Then the card adds more interest each month than you pay, and the balance never goes down. The calculator tells you the smallest payment that starts to reduce it.

Does it count new purchases on the card?

No. It assumes you stop using the card while you pay it off. New purchases add to the balance and make the payoff longer.

Is the result exact for my card?

It is a close estimate. It adds interest once a month at the yearly rate divided by 12; many cards charge interest daily and some add fees, which makes the real cost a little higher.

Can I use another currency?

Yes. Pick your currency symbol at the top. The maths is the same; only the symbol changes.
