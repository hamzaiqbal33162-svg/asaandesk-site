What the carbohydrate calculator tells you

This carbohydrate calculator turns your daily calorie needs into grams of carbs. It first estimates how many calories keep your weight steady, then shows the grams of carbohydrate that would supply 40%, 55%, 65% or 75% of those calories, for keeping your weight and for losing or gaining 1 or 2 lb (0.5 or 1 kg) a week.

The headline uses 55%, the middle of the 45 to 65% range that the U.S. National Academies set for adults. The grams are total carbohydrate: sugars, starch and fibre together. They are estimates for planning meals, not medical advice; people with diabetes, kidney disease or a history of eating disorders should set targets with their doctor or dietitian.

How to use this calculator, step by step

  • Choose metric (kg and cm) or U.S. units (lb and feet and inches).
  • Pick your sex and enter your age (18 or older), weight and height.
  • Choose how active you are, from Sedentary (little or no exercise) to Extra active (very intense daily exercise or a physical job). Exercise here means 15 to 30 minutes with a raised heart rate.
  • Optional: under Formula, switch the BMR formula to Revised Harris-Benedict, or to Katch-McArdle if you know your body fat percentage.
  • Read the headline for keeping your weight, then the table for your goal. On U.S. units a second table gives the same amounts in ounces and pounds.

The 130 g minimum is only about 520 kcal, so even a 1,500 kcal diet at 40% (150 g) is above it. Ketogenic diets, usually under about 50 g a day, go far below it and are best followed with professional advice.

Worked examples

Keeping weight, metric. The default 30-year-old man (75 kg, 178 cm, light exercise) has a BMR of 10 × 75 + 6.25 × 178 − 5 × 30 + 5 = 1,717.5 kcal. Times 1.375 that is 2,362 kcal a day. At 55%: 2,362 × 0.55 ÷ 4 = 325 g of carbs a day; the 45 to 65% band is 266 to 384 g.

Weight loss, Pakistan. A 35-year-old woman in Lahore, 62 kg and 160 cm, who does little exercise has a BMR of 1,284 kcal and needs about 1,541 kcal a day. At 55% that is 212 g of carbs, which might come from roti, rice, daal, fruit and vegetables across the day. Losing 0.5 kg a week would mean 1,041 kcal, under the 1,200 kcal floor, so the calculator marks that row; a slower pace, or more activity, is the safer route.

Endurance training. A 60 kg runner training 1 to 3 hours a day needs 6 to 10 g of carbs per kg, or 360 to 600 g a day, according to sports nutrition guidance. Entered as a 28-year-old man, 170 cm and very active, the calculator gives 2,635 kcal and 428 to 494 g at 65 to 75%, inside that range.

Types of carbohydrates and the carbohydrate formula

Chemically, carbohydrates are made of carbon, hydrogen and oxygen, often written with the general formula Cₓ(H₂O)ᵧ. Glucose, the sugar the body runs on, is C₆H₁₂O₆; table sugar (sucrose) is C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁; starch is a long chain of glucose units, (C₆H₁₀O₅)ₙ.

In food there are three main types. Sugars are the simplest: glucose and fructose in fruit and honey, lactose in milk, sucrose in table sugar. Starches are long chains of glucose found in wheat, rice, maize, potatoes and pulses. Fibre is the part of plants that the gut cannot digest; it feeds gut bacteria, helps bowel movements and keeps you full.

Quality matters as much as grams. Whole grains such as whole wheat atta, brown rice and oats keep their fibre, while refined white flour, white bread and sugary drinks lose it and raise blood sugar faster. WHO advises that most carbohydrate should come from whole grains, vegetables, fruit and pulses, and that free sugars (added sugar, honey, syrups and fruit juice) stay below 10% of calories.

Carbs for weight loss, diabetes and sport

Weight loss depends on total calories, so cutting carbs helps only if it lowers what you eat overall. Use the loss rows of the table and pick a share you can keep up; many people find 40 to 45% with more protein and vegetables easier, while others do well at 55%.

For diabetes, carb counting means adding up the grams in each meal to match medicine or insulin and to keep blood sugar steadier. In U.S. plans one carb choice is about 15 g. The American Diabetes Association says there is no single ideal split of carbs, protein and fat for everyone, so work out a daily target with your care team.

Athletes need more. Guidance from the Victorian Government's Better Health Channel, in line with sports nutrition position statements, gives 3 to 5 g per kg of body weight a day for light exercise, 5 to 7 g for about an hour a day, 6 to 10 g for endurance training of 1 to 3 hours and 8 to 12 g for more than 4 hours. During exercise longer than an hour, 30 to 60 g an hour is usually enough, and up to 90 g an hour for efforts over 4 hours, such as a marathon or ultra race for slower runners.

How to calculate carbohydrates in food

  • Find the carbohydrate line on the label and note whether it is per 100 g or per serving.
  • Weigh your portion and scale it: a food with 60 g of carbs per 100 g gives 60 × 80 ÷ 100 = 48 g in an 80 g portion.
  • U.S. labels show Total Carbohydrate including fibre. UK and EU labels list carbohydrate without fibre, which appears on its own line, so their figure is lower for the same food.
  • For a recipe, add the carbs of every ingredient and divide by the number of servings.
  • Add the meals of a day together and compare the total with the grams from this calculator.

How it works

The calculator estimates the calories you need to keep your weight (your BMR from the Mifflin-St Jeor formula times your activity factor), then subtracts or adds 500 or 1,000 kcal a day for losing or gaining about 1 or 2 lb (0.5 or 1 kg) a week. For each of these calorie levels it shows how many grams of carbohydrate give 40%, 55%, 65% and 75% of the energy, using 4 kcal per gram. Most health bodies suggest 45 to 65% of calories from carbs for adults; the wider table helps if you follow a lower or higher carb plan. Rows under 1,200 kcal (women) or 1,500 kcal (men) a day are marked as under the safe minimum.

The formula

  • BMR (Mifflin-St Jeor): 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age + 5 for men, − 161 for women
  • Calories to keep your weight: BMR × activity factor, rounded to whole kcal
  • Goal rows: that number − 500 or − 1,000 kcal (lose 1 or 2 lb a week), + 500 or + 1,000 kcal (gain)
  • Carbs (g) = calories × share ÷ 4 kcal per gram, for shares of 40%, 55%, 65% and 75%
  • 1 oz = 28.35 g; 1 lb = 453.59 g

A worked example

A 25-year-old man, 5 ft 10 in and 160 lb, who exercises 1 to 3 times a week needs about 1,717 × 1.375 ≈ 2,361 kcal a day. At 55% of that, carbs give 2,361 × 0.55 ≈ 1,299 kcal, which is 1,299 ÷ 4 ≈ 325 g a day. To lose 1 lb a week (1,861 kcal) the same share is about 256 g.

Questions

How many carbs should I eat a day?

For most adults 45 to 65% of daily calories, which is shown in the result. The minimum the body needs to fuel the brain is about 130 g a day. Endurance athletes often eat more; people on a low carb plan eat less, ideally with advice from a dietitian.

How do I calculate carbohydrates needed per day?

Estimate the calories that keep your weight, multiply by the share you want from carbs (for example 0.55), then divide by 4, because each gram of carbohydrate gives 4 kcal. At 2,000 kcal and 55% that is 2,000 × 0.55 ÷ 4 = 275 g.

How many carbs should I eat to lose weight?

Start from the 'lose' rows of the table, which take 500 or 1,000 kcal a day off your maintenance calories, and choose a share between 40 and 55%. What matters most is the calorie gap and eating enough protein and fibre to stay full.

Do these grams include sugar and fibre?

Yes, they are total carbohydrate. Try to get most of it from whole grains, vegetables, fruit, beans and lentils, and keep free sugar (added sugar, honey, syrups and juice) under 10% of your calories.

Is this carbohydrate calculator suitable for diabetics?

It gives a general starting point, but there is no single right carb level for everyone with diabetes. Your doctor or diabetes educator will set grams per meal around your medicines, insulin and blood sugar readings; carb counting usually works in portions of about 15 g.

How many carbs do I need for running or a marathon?

Sports nutrition guidance suggests 6 to 10 g per kg of body weight a day for 1 to 3 hours of endurance training, and 30 to 60 g an hour during runs longer than an hour. Very long events of more than 4 hours may call for up to 90 g an hour.

How many carbs do I need for muscle gain?

Use the 'gain' rows, which add 500 or 1,000 kcal a day, and a share of about 45 to 55% so there is room for protein. Carbs refill muscle glycogen and fuel hard training; protein builds the muscle.

What is the general formula of carbohydrates?

Carbohydrates contain carbon, hydrogen and oxygen and are often written as Cₓ(H₂O)ᵧ. Glucose is C₆H₁₂O₆, sucrose is C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁ and starch is a chain of glucose units, (C₆H₁₀O₅)ₙ.

Why are some rows marked as under the safe minimum?

Losing 1 kg a week means eating 1,000 kcal less than you burn. For smaller or less active people that can fall under 1,200 kcal (women) or 1,500 kcal (men) a day, which is hard to do safely without a doctor. Those rows are shown only for reference.

Why do other calculators show more grams?

This calculator uses the standard value of 4 kcal per gram of carbohydrate, the one food labels use. Some calculators use a lower factor of 3.75 kcal per gram, which gives about 7% more grams for the same calories.
