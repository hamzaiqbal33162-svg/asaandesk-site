How it works

Pick how many drinks of each kind you had and their size, then enter your sex, body weight and the time since your first drink. The calculator adds up the pure alcohol in the drinks, spreads it over the water in an average body of your weight and sex (the Widmark method), and then takes off the amount an average body clears each hour. The result is a rough estimate for an average person, not a measurement: never use it to decide whether you can drive. Strengths are under Drink strength, and a drink that is not beer, wine or a spirit can be added under Another drink.

The formula

  • Pure alcohol (g) = number of drinks × size in mL × strength % ÷ 100 × 0.8 g per mL
  • BAC % = alcohol in g ÷ (body weight in g × r) × 100 − 0.015 × hours since the first drink
  • r (share of the body that holds the alcohol): 0.73 for men, 0.66 for women
  • Hours until 0% = BAC ÷ 0.015, rounded up to a whole hour
  • One US standard drink = 14 g of pure alcohol

A worked example

A 70 kg man has two 330 mL beers at 5% and his first drink was 2 hours ago. Alcohol = 2 × 330 × 0.05 × 0.8 = 26.4 g. BAC = 26.4 ÷ (70,000 × 0.73) × 100 = 0.052%, minus 0.015 × 2 = 0.030, leaves about 0.022%. That takes about 2 more hours to reach 0%.

Questions

Is it safe to drive if the result is under the legal limit?

No. The result is an estimate for an average body and can be well below your real level. Alcohol slows reactions and judgement even at low levels, and many countries have a limit of 0.02% or zero. If you have been drinking, do not drive: take a taxi, public transport or a sober driver.

How accurate is this estimate?

Only roughly. Drinking on an empty stomach, drinking fast, medicines, liver health, body fat and even the time of day change how high your level goes and how fast it falls. A breath or blood test is the only way to know your actual level.

Can coffee, water or a cold shower sober me up faster?

No. The liver removes alcohol at its own steady pace, roughly 0.015% an hour for most people. Coffee may make you feel more awake, but your blood alcohol and your reactions stay the same. Only time lowers it.

Why do women reach a higher level from the same drinks?

On average women have less body water for the same weight, and alcohol spreads only through body water, so the same amount ends up more concentrated. The calculator uses 0.66 for women and 0.73 for men to reflect this.

What counts as one drink?

In the US a standard drink holds 14 g of pure alcohol: about 355 mL (12 fl oz) of 5% beer, 150 mL (5 fl oz) of 12% wine, or 44 mL (1.5 fl oz) of 40% spirits. Other countries use other sizes, such as 8 g in the UK and 10 g in Australia, so the calculator works from size and strength instead.
