What a BAC calculator estimates

A BAC calculator estimates your blood alcohol content: how many grams of alcohol are in every 100 mL of blood, written as a percent. A BAC of 0.08% means 0.08 g per 100 mL, the same as 80 mg per 100 mL or 0.8 g per litre (0.8 per mille, the unit used in much of Europe).

This calculator works out three things from what you drank: your estimated BAC now, how many more hours it takes to fall to 0%, and the typical effects at that level. A chart shows the estimate falling hour by hour after the first drink.

It is an estimate for an average body, not a breath or blood test. Real levels can be noticeably higher, so never use the result to decide whether you can drive, and treat any alcohol before driving as unsafe.

How to use this calculator, step by step

  • Choose Male or Female and enter your body weight in kg, lb or g.
  • Under Time since your first drink, enter the hours and minutes since you started drinking, not since your last drink.
  • For Beer, Wine and Spirits (shots), type how many you had and pick the size of each, from a 250 mL glass of beer to a 750 mL bottle of spirits. Leave a row empty if you had none.
  • Open Drink strength (alcohol by volume) if your drinks differ from the defaults of 5% beer, 12% wine and 40% spirits; the label on the can or bottle shows the ABV.
  • Use Another drink for cider, liqueurs, cocktails or anything else: enter the count, the size of each in mL, litres, cups, fluid ounces or pints, and its strength (8% by default).
  • Read the estimated BAC at the top, then the time to 0%, the typical effects, the peak estimate and the grams of pure alcohol.

The BAC formula, step by step

The method goes back to the Swedish scientist Erik Widmark, who worked it out in the 1920s and 1930s. First find the pure alcohol: millilitres of drink × strength ÷ 100 × 0.8, because 1 mL of ethanol weighs about 0.8 g. A 330 mL can of 5% beer holds 330 × 0.05 × 0.8 = 13.2 g.

Next spread that alcohol over the body. Alcohol mixes into body water, not fat, so the formula multiplies weight by a factor r: 0.73 for men and 0.66 for women in this calculator. Peak BAC % = grams ÷ (weight in grams × r) × 100.

Finally subtract what the liver has removed: 0.015% for every hour since the first drink. That is the bac formula for males and females alike; only r differs, which is why a woman of the same weight usually reaches a higher level from the same drinks.

Worked examples

Wine, woman, metric. A 60 kg woman has two 150 mL glasses of 12% wine over 1.5 hours. Alcohol = 2 × 150 × 0.12 × 0.8 = 28.8 g. Peak = 28.8 ÷ (60,000 × 0.66) × 100 = 0.073%. After 1.5 hours: 0.073 − 0.0225 = about 0.050%, the drink-driving limit in Scotland and Australia, with about 4 more hours to reach 0%.

Shots, man, US units. A 180 lb (81.6 kg) man drinks four 1.5 fl oz (44 mL) shots of 40% whiskey and started 3 hours ago. Alcohol = 4 × 44 × 0.40 × 0.8 = 56.3 g, about 4 US standard drinks. Peak = 0.094%; after 3 hours, about 0.049%, needing about 4 more hours.

The same four shots with no time passed give 0.094%, above the 0.08% US limit, and about 7 hours to clear. Drinking fast does not change the total, but it pushes the peak higher.

Binge level. NIAAA defines binge drinking as a pattern that brings BAC to 0.08% or more, typically 4 or more drinks for women or 5 or more for men in about 2 hours. Five 355 mL beers at 5% for the 180 lb man give a peak near 0.12%, and still about 0.089% after 2 hours.

For driving, the U.S. National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) describes poorer vision and multitasking from 0.02%, weaker steering and slower emergency responses at 0.05%, and problems with concentration, speed control and perception at 0.08%. NHTSA reports that a driver at 0.08% is about 4 times as likely to crash as a sober driver.

BAC over time: how fast alcohol leaves the body

Once absorbed, alcohol is removed at a fairly steady rate, mostly by the liver. Forensic reviews put the typical rate between 0.010% and 0.025% an hour, with an average near 0.015%, the figure this calculator uses. That is why each hour on the chart drops by the same amount, and why someone at 0.09% needs about 6 hours to reach zero.

Coffee, water, food after drinking, exercise and cold showers do not speed this up. Food in the stomach before or while drinking does slow absorption and lowers the peak, but this calculator assumes all alcohol is absorbed at once, so it cannot model a meal. Regular heavy drinkers may clear alcohol a little faster, yet their BAC from a given amount is the same and their risk of harm is higher.

Breastfeeding: the CDC says alcohol in breast milk tracks blood alcohol and is usually highest 30 to 60 minutes after a drink. It advises waiting at least 2 hours after a single drink before nursing, and notes that pumping and throwing away milk does not remove alcohol faster.

A 'drink' is not the same size everywhere, which is why this calculator works from millilitres and strength instead. Legal limits also differ by country, licence type and vehicle; the figures below were checked in November 2026 and can change.

Many countries are stricter still, with zero tolerance for alcohol in drivers, and new or professional drivers often face lower limits everywhere.

Common mistakes and when to get help

  • Counting from the last drink. The clock starts at the first drink, but alcohol from recent drinks may still be absorbing, so your real level can be rising.
  • Assuming a 'drink' is a standard drink. A large 250 mL glass of 13% wine holds 26 g of alcohol, almost two US standard drinks.
  • Forgetting strong beers and home pours. Craft beers of 7% to 9% and generous spirit pours raise the total fast; enter the real size and ABV.
  • Using the result to drive. The estimate can be lower than your actual level, and impairment starts well below any legal limit.
  • Waiting it out with someone who is very drunk. Confusion, vomiting, seizures, slow or irregular breathing, cold or bluish skin, or not waking up are emergency signs. BAC can keep rising after a person passes out. Call 1122 in Pakistan, 911 in the US, or 999 or 112 in the UK and Europe, keep them on their side and stay with them.

How it works

Pick how many drinks of each kind you had and their size, then enter your sex, body weight and the time since your first drink. The calculator adds up the pure alcohol in the drinks, spreads it over the water in an average body of your weight and sex (the Widmark method), and then takes off the amount an average body clears each hour. The result is a rough estimate for an average person, not a measurement: never use it to decide whether you can drive. Strengths are under Drink strength, and a drink that is not beer, wine or a spirit can be added under Another drink.

The formula

  • Pure alcohol (g) = number of drinks × size in mL × strength % ÷ 100 × 0.8 g per mL
  • BAC % = alcohol in g ÷ (body weight in g × r) × 100 − 0.015 × hours since the first drink
  • r (share of the body that holds the alcohol): 0.73 for men, 0.66 for women
  • Hours until 0% = BAC ÷ 0.015, rounded up to a whole hour
  • One US standard drink = 14 g of pure alcohol
  • 0.08% BAC = 0.08 g per 100 mL of blood = 80 mg/100 mL = 0.8 g per litre (0.8 per mille)

A worked example

A 70 kg man has two 330 mL beers at 5% and his first drink was 2 hours ago. Alcohol = 2 × 330 × 0.05 × 0.8 = 26.4 g. BAC = 26.4 ÷ (70,000 × 0.73) × 100 = 0.052%, minus 0.015 × 2 = 0.030, leaves about 0.022%. That takes about 2 more hours to reach 0%.

Questions

How do I calculate BAC?

Multiply each drink's size in mL by its strength and by 0.8 to get grams of alcohol, then divide the total by your weight in grams times 0.73 (men) or 0.66 (women) and multiply by 100. Subtract 0.015 for each hour since the first drink. This calculator does all of that for you and shows the steps' results.

Is it safe to drive if the result is under the legal limit?

No. The result is an estimate for an average body and can be well below your real level. Alcohol slows reactions and judgement even at low levels, and many places have a limit of 0.02% or zero. If you have been drinking, do not drive: take a taxi, public transport or a sober driver.

How accurate is this estimate?

Only roughly. Drinking on an empty stomach, drinking fast, medicines, liver health, body fat and even the time of day change how high your level goes and how fast it falls. A breath or blood test is the only way to know your actual level.

How long does it take for BAC to go down?

About 0.015% an hour on average, though people range from roughly 0.010% to 0.025%. A BAC of 0.08% therefore takes around 5 to 6 hours to reach zero. The calculator's chart shows this fall hour by hour.

Can coffee, water or a cold shower sober me up faster?

No. The liver removes alcohol at its own steady pace, and nothing you drink or do speeds it up. Coffee may make you feel more awake, but your blood alcohol and your reactions stay the same. Only time lowers it.

Why do women reach a higher BAC from the same drinks?

On average women have less body water for the same weight, and alcohol spreads only through body water, so the same amount ends up more concentrated. The calculator uses 0.66 for women and 0.73 for men to reflect this.

Does eating food lower my BAC?

Food in your stomach before or while drinking slows absorption, so the peak is usually lower and later. It does not remove alcohol that is already in your blood. This calculator assumes no food, so its estimate is closer to drinking on an empty stomach.

What counts as one drink?

In the US a standard drink holds 14 g of pure alcohol: about 355 mL (12 fl oz) of 5% beer, 150 mL (5 fl oz) of 12% wine, or 44 mL (1.5 fl oz) of 40% spirits. The UK counts units of 8 g, Australia 10 g and Canada 13.45 g, so the calculator works from size and strength instead.

How many drinks put you at 0.08%?

It depends on weight, sex and time. For a 70 kg man, about 41 g of alcohol (roughly three 330 mL beers at 5% drunk quickly) reaches 0.08%; a 60 kg woman gets there with about 32 g. Enter your own drinks to see your estimate.

Can I breastfeed after one drink?

The CDC advises waiting at least 2 hours after a single standard drink before nursing, because alcohol in milk follows the level in blood. Pumping and discarding milk does not clear alcohol faster; only time does.

What BAC level is dangerous?

From about 0.30% a person may pass out and be unable to protect their airway, and from 0.40% coma and death become real risks. Anyone who is confused, vomiting, breathing slowly or cannot be woken needs emergency help right away.
