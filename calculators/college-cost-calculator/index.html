How it works

Pick a US average or type your own yearly cost, then say when college starts, how long it lasts and how much of it you plan to pay from savings. Each year of college costs today's price grown by the yearly rise until that year. Adding the years gives the total. Because your savings earn a return, each year's cost is also brought back to today's money at your return after tax. The monthly saving is the level amount that, saved from now until the last year of college, is worth that much today. Money you have already saved is taken off first.

The formula

  • Cost of college year k (k = 0 for the first year) = today's cost × (1 + rise)^(years until start + k)
  • Return after tax ra = return × (1 − tax on the return)
  • In today's money = sum of each year's cost ÷ (1 + ra)^(years until start + k)
  • Still to save = today's money × your share − savings so far
  • Monthly saving = still to save × i ÷ (1 − (1 + i)^−N), with i = ra ÷ 12 and N = (years until start + years of study) × 12; at 0%, still to save ÷ N

A worked example

A $30,990 college year rising 5% a year, starting in 3 years for 4 years, costs $35,875 in the first year and $154,625 in total. At a 5% return taxed 25% (3.75% after tax) that is $130,836 in today's money, or $1,773 a month for 7 years. Paying 35% from savings needs $621 a month.

Questions

Where do the average costs come from?

They are the College Board's average total budgets for 2025-26: tuition and fees plus housing, food, books and other costs for one year. Your own college may cost more or less; choose My own yearly cost to type it.

Why do I keep saving while my child is in college?

The plan spreads the saving evenly from today until the last year of college. Money saved in the later years pays for the later years, so the monthly amount is lower than if you had to have it all by the first day.

What does in today's money mean?

It is how much you would need to put aside right now, earning your return after tax, to pay every year's bill when it comes. It is lower than the total because the money grows while it waits.

What rate should I use for the cost rise?

College prices have often risen faster than general inflation. 5% a year is a cautious guess; use 3% to 4% if you expect slower rises.

What is a 529 plan?

A US savings plan for education. Its growth is free of federal tax when spent on qualified education costs, so set the tax on the return to 0% if you use one.
