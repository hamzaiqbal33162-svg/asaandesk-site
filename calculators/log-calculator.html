How it works

A logarithm answers one question: to what power must the base be raised to give the number? So log₁₀(1000) = 3 because 10³ = 1000. Choose what you want to find, pick a base (10, e, 2 or type your own) and fill in the other values. The answer comes with the change-of-base step that works on any calculator, ln(x) ÷ ln(b), and a check that raises the base back to the power. Whole answers and simple fractions are found exactly, so log₈(2) shows 1/3 as well as 0.33333333333333.

The formula

  • log_b(x) = y means b^y = x (x > 0, b > 0 and b ≠ 1)
  • Change of base: log_b(x) = ln(x) ÷ ln(b) = log₁₀(x) ÷ log₁₀(b)
  • Solve for the number: x = b^y
  • Solve for the base: b = x^(1/y), when y ≠ 0
  • ln means the log with base e ≈ 2.718281828459

A worked example

log₂(1/8): 2 to what power gives 1/8? Since 2³ = 8, the power −3 gives 1/8, so the answer is −3. By change of base: ln(0.125) ÷ ln(2) = −2.0794415416798 ÷ 0.69314718055995 = −3.

Questions

What is the difference between log and ln?

ln is the natural log, with base e (about 2.718). log on its own usually means base 10 in school and science, and base 2 is common in computing. They differ only by a fixed factor: ln(x) = log₁₀(x) × 2.302585093.

Why must x be positive?

Any positive base raised to any power gives a positive number. No power of 10 equals 0 or −5, so the log of 0 or of a negative number has no real answer.

Why can the base not be 1?

1 to any power is still 1, so it can never reach other numbers, and log₁(8) has no answer. The base also has to be positive, so bases such as 0 or −2 are not allowed.

How do I find the base when I know x and y?

Choose The base b, then type x and y. log_b(81) = 4 means b⁴ = 81, so b is the fourth root of 81, which is 3. The calculator does this with b = x^(1/y).
