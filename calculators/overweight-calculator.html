What the overweight calculator tells you

This overweight calculator answers a simple question: am I overweight for my height? It works out your body mass index (BMI), names your weight class, shows the normal weight range for your height and tells you how many kilograms or pounds you are above or below it.

Adults aged 20 and over are classed with the World Health Organization (WHO) BMI bands, the same for men and women. Children and teenagers aged 2 to 19 are compared with others of the same age and sex on the CDC growth charts, so the calculator switches to a BMI-for-age percentile for them. The result is a screening estimate, not a diagnosis; a doctor looks at more than BMI before calling weight a health problem.

How to use this calculator, step by step

  • Choose metric (kg and cm) or U.S. units (pounds, feet and inches).
  • Pick Male or Female. For adults the bands are the same for both; for children the growth charts differ by sex.
  • Enter the age in years. For ages 2 to 19 an Extra months box appears, because a few months make a difference on a child's chart.
  • Enter height and weight, measured without shoes and in light clothes.
  • Read your weight class at the top, then your BMI, the normal (or healthy) range for your height and the gap to that range. The gauge shows where you sit on the scale.

Worked examples

Adult man, metric. The default man is 1.75 m tall and weighs 85 kg. BMI = 85 ÷ 1.75² = 85 ÷ 3.0625 = 27.8, so he is overweight. The top of his normal range is 25 × 3.0625 = 76.6 kg, so he is 8.4 kg above it. As a percentage, 8.4 ÷ 76.6 × 100 = 11% over the top of the range, which is how to calculate an overweight percentage.

Woman in Pakistan, in kg. A woman in Karachi is 160 cm tall and weighs 68 kg. Her BMI is 68 ÷ 1.6² = 26.6, overweight on the WHO scale and 4.0 kg above her normal range of 47.4 to 64.0 kg. Health risks for South Asians start at a lower BMI, so NICE in the UK uses 23 as the start of overweight and 27.5 as the start of obesity for people of South Asian background; on that scale she is overweight and 2.4 kg short of the obesity line of 70.4 kg.

Child. A 12-year-old boy 150 cm tall weighing 52 kg has a BMI of 23.1, which is the 92nd percentile for his age on the CDC charts: overweight. His healthy range at that height and age is 33.8 to 47.3 kg. Because he is still growing, doctors usually aim to slow weight gain while he grows taller rather than to make him lose weight.

Overweight and obesity in children and teens

A child's BMI is judged against children of the same age and sex, because healthy BMI changes as children grow. The CDC classes a BMI below the 5th percentile as underweight, the 5th to below the 85th as healthy weight, the 85th to below the 95th as overweight and the 95th or above as obesity. A BMI of 120% of the 95th percentile or more is called severe obesity, and the calculator flags it.

Older charts called the 85th to 95th percentile 'at risk of overweight'. An expert committee in 2007 recommended the plain terms overweight and obesity, which the CDC and this calculator use. WHO uses its own growth reference for ages 5 to 19, so a school or clinic using WHO charts may give a slightly different label.

Why excess weight matters, and what drives it

WHO reported in 2026 that about 2.6 billion adults, 45% of all adults, were overweight in 2024, and 940 million of them were living with obesity. Excess body fat raises the risk of type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, heart disease, stroke, fatty liver, sleep apnoea, joint pain and several cancers, and the risk climbs as BMI rises.

Weight gain comes from taking in more energy than the body uses over months or years, but genes, sleep, stress, medicines and the food around us all shape that balance. Genes are not destiny: a meta-analysis of more than 218,000 adults (Kilpeläinen and colleagues, 2011) found that the effect of the best-known obesity gene, FTO, was about 27% smaller in physically active people.

Looking beyond BMI

BMI cannot tell muscle from fat, so a muscular athlete can read overweight with little body fat, and an older adult with little muscle can read normal while carrying too much fat. Two quick checks help:

  • Waist-to-height ratio: keep your waist below half your height. NICE counts 0.5 to 0.59 as increased health risk and 0.6 or more as high risk.
  • Waist size: WHO links a waist of 94 cm (37 in) or more in men and 80 cm (31.5 in) or more in women with increased risk, and 102 cm and 88 cm with substantially increased risk.
  • Body fat percentage: a tape-measure estimate from the Body Fat Calculator separates fat from muscle better than BMI.

What to do if you are overweight

  • Start small: losing 5 to 10% of your weight improves blood pressure, blood sugar and cholesterol for many people, according to the CDC.
  • Aim for about 0.5 to 1 kg (1 to 2 lb) a week, the steady pace the NHS recommends.
  • Be active for 150 to 300 minutes a week at moderate intensity, as WHO advises, and add muscle-strengthening on 2 or more days.
  • Cut sugary drinks, fried snacks and large portions; fill half your plate with vegetables and add pulses, whole grains and lean protein.
  • Ask a doctor for a check of blood pressure, blood sugar and cholesterol if your BMI is 25 or more (23 or more for South Asians), and before any big diet change.
  • For children, change the family's habits together and talk to a doctor rather than putting the child on a diet.

How it works

Your weight is compared with your height through the body mass index (weight in kilograms divided by height in metres squared). For adults 20 and over, the World Health Organization calls a BMI under 18.5 underweight, 18.5 to 25 normal, 25 to 30 overweight and 30 or more obese, in three classes. The normal range for your height is 18.5 and 25 times your height squared, and the result shows how far your weight is from it. Children and teens from 2 to 19 are compared with others of the same age and sex on the CDC growth charts instead, and their status comes from the BMI-for-age percentile.

The formula

  • BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²
  • Adults: under 18.5 underweight; 18.5 to 25 normal; 25 to 30 overweight; 30 to 35 obese class I; 35 to 40 obese class II; 40 and over obese class III
  • Normal weight range = 18.5 × height² to 25 × height²
  • Amount over = your weight − 25 × height²; percent over = amount over ÷ (25 × height²) × 100
  • Children 2 to 19: under the 5th percentile underweight; 5th to under the 85th healthy weight; 85th to under the 95th overweight; 95th and over obesity (120% of the 95th percentile or more: severe obesity)

A worked example

A man 5 ft 10 in (1.778 m) tall weighing 190 lb (86.2 kg) has a BMI of 86.2 ÷ 1.778² ≈ 27.3, which is overweight. His normal range is 128.9 to 174.2 lb, so he is about 15.8 lb above it.

Questions

How do you calculate if you are overweight?

Divide your weight in kg by your height in metres squared to get your BMI. For adults, 25 to under 30 is overweight and 30 or more is obese. This calculator does the sum and also shows how far you are from the normal range.

What counts as overweight?

For adults, a BMI of 25 up to 30 is overweight and 30 or more is obese. For children and teens, a BMI at or above the 85th percentile for their age and sex is overweight, and at or above the 95th percentile is obesity.

What is overweight for a 5'4 female?

At 5 ft 4 in (162.6 cm), overweight starts at about 146 lb (66.1 kg) and obesity at about 175 lb (79.3 kg). At 5 ft 6 in the figures are about 155 lb and 186 lb.

What is overweight for a 6 foot male?

At 6 ft (182.9 cm), overweight starts at about 184 lb (83.6 kg) and obesity at about 221 lb (100.3 kg). A very muscular man can be above these and still have little body fat.

How many pounds overweight am I?

Enter your height and weight and read 'Above the normal range by'. It is your weight minus the weight at a BMI of 25 for your height, shown in pounds or kilograms.

How do I calculate my overweight percentage?

Take the amount you are above the normal range, divide by the top of the range and multiply by 100. At 85 kg with a range up to 76.6 kg that is 8.4 ÷ 76.6 × 100 = 11%.

Why is my child called overweight and not at risk of overweight?

Older charts used the words at risk of overweight for the 85th to 95th percentile. Since 2007 the CDC and child health experts use overweight for that band and obesity for the 95th percentile and up, and this calculator uses the current terms.

Is the BMI cut-off different in Pakistan?

The calculator uses the WHO bands that apply worldwide. Because South Asians develop diabetes and heart disease at lower BMIs, NICE in the UK uses 23 for overweight and 27.5 for obesity in people of South Asian background, and many doctors in the region follow similar lower cut-offs.

Can a fit person show as overweight?

Yes. BMI counts muscle the same as fat, so people with a lot of muscle can read as overweight while having little body fat. A waist measurement or the body fat calculator gives a better picture for them.

How much should I lose?

The result shows how far your weight is above the top of the normal range. Even losing 5 to 10% of your weight can improve blood pressure and blood sugar, so a small, steady goal is a good start. Talk to a doctor before a big change.
