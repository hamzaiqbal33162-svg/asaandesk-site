What the grade calculator works out

This grade calculator answers the two questions students ask most near the end of a term: what is my grade right now, and what do I need on the final? The Final exam tab gives the score you need on the final exam for the course grade you want. The Weighted grade tab averages your assignments, quizzes and exams by their weights and can tell you what you need on the work that is still to come.

Both use a weighted average. Each grade is multiplied by its weight, the results are added, and the total is divided by the sum of the weights. That is why a midterm worth 25% moves your grade far more than a homework set worth 5%.

How to use the calculator

  • Final exam tab: enter "Your grade now" (your course grade before the final), the "Course grade you want" and how much "The final counts for" as a percent of the grade. All three are percentages.
  • Weighted grade tab: choose whether grades are a Percent or Letters, and whether weights are a Percent of the course or Points. Set the number of assignments and exams (up to 15) and fill in each one's grade and weight; the names are optional.
  • To plan ahead, tick "Work out the grade I need on the rest of the course", enter the course grade you want and the weight of the work still to come, in the same kind of weight as above.
  • Read the answer at the top. The working underneath shows every grade times its weight, so you can check it against your syllabus.

Understanding the results

On the Final exam tab the headline is the score you need on the final, with the letter it matches. Two rows show the course grade you would end with if you scored 100% or 0% on the final, which marks the best and worst you can still do. A table lists your course grade for final scores from 100% down to 0%. If even 100% is not enough, the answer says "Not possible"; if even 0% keeps your target, it says "Any score".

On the Weighted grade tab the headline is your average so far, with its letter, over the weight you have entered. The weights do not have to reach 100% yet: three items worth 50% give your average on that half of the course. With planning ticked, a second headline shows the grade you need on the remaining weight.

Worked examples

What do I need on my final? Your grade is 82% before a final worth 30%, and you want 85%. The rest of the course keeps 82 × 0.7 = 57.4 points, so the final must bring (85 − 57.4) ÷ 0.3 = 92%. A perfect final would give 87.4% and a zero would leave you at 57.4%.

A course with points instead of percentages. Homework worth 50 points averages 90%, a quiz worth 20 points 70%, and a test worth 100 points 81%. Choose Points for the weights: (90 × 50 + 70 × 20 + 81 × 100) ÷ 170 = 82.4%, a B- on the US scale.

A Pakistani semester course. Suppose a course splits its marks into sessional work 25%, midterm 25% and final 50%. You scored 84% on the sessionals and 72% on the midterm, so your average so far is 78% over half the course. To finish with 80%, tick planning, enter 80 and a remaining weight of 50: you need 82% on the final. On the HEC table of percentage bands, 80 to 84 is an A-.

Letter grades and percentages

When you choose letters, or read the letter next to a percent, this calculator uses the US bands below and the 4.0 points that go with them. They are a common scale, not a law: College Board points out that some schools drop the plus and minus grades and others draw the lines in different places. Always check your syllabus.

Grades in Pakistan: universities, O Level and A Level

Pakistani universities that follow HEC's 2019 semester guidelines use different bands from the US ones: an A from 85%, A- 80 to 84, B+ 75 to 79, B 71 to 74, down to D at 50 to 53 and F below 50. Use this calculator for the weighted percentage, then read your letter from your university's table, or turn the letters into a CGPA with the GPA Calculator.

Cambridge O Level and A Level grades work differently again. Cambridge sets grade thresholds, the minimum marks for each grade, after every exam series and may move them from one series to the next so that a grade is no easier or harder to get than before. So a past paper's percentage cannot be turned into a grade with a fixed table; compare it with the published threshold table for that series.

Working out a grade by hand or in Excel

For one test, your percentage is the marks you got divided by the marks available, times 100: 43 out of 50 is 86%. For a whole course, multiply each percentage by its weight, add them up and divide by the total weight, which is what the Weighted grade tab does.

In Excel, the IFS function turns a percentage into a letter by checking conditions in order and returning the first that is true, for example =IFS(A2>=90,"A",A2>=80,"B",A2>=70,"C",A2>=60,"D",TRUE,"F"). It replaces a long chain of nested IF formulas.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Use your course grade before the final, not your last test score, in "Your grade now".
  • Check that your weights match the syllabus. If they add up to more than 100%, the calculator warns you; switch to points if your course uses them.
  • Extra credit can push a grade above 100%; grades up to 200% are accepted for that reason.
  • Ask how your teacher rounds. 89.5% may be shown as 90% on one report and stay a B+ on another.

How it works

The Final exam tab needs three numbers: your course grade now, the course grade you want, and how much the final counts. It shows the score you need on the final, the best and worst course grade you can still get, and a table of course grades for a range of final scores. The Weighted grade tab adds up your assignments and exams: give each a grade (percent, or a letter on the 4.0 scale) and a weight (percent of the course, or points). Your average is each grade times its weight, added up, divided by the total weight, so the weights do not have to reach 100% yet. Tick the planning box, enter the grade you want and the weight still to come, and you get the grade you need on the rest. A target that even full marks cannot reach is shown as not possible.

The formula

  • Weighted average = Σ(grade × weight) ÷ Σ(weight)
  • Needed on the rest = (goal × (weight so far + weight to come) − Σ(grade × weight)) ÷ weight to come
  • Needed on the final = (grade wanted − grade now × (1 − final weight)) ÷ final weight
  • Letters: A+ 97%+, A 93%, A- 90%, B+ 87%, B 83%, B- 80%, C+ 77%, C 73%, C- 70%, D+ 67%, D 63%, D- 60%, F below 60%; points A+ 4.3 ... D- 0.7, F 0

A worked example

You have 88% before a final that counts for 40% of the grade and you want 85% in the course. The rest of the course keeps 88% × 0.6 = 52.8 points, so the final must bring (85 − 52.8) ÷ 0.4 = 80.5%.

Questions

How do I work out the grade I need on my final?

Take away the part of the course grade you already have (your grade now × the share of the course that is not the final) from the grade you want, then divide by the final's share. The Final exam tab does this and lists the course grade for other final scores too.

How do I calculate my grade percentage?

Divide the marks you got by the marks available and multiply by 100, so 43 out of 50 is 86%. For a course with several parts, use the Weighted grade tab to combine them by their weights.

Do my weights have to add up to 100%?

No. The average is divided by the total weight you have entered, so after three assignments worth 50% you see your average on that half of the course. With points as weights the total can be any number.

How do I calculate a weighted grade with points?

Choose Points under "Weights as" and enter each item's points value as its weight. The calculator multiplies each percentage by its points, adds them and divides by the total points, so a 100-point test counts five times as much as a 20-point quiz.

How are letter grades averaged?

Each letter becomes points on the 4.0 scale (A 4.0, B+ 3.3 and so on), the points are averaged with the weights, and the result is shown with the nearest letter, for example 2.72 is a B-.

What percentage is an A?

On the common US scale used here an A is 93% to under 97%, with A+ from 97% and A- from 90%. Many Pakistani universities following HEC's 2019 guidelines give an A from 85%. Your syllabus decides.

What is the grade formula in Excel?

Use IFS to test the cutoffs from the top down, such as =IFS(A2>=90,"A",A2>=80,"B",A2>=70,"C",A2>=60,"D",TRUE,"F"). Change the numbers to your school's bands.

Can I use this for O Level or A Level grades?

Only for the percentage. Cambridge sets new grade thresholds after each exam series, so the marks needed for a grade change from series to series. Compare your marks with the threshold table for that series.

What does 'not possible' mean?

Even a perfect score on the work still to come would not lift your course grade to the target. The warning shows the best grade you can still reach; only extra credit or a change in weights could close the gap.
