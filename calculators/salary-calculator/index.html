What the salary calculator does

This salary calculator turns one pay figure into every other pay period. Type an hourly wage and it shows the yearly, monthly, biweekly and weekly equivalents; type a yearly or monthly salary and it shows what that is worth per day and per hour. It works for hourly to salary and salary to hourly conversions alike.

It also answers a question most pay converters skip: what your paid holidays and vacation days are worth. Every result is shown twice, once counting every working day as paid and once with your days off taken out, so you can compare an hourly job with a salaried one fairly.

Typical uses are checking a job offer, comparing an hourly contract with a salaried post, finding what one day of unpaid leave costs you, setting a freelance day rate that matches a salary, or turning a biweekly paycheck into the monthly figure a landlord or bank asks for.

All amounts are before tax and deductions. You can pick any currency from the menu at the top, including Pakistani rupees, US dollars, pounds, euros and Gulf currencies, because the sums are the same in every currency.

How to use it, step by step

  • Pay amount: the figure you know, for example 28 or 4,500 or 1,800,000.
  • Paid: what that amount is per. Choose per hour, per day, per week, every two weeks, twice a month, per month, per quarter or per year.
  • Hours worked a week and days worked a week: your normal schedule. The defaults are 40 hours over 5 days; a six-day week of 48 hours is entered as 48 and 6, and half days are allowed.
  • Paid time off (optional): open this section to set your paid holidays a year (10 by default) and paid vacation days a year (15 by default). Set both to 0 if you have no paid days off or want plain conversions.
  • Currency: choose the symbol at the top of the calculator; it changes only how amounts are shown.

Results update as you type. The page address keeps your numbers, so you can bookmark or share a conversion.

Understanding the results: unadjusted and adjusted pay

The headline is your yearly pay with the monthly figure beside it. Below it you see the pay per hour, the days you actually work in a year (working days minus holidays and vacation), the working hours a year, and one extra line that depends on how you are paid.

The key rule is how the calculator reads your amount. An hourly or daily figure is a rate: it is paid for each hour or day you work, so it is multiplied up over all 52 weeks. A weekly, biweekly, monthly or yearly figure is the pay you receive, and paid days off are already inside it.

The table then gives every period in two columns:

  • Unadjusted: every working day in the year is paid. For an hourly rate this is the pay if your holidays and vacation are paid; for a salary it is the same pay spread over only the days you actually work, which shows what each day at work earns.
  • Adjusted for days off: holidays and vacation are taken out. For an hourly rate this is the pay if those days are unpaid; for a salary it is simply your pay as entered.
  • Extra line for a rate: what the year comes to if the days off were unpaid. Extra line for a salary: the pay per hour actually worked, which is higher than the simple per hour figure.

A year is 52 weeks here, a month is the yearly figure divided by 12, and twice a month is the yearly figure divided by 24. When you set no days off, both columns are the same.

A quick mental shortcut: at full time, the yearly salary is about 2,000 times the hourly rate, so $20 an hour is roughly $40,000 a year and $40,000 a year is roughly $20 an hour. Worked examples

  • Hourly job: $22.50 an hour, 37.5 hours over five days a week, 8 paid holidays and 10 vacation days. Working hours are 52 × 37.5 = 1,950, so the yearly pay with every day paid is $43,875, or $3,656.25 a month. If the 18 days off were unpaid, the year would come to $40,837.50.
  • Salaried job: $75,000 a year, 40 hours and 5 days a week, 11 paid holidays and 11 vacation days. That is $6,250 a month, $2,884.62 every two weeks and $36.06 per working hour. Because you work only 238 of the 260 working days, each hour actually worked earns $39.39, and a contractor who is paid only for days worked would need about $81,933 a year to match.
  • Biweekly paycheck: $2,600 every two weeks is 26 × $2,600 = $67,600 a year, $5,633.33 a month, $2,816.67 twice a month and $32.50 an hour at 40 hours a week.
  • Pakistan monthly salary: Rs 150,000 a month for a six-day, 48-hour week, with 10 paid public holidays and 14 days of paid leave. The year is Rs 1,800,000 over 312 working days and 2,496 working hours, so Rs 5,769.23 per working day and Rs 721.15 per working hour. Spread over the 288 days actually worked, each day earns Rs 6,250 and each hour Rs 781.25.
  • Comparing two offers: $31 an hour with no paid days off, or a $62,000 salary with 25 paid days off, both 40 hours a week. If you take 25 days off in the hourly job, they are unpaid, so the year pays $31 × 8 × 235 = $58,280. The salary pays $62,000 for the same 235 days at work, which is $32.98 per hour actually worked, so it is the better offer despite the lower rate on paper.

Wage, salary, benefits and what drives pay

In the United States, the Fair Labor Standards Act requires overtime of at least one and a half times the regular rate for every hour over 40 in a workweek, for employees it covers. Many salaried professional and management jobs are exempt from that rule, so working late does not raise their pay, while most hourly jobs do earn overtime.

The same law does not require pay for time not worked, such as vacations or federal holidays, according to the US Department of Labor; paid time off is a matter of agreement between employer and employee. That is why the calculator treats an hourly rate as paid only for days worked unless you count your paid days.

Contractors and freelancers usually get no paid days off, no employer health plan and no employer share of payroll tax, which in the US is 7.65% of wages (6.2% Social Security plus 1.45% Medicare). When you compare a contract rate with a salary, put the salary in, set your paid days off, and look at the pay per hour actually worked: that is the lowest hourly rate that matches it before any benefits.

Benefits are a large part of what a job is worth. In June 2026, US private employers spent an average of $46.89 per hour worked on pay and benefits together: $32.82 (70.0%) went to wages and salaries and $14.07 (30.0%) to benefits such as paid leave, insurance, retirement plans and legally required payroll taxes, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics.

Paid holidays and vacation days

US federal employees have 11 paid public holidays a year, set by law in 5 U.S.C. 6103. Private employers choose their own list, and many give fewer. When a holiday falls on a weekend, federal offices usually close on the nearest weekday instead.

Together that is often 20 to 30 paid days off a year, roughly 8% to 12% of a 260-day working year. Leaving them out understates what a salaried job is worth per day at work.

Pakistan: monthly salary, pay per day and salary tax

Pakistani salaries are usually quoted per month, so choose Per month, pick Rs (PKR) at the top and set your real schedule, for example 6 days and 48 hours a week. The calculator gives the yearly total, the pay per working day and per hour, and the value of your paid leave.

For deductions for absent days, employers differ: some divide the monthly salary by the calendar days in the month and some by the working days, so check your appointment letter or payroll policy. The calculator's daily figure is the yearly pay divided by the working days in a year.

This page does not include government pay scales (BPS) for federal, Punjab, Sindh or Khyber Pakhtunkhwa employees. To use it with one, enter your total monthly pay, basic pay plus allowances, as a monthly amount.

Salary tax in Pakistan is worked out on the yearly salary and then deducted by the employer from each month's pay. With the FBR salary slabs for tax year 2027 (July 2026 to June 2027, Finance Act 2026), and salary as the only income, the tax comes to:

How to work out a salary increase percentage

A raise in percent is (new pay − old pay) ÷ old pay × 100. Going from Rs 120,000 to Rs 138,000 a month is 18,000 ÷ 120,000 × 100 = 15%; going from $62,000 to $65,100 a year is 3,100 ÷ 62,000 × 100 = 5%. Use the same period for both figures, or convert one first with this calculator.

To turn an hourly raise into a yearly one, multiply by the hours in your year: an extra $1.50 an hour at 2,080 hours is $3,120 a year. To find a new salary from a percentage, multiply the old pay by 1 plus the rate, so a 7% raise on $58,000 gives $58,000 × 1.07 = $62,060.

  • In a spreadsheet, with the hourly rate in A2: yearly pay is =A2*40*52 and monthly pay is =A2*40*52/12.
  • With a yearly salary in B2: the hourly rate is =B2/2080 and the daily rate for a five-day week is =B2/260.
  • With old pay in C2 and new pay in D2: the raise is =(D2-C2)/C2, formatted as a percentage.

The Percentage Calculator does percent increase and decrease with the steps shown.

Tips, common mistakes and related calculators

  • Every two weeks is not twice a month: biweekly pay gives 26 paychecks a year and twice-monthly pay gives 24, so a biweekly check is smaller and two months of most years have three paydays.
  • The US federal government divides a yearly salary by 2,087 hours, not 2,080, to allow for leap years (US Office of Personnel Management). Enter 40.13 hours a week to match it.
  • Regular overtime is not added automatically. For 40 hours plus 5 hours at time and a half, enter 47.5 hours: the pay comes out right, though the hours a year row then counts paid hours rather than hours worked.
  • Bonuses and commission are not included. Add a regular yearly bonus to the salary before converting: $60,000 plus a $5,000 bonus is $65,000 a year, or $5,416.67 a month.
  • To see what a day of unpaid leave costs, read the daily figure in the adjusted column for a salary: $62,000 a year over 260 working days is $238.46 a day, and Rs 1,800,000 over 312 working days is Rs 5,769.23 a day.
  • Part-time work converts the same way: enter the hours and days you really work, for example 20 hours over 4 days, and the yearly and monthly figures follow.
  • Unpaid days, such as unpaid leave or a seasonal break, are not the same as paid time off; leave them out of both boxes and lower the days or weeks you work instead.
  • Pay before tax is not take-home pay. For pay after tax in the US use the Take-Home Paycheck Calculator or the Income Tax Calculator.
  • For shift work, add up the week first with the Time Card Calculator or the Hours Calculator; for sales pay, see the Commission Calculator, and plan the month with the Budget Calculator.

How it works

Type a pay amount and say what it is per: an hour, a day, a week, two weeks, half a month, a month, a quarter or a year. Set the hours and days you work in a week, and under Paid time off the holidays and vacation days you get. The calculator uses a 52-week year. An hourly or daily amount is treated as a rate for the days you work, so it is multiplied out to a year; a weekly, monthly or yearly amount is treated as the pay you actually receive, with paid days off already inside it. Every other period is then that yearly figure divided up, shown two ways: counting every working day, and with your days off taken out.

The formula

  • Working days a year D = 52 × days a week; working hours a year H = 52 × hours a week; days off O = holidays + vacation
  • Hourly or daily rate: unadjusted year = rate × H (or × D); adjusted year = unadjusted × (D − O) ÷ D
  • Weekly or longer pay: adjusted year = pay × periods a year (52, 26, 24, 12, 4, 1); unadjusted year = adjusted × D ÷ (D − O)
  • Each period = yearly figure ÷ H (hour), ÷ D (day), ÷ 52, ÷ 26, ÷ 24, ÷ 12 or ÷ 4

A worked example

At $22.50 an hour for 37.5 hours over five days a week, a year has 1,950 working hours, so the unadjusted pay is $43,875. With 8 holidays and 10 vacation days taken out of 260 working days, 242 days are worked: $43,875 × 242 ÷ 260 = $40,837.50 a year if those days are unpaid.

Questions

How do I convert an hourly wage to a yearly salary?

Multiply the hourly rate by the hours you work in a week and then by 52. At $25 an hour and 40 hours a week that is $25 × 40 × 52 = $52,000 a year. If your holidays and vacation are unpaid, take those days out, which the adjusted column does for you.

How do I calculate salary per day?

Divide the yearly pay by the days you work in a year: 260 for a five-day week or 312 for a six-day week, before holidays. A $52,000 salary is $200 per working day. Payroll offices may instead divide a month's pay by that month's calendar or working days, so your payslip can differ slightly.

How do I calculate my monthly salary from a yearly one?

Divide the yearly salary by 12, so $54,000 a year is $4,500 a month. Do not multiply weekly pay by 4: a month averages about 4.33 weeks, so $1,000 a week is about $4,333 a month.

How many working hours are in a year?

With 40 hours a week and 52 weeks it is 2,080 hours, which is the number most employers use. The US federal government uses 2,087 to allow for leap years. Change the hours a week here to match your job.

What is the difference between unadjusted and adjusted pay?

An hourly worker is usually paid only for hours worked, while a salary already pays for holidays and vacation. The unadjusted column counts every working day; the adjusted one takes your days off out. For a salary, the gap shows how much your paid time off is worth.

Is twice a month the same as every two weeks?

No. Twice a month is 24 paychecks a year, while every two weeks is 26, so each biweekly check is a little smaller. The yearly total is the same.

How do I calculate a salary increase percentage?

Subtract the old pay from the new pay, divide by the old pay and multiply by 100. A rise from $48,000 to $50,400 is $2,400 ÷ $48,000 × 100 = 5%. Compare both figures over the same period, such as both monthly or both yearly.

What is the salary formula in Excel?

With an hourly rate in A2, yearly pay is =A2*40*52; with a yearly salary in B2, the hourly rate is =B2/2080 and the monthly pay is =B2/12. Change 40 and 52 to your own hours and weeks.

How do I calculate salary tax in Pakistan?

Multiply your monthly taxable salary by 12, find the FBR slab for that yearly amount, work out the tax and divide it by 12 for the monthly deduction. For tax year 2027, Rs 200,000 a month (Rs 2,400,000 a year) pays Rs 156,000 a year, or Rs 13,000 a month. The table above lists more salaries.

Can I use it for Pakistani rupees or other currencies?

Yes. Pick the currency symbol at the top, for example Rs for Pakistani rupees, AED or SAR. The conversions work the same way in any currency; only the symbol changes.

Is this a government pay scale calculator?

No, it does not hold the BPS pay scales or allowances for federal or provincial employees. Enter your total monthly pay as a monthly amount to see the yearly, daily and hourly equivalents.

Does this include tax?

No, all amounts are before tax and deductions. To see what you take home in the US, use the take-home paycheck calculator; for Pakistan, use the FBR salary slabs explained above.
