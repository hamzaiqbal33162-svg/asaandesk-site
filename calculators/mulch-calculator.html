What the mulch calculator works out

This mulch calculator tells you how much mulch you need to cover your garden beds to the depth you want, in cubic yards, cubic feet, cubic meters and liters, and how many bags that is. Add a price and it gives the total cost, so you can compare buying bags with buying bulk mulch by the yard.

It works for anything spread in a layer: bark, wood chips, shredded leaves, pine straw, compost, topsoil or decorative stone. Measure in feet, yards, inches, meters or centimeters, or type a total area in square feet, square meters, square yards or acres.

How to use the mulch calculator

  • Choose US or Metric at the top; this sets the units the result leads with (cubic yards or cubic meters) and the default bag size.
  • Measure by: keep "Length and width of each bed" and type each bed's size, with its own unit menu. Three beds are open; nine more are under Beds 4 to 12. Or choose "Total area" if you already know the area.
  • Mulch depth: type the depth you want, such as 3 in or 7.5 cm. The help line gives the usual range.
  • Price and bags: set the bag size you will buy (for example 2 ft³ or 50 L), and optionally a price per cubic yard, cubic foot, cubic meter, liter or bag. Pick your currency from the menu.
  • Read the volume and bags at the top, the other units and total area below, and the cost if you gave a price.

How to calculate mulch by hand

The mulch formula is area × depth = volume, with both in the same unit. In US units the quickest version is: square feet × depth in inches ÷ 324 = cubic yards, because one cubic yard (27 ft³) spread 1 inch deep covers 324 square feet. For the three beds above, 144 × 3 ÷ 324 = 1.33 cubic yards.

For bags, turn the volume into cubic feet (cubic yards × 27, or square feet × depth in inches ÷ 12) and divide by the bag size: 144 × 3 ÷ 12 = 36 ft³, and 36 ÷ 2 = 18 bags of 2 ft³. Always round up; part of a bag cannot be bought.

In metric, square meters × depth in centimeters ÷ 100 = cubic meters, and cubic meters × 1,000 = liters. A border 15 m long and 1.2 m wide is 18 m²; at 7.5 cm deep that is 18 × 7.5 ÷ 100 = 1.35 m³, or 1,350 liters, which is 27 bags of 50 L. At an example price of Rs 450 a bag that border would cost Rs 12,150.

Mulch for circles, tree rings and odd shapes

The bed boxes take rectangles. For a round bed or a ring around a tree, work out the area with π × radius² and use Total area. A ring 8 feet across has a radius of 4 ft, so its area is 3.1416 × 4 × 4 = 50.27 ft². At 3 inches deep that needs 0.47 yd³ (12.6 ft³), or 7 bags of 2 ft³.

For a ring with a bare circle in the middle, take the inner circle from the outer: π × (outer radius² − inner radius²). A triangle is half the base × the height, and an L-shaped bed can be split into two rectangles and entered as two beds.

Clemson University's Home & Garden Information Center suggests a mulch circle 3 to 4 feet across for a newly planted tree, and about 2 feet of circle diameter for each inch of trunk diameter on an established tree, reaching at least 6 to 12 inches past the drip line. A tree with a 4-inch trunk would get a ring about 8 feet across, the example above.

How deep should mulch be?

Deeper is not better. Too much fine mulch can starve roots of air, and too little lets weeds through. Clemson gives these depths by material, and the Royal Horticultural Society recommends a layer of at least 5 cm (2 in), ideally 7.5 cm (3 in), for biodegradable mulches laid on moist, weeded soil that is not frozen.

Suggested mulch depths (Clemson HGIC)

Keep mulch 2 to 3 inches away from the stems of woody plants and 6 to 12 inches away from house walls, where wood mulch could help termites cross treated soil. Do not pile mulch up against tree trunks.

Playgrounds are different: the mulch is there to soften falls. The US Consumer Product Safety Commission's playground handbook says never to use less than 9 inches of loose-fill such as wood chips (6 inches for shredded rubber), with 9 inches of wood chips rated for falls from up to 10 feet. Because loose fill compresses at least 25% with use, it advises filling to 12 inches to keep 9. A 30 × 20 ft play area filled 12 inches deep needs 22.22 yd³ of wood chips, or 300 bags of 2 ft³.

Types of mulch and what they do

Mulch is any layer spread on top of the soil. A good layer cuts water loss from evaporation, keeps the soil cooler in summer and warmer in winter, holds back weeds when it is weed-free and thick enough, and stops rain splashing soil and disease onto plants. Organic mulches also improve the soil as they rot.

Organic mulches include wood chips, bark, pine straw, leaves, grass clippings, leaf mould, compost, well-rotted manure and straw. They break down, so they need topping up: Clemson notes that pine needles and bark usually last about 2 to 4 years, while grass clippings and leaves rot quickly. Small wood chips use soil nitrogen as they decompose, so plants under them may need extra nitrogen fertilizer, and mulch that smells sour (of vinegar, ammonia or sulfur) can harm plants.

Inorganic mulches such as gravel, pebbles, crushed stone, rubber, landscape fabric and plastic do not decompose. Stone reflects sunlight and can make a bed very hot in summer, and black plastic is generally not recommended because it blocks air and water. If you use landscape fabric, the RHS advises a permeable one, and Clemson suggests several inches of another mulch on top.

In farming, mulching means the same thing on a larger scale: covering the soil between crops with straw, crop residues or plastic film to keep moisture in and weeds down. The volume sums are identical, only the areas are bigger.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Measure each bed rather than guessing the total. A few feet out on a long border adds up to whole bags.
  • Check the depth unit. 3 typed in feet instead of inches gives twelve times too much mulch.
  • Buy a little extra for settling, uneven ground and spills, and remember that old mulch already on the bed counts towards the depth.
  • Compare bags and bulk on the same volume. Bulk is often cheaper per cubic yard but may carry a delivery fee; bags are easier to carry and store.
  • Spread mulch after weeding and watering, on moist soil, and keep it off stems, trunks and house walls.
  • The same calculator works for topsoil, compost or gravel; only the depth you choose changes.

How it works

Type the length and width of each bed (up to three are open, nine more are under Beds 4 to 12), or switch to Total area if you already know it, and set the depth. The amount appears as you type in cubic yards or cubic meters, with cubic feet and liters, and the number of bags. Each box has its own unit menu, so a bed measured in feet and another in meters add up correctly. Under Price and bags you can set the bag size you buy and a price per volume or per bag to see the total cost.

The formula

  • Area = sum of length × width of every bed (or the total area you type)
  • Volume = area × depth
  • Bags = volume ÷ bag size, rounded up
  • Cost = volume in the price unit × price, or bags × price per bag
  • 1 yd³ = 27 ft³ = 0.764554857984 m³; 1 ft³ = 28.316846592 L; 1 acre = 43,560 ft²

A worked example

One acre covered 3 inches deep: 43,560 ft² × 0.25 ft = 10,890 ft³, which is 403.33 yd³ or 308.37 m³. In 2 ft³ bags that is 5,445 bags; at $4 a bag the mulch costs $21,780.

Questions

How do I calculate how much mulch I need?

Multiply the area of the bed by the depth you want, in the same units. In US units, square feet × depth in inches ÷ 324 gives cubic yards: 144 ft² at 3 inches is 1.33 yd³. Type the beds and depth here and the calculator also gives cubic feet, liters and bags.

How many square feet does a yard of mulch cover?

One cubic yard covers 324 ft² at 1 inch, 162 ft² at 2 inches, 108 ft² at 3 inches and 81 ft² at 4 inches. Divide 324 by the depth in inches for any other depth.

How many bags of mulch make a cubic yard?

A cubic yard is 27 cubic feet, so it takes 13.5 bags of 2 cubic feet or 9 bags of 3 cubic feet. In metric, a cubic meter is 1,000 liters, so 20 bags of 50 L.

How many bags of mulch do I need?

Work out the cubic feet (square feet × depth in inches ÷ 12) and divide by the bag size, rounding up. 144 ft² at 3 inches is 36 ft³, which is 18 bags of 2 ft³ or 12 bags of 3 ft³. Set your bag size under Price and bags and the calculator counts them for you.

How do I calculate mulch for a circle or around a tree?

Find the area with π × radius², then use Total area. A ring 8 feet across is 50.27 ft², which needs 0.47 yd³ or 7 bags of 2 ft³ at 3 inches deep. Keep the mulch a few inches away from the trunk.

How deep should mulch be?

About 2 to 4 inches (5 to 10 cm) for most beds. Clemson's extension service suggests no more than 2 to 3 inches of fine wood or bark mulch and up to 4 inches of coarse bark nuggets, and the RHS recommends 5 cm at least, ideally 7.5 cm. Keep mulch a few inches away from tree trunks and plant stems.

How much mulch do I need for a playground?

The US Consumer Product Safety Commission says at least 9 inches of loose-fill such as wood chips, filled to about 12 inches because it compresses by at least 25%. A 30 × 20 ft area at 12 inches needs 22.22 cubic yards. Shredded rubber needs 6 inches.

How do I calculate mulch in square meters?

Switch to Metric, type the beds in meters (or the total area in m²) and the depth in centimeters. By hand, m² × depth in cm ÷ 100 = m³, so 18 m² at 7.5 cm is 1.35 m³, or 27 bags of 50 L.

My beds are odd shapes. What should I do?

Split each bed into rough rectangles and enter each one as its own row. For a round bed, work out its area with π × radius², or with the Square Footage Calculator, and use Total area.

Is bulk mulch cheaper than bags?

Usually, once you need more than two or three cubic yards. Enter both prices (per cubic yard for bulk, per bag for bags) to compare the totals, and remember that bulk delivery often has a fee.

What is mulch made of and what is it used for?

Mulch is any loose layer on top of the soil: bark, wood chips, pine straw, leaves, grass clippings, compost, straw, gravel, rubber or plastic sheet. It keeps moisture in, evens out soil temperature, holds back weeds and stops soil splashing, and organic kinds feed the soil as they break down.

What is mulching in agriculture?

It is covering the soil around crops with straw, crop residues or plastic film to save water, keep weeds down and protect the soil. The amount needed is worked out the same way: field area × layer depth.
