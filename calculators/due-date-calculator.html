How it works

Choose the date you know and enter it. From the first day of your last period, the due date is 40 weeks (280 days) later, moved later for cycles longer than 28 days and earlier for shorter ones, because ovulation moves with the cycle. From a scan, the age the scan gave is counted back to its starting point. From a conception date it is 266 days later, and from an IVF transfer the embryo's age is taken into account. Today's date then shows how far along the pregnancy is, the trimester and the average size of a baby that week.

The formula

  • Due date = first day of last period + 280 days + (cycle length − 28) days (Naegele's rule)
  • From an ultrasound: due date = scan date − (7 × weeks + days) + 280 days
  • From a conception date: due date = conception + 266 days
  • From an IVF transfer: due date = transfer date + 266 days − embryo age (day 3: + 263, day 5: + 261, day 6: + 260)
  • Weeks pregnant today = days since (due date − 280 days), in weeks and days

A worked example

The last period started on September 22, 2026 with a 24-day cycle. A 24-day cycle is 4 days shorter than 28, so the due date is September 22 + 280 − 4 days = June 25, 2027. On November 21, 2026 the pregnancy is 9 weeks 1 day along, in week 10.

Questions

How is a due date worked out?

The usual method adds 280 days, or 40 weeks, to the first day of your last period. It assumes a 28-day cycle with ovulation on day 14, so for longer or shorter cycles the date moves by the difference. This is called Naegele's rule.

My scan gave a different due date. Which one is right?

A scan in the first trimester measures the baby directly and is usually more accurate than a period date, especially if your cycles are irregular. Doctors normally change the due date when an early scan differs by more than about a week. Use the ultrasound option to see the dates from your scan.

How accurate is the due date?

It is an estimate. Only a small share of babies arrive on the due date itself; most are born within two weeks of it, either side. First babies tend to come a little later than later ones.

Why does an IVF due date depend on the embryo's age?

A day-5 embryo is already five days past fertilisation when it is transferred, so the pregnancy counts as 2 weeks and 5 days on the transfer day. A day-3 embryo counts as 2 weeks and 3 days, so its due date is two days later than a day-5 transfer on the same date.
