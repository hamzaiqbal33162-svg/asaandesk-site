What a concrete calculator works out

A concrete calculator turns the size of a pour into the amount of concrete to buy. You give the length, width and depth (or the diameter of a round shape), and it returns the volume in cubic yards, cubic feet and cubic meters, the weight of bagged pre-mixed concrete, and how many 60 lb or 80 lb bags that weight fills. Ready-mix trucks in the US sell by the cubic yard, Pakistani and South Asian contractors usually quote in cubic feet (cft), and most other countries use cubic meters, so the page shows all three.

Concrete itself is a mix of cement, water, sand and gravel or crushed stone. The cement and water form a paste that coats the aggregate and hardens into stone-like material. Bagged concrete mix has the dry cement and aggregate already blended; you add only water. The volume this calculator gives is the finished, placed concrete, which is what both bags and trucks are sold by.

How to use the calculator, step by step

  • Choose the shape: Slab or footing (also walls, pads and square footings), Hole or column (post holes, Sonotube or cylinder forms), Round slab or tube (a round pad, or a ring with a hollow centre), Curb and gutter, or Steps.
  • Type the sizes. Each box has its own unit menu (feet, inches, yards, meters or centimeters), so a slab can be 16 ft by 12 ft with the thickness in inches. The US / metric switch only sets the starting units and which totals are shown first.
  • For Steps, enter the Run (tread depth), the Rise (step height), the Width, the Top platform depth and the Number of risers; count the top platform as one riser.
  • Set Quantity when you pour several identical items, such as 10 post holes or 4 columns.
  • Open Extra and bag size to add a margin for spills and uneven ground (many builders add 5 to 10%) and, if your bags are not 60 or 80 lb, type the weight of your bag in lb or kg.

A picture of the shape with your sizes marked appears under the result, so you can check that the right length went into the right box.

Understanding the results

Concrete needed is the volume, in cubic yards with the US setting or cubic meters with metric; the other two volume units follow below it. Pre-mixed concrete weight is that volume times 133 lb per cubic foot (2,130 kg per cubic meter), a typical figure for dry bagged mix. The bag rows divide the weight by 60 lb, 80 lb and your own bag size, show the exact number with two decimals, and give the whole number to buy, always rounded up.

When you have not added any extra, a note shows what the volume would be with 5% and 10% more, so you can see the margin before you order. Once you add extra, the note gives the volume before the extra instead.

Worked examples: patio, post holes, columns and steps

Patio slab. A 16 ft × 12 ft patio poured 4 in thick holds 16 × 12 × (4 ÷ 12) = 64 ft³, which is 2.37 yd³ or 1.81 m³. Add 10% extra and it becomes 70.4 ft³ (2.61 yd³), or 117.04 bags of 80 lb, so buy 118. At more than two cubic yards, a ready-mix delivery is usually less work than mixing well over a hundred bags by hand.

Fence posts. Ten post holes, each 10 in across and 3 ft deep, take 16.36 ft³ (0.61 yd³) in total: 27.2 bags of 80 lb, so 28 bags. The post itself fills part of each hole, so the real amount is a little lower; the spare covers holes that come out wider than planned.

Round columns. Four Sonotube forms 12 in in diameter and 8 ft tall hold 25.13 ft³ (0.93 yd³), about 41.78 bags of 80 lb. A round patio 10 ft across and 4 in thick, entered as Round slab or tube with an inner diameter of 0, needs 26.18 ft³ (0.97 yd³).

Front steps. Three risers of 7 in, treads 11 in deep, 36 in wide, with a 36 in top platform, come to 20.56 ft³ (0.76 yd³) or 34.19 bags of 80 lb.

Metric. A 5 m × 4 m shed base 10 cm thick is exactly 2 m³ (4,260 kg of pre-mix, 170.4 bags of 25 kg). With 5% extra the order is 2.1 m³, or 178.92 bags of 25 kg, so 179 bags.

Concrete quantity in cft and mix ratios (Pakistan and South Asia)

On building sites in Pakistan and India, concrete for floors, lintels and roof slabs is usually measured in cubic feet (cft), with lengths in feet and thickness in inches. A room floor 14 ft × 12 ft with a 3 in layer of plain concrete under the tiles is 14 × 12 × (3 ÷ 12) = 42 cft, which the calculator also shows as 1.19 m³ and 1.56 yd³.

Site-mixed concrete is often ordered by a nominal mix: parts of cement to sand to crushed stone by volume. The table lists the common grades. The M number is the target compressive strength in megapascals after 28 days.

The volume this page gives is wet, placed concrete. Loose dry materials take up more room than the concrete they make, so cement, sand and stone quantities are worked out from a larger dry volume that your engineer or contractor will specify. Structural work such as roof slabs, beams and columns also needs steel reinforcement and a design that meets local building rules. Concrete rates in Pakistan change by city and month, so multiply the cft or m³ figure by a current quote rather than an old price list.

Mixing, curing and strength

Strength depends as much on what happens after the pour as on the mix. The same data sheet lists typical compressive strength for its general-purpose mix of 2,500 psi (17.2 MPa) at 7 days and 4,000 psi (27.5 MPa) at 28 days, and asks for about 6 pints (2.8 L) of water per 80 lb bag to start, up to about 9 pints (4.3 L). Too much water makes the mix easier to place but weaker.

Curing means keeping the new concrete damp and at a moderate temperature while the cement reacts. The maker advises starting as soon as possible and continuing for 5 days in warm weather (70 °F, 21 °C or higher) or 7 days at 50 to 70 °F (10 to 21 °C), using a curing compound, wet burlap, plastic sheeting or a sprinkler. In hot, dry and windy weather the surface loses water fast and can crack, which is a real risk in Pakistani summers.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Measure the depth in several places. Ground is rarely level, and an extra half inch over a whole slab adds 12.5% to a 4 in pour.
  • Keep units straight. Typing a 4 in thickness into a box set to feet gives a slab 12 times too thick; check the unit menu next to every box.
  • For a wall, use Slab or footing with the wall's length, height and thickness.
  • For a triangular pad, enter its base and height as the length and width, then halve the result.
  • For post holes the maker's advice is a hole about 3 times the post's width and one third of the post's height deep; enter those sizes as the diameter and depth.
  • Cut control joints in slabs. The same data sheet suggests grooves 1 in deep every 6 to 8 ft so that shrinkage cracks form along the joints instead of across the slab.
  • Round the order up. Running short in the middle of a pour leaves a weak joint where old and new concrete meet.

How it works

Pick the shape you are pouring and type its sizes. Every box has its own unit menu, so a slab can be measured in feet with its thickness in inches, or in meters and centimeters. The amount of concrete appears as you type, in cubic yards (how ready-mix trucks sell it in the US) or cubic meters, together with the weight of pre-mixed concrete and the number of 60 lb and 80 lb bags. Under Extra and bag size you can add a margin for spills and uneven ground and give the weight of the bag you plan to buy.

The formula

  • Slab, wall or square footing: V = length × width × thickness
  • Round hole or column: V = π × (diameter ÷ 2)² × depth
  • Round slab or tube: V = π ÷ 4 × (outer diameter² − inner diameter²) × height
  • Curb and gutter: cross-section = curb depth × (curb height + flag thickness) + gutter width × flag thickness; V = cross-section × length
  • Steps (n risers, run r, rise h, width w, top platform p): V = w × (r × h × (1 + 2 + … + (n − 1)) + p × h × n)
  • All volumes × quantity × (1 + extra %); 1 yd³ = 27 ft³, 1 ft³ = 0.028316846592 m³
  • Weight of pre-mixed concrete: pounds = ft³ × 133; kilograms = m³ × 2,130; bags = weight ÷ bag weight

A worked example

A patio slab 16 ft long, 12 ft wide and 4 in thick: 16 × 12 × 4/12 = 64 ft³, which is 2.37 yd³ or 1.81 m³. At 133 lb/ft³ that is 8,512 lb of pre-mixed concrete: 141.87 bags of 60 lb (buy 142) or 106.4 bags of 80 lb (buy 107). With 10% extra it becomes 70.4 ft³ (2.61 yd³) and 117.04 bags of 80 lb.

Questions

How many bags of concrete do I need for a slab?

Enter the slab's length, width and thickness and read the 60 lb and 80 lb bag counts. The exact figure is shown with two decimals and the number to buy is rounded up. For larger pours (more than about 1 cubic yard) a ready-mix truck is usually cheaper and easier than bags.

How do I calculate concrete in cubic yards?

Multiply length × width × thickness in feet to get cubic feet, then divide by 27. A 16 ft × 12 ft slab 4 in thick is 64 ft³, which is 64 ÷ 27 = 2.37 cubic yards.

How many 80 lb bags of concrete make a yard?

About 45, because an 80 lb bag yields roughly 0.6 ft³ and a cubic yard is 27 ft³. With 60 lb bags it takes about 60.

How do I calculate concrete quantity in cft?

Multiply the length and width in feet by the thickness in feet (inches ÷ 12). A 14 ft × 12 ft floor 3 in thick is 14 × 12 × 0.25 = 42 cft; the calculator shows the cubic feet line in every result.

How do I calculate concrete in cubic meters?

Multiply length × width × thickness, all in meters. A 5 m × 4 m slab 10 cm (0.1 m) thick is 2 m³; switch the calculator to metric to see m³ first.

How thick should a concrete slab be?

It depends on the load, the soil and local rules. Bagged general-purpose mix is meant for pours of at least 2 in (5 cm), the field help on this page suggests about 4 in for patios and paths and 5 to 6 in for driveways, and structural slabs follow an engineer's design.

Should I order extra concrete?

Yes. Forms bulge, the ground is never perfectly flat and some concrete is always left in the mixer or barrow, so builders usually add 5 to 10 percent. Use the extra box under Extra and bag size to include it in every total.

Why is the weight only an estimate?

Pre-mixed concrete is taken as 133 lb per cubic foot (2,130 kg per cubic meter), a typical value for dry bagged mixes. The real yield depends on the brand and how much water you add, so check the yield printed on the bag.

How do I work out a round column or a Sonotube?

Use Hole or column for a solid round column and type its diameter and height. For a ring or hollow tube, use Round slab or tube and give both the outer and the inner diameter; an inner diameter of 0 gives a solid round slab.

What is the 1:2:4 concrete ratio?

It is a nominal mix of 1 part cement, 2 parts sand and 4 parts crushed stone by volume, listed as grade M15 (about 15 MPa after 28 days). The calculator gives the total concrete volume; the split into materials depends on the dry-volume allowance your engineer uses.

How long does concrete take to cure?

Keep it damp for at least 5 days in warm weather and 7 days in cooler weather, as one major maker advises. Concrete keeps gaining strength for weeks; its rated strength is usually the 28-day figure.
