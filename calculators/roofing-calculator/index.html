What the roofing calculator works out

This roofing calculator estimates the area of a sloped roof from the size of the house, the roof pitch and the eaves overhang, then turns it into roofing squares, shingle bundles, roll roofing and felt underlayment rolls. Add a price and it gives the cost.

It works in square feet or square meters. If you already know the roof area, from a plan or a roofer's report, the second tab skips the slope and overhang and goes straight to the materials.

How to use the roofing calculator

  • Pick a tab: From the house size, or I know the roof area. Choose US or Metric units at the top.
  • House footprint from: type the ground-floor area, or choose Length and width for a rectangular house, which makes the overhang more exact.
  • Roof slope as: pick a pitch from 1/12 to 24/12, or type the angle in degrees (0° to 89°).
  • Eaves overhang: how far the roof sticks out past the walls, such as 1 ft or 0.3 m.
  • Price: optionally type a material or installed price per ft², m², yd² or per square (100 ft²), and pick your currency.
  • Read the roof area and squares at the top, the slope factor and flat area below, and the materials table.

Roof pitch and the slope factor

Roof pitch is the rise of the roof over a horizontal run, written in the US as inches of rise per 12 inches (a 6/12 or "6 in 12" roof rises 6 inches every foot). It can also be given as the angle in degrees between the roof and the horizontal. To measure it, hold a level 12 inches long against the roof or a rafter and measure straight up to the roof surface.

A sloped roof is larger than the flat area under it, and the slope factor says by how much: √(1 + (rise ÷ 12)²), or 1 ÷ cos(angle). Multiply the flat area (with overhang) by it to get the real roof area. The table shows the factor for common pitches and what it does to a 1,000 ft² flat area.

How to calculate roofing squares and shingles

Roofers in the US price and order by the square: 100 ft² (about 9.29 m²) of roof surface. Divide the roof area in square feet by 100 and add an allowance for cutting and waste; the calculator adds 10%. Most asphalt shingles come about three bundles to the square, so bundles = roof area × 3 ÷ 100.

If you already know the area, use I know the roof area. A 1,850 ft² roof needs 21 squares with the 10% extra, 56 shingle bundles, 19 rolls of roll roofing, or 5 rolls of #15 felt or 10 rolls of #30 felt as underlayment.

Felt underlayment (tar paper) is an asphalt-soaked paper or felt laid under the shingles as an extra waterproof layer; it is sold in 3-foot-wide rolls, commonly as 15 lb and 30 lb grades. Synthetic underlayments made with mesh or fiberglass are also common, so check the coverage on the roll you buy.

Metric roofs and roofing sheets

Switch to Metric for meters and square meters. A 12 × 9 m house with a 20° roof and a 0.45 m overhang has a flat area of 129.7 m², a slope factor of 1.0642 (20° is a pitch of 4.37/12) and a roof of 138 m². At an example installed price of Rs 3,500 per m², that is about Rs 483,013.

For metal or fibre-cement roofing sheets, divide the roof area by the area one sheet really covers after the side and end overlaps, which the supplier lists as its effective (cover) width. With sheets covering an effective 0.8 m × 3 m, or 2.4 m² each, the 138 m² roof needs 138 ÷ 2.4 = 57.5, so 58 sheets before any allowance for cutting. Clay and concrete tiles are counted the same way, from the number per square meter the maker gives.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Do not forget the overhang. On the 46 × 30 ft house a 1 ft overhang adds 172 ft² of flat area before the slope.
  • Use Length and width for long, narrow houses; the Total area option treats the house as a square, which slightly underestimates the overhang strip.
  • Measure the pitch on the roof itself, not from a photo. A 4/12 and a 6/12 roof can look alike from the ground but differ by 6% in area.
  • Allow more waste for roofs with many valleys, hips, dormers or skylights; 10% is for a fairly simple roof.
  • Very low slopes need different materials. Canada's National Building Code, for example, treats roofs below 4/12 as low slope, and shingle makers set their own minimum pitch, so check the product instructions.
  • Remember extras that are not in the area: ridge caps, starter strips, drip edge, flashing, nails and vents.

How it works

Give the size of the house's ground floor, as a total area or as length and width, the roof slope (a pitch such as 6 in 12, or an angle in degrees) and how far the roof reaches past the walls. The roof area appears as you type, with the number of roof squares to buy (10% extra) and a list of common US roofing products: shingle bundles, roll roofing and #15 or #30 felt underlayment. Add a price per square foot, square meter or roofing square to see the cost. If you already know the roof area, use the second tab.

The formula

  • Overhang strip, house as a square of area A: 4e√A + 4e²; house of length L and width W: 2e(L + W) + 4e² (e = eaves overhang)
  • Flat area with overhang = footprint + 1.1 × overhang strip (10% for gable ends, hips and trim)
  • Slope factor = √(1 + (rise ÷ 12)²) for a pitch of rise in 12, or 1 ÷ cos(angle)
  • Roof area = flat area × slope factor
  • Roof squares = roof area (ft²) × 1.1 ÷ 100, rounded up; shingle bundles = roof area × 3 ÷ 100; roll roofing = area ÷ 100; #15 felt = area ÷ 400; #30 felt = area ÷ 200 (each rounded up)

A worked example

A 2,000 ft² house with a 6 in 12 roof and a 1 ft overhang: the strip is 4 × 1 × √2,000 + 4 = 182.9 ft², so the flat area is 2,000 + 1.1 × 182.9 = 2,201.2 ft². The slope factor of 6/12 is √1.25 = 1.118, giving a roof of about 2,461 ft²: 28 squares with 10% extra, 74 shingle bundles and 13 rolls of #30 felt.

Questions

How do I calculate roofing squares?

Find the roof area in square feet, divide by 100 and add about 10% for waste. A 1,850 ft² roof is 18.5 squares, so 21 with the 10% extra, rounded up. The calculator does this from the house size and pitch too.

What is a roofing square?

Roofers in the United States measure roofs in squares: one square is 100 square feet (about 9.29 m²) of roof surface. Most asphalt shingles come in bundles of about 33.3 square feet, so three bundles cover one square.

How do I calculate the square feet of a roof?

Take the house footprint plus the overhang and multiply by the slope factor for your pitch. A 46 × 30 ft house with a 1 ft overhang and a 5/12 roof gives 1,552 ft² × 1.0833 = about 1,681 ft² of roof.

How do I find my roof pitch?

Pitch is how many inches the roof rises for every 12 inches it runs across. Hold a level 12 inches long against a rafter or the roof surface and measure straight up to the roof: 6 inches means a 6 in 12 pitch, which is an angle of about 26.6°.

How do I calculate roofing sheets for a building?

Work out the roof area here, then divide by the effective area of one sheet (cover width × length after overlaps) from the supplier, and round up. A 138 m² roof with sheets covering 2.4 m² each needs 58 sheets, plus a few for cutting.

Does it work for a hip roof?

Yes, if all the faces have the same pitch: the roof area is the flat area times the slope factor whether the ends are gables or hips. Hip roofs have more cutting along the hips, so allow a little more waste.

Why does the overhang matter so much?

The roof is bigger than the house because it reaches past every wall. A 1 ft overhang on a 2,000 ft² house adds about 200 ft² before the slope is applied. If your house is long and narrow, enter its length and width so the overhang strip is worked out exactly.

Why add 10% to the squares?

Shingles are cut at hips, valleys, edges and around chimneys, and some are wasted. Ten percent is a common allowance for a simple roof; roofs with many valleys or dormers may need 15% or more.

What is roofing felt or underlayment?

It is a layer laid on the roof deck under the shingles to add waterproofing. Traditional felt is paper or felt soaked in asphalt, sold in 3-foot rolls in 15 lb and 30 lb grades; synthetic underlayments are also used. The calculator counts #15 rolls at about 400 ft² and #30 rolls at about 200 ft².

Does this work for metal or tile roofs?

The roof area does: it is the same for every material. The bundle and felt counts are for asphalt shingles and felt rolls in common US sizes. Metal sheets and clay or concrete tiles vary by product, so give the roof area to your supplier.
