What half-life means

Half-life is the time it takes for half of something that decays to be gone. In physics it is the time for half the nuclei of a radioactive isotope to decay; in pharmacology it is the time for the amount of a drug in the body to fall by half; in chemistry it is the time for half of a reactant to be used up in a first-order reaction. The rule is the same in each case: in every half-life, half of whatever is left disappears.

This half-life calculator solves the decay formula Nₜ = N₀ × (1/2)^(t ÷ t½) for any one of its four values, shows the steps, and adds the mean lifetime and decay constant, a halving table and a decay curve. Its second tab converts between half-life, mean lifetime and decay constant.

How to use the half-life calculator

  • On the Decay tab, pick what you want to find under What do you want to find?: Amount left, Starting amount, Time or Half-life. The box for that value disappears.
  • Fill in the other three: Starting amount N₀ (100 to start), Amount left Nₜ (25), Time passed t (10) and Half-life t½ (5). Amounts can be grams, milligrams, atoms, counts per minute or percent, as long as both amounts use the same unit.
  • Use one time unit for every time you type. Under More options, the Time unit menu (No unit, Seconds, Minutes, Hours, Days or Years; Years to start) only labels the answers; it does not convert.
  • On the Half-life, τ and λ tab, choose under I know the whether you have the Half-life t½, the Mean lifetime τ or the Decay constant λ, and type it to get the other two.

Understanding the results

  • The headline is the value you asked for. For the amount left, the line under it also gives the share of the start that remains (25% for the opening example).
  • Mean lifetime τ: the average time a single atom or molecule lasts before it decays, t½ ÷ ln 2, about 1.44 times the half-life.
  • Decay constant λ: the rate of decay per unit of time, ln 2 ÷ t½. For a half-life of 5 years it is 0.1386 per year. In chemistry this is the rate constant k of a first-order reaction.
  • Half-lives passed: t ÷ t½, which need not be a whole number (0.737 half-lives for a sample with 60% left).
  • Share left: Nₜ ÷ N₀ as a percentage.
  • After each half-life: a table (open it under the results) of the time, the amount left and the share left after 0, 1, 2 … half-lives, and the Amount left over time chart, which shows the curve falling by half over each equal step.

Worked examples with steps

Amount left. 100 g with a half-life of 5 years, after 10 years: t ÷ t½ = 10 ÷ 5 = 2 half-lives, so Nₜ = 100 × (1/2)² = 25 g. The calculator adds τ = 7.2134752044448 years and λ = 0.13862943611199 per year.

Time. How long until a cobalt-60 source (half-life 5.27 years) falls to 10% of its strength? Find Time with N₀ = 100, Nₜ = 10 and t½ = 5.27: N₀ ÷ Nₜ = 10, log₂(10) = 3.3219280948874 half-lives, and t = 3.3219280948874 × 5.27 = 17.506561060056 years, so about 17.5 years.

Half-life. A sample falls from 80 g to 10 g in 24 days. Find Half-life: 80 ÷ 10 = 8 = 2³, so 3 half-lives have passed and t½ = 24 ÷ 3 = 8 days, close to iodine-131's 8.06 days.

Starting amount. If 12.5 mg is left after 15 years of a substance with a 5-year half-life, 3 half-lives have passed and the start was 12.5 × 2³ = 100 mg.

Carbon dating. Carbon-14 has a half-life of about 5,730 years. A wooden object with 60% of the carbon-14 of living wood gives N₀ ÷ Nₜ = 100 ÷ 60 = 1.6667, which is log₂(1.6667) = 0.73696559416621 half-lives, so t = 0.737 × 5,730 ≈ 4,223 years. Radiocarbon laboratories still report raw ages with the older Libby half-life of 5,568 years by convention and then calibrate them against tree-ring records, so a published date can differ from this simple estimate.

Half-life of drugs and caffeine

When a medicine is cleared at a rate proportional to how much is present, its level follows exactly this halving pattern. The elimination half-life is the time for the amount in the blood to fall by half. A medical review in StatPearls notes that 94% to 97% of a drug is eliminated after 4 to 5 half-lives, the same numbers as the table above (93.75% and 96.875%). This page gives a mathematical estimate, not medical advice: real half-lives vary from person to person, so follow the dosing on the label or from your doctor or pharmacist.

Caffeine is a familiar example. The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) gives an average half-life of about 4 hours in healthy adults, with a range of 2 to 8 hours; it varies with age, body weight, pregnancy, other medicines and liver health. A 200 mg coffee at 2 pm, with a 4-hour half-life, leaves 200 × (1/2)² = 50 mg at 10 pm and 25 mg at 2 am.

For several doses, work out what is left of each dose and add them. With 100 mg at 8 am and another 100 mg at noon and a 4-hour half-life, at 8 pm the first dose has had 12 hours (12.5 mg left) and the second 8 hours (25 mg left), so about 37.5 mg is in the body. Run the calculator once per dose.

Half-life in chemistry and from a graph

In chemistry, a first-order reaction has a half-life that does not depend on how much reactant you start with: t½ = 0.693 ÷ k, where k is the rate constant. That is the decay constant λ here, so the Half-life, τ and λ tab doubles as a first-order half-life calculator: a rate constant of 0.0231 per minute gives t½ = 30.006371452811, about 30 minutes. Zero-order reactions (t½ = [A]₀ ÷ 2k) and second-order reactions (t½ = 1 ÷ k[A]₀) do not follow this rule, because their half-life changes with the starting concentration [A]₀.

To find a half-life from a decay graph, read the starting value on the vertical axis, find half of it, go across to the curve and read the time below. Then check from a second point: from half to a quarter should take the same time. Taking the average of several such readings reduces the error from a hand-drawn curve or from the random scatter of a real count.

For school, common mistakes and related calculators

The Cambridge O Level Physics syllabus (5054, exams in 2026 to 2028) asks students to define half-life as the time for half the nuclei of an isotope to decay, use it in calculations with tables and decay curves, describe carbon-14 dating and explain how half-life decides which isotope suits smoke alarms, sterilising equipment or cancer treatment. O Level Mathematics (4024) covers exponential growth and decay in general.

  • Mixing time units: a half-life in days with a time in hours gives a wrong answer. Convert first.
  • Subtracting half the original each time: each half-life removes half of what is left, not half of the starting amount. Linear thinking says 100 g is gone after 2 half-lives; in fact 25 g remains.
  • Using the share decayed instead of the share left: if 75% has decayed, Nₜ is 25% of N₀.
  • Expecting an amount left larger than the start: decay only goes down, so the calculator asks for Nₜ smaller than N₀.

How it works

The half-life is the time it takes for half of a decaying amount to disappear. After one half-life 50% is left, after two 25%, after three 12.5%, and so on. Choose what you want to find and fill in the other three values; use one time unit for everything (the unit menu under More options only labels the answers). The result also gives the mean lifetime τ and the decay constant λ, how many half-lives have passed, a table of the amount after each half-life and a decay curve. The second tab converts between half-life, mean lifetime and decay constant.

The formula

  • Amount left: Nₜ = N₀ × (1/2)^(t ÷ t½)
  • Starting amount: N₀ = Nₜ × 2^(t ÷ t½)
  • Time: t = t½ × log₂(N₀ ÷ Nₜ)
  • Half-life: t½ = t × ln 2 ÷ ln(N₀ ÷ Nₜ)
  • Mean lifetime τ = t½ ÷ ln 2, decay constant λ = ln 2 ÷ t½ = 1 ÷ τ

A worked example

100 g of a substance with a half-life of 5 years: after 10 years, 10 ÷ 5 = 2 half-lives have passed, so 100 × (1/2)² = 25 g is left. Its mean lifetime is 5 ÷ 0.693 = 7.21 years and its decay constant is 0.693 ÷ 5 = 0.139 per year.

Questions

How do I calculate half-life from two amounts?

Divide the starting amount by the amount left, take log base 2 of that to get the number of half-lives, and divide the time by it: t½ = t × ln 2 ÷ ln(N₀ ÷ Nₜ). If 80 g becomes 10 g in 24 days, 80 ÷ 10 = 8 = 2³, so t½ = 24 ÷ 3 = 8 days. Choose Half-life under What do you want to find? and the steps show this.

What is the half-life formula?

The decay formula is Nₜ = N₀ × (1/2)^(t ÷ t½), where N₀ is the starting amount, Nₜ the amount after time t and t½ the half-life. The same rule can be written Nₜ = N₀ × e^(−λt) with λ = ln 2 ÷ t½.

What is half-life in simple words?

It is how long it takes for half of something to be gone. If a medicine has a half-life of 6 hours, half of a dose is left after 6 hours, a quarter after 12 and an eighth after 18.

How do I calculate the half-life of a drug in my body?

You need the drug's half-life, usually listed in its prescribing information, then the amount left is the dose × (1/2)^(hours ÷ half-life). This gives a rough mathematical estimate only: kidney and liver function, age and other medicines change real half-lives, so ask a doctor or pharmacist about timing doses.

How long does caffeine stay in your body?

EFSA gives an average half-life of about 4 hours for healthy adults, ranging from 2 to 8 hours. With 4 hours, a quarter of the caffeine is still there after 8 hours and an eighth after 12.

How do I calculate half-life from a graph?

Read the starting value, find the point where the curve has fallen to half of it, and read the time on the horizontal axis. Repeat from that point to the quarter value and average the two times for a better estimate.

What is the half-life formula for a first-order reaction?

t½ = 0.693 ÷ k, where 0.693 is ln 2 and k is the rate constant. It does not depend on the starting concentration. Type k as the decay constant λ in the second tab to get the half-life.

What is the difference between half-life, mean lifetime and decay constant?

They are three ways to describe the same decay. The half-life is the time until half is left. The mean lifetime τ is the average time one atom lasts, about 1.44 times the half-life. The decay constant λ is the share that decays per unit of time, and it equals 1 ÷ τ.

Which units should I use?

Any, as long as they match. If the half-life is in years, type the time in years too, and the answer comes out in years. The amounts can be grams, atoms, percent or anything else, as long as both amounts use the same unit.

Does anything ever decay to exactly zero?

In this model, no: each half-life halves what is left, so the amount gets smaller and smaller without reaching 0. In practice, after about 10 half-lives less than 0.1% is left.

How is half-life used in carbon dating?

Living things keep a steady share of carbon-14, which starts to decay when they die. Measuring how much is left gives the number of half-lives passed: with 50% left an object is about 5,730 years old, and with 12.5% left about 17,190 years old.
