What is lean body mass?

Lean body mass (LBM) is everything in your body that is not fat: muscle, bone, organs, skin, blood and body water. This lean body mass calculator estimates it in kg or lb from your height, weight and sex with three adult formulas, Boer, James and Hume, and adds the Peters formula for children aged 14 or younger.

The idea is simple: weight = lean mass + fat mass. Two people of the same weight can have very different lean mass, and lean tissue uses far more energy than fat, so lean mass is a better guide than total weight for some medicine doses, for protein targets and for resting energy use.

Formulas that use only height and weight give an average for a typical body. They cannot see whether your weight is muscle or fat, so treat the answer as an estimate, not a measurement.

How to use the lean body mass calculator

  • Pick Metric (kg and cm) or US units (lb, feet and inches).
  • Choose Male or Female; every formula has a separate version for each sex.
  • Answer Age 14 or younger? with Yes for a child, which adds the Peters formula and makes it the headline.
  • Enter height and weight, then read your lean body mass (the average of the adult formulas, or Peters for a child), the fat mass that leaves, a body fat estimate and your BMI.
  • Open the table to see each formula's lean mass, its share of your weight and the body fat it implies.

The lean body mass formulas compared

All three adult formulas are straight lines or simple curves in weight and height, fitted to measurements of real people decades ago. They agree within a few kilograms for most adults, which is why the calculator shows their average.

Worked examples in kg and lb

  • Karachi, a man of 172 cm and 78 kg (BMI 26.4): Boer 58.5 kg, James 59.5 kg, Hume 54.4 kg. Average lean mass 57.5 kg, 74% of his weight, so 20.5 kg of fat and an estimated 26% body fat.
  • US units, a woman of 5 ft 5 in and 145 lb (BMI 24.1): Boer 102.2 lb, James 103.4 lb, Hume 99.6 lb. Average 101.7 lb, 70% of her weight, leaving 43.3 lb of fat (about 30%).
  • Metric, a woman of 162 cm and 60 kg (BMI 22.9): average lean mass 43.2 kg (72%), fat mass 16.8 kg, an estimated 28% body fat.
  • A child: a 10-year-old girl, 138 cm and 32 kg. Peters gives 27.2 kg, 85% of her weight. The adult formulas would give 23.9 to 26.3 kg, which is why Peters is the one to use for children.
  • Very high BMI: a man of 175 cm and 130 kg (BMI 42.4). James is left out; Boer gives 80.4 kg and Hume 72.5 kg, an average of 76.5 kg (59%), leaving 53.5 kg of fat.

Lean body mass with a body fat percentage

If you already know your body fat percentage from a scan, a body composition scale or a tape-measure method, you do not need a height and weight formula. Lean body mass = weight × (1 − body fat ÷ 100). For the Karachi man above, a measured 22% body fat would mean 78 × 0.78 = 60.8 kg of lean mass, a little more than the formulas suggest.

Our Body Fat Calculator estimates body fat from a few tape measurements, and the result can be used in that sum. Methods such as DEXA scans and bioelectrical impedance (the technology in InBody machines and smart scales) measure body composition more directly, though impedance readings move with hydration.

Lean body mass versus muscle mass

Lean mass is not the same as muscle. It includes bone, organs, skin and the water in all of them, so muscle is only part of it, and a body composition report that lists both will always show lean mass as the larger number.

Changes in lean mass over a few days are mostly water. Over months, strength training and enough protein are the usual ways to build muscle and so raise lean mass, while fast weight loss can lower it. Measure under the same conditions each time, for example in the morning before eating.

Using lean body mass for protein, calories and medicine

  • Protein: some plans set protein per kg of lean mass rather than total weight, which avoids overestimating needs in people with a lot of body fat. The Protein Calculator gives daily grams.
  • Calories: the Katch-McArdle formula in our BMR Calculator works from lean mass (370 + 21.6 × lean mass in kg), which suits lean, muscular people.
  • Medicine: some drug doses are scaled to lean body weight, especially in people with obesity. Doses are set by the prescriber; use this page only to understand the idea.
  • Body surface area is another size measure used for doses; see the Body Surface Area Calculator.

Tips and limits

  • Very muscular people: the formulas assume an average build, so a bodybuilder's lean mass is usually underestimated and their body fat overestimated.
  • Older adults: lean mass tends to fall with age, which height and weight alone cannot show, so the result can be too high.
  • Heavier bodies: above a BMI of 40 the James formula is dropped, and the other two become less reliable too.
  • Units: check kg against lb and cm against inches; a unit slip is the most common cause of a strange result.

How it works

Lean body mass is your weight minus your body fat: muscle, bone, organs, skin and water. Doctors use it to dose some medicines, and it is a better base than total weight for protein and energy needs. Measuring it takes a body scan, so these formulas estimate it from height, weight and sex instead. Boer, James and Hume were fitted to adults; Peters is made for children, so it is added when you say the person is 14 or younger. Each formula's share of body weight is rounded to a whole percent, and the rest is shown as body fat. The headline is the average of the adult formulas, or the Peters result for a child.

The formula

  • Boer (1984): men 0.407 W + 0.267 H − 19.2; women 0.252 W + 0.473 H − 48.3
  • James (1976): men 1.1 W − 128 (W ÷ H)²; women 1.07 W − 148 (W ÷ H)²
  • Hume (1966): men 0.32810 W + 0.33929 H − 29.5336; women 0.29569 W + 0.41813 H − 43.2933
  • Peters (2011, children): eECV = 0.0215 × W^0.6469 × H^0.7236; lean body mass = 3.8 × eECV
  • W = weight in kg, H = height in cm; share of weight = lean mass ÷ weight, rounded to a whole percent; body fat = 100% − share

A worked example

A man 172 cm tall weighing 78 kg: Boer gives 0.407 × 78 + 0.267 × 172 − 19.2 ≈ 58.5 kg (75% of his weight, so about 25% body fat); James gives about 59.5 kg and Hume about 54.4 kg. Their average is 57.5 kg, leaving 20.5 kg of fat mass, an estimated 26% body fat.

Questions

How do I calculate lean body mass?

With a body fat percentage, multiply your weight by (1 − body fat ÷ 100). Without one, use a formula such as Boer: for men 0.407 × weight in kg + 0.267 × height in cm − 19.2. The calculator runs Boer, James and Hume and shows their average.

How do I calculate lean body mass in kg or pounds?

Choose Metric for kg or US units for lb at the top; the formulas work in kg and cm and the result is converted for you. A 5 ft 5 in, 145 lb woman gets about 101.7 lb, which is about 46.1 kg.

What is a normal lean body mass percentage?

It is 100% minus your body fat percentage. In the examples on this page, a man of 172 cm and 78 kg comes out at 74% lean and a woman of 162 cm and 60 kg at 72%; women usually carry a higher share of fat than men of the same size.

Which formula should I trust?

For most adults the three give results within a few kilograms of each other, which is why the average is shown. Boer was built for medical use. For children 14 and under use Peters, because the adult formulas were not made for them.

Why is the James formula sometimes missing?

James was fitted to people of ordinary weight. For heavier people its result turns down and can even fall below zero, so it is left out when the BMI is above 40.

Is lean body mass the same as muscle?

No. Lean mass is everything that is not fat, so it includes bone, organs, skin and body water as well as muscle. That is why a body composition report always shows lean mass as larger than muscle mass.

How do I use lean body mass for protein intake?

Multiply your lean mass in kg by the protein target per kg that your plan uses. Basing protein on lean mass rather than total weight keeps the target sensible for people with a lot of body fat.

Can I calculate lean body mass at home?

Yes, roughly. This page needs only height, weight and sex, and a tape-measure body fat estimate from the Body Fat Calculator can sharpen it. Home smart scales also estimate it, but their numbers shift with how much water you have drunk.

How can I raise my lean body mass?

Strength training two or three times a week and enough protein are the usual ways. Track it over months rather than days, because water shifts change the numbers from day to day.
