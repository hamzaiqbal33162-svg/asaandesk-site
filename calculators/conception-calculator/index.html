What is conception, and what does this calculator show?

Conception is the start of a pregnancy, when a sperm fertilises an egg. It can only happen in a short part of each menstrual cycle, around ovulation, so knowing those days is the most useful thing a couple trying for a baby can learn about timing.

This conception calculator works forward from the first day of your last period and your usual cycle length. It shows the days you are most likely to conceive this cycle, your likely ovulation day, the wider fertile days for sex, when a pregnancy test is worth doing, when your next period is due and the due date if you conceive this cycle, then repeats the pattern for six cycles.

It uses the calendar method, so it gives an estimate for a regular cycle, not a guarantee. Ovulation can move by several days from one month to the next, and nothing on this page is medical advice.

How to use the conception calculator

  • First day of your last period: pick the date your most recent period started (the first day of real bleeding, not spotting). It is set to today until you change it.
  • Usual cycle length: choose from 22 to 44 days. Count from the first day of one period to the first day of the next, and if it varies, use the average of your last few cycles.
  • Read the results at once; there is nothing else to enter. The calendar marks the conception days, the ovulation day and the fertile days, and the table lists your next 6 cycles.

Understanding your results

  • Most likely conception days: from 2 days before your likely ovulation day to 3 days after it, a 6-day span that covers the egg's short lifetime and normal variation in the ovulation day.
  • Ovulation window and most likely ovulation day: the single best guess (about 14 days before your next period) and two days either side.
  • Fertile days for sex: 5 days before ovulation to 2 days after. Sex on these days can lead to a pregnancy because sperm can wait in the body for the egg.
  • Take a pregnancy test from: about 9 days after ovulation, the earliest a sensitive test might work. A test is most reliable from the day your period is missed.
  • Next period expected and due date if you conceive this cycle: the due date moves one day later for each day your cycle is longer than 28 days.
  • Your next 6 cycles: the period start, conception days and due date for each, useful if you are planning when a baby would arrive.

The fertile window: what the research shows

The best-known study of timing followed 221 women trying to conceive and measured ovulation with daily hormone tests (Wilcox, Weinberg and Baird, 1995). Pregnancies came only from sex in a six-day span that ended on the day of ovulation, and the chance in a cycle rose from about 10% for sex five days before ovulation to about 33% on the day itself.

The reason is biology. ACOG says an egg lives for about a day after it is released, while sperm usually survive about 3 days and sometimes up to 5; the NHS notes that sperm can survive in the fallopian tubes for up to 7 days. So sex in the days before ovulation leaves sperm waiting when the egg arrives, and fertilisation, the moment of conception, happens around ovulation itself.

Your body also gives signs. Around ovulation, vaginal discharge often becomes thinner, clear and stretchy, like raw egg white (NHS). Ovulation test kits, which detect the hormone surge before ovulation, and a basal body temperature chart can confirm the pattern over a few months.

Working backwards: conception date from a due date, birth date or IVF

Many people want the opposite answer: when did conception happen? A due date set from the last period is 280 days (40 weeks) after that period started, and conception usually happens about two weeks after the period in a 28-day cycle, so it sits about 266 days before the due date. Our Pregnancy Conception Calculator works it out from a due date, a period or a scan.

From a birthday the estimate is rougher, because only about 1 baby in 20 arrives on the due date (ACOG) and a normal pregnancy lasts anywhere from 37 to 42 weeks (NHS). For IVF the date is known exactly: the Due Date Calculator uses the transfer date and the embryo's age.

Dates cannot settle which of two possible fathers it was. Conception can follow sex up to several days earlier, and even an early scan dates a pregnancy only to within several days, so when the dates are close only a DNA test can answer that question.

Improving your chances and when to get help

  • Have regular sex. The NHS says more than 8 in 10 couples where the woman is under 40 conceive within a year with sex every 2 or 3 days, without needing to time it.
  • If you do time it, aim for the fertile days, especially the two or three days before ovulation.
  • Take 400 micrograms of folic acid every day, ideally from 3 months before trying until 12 weeks of pregnancy (NHS). Some women, including those with diabetes, need a higher 5 mg dose from a doctor.
  • See a doctor if you have not conceived after a year of trying. ACOG advises a check after 6 months if you are older than 35, and the NHS suggests going sooner from 36 or if you already know of a possible fertility problem.
  • In Pakistan and elsewhere, a gynaecologist can check ovulation with blood tests or scans if your cycles are irregular; bring a record of your period dates to the visit.

Can timing choose a boy or a girl?

Some websites and old charts, including the so-called Chinese calendar, claim that timing sex can choose a baby's sex. The 1995 Wilcox study looked at this directly and found that the timing of sex relative to ovulation made no practical difference to whether the baby was a boy or a girl. This calculator does not offer a gender prediction for that reason; use the conception days to give yourself the best chance of a pregnancy instead.

How it works

Enter the first day of your last period and your usual cycle length. Ovulation is placed about 14 days before your next period. Conception can happen from two days before ovulation until the day after it, when the egg is still alive, so those six days are shown as the most likely conception days. Sex in the five days before ovulation can also lead to a pregnancy, because sperm survive for several days. The same pattern is repeated for your next six cycles.

The formula

  • Ovulation day O = first day of last period + (cycle length − 14) days
  • Most likely conception days = O − 2 to O + 3 days
  • Ovulation window = O − 2 to O + 2 days; fertile days for sex = O − 5 to O + 2 days
  • Pregnancy test from O + 9 days; next period = first day of last period + cycle length
  • Due date if you conceive this cycle = first day of last period + 280 + (cycle length − 28) days

A worked example

A period that started on November 8, 2026 with a 30-day cycle puts ovulation on November 8 + 16 = November 24. The most likely conception days are November 22 to 27, the next period is due on December 8, and a baby conceived this cycle would be due around August 17, 2027.

Questions

How do I calculate my conception date?

Add your cycle length minus 14 days to the first day of your last period to find the likely ovulation day; conception is most likely from 2 days before it to 3 days after. With a 28-day cycle that is about 14 days after your period started. The calculator does this and shows the dates on a calendar.

How do I calculate the conception date from a due date?

Take about 266 days (38 weeks) off the due date. This assumes the due date was set from a 28-day cycle or an early scan, and the real date can be a few days either side. The Pregnancy Conception Calculator on this site does it for you.

What is the conception date in pregnancy?

It is the day the egg was fertilised, usually about two weeks after the first day of the last period. Pregnancy weeks are counted from the period, not from conception, so on the conception day you are already counted as about 2 weeks pregnant.

How often should we have sex to conceive?

Every two to three days through the cycle gives a good chance without needing exact timing, and that is what the NHS suggests. If you prefer to time it, focus on the fertile days shown, especially the two days before ovulation.

Can a conception calculator tell which of 2 possible fathers it is?

No. Sperm can survive for several days and ovulation dates vary, so sex on quite different days can lead to the same pregnancy. When the dates are close, only a DNA paternity test gives a reliable answer.

Can timing sex help us have a baby boy?

There is no good evidence for it. The 1995 Wilcox study found that the timing of sex relative to ovulation had no practical effect on the baby's sex, so this calculator does not give gender predictions.

When should I take a pregnancy test?

The calculator shows a date about 9 days after ovulation, when some very sensitive tests may already work. The NHS says tests are most reliable from the first day of a missed period, so if an early test is negative, test again a few days later.

How long does it usually take to get pregnant?

The NHS says more than 8 in 10 couples where the woman is under 40 conceive within a year of regular sex. If you have not conceived after a year, see a doctor; ACOG advises going after 6 months if you are older than 35.

What if my cycles are irregular?

Then the dates here are rough. Use your average cycle length, and consider ovulation test kits or tracking body temperature to find your fertile days more exactly.

Is this different from the ovulation calculator?

It uses the same cycle maths. This page puts the days when conception itself can happen first (ovulation minus two days to the day after), while the ovulation calculator leads with the ovulation day and window.
