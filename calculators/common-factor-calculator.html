What a common factor is

A common factor of two or more whole numbers is a number that divides every one of them exactly, with nothing left over. 4 divides both 20 (20 ÷ 4 = 5) and 28 (28 ÷ 4 = 7), so 4 is a common factor of 20 and 28. This common factor calculator lists all of them, picks out the greatest common factor, and shows the factor list of each number so you can see where the answer comes from.

Every set of whole numbers shares at least the factor 1. When 1 is the only one, as for 8 and 15, the numbers are called co-prime (or relatively prime). The largest shared factor has three names that mean the same thing: greatest common factor (GCF), highest common factor (HCF, the term the Cambridge O Level syllabus uses) and greatest common divisor (GCD).

How to use the common factor calculator

  • Type the numbers in the Whole numbers box, separated by commas, spaces or semicolons. The page opens with 24, 36, 60.
  • Use up to 50 positive whole numbers, each up to 1,000,000,000,000,000 (10¹⁵). Zero, negative numbers and decimals are not accepted; see below for how to handle decimals.
  • The answer updates as you type. Open the steps to see each factor list and how the shared ones are picked out.
  • One number on its own is allowed too: its common factors are then simply all its factors.

Understanding the results

  • Common factors: every number that divides all of your numbers, from smallest to largest. For 24, 36 and 60 these are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 12.
  • Greatest common factor: the largest of them, 12 here.
  • How many: the count of common factors, 6 here.
  • Co-prime: Yes when only 1 is shared; otherwise how many shared factors there are above 1.
  • Factors of each number: a table with each number, how many factors it has, and the full list. It is collapsed when you type more than 14 numbers.
  • The steps end with the shortcut that makes large numbers easy: the common factors are exactly the factors of the GCF.

How to find common factors, with steps

Method 1, list and compare. Write out all the factors of each number in pairs, then circle the ones in every list. The calculator does this for 84 and 126:

  • Factors of 84: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 12, 14, 21, 28, 42, 84 (12 factors).
  • Factors of 126: 1, 2, 3, 6, 7, 9, 14, 18, 21, 42, 63, 126 (12 factors).
  • In both lists: 1, 2, 3, 6, 7, 14, 21, 42. That is 8 common factors, and the greatest is 42.

Method 2, prime factors. Break each number into primes: 84 = 2² × 3 × 7 and 126 = 2 × 3² × 7. Take each prime they share with its lower power: 2 × 3 × 7 = 42. Every common factor is built from those shared primes, which is why there are (1 + 1) × (1 + 1) × (1 + 1) = 8 of them.

Method 3, the quick way for big numbers. Find the GCF first with Euclid's algorithm: divide the larger number by the smaller and keep the remainder, then repeat with the smaller number and the remainder until the remainder is 0. For 126 and 84: 126 = 1 × 84 + 42, then 84 = 2 × 42 + 0, so the GCF is 42. Now list the factors of 42 alone. This is how the calculator handles 1,234,567,890 and 987,654,321 in an instant: their GCF is 9, so the common factors are just 1, 3 and 9.

Where common factors are used

Simplifying fractions. Dividing the top and bottom of a fraction by a common factor gives an equal fraction with smaller numbers, and dividing by the GCF gives the simplest form in one step: 84/126 ÷ 42/42 = 2/3. The Fraction Calculator does this for any fraction.

Sharing into equal groups. A school in Islamabad has 48 juice boxes and 72 packets of biscuits and wants identical party bags with nothing left over. The number of bags must be a common factor of 48 and 72: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12 or 24. The largest choice, 24 bags, puts 2 juice boxes and 3 packets in each.

Tiles, strips and cuts. To cover a floor 360 cm by 480 cm with whole square tiles and no cutting, the tile side must be a common factor of 360 and 480; the largest is 120 cm. Cutting two ropes of 40 m and 60 m into equal pieces as long as possible works the same way: 20 m pieces.

Ratios. A ratio such as 40 : 60 is simplified by dividing both parts by a common factor; the GCF, 20, gives 2 : 3 straight away. The Ratio Calculator shows this with steps.

Common factors in algebra, with decimals and with fractions

With variables and exponents, find the GCF of the number parts with this calculator, then take each variable the terms all share with its lowest power. For 12x³y and 18x²y², the numbers give 6, x appears at least twice in both and y at least once, so the GCF is 6x²y. Factoring it out is the first step in factoring an expression: 12x³y + 18x²y² = 6x²y(2x + 3y). This common monomial factor step is how most algebra factoring problems begin.

With decimals, scale them to whole numbers first. For 1.2 and 1.8, multiply both by 10 to get 12 and 18, whose GCF is 6, then divide back by 10: the greatest common factor is 0.6, and 1.2 ÷ 0.6 = 2 and 1.8 ÷ 0.6 = 3 confirm it.

With fractions, the question is usually about a common denominator, which is a common multiple rather than a common factor. The lowest common denominator of 1/6 and 1/8 is the LCM of 6 and 8, which is 24, from the LCM Calculator.

For school: HCF in O Level and Matric maths

Finding the highest common factor and the lowest common multiple of two numbers is part of the Number section of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics syllabus (4024, exams in 2025 to 2027), next to writing a number as a product of its prime factors. Paper 1 of that exam allows no calculator, so practise the prime-factor and Euclid methods above and use this page to check your answers.

A tidy way to set out an exam answer is to write both prime factorizations one above the other, ring the primes they share, and multiply the ringed primes. The Prime Factorization Calculator gives those factorizations with a factor tree.

Common mistakes and related calculators

  • Leaving out 1, or the GCF itself: both are always common factors.
  • Missing a factor pair: test every number up to the square root, or work from the GCF's factors instead.
  • Mixing up factors and multiples: the common factors of 6 and 8 are 1 and 2, while their common multiples are 24, 48, 72 and on for ever.
  • Taking the higher power of a shared prime: for the GCF use the lower power; the higher power belongs to the LCM.

For the GCF alone with Euclid's steps, use the GCF Calculator; for every factor of a single number, the Factor Calculator.

How it works

Type two or more positive whole numbers, separated by commas or spaces. The calculator lists the factors of each number, then keeps only the ones that appear in every list: those are the common factors, and the largest of them is the greatest common factor (GCF). The table holds each number's full factor list.

The formula

  • Common factors of a, b, c, … = the numbers that divide every one of them exactly
  • They are exactly the factors of GCF(a, b, c, …)
  • The GCF comes from Euclid's algorithm; its factors from its prime factorization

A worked example

24: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24. 36: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18, 36. 60: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 10, 12, 15, 20, 30, 60. The numbers in all three lists are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 12, so the greatest common factor is 12, and indeed the factors of 12 are the same six numbers.

Questions

How do you find the common factors of two numbers?

List the factors of each number, then keep the ones that appear in both lists. For 12 and 16 the lists are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12 and 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, so the common factors are 1, 2 and 4. Type the two numbers here and the calculator lines the lists up for you.

What are the common factors of 20 and 28?

They are 1, 2 and 4, and the greatest common factor is 4. The factors of 20 are 1, 2, 4, 5, 10, 20 and the factors of 28 are 1, 2, 4, 7, 14, 28. In primes, 20 = 2² × 5 and 28 = 2² × 7 share only 2².

What are the common factors of 6 and 9?

Only 1 and 3, so the GCF is 3. The factors of 6 are 1, 2, 3, 6 and the factors of 9 are 1, 3, 9; 3 is the only prime they share.

How can I find common factors quickly?

Find the greatest common factor first, then list the factors of that one number: they are exactly the common factors. Euclid's algorithm finds the GCF in a few divisions even for very large numbers, which saves listing hundreds of factors.

Is there a formula for common factors?

There is no single formula that lists them, but there is a rule: the common factors of a, b, c and so on are the factors of GCF(a, b, c). If the GCF is p^a × q^b in primes, the number of common factors is (a + 1) × (b + 1).

How do I find common factors of large numbers?

Use Euclid's algorithm or prime factorization rather than listing. This calculator accepts numbers up to 10¹⁵ and finds, for example, that 1,234,567,890 and 987,654,321 have only 1, 3 and 9 in common.

Can I find a common factor with decimals?

Yes: multiply all the numbers by the same power of 10 to make them whole, find their GCF, then divide back. For 0.25 and 0.75, use 25 and 75, whose GCF is 25, so the answer is 0.25. The calculator itself takes whole numbers only.

Is 1 always a common factor?

Yes. 1 divides every whole number, so it is in every list. When it is the only common factor, the numbers are called co-prime.

What is the difference between common factors and common multiples?

Common factors divide into all the numbers, so there are only a few and the largest is the GCF. Common multiples are numbers all of them divide into; there are endlessly many and the smallest is the LCM.

Are GCF, HCF and GCD the same?

Yes. Greatest common factor, highest common factor and greatest common divisor are three names for the largest number that divides all the given numbers. Cambridge O Level papers say HCF, many American textbooks say GCF, and gcd is the usual name in programming.
