What percent off means

A percent off calculator turns a sale sign into a price. Percent off is a discount written as a share of the original price: 25% off means the price drops by a quarter, so you pay the other 75%. The calculator shows the final price and how much you save, or works backwards from a sale price to the original price or the percent off.

The quickest way to think about it is to look at what you pay, not what is taken away. At 20% off you pay 80% of the price, at 35% off you pay 65%, and at 50% off you pay half. Multiply the price by that share and you have the sale price in one step.

How to use the percent off calculator

  • Choose your currency at the top: rupees, dollars, pounds, euros or any other. Only the symbol changes.
  • Fill in any two of the four boxes: Original price, Percent off, Final price and Amount saved. Leave the other two empty and they are worked out. The defaults are an original price of 80 and 20% off.
  • To stack a coupon or an extra in-store discount, tick Add a second discount, taken after the first, and type the Second percent off (10% by default).
  • If you fill in more than two boxes, the calculator works from two of them (the original price and the percent off when both are given) and warns you if another number you typed does not match.

Understanding the results

The headline shows the values you did not type: for the defaults, a final price of 64.00 and a saving of 16.00. The two numbers you entered are repeated underneath, and the steps show the sum in full, for example Final price = 80.00 × (1 − 20%) = 64.00.

With a second discount you also see the total percent off, the price after the first discount and a small table that takes the discounts one at a time. The first percent off is always applied first; when you work backwards from a final price or a saving, the calculator finds the first discount that fits with the second one you gave.

Working the other way is just as easy. A jacket marked down from 150 to 105 has 45 off, and 45 ÷ 150 × 100 = 30% off. A sale price of 45 after 40% off means the original was 45 ÷ 0.60 = 75. And if a sign says you save 30 at 25% off, the original price was 30 ÷ 0.25 = 120 and you pay 90.

Stacked discounts: why 30% plus 10% is not 40%

When a shop offers an extra percent off a price that is already reduced, the second discount works on the lower price. Take shoes at Rs 12,500 with 30% off and then a further 10% off at the till. The first discount takes Rs 3,750 and leaves Rs 8,750. The second takes 10% of Rs 8,750, which is Rs 875, not Rs 1,250, leaving Rs 7,875.

In total you save Rs 4,625, which is 37% of the original price rather than 40%. The quick way to see it: you pay 70% and then 90% of that, and 0.70 × 0.90 = 0.63, so you pay 63% and save 37%. The order of the two discounts does not change the final price, because multiplication gives the same answer either way.

A fixed amount off, such as Rs 500 off a bill over Rs 5,000, works differently and does depend on the order. For those, use the Discount Calculator.

Percent off with sales tax, and in Excel

Sales tax or VAT is normally added after the discount, on the price you actually pay. So find the sale price first, then multiply by 1 plus the tax rate. An 80 item at 20% off costs 64.00; with 8% sales tax that becomes 64.00 × 1.08 = 69.12. Taking the tax off first and then the discount gives the same total, but the receipt shows the tax on the discounted price. The Sales Tax Calculator and the VAT Calculator do that second step for you.

In Excel or Google Sheets, put the price in A2 and the percent off in B2 (typed as 20%). The final price is =A2*(1-B2) and the saving is =A2*B2. To find the percent off from two prices, use =(A2-C2)/A2 with the sale price in C2 and format the cell as a percentage. For a stacked discount with the second percent in D2, use =A2*(1-B2)*(1-D2).

Tips and common mistakes

  • Do not add stacked discounts together; multiply what you pay at each step instead.
  • Check what the extra percent is taken from. If an offer takes it from the original price, the two percents really do add up; if it takes it from the sale price, as this calculator assumes, they do not.
  • Compare the final price, not the percent. 40% off a high list price can still cost more than 20% off in another shop.
  • To check a sale price quickly in your head, find 10% by moving the decimal point one place, then multiply: 30% of 60 is 3 × 6 = 18.
  • Keep the receipt price and tax separate when you check a bill; the calculator works on prices before tax.

How it works

A percent off takes that share of the price away, so you pay the rest: 20% off means you pay 80%. Fill in any two of the original price, the percent off, the final price and the amount saved, and the other two are worked out. Shops often add a second discount, such as an extra 15% off at the till. That second discount is taken from the already lower price, so the two do not simply add up; switch it on to see the real total and the price after each step.

The formula

  • Final price = original price × (1 − percent off ÷ 100)
  • Amount saved = original price − final price
  • Original price = final price ÷ (1 − percent off ÷ 100)
  • With a second discount: final price = original × (1 − first % ÷ 100) × (1 − second % ÷ 100)
  • Total percent off = 1 − (1 − first %) × (1 − second %); first % = 1 − (1 − total %) ÷ (1 − second %)

A worked example

Shoes priced at Rs 12,500 with 30% off cost 12,500 × 0.70 = Rs 8,750. An extra 10% off that price takes them to 8,750 × 0.90 = Rs 7,875. You save Rs 4,625 in total, which is 37% of Rs 12,500, not 40%.

Questions

How do I calculate percent off a price?

Multiply the price by the share you still pay. For 30% off, you pay 70%, so 60 × 0.70 = 42. The saving is the difference, 18. Type the price and the percent off and the calculator shows both.

How do I calculate 50 percent off?

Halve the price. 50% off 120 is 60, and you save the other 60. For any percent, subtract it from 100 and multiply the price by what is left.

Is 20% off plus 15% off the same as 35% off?

No. The second discount is taken from the price that is already 20% lower, so you pay 80% × 85% = 68% of the price. That is 32% off in total. Turn on the second discount to see it worked out for your price.

How do I find the original price from a sale price?

Divide the sale price by what is left after the discount. At 40% off you pay 60%, so a 45 sale price means 45 ÷ 0.60 = 75. Type the final price and the percent off and leave the original price empty.

How do I work out the percent off from two prices?

Take the amount saved and divide it by the original price, then multiply by 100. From 150 down to 105 you save 45, and 45 ÷ 150 × 100 = 30% off.

How do I calculate percent off in Excel?

With the price in A2 and the percent in B2, the sale price is =A2*(1-B2). To find the percent off from a sale price in C2, use =(A2-C2)/A2 and format it as a percentage.

How do I include sales tax with a percent off?

Take the discount first, then add tax to the lower price: an 80 item at 20% off is 64.00, and with 8% tax it costs 69.12. Use the sales tax or VAT calculator for the tax step.

Does the order of two discounts matter?

Not for the final price: 20% then 15% gives the same price as 15% then 20%. It only changes how much each step takes off. A fixed amount off is different; for that, use the discount calculator.

Can I use it in rupees or other currencies?

Yes. Pick PKR, INR, GBP, EUR or any other currency at the top. Percent off works the same in every currency; only the symbol on the results changes.
