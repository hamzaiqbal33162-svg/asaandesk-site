How it works

Choose the kind of sequence. In an arithmetic sequence you add the same number (the common difference) each time: 3, 7, 11, 15, … adds 4. In a geometric sequence you multiply by the same number (the common ratio) each time: 3, 6, 12, 24, … multiplies by 2. In the Fibonacci sequence each term is the sum of the two before it: 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, … Type the first term, the step and which term you want; the result gives that term, the sum of all the terms up to it, the rule for any term, a table of the terms and a chart. Decimals are kept exact, and Fibonacci numbers show every digit.

The formula

  • Arithmetic: aₙ = a₁ + (n − 1) × d, and the sum Sₙ = n × (a₁ + aₙ) ÷ 2
  • Geometric: aₙ = a₁ × rⁿ⁻¹, and Sₙ = a₁ × (1 − rⁿ) ÷ (1 − r) (or n × a₁ when r = 1)
  • Infinite geometric series, when −1 < r < 1: S∞ = a₁ ÷ (1 − r)
  • Fibonacci: F₁ = F₂ = 1 and Fₙ = Fₙ₋₁ + Fₙ₋₂
  • Sum of the first n Fibonacci numbers: Fₙ₊₂ − 1

A worked example

Arithmetic, first term 2 and difference 5: the 20th term is 2 + 19 × 5 = 97, and the first 20 terms add up to 20 × (2 + 97) ÷ 2 = 990. Geometric, first term 3 and ratio 0.5: the 6th term is 3 × 0.5⁵ = 0.09375, and since the ratio is below 1 the whole infinite series adds up to 3 ÷ (1 − 0.5) = 6.

Questions

How do I know if a sequence is arithmetic or geometric?

Subtract each term from the next: if the difference is always the same, it is arithmetic. Divide each term by the one before: if the ratio is always the same, it is geometric. 2, 4, 6, 8 is arithmetic (add 2); 2, 4, 8, 16 is geometric (times 2).

When does an infinite geometric series have a sum?

Only when the ratio is between −1 and 1. Then each term is smaller than the one before, the terms get closer to 0, and the total settles at a₁ ÷ (1 − r). With a ratio of 1 or more (or −1 or less) the total never settles.

Can the difference or the ratio be negative or a decimal?

Yes. A negative difference makes the terms go down, a negative ratio makes their signs switch back and forth, and a ratio such as 0.5 halves each term. Decimals you type are kept exact, so no rounding builds up along the sequence.

Why does the Fibonacci sequence start with 1, 1?

This calculator counts F₁ = 1 and F₂ = 1, so the 10th term is 55. Some books start from F₀ = 0; their n-th term is the same number one place later.

How large can n be?

Up to a billion for arithmetic sequences, a million for geometric ones and 10,000 for Fibonacci numbers. Very big results use the × 10ⁿ form with 14 significant digits; Fibonacci results also list every digit up to 400 digits.
