What a healthy weight is

A healthy weight calculator shows the range of body weights that health services link with the lowest risk of weight-related disease for your height. For adults that range is a body mass index (BMI) of 18.5 to 25, the cut-offs used by the World Health Organization (WHO), the U.S. CDC and the NHS. BMI is weight in kg divided by height in metres squared, so the range is simply 18.5 and 25 times your height squared.

The result is a range, not one perfect number. Anywhere inside it is counted as healthy, and the right spot for you depends on muscle, frame, health conditions and how you feel. This page gives a general estimate, not medical advice.

How to use the healthy weight calculator

  • Choose metric (cm and kg) or U.S. units (feet, inches and pounds).
  • Enter your height. The healthy range depends on height alone, so it is the same for men and women and for every adult age.
  • Optionally type your weight to see your BMI, your category and how far you are from the range. Leave it empty if you only want the range.
  • Read the range at the top, then the table that gives the weight at each BMI mark for your height.

Worked examples

A woman 5 ft 4 in tall. In metres that is 1.626 m, and 1.626² = 2.643 m². The healthy range is 18.5 × 2.643 = 48.9 kg to 25 × 2.643 = 66.1 kg, or 108 to 146 lb. At 150 lb (68.0 kg) her BMI is 25.7, so the calculator shows her about 4 lb above the healthy range.

A man in Pakistan, 168 cm and 78 kg. Height squared is 1.68² = 2.822 m², so the WHO range is 52.2 to 70.6 kg and his BMI is 27.6, about 7.4 kg above it. For people of South Asian background, NICE in the UK advises lower cut-offs: overweight from a BMI of 23 and obesity from 27.5. On that scale the upper healthy weight for his height is 64.9 kg, and 27.6 would already count as obesity.

Healthy weight by age, for children and in pregnancy

For adults aged 18 and over the healthy range does not change with age, so a healthy weight calculator for age and height gives the same range at 25 and at 65. What changes with age is body composition: people tend to lose muscle and gain fat, so waist size becomes more useful as you get older.

Children and teenagers are different, because healthy weight changes as they grow. They are compared with others of the same age and sex on growth charts. The CDC classes a BMI below the 5th percentile as underweight, the 5th to below the 85th as healthy, the 85th to below the 95th as overweight and the 95th or above as obesity. Use the BMI Calculator, which has a child mode, for anyone aged 2 to 19.

In pregnancy the goal is healthy weight gain rather than a healthy weight. U.S. guidance from the Institute of Medicine (2009) links the gain to BMI before pregnancy: 28 to 40 lb (12.5 to 18 kg) if underweight, 25 to 35 lb (11.5 to 16 kg) at a healthy BMI, 15 to 25 lb (7 to 11.5 kg) if overweight and 11 to 20 lb (5 to 9 kg) with obesity.

Why weight outside the healthy range matters

WHO reported in 2026 that about 2.6 billion adults worldwide, 45%, were overweight in 2024, and 940 million of them, 16%, were living with obesity. A high BMI raises the risk of type 2 diabetes, heart disease, stroke, fatty liver, kidney disease, joint problems and several cancers.

Being underweight carries risks too: low energy, weaker immunity, bone loss and, for women, missed periods and fertility problems. Unplanned weight loss can signal an illness such as thyroid disease or tuberculosis, so it is worth seeing a doctor about it.

Reaching and keeping a healthy weight

  • Aim for a steady loss of about 0.5 to 1 kg (1 to 2 lb) a week, the pace the NHS recommends; that is roughly 2 to 4 kg a month.
  • Losing even 5% of your weight improves blood sugar, blood pressure and cholesterol for many people, even before you reach the range.
  • Build meals around vegetables, fruit, pulses, whole grains and lean protein, and cut sugary drinks and fried snacks.
  • Be active for at least 150 to 300 minutes a week at moderate intensity, as WHO advises for adults, plus muscle-strengthening twice a week.
  • To gain weight healthily, eat more often with nutrient-dense foods such as nuts, dairy, eggs and whole grains, and add strength training.

How it works

For adults, a BMI from 18.5 up to 25 is counted as healthy by the World Health Organization and most health services. Because BMI is weight divided by height squared, turning it around gives the weight range for any height: multiply 18.5 and 25 by your height in metres squared. The same sum gives the weight where each band starts: 30 for obesity, 35 and 40 for the higher classes. Type your weight too if you want to see where it falls.

The formula

  • Lowest healthy weight (kg) = 18.5 × height (m)²
  • Highest healthy weight (kg) = 25 × height (m)²
  • Middle of the range (kg) = 21.75 × height (m)²
  • Weight at any BMI mark = BMI × height (m)²; pounds = kilograms ÷ 0.45359237
  • Your BMI = your weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²

A worked example

At 165 cm, height squared is 1.65 × 1.65 = 2.7225 m². The healthy range is 18.5 × 2.7225 ≈ 50.4 kg to 25 × 2.7225 ≈ 68.1 kg, and obesity starts at 30 × 2.7225 ≈ 81.7 kg.

Questions

How do I calculate a healthy weight range?

Square your height in metres and multiply by 18.5 for the lowest healthy weight and by 25 for the highest. At 1.70 m that is 2.89 × 18.5 = 53.5 kg to 2.89 × 25 = 72.3 kg.

What is a healthy weight for a 5'4 female?

At 5 ft 4 in (162.6 cm) the healthy range is about 108 to 146 lb, or 48.9 to 66.1 kg. At 5 ft 2 in it is 101 to 137 lb, and at 5 ft 6 in it is 115 to 155 lb.

Is the healthy range the same for men and women?

Yes. The BMI bands for adults are the same for both, so the range depends only on height. Women usually carry more body fat than men at the same BMI, which the body fat calculator can show.

Does my healthy weight change with age?

Not for adults: the range of BMI 18.5 to 25 applies from 18 onwards. Because muscle tends to fall and fat to rise with age, waist size is a useful extra check for older adults.

Does this work for children?

No. Children's healthy weight changes with age and sex as they grow, so they are compared with other children of the same age on growth charts. Use the BMI calculator, which has a child mode for ages 2 to 19.

How can I find a healthy weight without BMI?

Measure your waist. Keeping it below half your height, or under 94 cm for men and 80 cm for women, points to a healthy amount of belly fat. A body fat percentage estimate is another option.

Where in the range should I aim?

Anywhere in the range is counted as healthy. A frame with more muscle often sits higher, and many people feel best somewhere in the middle. Waist size and how you feel matter too, so talk to a doctor before a big change.

What is healthy weight loss per week?

About 0.5 to 1 kg (1 to 2 lb) a week is the steady pace the NHS recommends. Faster loss is harder to keep up and more likely to come back.

Why do some charts give a different range?

Some health services use lower cut-offs for people of South Asian, Chinese and some other backgrounds, with overweight from a BMI of 23 and obesity from 27.5, because health risks start at a lower BMI. This page uses the general WHO range of 18.5 to 25.
