What a CD calculator tells you

A CD calculator shows how much a certificate of deposit will be worth on the day it matures and how much interest it earns along the way. You give it the deposit, the rate, how often interest compounds and the term; it gives the balance at maturity, the total interest, the APY and a month-by-month schedule.

A certificate of deposit is a savings deposit you agree to leave with a bank or credit union for a fixed term, from a few months to several years, at a rate that is set when you open it. In return for locking the money away, you usually get a higher rate than an ordinary savings account. The same product is called a fixed deposit, term deposit or time deposit in many countries, and a term deposit receipt (TDR) at many banks in Pakistan; the arithmetic is identical, so this calculator works for all of them.

How to use the CD calculator

  • Pick your currency at the top. It changes only the symbol, so rupees, dollars or pounds all work.
  • Amount deposited: what you put in on day one. The default is 10,000.
  • Interest rate: the yearly rate on the offer, 4.5% by default.
  • Compounded: how often interest is added to the balance. Choose Yearly when the bank quotes an APY, because an APY already includes the effect of compounding. Choose Twice a year, Quarterly, Monthly, Daily or Continuously when the bank quotes a nominal rate with that compounding.
  • Term: 3, 6 or 9 months, 1 year, 18 months, or 2 to 5 years. For anything else, such as a 7-month or 13-month special, choose Other length and type the number of months or years.
  • Tax on interest (under Tax): your income tax rate on interest, if you pay tax on it. Leave it at 0% for a before-tax answer.

How to calculate CD interest

The CD formula is the compound interest formula. With deposit P, yearly rate r as a decimal, compounding m times a year and a term of t years, the balance at maturity is A = P × (1 + r ÷ m)^(m × t), and the interest is A − P. For $10,000 at 5% compounded monthly for 2 years: 10,000 × (1 + 0.05 ÷ 12)^24 = $11,049.41, so the interest is $1,049.41.

APY, the annual percentage yield, is what one year of compounding produces: APY = (1 + r ÷ m)^m − 1. US banks must quote it under the Truth in Savings rules (Regulation DD), which makes offers with different compounding easy to compare. A 5% rate compounded monthly is an APY of 5.116%. APR, by contrast, is a borrowing cost and does not include compounding, so do not compare a CD's APY with a loan's APR.

Worked CD examples

A 3-year CD. $10,000 at a 5% APY (enter 5% with Yearly compounding) for 3 years grows to $11,576.25. The interest is $500 in the first year, $525 in the second and $551.25 in the third.

A 6-month CD compounded monthly. $25,000 at 4.40% compounded monthly for 6 months matures at $25,555.07, so the interest is $555.07. The calculator shows an APY of 4.490% and growth over the term of 2.220%, about half the APY because the money is only in for half a year.

A 7-month special. Banks often run odd terms. $15,000 at 4.25% compounded daily for 7 months (choose Other length, 7 months) matures at $15,376.50, an APY of 4.341%.

A term deposit in Pakistan. Suppose a bank offers 10.5% a year, paid at maturity, on a 1-year deposit of Rs 1,000,000, and you are on the Active Taxpayers List, so 20% tax is withheld from the profit. Simply, the profit is Rs 105,000, the tax Rs 21,000 and you receive Rs 84,000 on top of your deposit. If you enter 20% tax, the calculator shows Rs 1,083,224.91, slightly less, because it takes the tax out every month and the tax cannot compound. For someone not on the Active Taxpayers List, the 40% rate leaves about Rs 63,000 of the Rs 105,000, so registering as a filer is worth Rs 21,000 a year on this deposit alone.

Safety, early withdrawal and tax

In the United States, CDs at an FDIC-insured bank are covered by deposit insurance up to $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, for each account ownership category, so a single account and a joint account at the same bank are insured separately. Share certificates at federally insured credit unions are covered in the same way by the NCUA. Above those limits, spread money across banks or ownership categories.

The trade-off for a fixed rate is that the money is committed. If you take it out before maturity, the bank charges an early withdrawal penalty, usually expressed as a number of months of interest, and it can be large enough to eat into the deposit on a short CD. The penalty is set by each bank and must be disclosed before you open the account, so read it before you choose a long term.

CD interest is taxable income in the US. The bank sends Form 1099-INT when you earn $10 or more, and you report the interest each year it is credited, even on a multi-year CD that you have not cashed. If you pay an early withdrawal penalty, it appears separately on the 1099-INT and you can deduct it as an adjustment on Schedule 1 of Form 1040. In Pakistan, banks withhold tax on profit at the time they pay it, at the rate for your filer status.

Types of CDs and how a CD ladder works

  • Traditional CD: a fixed rate for a fixed term, with a penalty for early withdrawal.
  • No-penalty (liquid) CD: you can withdraw without a penalty after a short initial period, in return for a lower rate.
  • Bump-up or step-up CD: lets you raise the rate once or more if the bank's rates rise, or raises it on a set schedule.
  • Jumbo CD: a CD with a high minimum deposit, often $100,000, sometimes at a slightly higher rate.
  • Callable CD: the bank may end it early, usually when rates have fallen, so you may not keep the high rate for the full term.
  • Brokered CD: bought through a brokerage account rather than directly from a bank; it can be sold before maturity at the market price, which may be below what you paid.

A CD ladder spreads your money across several maturities so that part of it becomes available regularly. With $20,000 you might buy four $5,000 CDs maturing in 1, 2, 3 and 4 years. Each year one matures; you can spend it or reinvest it in a new 4-year CD, usually the term with the best rate. After the first few years, all your money earns long-term rates while some of it still comes free every year, and you are less exposed to bad timing if rates move.

CDs compared with other safe places for savings

A high-yield savings account or money market account lets you add and withdraw money at any time, but the rate can change at any time too. A CD locks in the rate, which helps when rates are expected to fall and hurts when they rise.

US Treasury bills, notes and bonds are backed by the federal government, can be sold before maturity and their interest is exempt from state and local income tax. In Pakistan, government savings schemes and treasury bills fill a similar role. Bonds and bond funds can pay more, but their market value moves with interest rates.

If you carry credit card or other high-interest debt, paying it off almost always beats any CD: clearing a card at 25% is a guaranteed 25% return, far above what a deposit pays.

Fixed deposit and CD rates in Pakistan

Term deposit rates in Pakistan move with the State Bank of Pakistan's policy rate. The SBP raised the policy rate by 50 basis points to 12% with effect from 27 October 2026, so deposit rates offered after that date reflect it. Banks set their own rates for each term and amount, and they change often, so compare current rate sheets before you choose.

Under the withholding rate card for 2026-27 (Finance Act 2026), tax on profit paid by a bank on a deposit is 20% for people on the Active Taxpayers List and 40% for others. Islamic banks offer term deposits too, usually on a profit-sharing basis, so they quote an expected profit rate rather than a fixed interest rate; the calculator still gives a sound estimate of the outcome.

Many Pakistani term deposits pay profit monthly or quarterly into your current account instead of compounding it. In that case each payout is the deposit × rate × months ÷ 12, and the balance at maturity is simply your deposit. Choose the compounding only when the profit is added to the deposit itself.

Tips and related tools

  • Compare APYs, not headline rates, so offers with different compounding are on equal terms.
  • Match the term to when you need the money; a better rate is not worth a penalty that cancels it.
  • Check what happens at maturity. Many CDs renew automatically at the current rate unless you act within a short grace period.
  • Keep each bank's total under the insurance limit for your ownership category.
  • Look at the real return: a 4.5% CD in a year of 3% inflation grows your buying power by only about 1.5%.
  • Put a reminder in your calendar a week before maturity so you can compare rates and decide where the money goes next.

For savings you keep adding to, try the Savings Calculator. The Compound Interest Calculator converts between compounding frequencies, and the Inflation Calculator shows what your maturity value will buy.

How it works

Enter the amount you deposit, the yearly rate the bank offers and how often it compounds, then pick the term (or choose Other length for any number of months or years). The calculator grows the deposit month by month at the matching monthly rate until the CD matures. If you enter a tax rate, that share of each month's interest is taken out. Banks in the US quote CDs as an APY, which is a rate compounded yearly, so choose Yearly when you type an APY.

The formula

  • Monthly rate j = (1 + r ÷ m)^(m ÷ 12) − 1 (continuous: e^(r ÷ 12) − 1; daily uses 365.25 days)
  • No tax: balance at maturity = deposit × (1 + j)^months = deposit × (1 + r ÷ m)^(m × years)
  • With tax: balance = deposit × (1 + j × (1 − tax rate))^months
  • Total interest = the sum of each month's interest; interest after tax = balance − deposit
  • APY = (1 + r ÷ m)^m − 1

A worked example

$10,000 at 5% compounded yearly for 3 years grows to 10,000 × 1.05³ = $11,576.25, so the CD earns $1,576.25. The third year alone earns $551.25, because it earns interest on the first two years' interest too.

Questions

How do I calculate CD interest?

Use A = P × (1 + r ÷ m)^(m × t), where P is the deposit, r the yearly rate, m the compounding periods a year and t the years; the interest is A − P. $10,000 at 5% compounded yearly for 3 years gives $11,576.25, or $1,576.25 of interest.

What is a CD?

A certificate of deposit is a savings deposit that you leave with a bank for a fixed term, such as 6 months or 5 years, in return for a fixed rate. Many countries call it a fixed deposit or term deposit. Taking the money out early usually costs a penalty, often a few months of interest.

Should I enter the APY or the interest rate?

Either works. If the bank gives an APY, enter it with Yearly compounding. If it gives a rate and says it compounds monthly or daily, enter that rate and that compounding; the APY line then shows the yearly rate the two are equal to.

How much does a $10,000 CD earn in 6 months?

At a 4.5% APY, about $222, because six months is half a year: 10,000 × (1.045^0.5 − 1) = $222.52. Choose 6 months as the term and the calculator shows the exact figure for your rate and compounding.

Is CD interest compounded monthly?

It depends on the bank; many US CDs compound daily or monthly and quote the result as an APY. The difference is small: 5% compounded monthly is a 5.116% APY. Choose the compounding your bank states to match its figures.

How is tax on CD interest handled?

The share you enter is taken from each month's interest, which also slightly lowers the compounding. In practice most banks pay the interest in full and report it once a year, so if you pay the tax from other money your CD will end a little higher than shown.

What happens if I withdraw from a CD early?

You usually pay a penalty set by the bank, often several months of interest, and on a short CD it can take part of your deposit. In the US the penalty is shown on your Form 1099-INT and can be deducted on Schedule 1.

Are CDs safe?

CDs at FDIC-insured banks are insured up to $250,000 per depositor, per bank, for each ownership category, and credit union certificates have the same NCUA cover. The main risks are inflation and being locked in if rates rise.

Is a longer CD always better?

A longer term often pays a higher rate, but your money is locked in and an early withdrawal costs a penalty. If rates may rise, some savers split the money into several CDs that end in different years (a CD ladder).

How is profit on a fixed deposit taxed in Pakistan?

For 2026-27, banks withhold 20% of the profit for people on the Active Taxpayers List and 40% for others, under section 151 of the Income Tax Ordinance. Enter 20 or 40 in the tax box to see your profit after tax.
