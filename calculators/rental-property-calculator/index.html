What a rental property calculator shows

A rental property calculator tells you whether a property you plan to let out is a good investment. It turns the price, rent, loan and running costs into the numbers investors compare: monthly cash flow, cap rate, cash-on-cash return, rental yield, total profit when you sell and the yearly return, known as the internal rate of return (IRR).

This page follows the property year by year for as long as you plan to keep it. Rent and costs rise each year at the rates you choose, a share of the year is lost to vacancy, the loan is paid down, and at the end the property is sold. Because the sale is included, the result shows the whole investment, not just the first year's rent.

How to use the rental property calculator, step by step

  • Choose your currency, then enter the Purchase price, the Monthly rent and the Years you keep it.
  • Paying with: pick A loan and enter the Down payment, Interest rate and Loan term, or pick Cash.
  • Under More options, Buying: Closing costs, and whether it Needs repairs before renting (Repair cost and Value after repairs).
  • Running costs: Property tax, Insurance, HOA or building fee, Maintenance and Other costs per year, each with its own yearly rise.
  • Income: how much the rent rises each year, any Other income such as parking or laundry, Vacancy (the share of the year it stands empty) and any Management fee.
  • Selling: let the value grow by a yearly rate or enter a known Sale price, and set the Selling costs as a percent of the price.

How to calculate rental property cash flow, cap rate and ROI

Start with the rent you will actually collect. On the defaults, 12 × $2,000 = $24,000, less 5% vacancy, is $22,800. Subtract the running costs, $3,000 property tax, $1,200 insurance, $2,000 maintenance and $500 other, which total $6,700. That leaves a net operating income of $16,100. The cap rate formula is NOI ÷ price: $16,100 ÷ $200,000 = 8.05%.

Cash flow subtracts the loan: the $160,000 loan at 6.5% costs $1,011.31 a month, $12,135.71 a year, so the first-year cash flow is $16,100 − $12,135.71 = $3,964.29. The cash-on-cash return divides it by your own money, $40,000 down plus $6,000 closing costs: $3,964.29 ÷ $46,000 = 8.62%.

Return on investment over the whole hold needs the sale. After 10 years at 3% growth the home is worth about $268,783; after 8% selling costs and the $135,642 still owed, the sale leaves $111,638.81. Add the ten years of cash flow and subtract the $46,000, and the total profit is $128,850.21. The IRR, 18.20% a year, also counts when each amount arrives.

Paying cash gives the largest profit and the steadiest cash flow but a lower yearly return, because far more of your money is tied up: $206,000 instead of $46,000. A loan raises the return on your own cash as long as the property earns more than the loan rate, and it also raises the risk; with a higher rate or a weak year, cash flow can turn negative.

Vacancy and management are easy to underestimate. Raising vacancy to 10% and paying a manager 8% of the rent cuts the first-year cash flow from $3,964.29 to $1,036.29. Value growth matters just as much: with no rise in value, the profit falls by about half.

Rules of thumb and their limits

  • Gross yield: a year of rent ÷ price. The defaults give 12.00%; it ignores every cost, so use it only to sort listings.
  • The 1% rule some investors use: monthly rent of at least 1% of the price. $2,000 on $200,000 meets it exactly, but it says nothing about taxes, repairs or the loan.
  • The 50% rule: assume running costs and vacancy take about half the rent. On the defaults they take $7,900 of $24,000, about a third, so check real figures for your area.
  • Cap rate: compare it with similar properties nearby. A higher cap rate means more income for the price, often with more risk or more work.
  • Always finish with the full projection here: only cash flow, the loan and the sale together show whether the investment works.

Tax on rental income: Pakistan and the US

The results are before income tax. In Pakistan, rent paid by tenants who must withhold tax is subject to withholding under section 155 of the Income Tax Ordinance, 2001. The FBR's withholding tax rate card (updated to 30 June 2026 under the Finance Act, 2026) sets these rates for individuals and AOPs on the gross rent for the year: nil up to Rs 300,000; 5% of the amount above Rs 300,000 up to Rs 600,000; Rs 15,000 plus 10% of the amount above Rs 600,000 up to Rs 2,000,000; and Rs 155,000 plus 25% of the amount above Rs 2,000,000. For companies the rate is 15% (30% for those not on the active taxpayers list).

So a flat let at Rs 100,000 a month, Rs 1,200,000 a year, has Rs 15,000 + 10% × Rs 600,000 = Rs 75,000 withheld, 6.25% of the rent. Rates change with each Finance Act, so check the current FBR card for your tax year.

In the US, IRS Publication 527 (for 2025 returns) says residential rental buildings are depreciated over 27.5 years with the straight-line method and a mid-month convention, and land cannot be depreciated. If $40,000 of a $200,000 price is land, the yearly deduction for a full year is $160,000 ÷ 27.5 = $5,818.18. Depreciation lowers taxable rental income but also lowers your basis, which raises the taxable gain when you sell.

A Pakistan example in rupees

Take a Rs 25,000,000 flat bought with cash, Rs 1,000,000 of buying costs, rent of Rs 100,000 a month rising 10% a year, 8% vacancy, Rs 30,000 property tax, Rs 60,000 building maintenance charges and Rs 100,000 upkeep a year, value rising 8% a year and 2% selling costs, held 10 years. These are example inputs; use your own city's figures.

The gross yield is 4.80% and the cap rate 3.66%, so the first-year cash flow, Rs 76,166.67 a month, is only 3.52% of the Rs 26,000,000 put in. Over 10 years, though, rising rent and value lift the total profit to Rs 41,561,254.48 and the return to 11.29% a year, before tax. The example shows why low-yield property depends heavily on prices rising: try a lower value rise to see how sensitive it is.

Tips, common mistakes and related calculators

  • Use the rent you can really get, from similar lets nearby, not the asking rent.
  • Budget for vacancy, repairs and big replacements such as a roof or water tank; the default maintenance of $2,000 a year, 1% of the price, is only a starting point.
  • Test a bad case: lower rent rises, higher costs, a higher loan rate and no value growth.
  • Keep cash in reserve. A few empty months or one major repair can wipe out a year's cash flow.
  • Remember buying and selling costs: the IRR is negative if you sell after one year on the defaults.
  • The currency menu only changes the symbol; enter local costs and rates yourself.

How it works

Type the price, the rent, how long you will keep the property and how you pay for it. The calculator follows the property year by year. Rent and other income rise each year; vacancy and the management fee come off; property tax, insurance, fees, maintenance and other costs rise at their own rates. What is left is the net operating income, and after the loan payment it is your cash flow. At the end you sell: the value grows each year (or moves smoothly to the sale price you know), selling costs and the loan balance come off, and the rest is your cash. The return per year (IRR) is the rate at which everything you put in and everything you get back break even.

The formula

  • Cash you put in = down payment + closing costs + repairs (paying cash: price + closing costs + repairs)
  • Income in year y = 12 × (rent + other income) × (1 + rise)^(y − 1) × (1 − vacancy) × (1 − management fee)
  • Running costs in year y = sum of each cost × (1 + its rise)^(y − 1)
  • Net operating income (NOI) = income − running costs; cash flow = NOI − loan payments
  • Value after y years = starting value × (1 + value rise)^y, or start × (sale price ÷ start)^(y ÷ years kept)
  • Cash if sold = value × (1 − selling costs) − loan balance; equity = value − loan balance
  • Total profit = all cash flows + cash from the sale − cash you put in
  • IRR: the yearly rate r with −cash in + cash flow₁ ÷ (1 + r) + ... + (cash flow + sale) ÷ (1 + r)^years = 0
  • Cap rate = first-year NOI ÷ purchase price; cash-on-cash = cash flow ÷ cash you put in; gross yield = a year of rent ÷ price

A worked example

The defaults: a $200,000 home with 20% down and a 6.5% 30-year loan costs $1,011.31 a month. Rent of $2,000 less 5% vacancy is $22,800 in the first year; running costs are $6,700, so NOI is $16,100 (a cap rate of 8.05%) and the cash flow after $12,135.71 of loan payments is $3,964.29, or 8.62% on the $46,000 put in. Held 10 years and sold, the total profit is $128,850.21, a return of 18.20% a year.

Questions

How do I calculate rental property cash flow?

Take the rent you expect to collect after vacancy, subtract property tax, insurance, fees, maintenance and other costs, then subtract the loan payments. On the defaults that is $22,800 − $6,700 − $12,135.71 = $3,964.29 in the first year, or $330.36 a month.

How do I calculate the cap rate of a rental property?

Divide the first year's net operating income (rent collected minus running costs, before the loan) by the purchase price. $16,100 ÷ $200,000 = 8.05%.

What is a good cap rate?

There is no single answer: it depends on the city, the type of property and current interest rates. Compare it with similar properties in the same area; a higher cap rate means more income for the price, often with more risk or more work.

How do I calculate ROI on a rental property?

For one year, divide the cash flow by the cash you put in (the cash-on-cash return). For the whole investment, add every year's cash flow and the cash from the sale, subtract what you put in, and divide by it; the IRR also allows for when each amount arrives.

What is the difference between cash flow and NOI?

Net operating income is the rent collected minus the running costs, as if you had no loan. Cash flow is what is left after the loan payment too, the money that actually reaches your pocket.

Why is the IRR so different from the cash-on-cash return?

Cash-on-cash looks at one year of cash flow only. IRR includes every year's cash flow and the sale at the end, and allows for when each amount arrives, so a big gain on the sale lifts it a lot.

What should I enter for vacancy?

The share of the year you expect the home to stand empty between tenants. 5% is about 18 days a year; use more for short lets or areas with weak demand.

How is rental income taxed in Pakistan?

Tenants who must withhold deduct tax under section 155 on the gross rent for the year: for individuals, nil up to Rs 300,000, then 5%, 10% and 25% bands under the FBR rate card for 2026-27. Rs 1,200,000 of rent a year has Rs 75,000 withheld. Rates change with each Finance Act.

How do I calculate rental property depreciation?

In the US, take the cost of the building without the land and divide it by 27.5 years, the recovery period in IRS Publication 527. A $160,000 building gives $5,818.18 for a full year; the first year is prorated by month.

Does it include income tax?

No. Income tax on rent, depreciation allowances and tax on the gain when you sell depend on your country and situation, so the results are before tax. The currency menu only changes the symbol shown.
