What the discount calculator does

A discount calculator tells you what you will pay after a price cut and how much you save. It also works backwards: give it the sale price and the percent off, and it finds the original price; give it the original price and the sale price, and it finds the discount percentage.

Shops show discounts in two ways, as a percent off (35% off) or as a fixed amount off ($25 off or Rs 500 off). The calculator handles both, in rupees, dollars or any other currency, and shows each step of the working so you can check it or copy it into homework.

How to use the discount calculator

  • Original price: the price before the discount.
  • Discount: type the number and choose % off or an amount off with the currency symbol.
  • Price after discount: the sale price you pay.
  • Amount saved: the money taken off (shown with % off; with an amount off, the amount off is the saving).
  • Fill in any two of the boxes and leave the others empty. The calculator fills in the rest and lists the steps.
  • Choose your currency, for example Pakistani rupee for prices in PKR.

If you fill in more than two boxes and they do not agree, the calculator uses the first pair in this order (original price, discount, price after discount, amount saved) and warns you which box did not match. A fixed amount off larger than the original price is refused.

In Excel or Google Sheets, with the original price in A1 and the discount as a percent in B1, the sale price is =A1*(1-B1) and the saving is =A1*B1. With the original in A1 and the sale price in C1, the discount percentage is =(A1-C1)/A1, formatted as a percent.

Percent off or amount off: which saves more?

A fixed amount off is worth more on cheap items and less on expensive ones, while a percent off grows with the price. To compare, turn the amount off into a percent: $25 off a $140 item is 17.86% off. So on that item, a 20% off offer saves more, and a 15% off offer saves less.

The break-even price is where the two give the same saving: amount off ÷ percent. For $25 off against 20% off, that is 25 ÷ 0.20 = $125. Above $125 the 20% saves more; below it the $25 does.

Stacked discounts and discounts with sales tax

Two discounts in a row do not add up. An extra 10% off an item already reduced by 20% is 10% of the lower price: $250 less 20% is $200, and 10% off that is $180. Put $250 and $180 into the calculator and it shows the true combined discount, 28% off, not 30%.

Sales tax is normally worked out on the price after the discount, so the discount saves tax too. At 8.25% tax, the $180 price above comes to $194.85 with tax, while the full $250 would have been $270.63. Use the Sales Tax Calculator for the tax step, or the VAT Calculator where prices include VAT or GST.

Other meanings: discount rate and discount factor

In finance, discounting means working out what money in the future is worth today. The discount rate is the yearly rate used to do this, and the discount factor is what 1 in the future is worth now: 1 ÷ (1 + rate)^years. At 10% a year, 1,000 due in 3 years has a discount factor of 0.751315 and is worth 751.31 today, as the Present Value Calculator shows.

Banks use discount rate in a third sense: the rate a central bank charges when it lends to banks. These are different ideas from a shop discount, so use the present value tool for them.

How it works

A discount is either a percent of the price or a fixed amount taken off it. The four numbers on a price tag are tied together: the original price, the discount, the price you pay and the amount you save. Fill in any two and the calculator works out the other two, for example the original price from a sale price and the percent off. With a fixed amount off, the amount off is the amount saved, so two of the three boxes are enough, and the discount is also shown as a percent.

The formula

  • Amount saved = original price × discount % ÷ 100
  • Price after discount = original price − amount saved
  • Original price = price after discount ÷ (1 − discount % ÷ 100)
  • Discount % = amount saved ÷ original price × 100
  • Fixed amount off: price after discount = original price − amount off

A worked example

A shirt costs Rs 4,800 with 35% off. You save 4,800 × 35 ÷ 100 = Rs 1,680 and pay 4,800 − 1,680 = Rs 3,120. The other way round: if you paid $63.75 after 15% off, the original price was 63.75 ÷ (1 − 0.15) = $75.00, so you saved $11.25.

Questions

How do I calculate a discount percentage?

Subtract the sale price from the original price, divide by the original price and multiply by 100. From $250 down to $180 is (250 − 180) ÷ 250 × 100 = 28% off. Type both prices and leave the discount empty to get it.

How do I calculate the discount amount?

Multiply the original price by the percent off and divide by 100. A Rs 4,800 shirt at 35% off saves 4,800 × 35 ÷ 100 = Rs 1,680.

How do I calculate the discount price?

Take the amount saved off the original price, or multiply the price by what is left: 100% − 35% = 65%, so Rs 4,800 × 0.65 = Rs 3,120.

How do I work out the original price from the sale price?

Divide the sale price by what is left after the discount. After 15% off you pay 85% of the price, so a sale price of $63.75 means 63.75 ÷ 0.85 = $75.00. Type the sale price and the percent off, leave the original price empty, and it is filled in for you.

What if the shop takes a fixed amount off instead of a percent?

Switch the discount box to the money symbol and type the amount off. The calculator gives the price after discount and shows the same discount as a percent, which makes it easy to compare with a percent-off offer.

Is 20% off and then 10% off the same as 30% off?

No. The second discount is taken from the already reduced price, so together they make 28% off. On $250 you pay $180, not $175.

How do I calculate a discount with sales tax?

Take the discount off first, then add the tax to the lower price. $180 after discount plus 8.25% tax is $194.85.

What happens if I fill in three or four boxes?

Two values are enough. If you fill in more, the result uses the first pair in this order: original price, discount, price after discount, amount saved, and warns you when another box does not match.
