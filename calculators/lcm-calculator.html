Steps

  1. Method 1, prime factors. Write each number as a product of primes:
  2. 12 = 2 × 2 × 3
  3. 18 = 2 × 3 × 3
  4. 30 = 2 × 3 × 5
  5. Take every prime the most times it appears in any one number: 2 twice (in 12), 3 twice (in 18), 5 once (in 30).
  6. LCM = 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 × 5 = 180
  7. Method 2, greatest common divisor (GCD): LCM(a, b) = a × b ÷ GCD(a, b).
  8. GCD(12, 18) = 6, so LCM(12, 18) = 12 × 186 = 2166 = 36
  9. GCD(36, 30) = 6, so LCM(36, 30) = 36 × 306 = 10806 = 180

How it works

Type two or more positive whole numbers, separated by commas or spaces. The least common multiple (LCM) is the smallest number that every one of them divides into exactly. The answer updates as you type and is worked out two ways: by splitting each number into primes, and by the greatest common divisor (GCD). Numbers of any length are handled exactly.

The formula

  • LCM(a, b) = a × b ÷ GCD(a, b)
  • LCM(a, b, c) = LCM(LCM(a, b), c), and so on for longer lists
  • With prime factors: LCM = the product of every prime, each taken the most times it appears in any one number
  • GCD(a, b) by Euclid's algorithm: GCD(a, b) = GCD(b, a mod b), until the remainder is 0

A worked example

LCM of 12, 18 and 30: 12 = 2 × 2 × 3, 18 = 2 × 3 × 3 and 30 = 2 × 3 × 5. Take 2 twice (from 12), 3 twice (from 18) and 5 once (from 30): 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 × 5 = 180. Check with the GCD method: LCM(12, 18) = 216 ÷ 6 = 36, then LCM(36, 30) = 1080 ÷ 6 = 180.

Questions

What is the least common multiple used for?

Mostly for adding and subtracting fractions: the LCM of the bottom numbers is the lowest common denominator. It also answers timing questions, such as when two buses that leave every 12 and 18 minutes next leave together (after 36 minutes).

Can I use 0, negative numbers or decimals?

No. The least common multiple is defined here for positive whole numbers, so 0, negative numbers and decimals such as 1.5 are refused with a message.

How are the LCM and the GCF related?

For two numbers, LCM × GCF = the product of the numbers. For 4 and 6: the GCF is 2, so the LCM is 4 × 6 ÷ 2 = 12. That is the GCD method shown in the steps.

How big can the numbers be?

Up to 100 numbers of up to 100 digits each. The answer is always exact. The prime factor method is shown when the numbers can be split into primes quickly (up to about 20 digits); for longer numbers only the GCD method is shown.
