What the hours calculator does

This hours calculator counts the hours and minutes between two clock times, such as the start and end of a work shift, a class or a trip. It also handles night shifts that run past midnight, and spans of several days when you add a date to each time.

Every answer is shown four ways: in hours and minutes, in decimal hours for a time sheet or payroll, in minutes, and in the hours:minutes form many forms ask for.

How to use it

  • Pick a tab: Between two times for a span within a day or overnight, or Between two dates and times for anything longer.
  • Type the Start time, like 8:30, 8:30 PM or 20:30, and pick AM or PM. A time with a leading zero or over 12, such as 08:30 or 17:45, is read as 24-hour time, and the words noon and midnight work too.
  • Type the End time the same way. In the dates tab, also pick the Start date and End date.
  • Read the result. On the first tab, an end time earlier than the start is counted on the next day, and a note says so.

How to calculate hours worked

To count hours by hand, turn both times into minutes after midnight, take the start from the end and divide by 60. For a PM time from 1 to 11, add 12 to the hour first. From 7:45 AM (465 minutes) to 4:10 PM (16:10, which is 970 minutes) is 505 minutes, or 8 hours 25 minutes.

For a night shift the end is on the next day, so add 24 hours (1,440 minutes) when the answer comes out negative. From 10:00 PM to 6:30 AM gives 390 − 1,320 = −930, and −930 + 1,440 = 510 minutes: 8 hours 30 minutes, the same as the calculator shows.

This calculator does not take off breaks. For hours worked with lunch, subtract the unpaid break yourself: the 7:45 AM to 4:10 PM shift with a 30-minute lunch is 8 hours 25 minutes − 30 minutes = 7 hours 55 minutes, or 7.92 hours. At $18 an hour that shift pays 7.9167 × $18 = $142.50. To do this for a whole week, with breaks and overtime, use the time card calculator.

Going the other way, multiply the decimal part by 60: 7.6 hours is 7 hours and 0.6 × 60 = 36 minutes, not 7 hours 60 minutes. Mixing up 7.6 hours and 7:60 (which would be 8:00) is one of the most common time sheet errors.

Hours formulas in Excel and Google Sheets

Spreadsheets store a time as a fraction of a day: Microsoft's documentation describes times from 0:00:00 to 23:59:59 as values from 0 to 0.99988426. So with the start time in A2 and the end time in B2, =B2-A2 gives the time between, and =(B2-A2)*24 turns it into decimal hours. Google Sheets works the same way.

For a shift that passes midnight, =MOD(B2-A2,1)*24 adds the missing day, just as this calculator does. To add up a week of shifts and show more than 24 hours, format the total cell with the custom format [h]:mm; without the square brackets the hours start again from 0 after 23.

The 12-hour and 24-hour clock

The 12-hour clock runs from 12 to 11 twice a day, with AM for the hours from midnight to just before noon and PM for noon onwards. That makes 12 AM midnight and 12 PM noon, which trips many people up: 12:00 AM to 12:00 PM is 12 hours, not none. The 24-hour clock avoids the problem by running from 00:00 to 23:59, so 1:15 PM is 13:15 and 9:40 PM is 21:40, and 13:15 to 21:40 is 8 hours 25 minutes.

An hour is 60 minutes and a minute 60 seconds, so an hour holds 3,600 seconds. These are the hours in longer periods:

Work hours, breaks and overtime rules

Whether a break counts as work time depends on local law and your contract. Under the US Fair Labor Standards Act, short rest breaks of usually 20 minutes or less count as hours worked, while a bona fide meal period, typically 30 minutes or more with no duties, generally does not have to be paid. Covered employees earn overtime of at least one and a half times their regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek, which the Department of Labor defines as a fixed period of 168 hours.

Five shifts of 8 hours 25 minutes add up to 42 hours 5 minutes (42.08 hours), so a covered US worker on that pattern would have 2.08 hours of overtime that week. Rules elsewhere, including in Pakistan, are set by each country's or province's labour laws, so check the law and your contract before relying on a figure for pay.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Check AM and PM before anything else; a wrong switch changes the answer by 12 hours.
  • Type times with a colon or a dot, like 8:30 or 8.30. Four digits such as 0830 also work.
  • The calculator counts plain clock hours. On a night when clocks change for daylight saving time, the real span is one hour longer or shorter.
  • When the start and end are the same time on the first tab, the answer is 0. For a full 24 hours, use the dates tab with two dates.
  • Round only at the end. Adding rounded decimal hours for each day can be a few hundredths off the true weekly total.

How it works

Type the start and end times and pick AM or PM for each, or type a 24-hour time such as 17:45 (then the switch is not needed). The result shows the time between them in hours and minutes, in decimal hours for a time sheet, and in minutes. When the end time is earlier in the day than the start, as with a night shift from 10 PM to 6 AM, the end is counted on the next day. For spans longer than a day, use the two dates tab and add a date to each time.

The formula

  • Clock time in minutes after midnight: 12:xx AM = 0:xx, 1-11 AM as they are, 12:xx PM = 12:xx, 1-11 PM = hour + 12
  • Same day: minutes = end − start; when that is negative, add 24 hours (1,440 minutes)
  • Two dates: minutes = (end date and time) − (start date and time)
  • Decimal hours = minutes ÷ 60; hours and minutes = whole hours, then the minutes left over

A worked example

From 10:15 PM to 6:45 AM: 10:15 PM is 22:15, which is 1,335 minutes after midnight, and 6:45 AM is 405 minutes. 405 − 1,335 is negative, so add 1,440: 510 minutes. That is 8 hours 30 minutes, or 8.5 decimal hours.

Questions

How do I calculate hours worked?

Type the clock-in time as the start and the clock-out time as the end, and read the hours and minutes. Take off any unpaid break yourself, or use the time card calculator, which does breaks, a whole week and pay.

How do I count hours for a night shift?

Type the start and end as they are on the clock, for example 10:15 PM and 6:45 AM. Because the end comes earlier in the day than the start, it is counted on the next morning, which gives 8 hours 30 minutes.

What are decimal hours?

Hours written as one number, which is how many time sheets and pay systems want them: 8 hours 30 minutes is 8.5 hours, 7 hours 45 minutes is 7.75 hours and 20 minutes is about 0.33 hours. Divide the minutes by 60 to get the decimal part.

How do I calculate hours and pay?

Multiply the decimal hours by the hourly rate. A shift of 7 hours 55 minutes is 7.92 hours (7.9167 before rounding), so at $18 an hour it pays $142.50. Use the unrounded figure, or the minutes ÷ 60, to avoid small errors.

How do I calculate hours and minutes in Excel?

With the start time in A2 and the end time in B2, =B2-A2 gives the time between and =(B2-A2)*24 gives decimal hours. Use =MOD(B2-A2,1)*24 for a shift that passes midnight, and format totals as [h]:mm to show more than 24 hours.

Is 12 AM midnight or noon?

12 AM is midnight, the start of the day, and 12 PM is noon. So 12:30 AM is half an hour after midnight. If that is confusing, type the time in 24-hour form instead, such as 00:30 or 12:30.

Can I count the hours between two days?

Yes. Open the two dates tab, pick a date for the start and for the end, and type the times. The answer is shown in days, hours and minutes and also as total hours, so Monday 9:00 AM to Wednesday 5:30 PM is 2 days 8 hours 30 minutes, or 56.5 hours.

How many hours are in a week, a month and a year?

A week has 168 hours, a 30-day month 720 and a 31-day month 744. A common year has 8,760 hours and a leap year 8,784. A full-time job of 40 hours a week for 52 weeks comes to 2,080 hours a year.

Does the hours calculator take off lunch breaks?

No, it measures the whole span between the two times. Subtract a lunch or other unpaid break from the answer, or enter the time before and after the break as two separate spans and add them.
