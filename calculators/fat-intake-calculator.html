How much fat should you eat a day?

This fat intake calculator turns a daily fat target into grams you can check on food labels. It first estimates how many calories you need, then shows the grams of total fat that would supply 20% to 35% of them, the Acceptable Macronutrient Distribution Range set for adults by the US Institute of Medicine, plus a ceiling for saturated fat.

Fat is not the enemy. It carries the fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E and K, supplies essential fatty acids your body cannot make, and keeps meals satisfying. The World Health Organization says at least 15% of an adult's energy should come from fat, and no more than 30% to help avoid unhealthy weight gain, so a sensible target for most people sits between about 20% and 30 to 35%.

What matters most is the type of fat. WHO advises keeping saturated fat to no more than 10% of energy and trans fat to no more than 1%, and replacing them with unsaturated fats from plants and fish. The results are an estimate for healthy adults, not medical advice.

How to use the fat intake calculator

  • Choose Metric or US units, then enter your sex, age (18 to 80), weight and height.
  • Pick how active you are, from Sedentary (little or no exercise) to Extra active (very intense exercise daily, or a physical job). Exercise here means 15 to 30 minutes with a raised heart rate.
  • Under Formula you can switch the BMR formula from Mifflin-St Jeor to the revised Harris-Benedict formula, or to Katch-McArdle with your body fat percentage if you are lean and muscular.
  • Read the total fat range to keep your weight, the saturated fat limits at 10% and 7% of calories, and your maintenance calories. The table repeats the grams for losing or gaining 0.5 kg (1 lb) or 1 kg (2 lb) a week.

Understanding your fat targets

Each row of the table uses the same arithmetic: calories × share ÷ 9, because every gram of fat gives about 9 kcal. The headline range is the 20% and 35% lines for maintenance. The table below shows what the shares mean at a few common calorie levels.

Worked examples

  • Lahore, keeping weight: a 32-year-old woman, 158 cm and 64 kg, with light exercise needs about 1,796 kcal a day. Her total fat range is 40 to 70 g, saturated fat under 20 g (or under 14 g for heart health). To lose 0.5 kg a week she would eat about 1,296 kcal with 29 to 50 g of fat; the 1 kg a week row (796 kcal) is marked as under the safe minimum.
  • US units, weight loss: a 45-year-old man, 5 ft 9 in and 200 lb, who is sedentary needs about 2,139 kcal a day, giving 48 to 83 g of fat and saturated fat under 24 g. Losing 1 lb a week means about 1,639 kcal with 36 to 64 g of fat.
  • Muscle gain: an active 24-year-old man, 176 cm and 72 kg, needs about 2,643 kcal a day to keep his weight, with 59 to 103 g of fat. On the gain 0.5 kg a week row (3,143 kcal) the range is 70 to 122 g, with saturated fat under 35 g.

Per kg of body weight, the woman's range is about 0.6 to 1.1 g of fat per kg (40 to 70 g at 64 kg). The calculator works from calories, as the dietary guidelines do, rather than from body weight.

Good fats and fats to limit

  • Choose most often: unsaturated fats from vegetable oils such as olive, canola, sunflower and corn oil, and from nuts, seeds, avocado and oily fish. WHO recommends replacing saturated and trans fats with these.
  • Limit: saturated fat, which WHO lists in fatty meat, butter, palm and coconut oil, cream, cheese, ghee and lard. If you cook with desi ghee, measuring it into the pot of salan, or using part vegetable oil, is an easy saving.
  • Avoid: industrial trans fat, which WHO says turns up in baked and fried foods and pre-packaged snacks. Check labels, including on vanaspati and baking fats, for trans fat or partially hydrogenated oil.
  • Replace, do not just remove: WHO also lists fibre-rich whole grains, fruit, vegetables and pulses as good swaps for saturated fat, rather than refined starch and sugar.

Fat for weight loss, muscle gain and special cases

For weight loss, the total calories decide how fast you lose; fat is simply the easiest nutrient to overeat because it has more than twice the calories per gram of protein or carbohydrate (9 against 4). Staying near the lower end of your range, without going under 20%, makes the calorie target easier to hit.

For muscle gain or bodybuilding, use the gain rows and keep fat within the same 20 to 35% band, with enough protein from the Protein Calculator. People with high cholesterol, diabetes, kidney or gallbladder disease, pregnant women and anyone under 18 should follow the advice of a doctor or dietitian, who may set different targets.

Tips for tracking fat intake

  • Read the Nutrition Facts or nutrition table per serving and per 100 g, and add up total fat and saturated fat across the day.
  • Measure cooking oil and ghee with a spoon for a week. Hidden fat in cooking is where most estimates go wrong.
  • Use the grams as a weekly average; one rich meal does not undo a good week.
  • Recalculate after losing or gaining a few kilograms, because your calorie needs, and so your fat range, change with your weight.

How it works

The calculator estimates the calories you need to keep your weight (your BMR from the Mifflin-St Jeor formula times your activity factor) and the calories to lose or gain about 1 or 2 lb (0.5 or 1 kg) a week. For each level it shows the grams of fat that supply 20% to 35% of the energy, the range most health bodies recommend for adults, and the most saturated fat to eat: under 10% of calories in general, or under 7% if you need to lower your cholesterol. Fat gives 9 kcal per gram. Rows under 1,200 kcal (women) or 1,500 kcal (men) a day are marked as under the safe minimum.

The formula

  • Calories to keep your weight: BMR × activity factor, rounded to whole kcal; goal rows ± 500 or ± 1,000 kcal
  • Total fat (g) = calories × 20% ÷ 9 to calories × 35% ÷ 9
  • Saturated fat under calories × 10% ÷ 9 (general) or calories × 7% ÷ 9 (heart health)

A worked example

A 32-year-old woman, 158 cm and 64 kg, who exercises 1 to 3 times a week needs about 1,796 kcal a day to keep her weight. Total fat: 1,796 × 0.20 ÷ 9 ≈ 40 g to 1,796 × 0.35 ÷ 9 ≈ 70 g a day; saturated fat under 1,796 × 0.10 ÷ 9 ≈ 20 g, or under 14 g at the 7% heart-health level.

Questions

How much fat should I eat a day?

For most adults 20 to 35% of daily calories, which the result turns into grams for you. Fat is needed for hormones, brain health and to absorb vitamins A, D, E and K, so going very low is not a good idea without medical advice.

How do I calculate fat intake in grams?

Multiply your daily calories by the share you want from fat and divide by 9. On 2,000 kcal, 30% is 2,000 × 0.30 ÷ 9 ≈ 67 g. The calculator does this for 20% and 35% at your own calorie level.

How do I calculate fat intake for weight loss?

Start from the calories for the loss rate you want, such as the Lose 0.5 kg a week row, and take 20 to 35% of those calories. A 45-year-old sedentary man of 200 lb eating 1,639 kcal would aim for about 36 to 64 g of fat.

What percentage of my calories should come from fat?

Between 20% and 35% for adults by the US Dietary Reference Intakes. WHO sets a minimum of 15% and an upper level of 30%, so staying between 20% and 30% meets both.

How much saturated fat is too much?

WHO and the US Dietary Guidelines say under 10% of calories, about 22 g on 2,000 kcal. If you need to lower your cholesterol, the calculator's 7% column or the American Heart Association's under-6% advice is stricter.

Why is saturated fat limited separately?

Saturated fat raises LDL cholesterol, which raises the risk of heart disease. That is why guidelines cap it on its own, even when total fat is within range, and advise swapping it for unsaturated fats.

Which fats are the healthy ones?

Unsaturated fats from vegetable oils, nuts, seeds, avocado and oily fish. Replacing butter, ghee, fatty meat and palm oil with these lowers heart risk. Avoid trans fat from partly hydrogenated oils.

How much fat should I eat for muscle gain?

Use the gain rows and keep fat at 20 to 35% of those calories; an active 72 kg man gaining 0.5 kg a week would eat about 70 to 122 g. Protein and total calories matter more for muscle than the exact fat figure.

Why are some rows marked as under the safe minimum?

Losing 1 kg a week means eating 1,000 kcal less than you burn, which for smaller or less active people can fall under 1,200 kcal (women) or 1,500 kcal (men) a day. That is hard to do safely without a doctor, so those rows are only for reference.
