What this UK mortgage calculator shows

This UK mortgage calculator works out your monthly mortgage repayment in pounds from the property price, your deposit, the term and the interest rate. Next to it you see the interest-only payment for the same loan, your loan to value (LTV), the total interest over the term, your full monthly cost with council tax and insurance, and an estimate of the Stamp Duty Land Tax you will pay in England or Northern Ireland.

A mortgage is a loan secured on the home you buy. In the UK most buyers take a repayment mortgage, where each monthly payment covers that month's interest and pays back some of the capital, so the loan is cleared by the end of the term. With interest-only, you pay only the interest and must repay the whole loan at the end from another source.

Mortgage deals are usually sold as a fixed or variable rate for a few years, after which the rate changes. The calculator keeps one rate for the whole term, which is the standard way to compare deals and to see what a rate change would mean for your budget.

The main kinds of deal are: a fixed rate, which stays the same for an agreed period, often two or five years; a tracker, which moves with the Bank of England's Bank Rate plus a set margin; a discount rate, which is a set amount below the lender's standard variable rate (SVR); and the SVR itself, the rate the lender charges when a deal ends. An offset mortgage links your savings to the loan so that you pay interest only on the difference. Whatever the deal, the repayment for a given rate is worked out the same way, so you can enter the rate of any deal here.

How to use the UK mortgage calculator

  • Enter the Property price (default £300,000).
  • Enter the Deposit as a % of the price (default 10%) or switch the unit to pounds.
  • Set the Mortgage term in years (default 25, up to 40) and the Interest rate (default 4.5% a year).
  • Open Yearly costs to keep Add council tax, insurance and other yearly costs ticked and set Council tax per year (default £2,000, or a % of the price), Home insurance per year (£400), Mortgage insurance per year (usually £0 in the UK) and Service charge, ground rent and other costs per year.
  • Open Stamp duty (England and Northern Ireland) to keep Estimate stamp duty ticked, choose the Buyer: moving home, first-time buyer, or second home or buy-to-let, and tick Buyer is not UK resident (2% extra) if that applies.
  • Open Dates to set the First payment date for the dated schedule.

How UK mortgage repayments are calculated

Lenders work in monthly steps. The yearly rate is divided by 12 to get the monthly rate, and the fixed repayment is the amount that clears the loan exactly after the last month: M = L × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n). For the default case, r = 0.045 ÷ 12 = 0.00375 and n = 300, so M = 270,000 × 0.00375 ÷ (1 − 1.00375^−300) = £1,500.75.

Each month the interest is the balance owed × r, and the rest of the repayment reduces the balance. In month 1 the interest is £1,012.50, the same as the interest-only payment, so only £488.25 repays capital. Over year 1 you pay £12,027.64 of interest and £5,981.34 of capital; by year 25 the interest is only £431.39. This is why the balance falls slowly at first and quickly near the end.

To build the same schedule in Excel or Google Sheets, use =PMT(rate/12, years*12, -loan) for the repayment, then a row per month with interest = balance × rate/12, capital = payment − interest and new balance = balance − capital. Real lenders may charge interest daily or add fees to the loan, so their figure can differ by a few pounds.

Deposit and loan to value (LTV)

Your deposit sets the loan to value: the mortgage as a share of the price. LTV = mortgage ÷ price × 100, so a £30,000 deposit on a £300,000 home gives £270,000 ÷ £300,000 = 90%. Lenders price their deals in LTV bands, and lower bands usually get lower rates, so a bigger deposit can cut your payment twice: a smaller loan and a cheaper rate.

Saving for the deposit: a Lifetime ISA lets you pay in up to £4,000 a year, with a 25% government bonus of up to £1,000 a year, and it can be used towards a first home. You must make your first payment before you turn 40, and conditions apply when you take the money out, so read GOV.UK's rules before you rely on it. Gifts from family are another common source; lenders usually ask for a letter confirming the money is a gift, not a loan.

Interest rates, the Bank Rate and the mortgage term

UK mortgage rates follow the Bank of England's Bank Rate and market expectations for it. The Bank raised Bank Rate by 0.25 percentage points to 4% on 5 November 2026, and its next decision is due on 17 December 2026. In September 2026 the effective interest rate on newly drawn secured loans to households, which are mostly mortgages, was 4.63%, and 4.03% on all outstanding ones (Bank of England, published 29 October 2026). Your own rate depends on the deal, your LTV and your credit record.

A longer term lowers the payment but adds a lot of interest, so many borrowers choose the shortest term they can afford.

Small rate moves matter on a big loan. A rise of a quarter of a point, from 4.5% to 4.75%, lifts the default repayment from £1,500.75 to £1,539.32, about £38.57 a month or £463 a year. That is the trade-off between deals: a fixed rate keeps your payment the same for the fixed period whatever Bank Rate does, while a tracker passes every rise and cut straight through to you.

When a fixed or tracker deal ends, the loan usually moves to the lender's standard variable rate unless you remortgage. Run the calculator again at the new rate, with the balance left and the years remaining, to see the change in your payment.

On the default £300,000 home a home mover pays 2% of £125,000 plus 5% of £50,000 = £5,000. On £500,000 it is £15,000.

More examples from the calculator for a home mover: £200,000 pays £1,500, £925,000 pays £36,250 and £1.5 million pays £93,750. The jump comes from the 10% band, which applies only to the part of the price between £925,000 and £1.5 million; the 12% band starts above that.

First-time buyers pay nothing on the first £300,000 and 5% from £300,001 to £500,000, so a £300,000 first home pays £0, a £450,000 one £7,500 and a £500,000 one £10,000. Above £500,000 the relief is lost completely: a £550,000 first home pays the standard £17,500.

The surcharges stack. A £500,000 buy-to-let pays £40,000, and a first-time buyer who is not UK resident pays the 2% surcharge on top of the relief rates, so a £400,000 first home costs £13,000 instead of £5,000.

Buying a second home or a buy-to-let usually adds 5 percentage points to every band, which takes the £300,000 example to £20,000. Buyers who are not UK resident usually pay a further 2% surcharge, which makes it £11,000 for a home mover. Scotland charges Land and Buildings Transaction Tax and Wales Land Transaction Tax instead, with their own bands, so the estimate here does not apply there. Always confirm the figure with your solicitor or HMRC's calculator.

Monthly costs, how much you can borrow, and tips

Use this checklist alongside the calculator before you commit to a mortgage.

  • Budget for the full monthly cost. Council tax is set by your home's band and your council, and insurance, service charge and ground rent (common on flats) add to it. With a £1,200 a year service charge, the default case costs £1,800.75 a month instead of £1,700.75.
  • How much you can borrow is decided by each lender's affordability check of your income, regular spending and credit history, so ask a lender or broker for an agreement in principle before you make offers. The House Affordability Calculator gives a first idea from your income and debts.
  • Remember the upfront cash: deposit, stamp duty, solicitor and survey fees, and any product fee. On the default home, the deposit and stamp duty alone are £35,000.
  • Check fees. A product fee added to the loan raises the amount you borrow and the interest you pay on it, so compare deals on their total cost over the fixed period.
  • Overpay if your deal allows it. Extra payments cut the balance and the interest; check your deal's overpayment allowance and any early repayment charge, then use the Mortgage Payoff Calculator to see what overpaying saves.
  • Couples and joint buyers: lenders assess both incomes for a joint mortgage, and the first-time buyer relief only applies if every buyer is a first-time buyer.
  • Remortgage before your deal ends. Comparing deals a few months early lets you avoid sliding onto the standard variable rate; enter the balance left, the years remaining and the new rate to see your new repayment.
  • Shorter is cheaper. On the default loan, 20 years instead of 25 costs £207.40 more a month but saves £40,267.51 of interest.

How it works

Enter the property price, your deposit as a percentage or in pounds, the mortgage term and the yearly interest rate. The mortgage is the price minus the deposit. A repayment mortgage pays off capital and interest together with one fixed monthly amount, worked out month by month at a twelfth of the yearly rate. The interest-only payment covers just the interest, so the whole loan is still owed at the end. Under Yearly costs, council tax, insurance and other yearly bills are added as one twelfth a month, and under Stamp duty the estimate applies the England and Northern Ireland bands to the price for a home mover, a first-time buyer or a second home, with the non-resident surcharge if you tick it.

The formula

  • Mortgage L = price − deposit; loan to value = L ÷ price
  • Monthly rate r = yearly rate ÷ 100 ÷ 12; number of repayments n = years × 12
  • Repayment M = L × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n); at 0% interest, M = L ÷ n
  • Interest-only payment = L × r
  • Stamp duty = the sum over the bands of (part of the price in the band × band rate)

A worked example

A £500,000 home with a 25% deposit needs a £375,000 mortgage (75% loan to value). Over 25 years at 5%, r = 0.05 ÷ 12 and n = 300, so the repayment is £2,192.21 a month and the 300 repayments add up to £657,663.80, of which £282,663.80 is interest. Interest-only would be £375,000 × 0.05 ÷ 12 = £1,562.50 a month. Stamp duty for a home mover is £15,000.

Questions

How do I calculate my monthly mortgage repayment in the UK?

Divide the yearly rate by 12 to get the monthly rate r, count the months n, and use M = L × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n). A £270,000 mortgage over 25 years at 4.5% comes to £1,500.75 a month. The calculator does this and adds council tax, insurance and stamp duty.

How do I calculate mortgage interest in the UK?

Each month's interest is the balance owed × the yearly rate ÷ 12, so £270,000 at 4.5% costs £1,012.50 in the first month. Over a whole repayment mortgage, total interest is the sum of all repayments minus the loan: £180,224.31 for that example over 25 years.

How do I calculate LTV for a UK mortgage?

Divide the mortgage by the property price and multiply by 100. A £270,000 mortgage on a £300,000 home is 90% LTV; a 25% deposit gives 75%. Lower LTV bands usually get better rates.

What is the difference between repayment and interest-only?

A repayment mortgage clears the loan by the end of the term because every payment also pays off some capital. Interest-only is cheaper each month but leaves the whole loan to repay at the end, so lenders ask how you will do that.

How much stamp duty will I pay?

In England and Northern Ireland there is no stamp duty on the first £125,000, then 2% up to £250,000, 5% up to £925,000, 10% up to £1.5 million and 12% above. On £300,000 a home mover pays £5,000. Second homes usually pay 5% more on every band, and non-UK residents a further 2%.

Do first-time buyers pay stamp duty?

Not on the first £300,000. They pay 5% on the part from £300,001 to £500,000, so a £450,000 first home costs £7,500. If the price is over £500,000 the relief is lost and the standard rates apply.

What is the UK mortgage base rate?

The Bank of England's Bank Rate is 4%, after a 0.25 point rise on 5 November 2026; the next decision is due on 17 December 2026. Tracker mortgages move with it, and fixed rates are priced from market expectations of it.

What is the average UK mortgage rate?

The Bank of England's effective rate on newly drawn secured loans to households was 4.63% in September 2026, and 4.03% across all outstanding loans. At 4.63%, a £270,000 mortgage over 25 years costs £1,520.74 a month.

Can I use it for a buy-to-let or as a non-UK resident?

Yes. Choose Second home or buy-to-let for the higher stamp duty rates and tick Buyer is not UK resident for the 2% surcharge. The interest-only payment row shows the cost of an interest-only buy-to-let loan.

How much can I borrow for a mortgage in the UK?

It depends on each lender's affordability check of your income, spending and credit record, and on your deposit. This calculator starts from the price; for a first idea from your income, use the house affordability calculator, then ask a lender for an agreement in principle.

Why does my lender's figure differ slightly?

Lenders may add product fees to the loan, charge interest daily, or move to a standard variable rate when a fixed deal ends. This calculator keeps one rate for the whole term, which is the usual way to compare deals.

Should I include council tax?

It is not part of the mortgage, but it is a fixed monthly bill for every home, so adding it gives a truer monthly cost. Find your band and yearly amount on your local council's website.
