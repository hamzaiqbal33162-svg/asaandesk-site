What an annuity payout calculator tells you

An annuity payout calculator shows how much income a lump sum can pay you, or how long a chosen income will last, while the money not yet paid out keeps earning a return. It is the second half of an annuity: first you build a balance, then you turn it into regular payments. That switch is called annuitization, and the years of payments that follow are the payout or distribution phase.

The same maths works for any pot of money you draw down on a schedule: an annuity bought from an insurer, a pension fund balance, a lump sum from selling a business, or savings you want to spend evenly over a set number of years. The calculator answers the two questions people ask most: what can I take each month, and how long will my money last?

To find out what your savings will grow to before the payouts start, use the Annuity Calculator first and bring its end balance here.

How to use the annuity payout calculator

  • Choose a tab. How much can I take? finds the payment for a fixed number of years. How long will it last? finds the time for a payment you choose.
  • Starting balance: the money at the start of the payout phase, such as the value of your annuity or pension pot on the day income begins.
  • Yearly return: what the money still in the account earns each year, from 0% to 100%. Use a figure after fees; for a fixed annuity it is the rate the insurer credits.
  • Years to pay out (first tab): how long the payments should last, from 0.1 to 100 years. Fractions work, so 12.5 years of monthly payments is 150 payments.
  • Payment you want (second tab): the amount you would like each period.
  • Paid: every year, every six months, every quarter, every month, twice a month or every two weeks. Payments are made at the end of each period.
  • Currency: pick dollars, rupees, pounds, euros or another currency from the list; the maths does not change.

If the number of years does not give a whole number of payments, the calculator rounds to the nearest payment and says so in a note. A term shorter than one payment period is refused.

The annuity payment formula, explained

The starting balance is the present value of all the payments you will receive. Each payment at the end of period t is worth PMT ÷ (1 + i)^t today, and adding n of them gives PV = PMT × (1 − (1 + i)^−n) ÷ i. Turn that around and you have the annuity payment formula: PMT = PV × i ÷ (1 − (1 + i)^−n). The part (1 − (1 + i)^−n) ÷ i is the present value annuity factor.

For the second tab the formula is solved for n instead: n = −ln(1 − PV × i ÷ PMT) ÷ ln(1 + i). If PMT is no more than PV × i, the bracket is zero or negative, which means the return alone covers the payment and the balance never runs out. That never-ending stream is called a perpetuity, and PV × i is the largest perpetual payment.

In Excel or Google Sheets, PMT(rate, nper, pv) gives the payment and NPER(rate, pmt, pv) the number of periods. To match this page, use the period rate it shows rather than the yearly rate ÷ 12: =PMT((1+4.5%)^(1/12)-1, 300, -300000) returns 1,652.17, the same as the example above.

Worked annuity payout examples

Example 1, how much can I take: $300,000 earning 4.5% a year, paid monthly for 25 years, gives $1,652.17 a month. The 300 payments total $495,651.15, so $195,651.15 is return. In year 1 the balance earns $13,094.34 and you receive $19,826.05, so it falls to $293,268.29; the falls get bigger every year as the balance and its earnings shrink.

Example 2, how long will it last: from the same $300,000 at 4.5%, a payment of $2,000 a month lasts 18.20 years. That is 219 payments: 218 of $2,000 and a last one of $864.51, $436,864.51 in all.

Example 3, money that never runs out: at 4.5% the balance earns $1,102.44 in the first month. Take $1,000 a month and the calculator shows Forever, because each payment is less than the return and the balance grows by $102.44 in the first month alone.

Example 4, in Pakistani rupees: suppose half of a Voluntary Pension System balance, Rs 6,127,705.43, goes into an income payment plan earning an assumed 10% a year. Paid monthly for 20 years it gives Rs 57,394.51 a month (Rs 688,734.15 a year). A payment of Rs 60,000 lasts 17.67 years, and up to Rs 48,863.18 a month could be paid forever without touching the balance.

Annuity payout options

When you annuitize, the contract offers several ways to be paid, and not every contract offers every option. This calculator works out the two that depend only on the balance, the return and the time: a fixed period and a fixed amount. Life options also depend on life expectancy: the person whose life expectancy sets the payments is called the annuitant, and the insurer prices the income from its own estimate of how long you will live.

Some contracts do not let you take extra money out once regular payments have started, so choose the option before you annuitize. Payments can also be fixed in amount or, in a variable annuity, rise and fall with the funds.

Lottery annuity payouts: Powerball and Mega Millions

Big US lottery jackpots are offered as an annuity or as a one-time cash payment. Powerball pays the annuity in 30 graduated payments over 29 years. Mega Millions pays one immediate payment followed by 29 yearly payments, each 5% bigger than the one before; its cash option is a single payment equal to the cash in the jackpot prize pool.

Because each Mega Millions payment is 5% larger than the last, the 30 payments add up to 66.44 times the first one. That factor is what 1 a year growing at 5% for 30 years adds up to; the Annuity Calculator gives 66,438,847.50 for 1,000,000 a year at 5% over 30 years. So if the annuity payments add up to $1 billion, the first is about $15,051,435 and the thirtieth about $61,953,748, before tax.

Lottery prizes are fully taxable in the United States and must be reported on your tax return. The graduated payments are set by the lottery, so this calculator's level payments do not match them exactly; use it instead to see what the cash option could pay you if you invested it and drew it down yourself.

Taxes and inflation during the payout years

In the United States, the part of each payment that returns money you already paid tax on is tax-free, and the rest is taxed as ordinary income. For a non-qualified annuity the tax-free part is worked out with the General Rule in IRS Publication 939, using IRS life expectancy tables; payments from a qualified plan usually use the Simplified Method in Publication 575. The tax-free part is set when payments begin and generally stays the same each year, and payments taken before age 59½ may face an extra 10% tax.

In Pakistan, up to 50% of a Voluntary Pension System balance can be withdrawn tax-free at retirement, and the rest can be placed in an approved income payment plan with a pension fund manager or used to buy an approved annuity plan from a life insurer, under the Income Tax Ordinance, 2001 as amended up to 30 June 2026. The older exemption for monthly instalments from an income payment plan (clause 23B) was removed by the Finance Act, 2022, so ask your pension fund manager how your instalments are taxed now.

Inflation is the hidden cost of a fixed income. To see a payment that keeps its buying power, enter a real return instead of the full return: (1 + return) ÷ (1 + inflation) − 1. With a 4.5% return and 2.5% inflation the real return is about 1.951%, and $300,000 over 25 years then pays $1,261.91 a month in today's money, rising with prices each year, instead of a flat $1,652.17.

Tips for planning annuity income

  • Payout rate is not a return. A $19,826.05 yearly income from $300,000 is a 6.61% payout rate, but most of it is your own money coming back; the return in that example is 4.5%.
  • Plan for a long life. Running out at 85 is worse than having money left at 95, so test a longer period or a lower return.
  • Keep some cash outside the annuity for repairs, health costs and emergencies, because many contracts limit extra withdrawals once payments start.
  • Ask for quotes in writing from more than one insurer or pension fund manager and compare the payment for the same option, start date and guarantee.
  • Work out the whole plan: the Retirement Calculator shows how much you need, the Pension Calculator compares a lump sum with a monthly pension, and the Present Value Calculator values any stream of payments.

How it works

This covers the paying-out years. Enter the balance you start with and the yearly return the money left in it earns. In the first tab, How much can I take?, pick how many years the payments should last and how often you are paid: the calculator finds the equal payment that uses up the balance exactly at the end. In the second tab, How long will it last?, enter the payment you want: it finds how long the money lasts, and the last payment is whatever is left. If each payment is no more than the return the balance earns, the money never runs out. Payments are taken at the end of each period, and the yearly return is turned into the equal rate for one payment period.

The formula

  • Payments a year k (1, 2, 4, 12, 24 or 26); rate per payment i = (1 + rate ÷ 100)^(1/k) − 1
  • Fixed time: n = years × k, payment = balance × i ÷ (1 − (1 + i)^−n); at 0%, payment = balance ÷ n
  • Fixed payment: lasts forever when payment ≤ balance × i; otherwise n = −ln(1 − balance × i ÷ payment) ÷ ln(1 + i) payments, years = n ÷ k
  • Each period: return = balance × i; balance = balance + return − payment

A worked example

Take $300,000 earning 4.5% a year out monthly over 25 years. The monthly rate is 1.045^(1/12) − 1 ≈ 0.3675%, and with 300 payments the formula gives $1,652.17 a month, or $19,826.05 a year. The 300 payments add up to $495,651.15, so $195,651.15 of it is return earned on the money still waiting to be paid out.

Questions

How do I calculate an annuity payout?

Divide the yearly return into a rate per payment, i = (1 + rate)^(1/k) − 1, then use payment = balance × i ÷ (1 − (1 + i)^−n), where n is the number of payments. The calculator does this for you and also shows the year-by-year balance.

How do I calculate the monthly annuity payment in Excel?

Use =PMT(rate, nper, pv) with the monthly rate, the number of months and the balance as a negative number. For $300,000 at 4.5% a year over 25 years, =PMT((1+4.5%)^(1/12)-1, 300, -300000) gives 1,652.17.

How long will my annuity or savings last?

Open the How long will it last? tab, enter the balance, the yearly return and the payment you want. For example, $300,000 at 4.5% paying $2,000 a month lasts 18.20 years; if the payment is no more than the return, it lasts forever.

What is an annuity payout rate?

It is the yearly income divided by the amount you put in. Because the income includes your own money being returned, a payout rate is always higher than the interest the money earns; in Example 1 it is 6.61% against a 4.5% return.

What is the annuity payout for a Powerball or Mega Millions jackpot?

Powerball pays the annuity in 30 graduated payments over 29 years, and Mega Millions pays one immediate payment and 29 yearly ones, each 5% bigger than the one before. On a $1 billion annuity with 5% steps, the first payment would be about $15.05 million and the last about $61.95 million, before tax.

How is a lifetime annuity payout calculated?

The insurer uses the balance, the interest rate it can earn and its estimate of how long the annuitant will live. Because life expectancy is part of the price, a lifetime payment cannot be found with a formula alone; use a fixed period equal to your life expectancy here for a rough comparison, and ask insurers for real quotes.

Why is the monthly rate not just the yearly rate divided by 12?

The yearly return is treated as what the money earns over a whole year. The monthly rate is the one that, earned 12 times, gives exactly that yearly growth, which is a little less than one twelfth.

What does it mean when the money lasts forever?

Each payment is no more than the return the balance earns in that period, so the balance never goes down. The result shows the most you can take each period without ever touching the starting balance.

Why is the last payment smaller?

The money rarely runs out exactly on a payment date. The last payment is whatever is left in the balance, with its return, at that point.

Can I use this annuity payout calculator with taxes and inflation?

It does not take tax off, so treat the result as a payment before tax and check your tax rules separately. For inflation, enter a real return, (1 + return) ÷ (1 + inflation) − 1, and the payment comes out in today's money.
