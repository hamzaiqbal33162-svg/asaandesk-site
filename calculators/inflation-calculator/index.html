What an inflation calculator shows

An inflation calculator tells you what an amount of money from one date is worth at another date, once rising prices are taken into account. This one uses the official US consumer price index, so you can see the value of a US dollar by year or by month from January 1913 to the latest published month, October 2026.

Inflation means that the same money buys less over time. If a basket of everyday goods cost $100 in 2016 and the same basket costs $140.63 in October 2026, prices rose by 40.63% and a 2016 dollar has the buying power of about $1.41 today. The calculator does that conversion for any amount and any pair of dates, forwards or backwards.

Two more tabs work without the index. Future value shows what prices could be after a number of years at an average inflation rate you choose, and Past value works back from today. Those tabs are the ones to use for planning, for salaries and for countries other than the US, such as Pakistan.

How to use the inflation calculator, step by step

  • Choose a tab: US prices (CPI), Future value or Past value.
  • Enter the Amount, for example 100 or a salary of 50,000.
  • On US prices (CPI), set From: a Month (or Whole year (average)) and a Year, then set To the same way. The default compares the 2016 average with the latest month.
  • Pick Whole year (average) when you only know the year, such as the price of a house bought in 1995. Pick a month when you know it, such as the month a salary started.
  • The To date can be earlier than the From date. The result then tells you what your amount was worth back then.
  • On Future value or Past value, enter the Average inflation rate in % a year and the Years from now or Years ago. A negative rate means falling prices (deflation).

Understanding the results

On the CPI tab the headline is the amount with the same buying power at the second date, with a sentence that reads it for you. Under it you see Total inflation between the two dates and, when they are more than a year apart, Average inflation a year. The two CPI values used are shown too, so you can check the sum yourself.

What the consumer price index (CPI) is

The consumer price index measures the average change over time in the prices that people pay for a representative basket of consumer goods and services. The US Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) publishes it every month and records about 80,000 item prices each month to build it.

This calculator uses CPI-U, the index for all urban consumers, which covers over 90 percent of the US population. A second index, CPI-W, follows wage earners and clerical workers, about 30 percent of the population. Most series use 1982-84 = 100 as the base, so an index of 337.511 means prices are about 3.4 times their 1982-84 level.

The CPI leaves out income and Social Security taxes, and investments such as stocks, bonds, real estate and life insurance, because those relate to saving rather than day-to-day spending. The figure for each month comes out during the following month: the October 2026 index was released on 10 November 2026, and the November figure is due on 10 December 2026.

There is one gap in the record. BLS could not collect prices for October 2025 during the lapse in federal funding, so no all-items index was published for that month and the 2025 annual average was worked out from 11 months. The calculator says so instead of filling in a guess.

How to calculate the inflation rate from the CPI

The inflation formula using the CPI is: inflation rate = (CPI at the later date ÷ CPI at the earlier date − 1) × 100. For a single year, compare that year with the year before. The 2022 average CPI was 292.655 and the 2021 average was 270.970, so inflation in 2022 was (292.655 ÷ 270.970 − 1) × 100 = 8.0%.

To adjust an amount, multiply it by the ratio of the two index values: amount × CPI later ÷ CPI earlier. A salary of $50,000 in January 2020 (CPI 257.971) needs to be $65,416.46 in October 2026 (CPI 337.511) to buy the same, a rise of 30.83% or 4.06% a year on average.

To turn a total into an average yearly rate, take the root for the number of years: (CPI later ÷ CPI earlier)^(1 ÷ years) − 1. In Excel or Google Sheets, with the earlier index in A1, the later index in B1 and the years in C1, use =(B1/A1)^(1/C1)−1 for the average rate and =100*B1/A1 for what $100 is worth.

Economists also measure inflation with the GDP deflator, which is nominal GDP divided by real GDP. It covers everything an economy produces, not only what consumers buy, so the IMF notes that it includes items such as military spending and is not a good measure of the cost of living. For prices you pay, use the CPI.

The fastest price rises in the series came in 1917 and 1918 (about 17% to 18% a year), after the Second World War in 1947, and around 1980, when the yearly average rose 13.5%. Prices fell in the early 1920s and in the Great Depression, by about 10% in both 1921 and 1932, and dipped slightly in 2009.

Over long periods the effect is large: from January 1980 to the 2025 average, prices rose 313.81%, or 3.17% a year, so $100 in early 1980 matches $413.81 in 2025.

Why prices rise, and the limits of the CPI

The IMF describes several causes of inflation. Demand-pull inflation happens when spending grows faster than the economy can produce. Cost-push inflation follows supply shocks such as natural disasters or a jump in oil prices that raise production costs. When money grows too fast for the economy, long-lasting high inflation can follow, and if people expect prices to rise, wages and contracts adjust in a way that keeps inflation going.

Deflation is the opposite: prices fall, so money buys more over time. That sounds good, but the four years of falling US prices from 1930 to 1933, about a quarter in all, came during the Great Depression. The US Federal Reserve aims for inflation of 2 percent over the longer run, measured with a different index, the price index for personal consumption expenditures (PCE).

No price index is perfect. The BLS notes the long debate, summed up by the 1996 Boskin report, that the CPI overstated the rise in the cost of living because it missed some substitution between products, did not fully account for quality change and was slow to add new goods. BLS has since moved to formulas that allow for substitution within categories, adjusts for quality changes, and in 2002 added the chained CPI-U, which has run about 0.2 percentage points a year below CPI-U on average from 2001 to 2023.

Your own inflation can also differ from the average. Someone who rents, drives a lot or pays for health care may see costs rise faster or slower than the basket. Core inflation, which leaves out the volatile prices of food and energy, is useful for spotting trends but not for adjusting your own budget.

Inflation in Pakistan: how PBS measures the CPI

In Pakistan the consumer price index is compiled by the Pakistan Bureau of Statistics (PBS). The current base year is 2015-16. The urban CPI covers 356 items in 68 markets across 35 cities, and the rural CPI covers 244 items in 27 markets. PBS publishes the national figure every month in its Monthly Review on Price Indices.

According to PBS, national CPI inflation was 9.6% year on year in October 2026, after 10.3% in September 2026 and 6.2% in October 2025. Average inflation by fiscal year (July to June) was 29.18% in 2022-23, 23.41% in 2023-24, 4.49% in 2024-25 and 7.05% in 2025-26. Compounding those four averages, prices rose by roughly 78% between fiscal years 2021-22 and 2025-26.

PBS calculates the inflation rate in Pakistan with the same formula as the US: the index for a month divided by the index for the same month a year earlier, minus 1. The US CPI tab here holds only US data, so for rupees use the Future value or Past value tab with a rate from PBS. The page shows a dollar sign, but the arithmetic is the same for any currency.

Example: Rs 10,000 of shopping today would cost Rs 19,671.51 in 10 years at an average of 7% a year, since 1.07^10 = 1.967151. Going backwards on the Past value tab at the same rate, Rs 100 now had the buying power of Rs 50.83 ten years ago.

Salary, savings and retirement: using inflation in real decisions

  • Salary: compare a raise with inflation over the same dates. If pay went from 50,000 in January 2020 to 60,000 in October 2026, the real value fell, because 65,416.46 was needed to keep up.
  • Savings: money kept as cash loses buying power. At 3% inflation, $100 buys only $74.41 of today's goods after 10 years. Savings keep their value only when they earn more than inflation after tax.
  • Inflation-linked bonds in the US: I bonds pay a fixed rate plus an inflation rate that resets every six months; you can buy up to $10,000 in electronic I bonds a year, cash them after 12 months, and lose the last three months of interest if you cash them before 5 years. TIPS, sold in 5, 10 and 30-year terms, have principal that rises with the CPI and never repay less than the original principal.
  • Retirement planning: use a long-run inflation rate in the Retirement Calculator, and judge results in today's money.
  • Long periods: prefer whole-year averages for old dates, and remember the index started in 1913, so earlier years such as the 1800s are not covered here.
  • Do not mix countries: US CPI figures say nothing about rupee prices, and the reverse.

How it works

The first tab uses the official US consumer price index, CPI-U, published every month by the Bureau of Labor Statistics. The index tracks the price of a fixed basket of goods and services, so the ratio of two index values tells you how much prices changed between two dates. Multiply your amount by that ratio to get the amount with the same buying power at the other date. Pick a month, or a whole year to use that year's average. The other two tabs skip the index and use one average rate you choose, which is handy for planning or for countries other than the US.

The formula

  • Value at the second date = amount × CPI at the second date ÷ CPI at the first date
  • Total inflation = (CPI later ÷ CPI earlier − 1) × 100
  • Average inflation a year = (CPI later ÷ CPI earlier)^(1 ÷ years between) − 1, where a whole-year average counts as July of that year
  • Future value at a flat rate r for n years = amount × (1 + r)^n
  • Past value at a flat rate r for n years ago = amount ÷ (1 + r)^n

A worked example

The 2016 average CPI is 240.007 and the October 2026 CPI is 337.511. So $100 in 2016 is worth 100 × 337.511 ÷ 240.007 = $140.63 in October 2026: total inflation of 40.63%. From the middle of 2016 to October 2026 is 10.25 years, so the average is 1.4063^(1 ÷ 10.25) − 1 = 3.38% a year.

Questions

Which inflation figures does this use?

The CPI-U for all items, U.S. city average, not seasonally adjusted, with 1982-84 = 100. It is the headline consumer price index the Bureau of Labor Statistics publishes each month and the one most often used to compare the value of money over time. The page says which month is the latest one in the table.

How do I calculate the inflation rate from the CPI?

Divide the later index by the earlier one, subtract 1 and multiply by 100. For 2022, 292.655 ÷ 270.970 − 1 gives 8.0%. For an average yearly rate over several years, take the ratio to the power of 1 ÷ years before subtracting 1.

What is the inflation formula?

Value later = amount × CPI later ÷ CPI earlier, and inflation = (CPI later ÷ CPI earlier − 1) × 100. With a flat rate r instead of the index, the future price is amount × (1 + r)^n and the past value is amount ÷ (1 + r)^n.

How much is $100 from 1980 worth today?

Using the 1980 average CPI of 82.4 and the October 2026 CPI of 337.511, $100 from 1980 is worth about $409.60. Prices have roughly quadrupled since then.

Why is there no figure for October 2025?

Prices for October 2025 were not collected because of the lapse in US federal funding that autumn, so the Bureau of Labor Statistics published no index for that month. We do not fill in a guess: pick September or November 2025, or the 2025 average, which BLS worked out from the other 11 months.

What is the difference between a month and the whole-year average?

A month uses that month's index. The whole-year average is the mean of the year's monthly figures, which smooths out seasonal ups and downs. It is a fair choice when you only know the year, such as an old salary or a house bought in 1995.

Can I use it as an inflation calculator for Pakistan or rupees?

The first tab is for US prices only. For rupees, use the Future value or Past value tab with an average rate from the Pakistan Bureau of Statistics; the page shows a dollar sign, but the sum is the same in any currency.

What is the inflation rate in Pakistan in 2026?

The Pakistan Bureau of Statistics reported national CPI inflation of 9.6% year on year for October 2026, and an average of 7.05% for fiscal year 2025-26 (July 2025 to June 2026). Check the latest PBS monthly review, because the figure changes every month.

What is the difference between inflation and deflation?

Inflation means prices rise on average, so money buys less; deflation means prices fall, so money buys more. Enter a negative rate on the Future value or Past value tab to see deflation.

How is the CPI different from the GDP deflator?

The CPI tracks the prices of goods and services that households buy. The GDP deflator covers everything the economy produces, including investment and government spending, so it is broader but less suited to measuring the cost of living.

How do I adjust a salary for inflation?

Enter the old salary as the amount, the month it was paid as From and the latest month as To. A salary of $50,000 in January 2020 needs to be $65,416.46 in October 2026 to buy the same.

Does inflation mean my savings lose value?

Money kept as cash buys less when prices rise. The Future value tab shows what an amount kept as cash will buy later, in today's money. Savings that earn more than the inflation rate keep or grow their buying power.
