What a lease calculator works out

A lease calculator gives the monthly payment for leasing any asset, such as a machine, a vehicle, computers, medical equipment or solar panels, from four numbers: what the asset is worth today, what it will still be worth when the lease ends, how long the lease runs and the yearly interest rate. On the second tab it does the opposite and finds the interest rate hidden in a payment you have been offered.

In a lease, the owner (the lessor) lets someone else (the lessee) use an asset for a fixed time in return for regular payments. The lessee never pays for the whole asset. The payments cover the value it loses during the lease, called depreciation, plus interest on the money the lessor has tied up in it. What is left at the end, the residual value, goes back to the lessor or is paid by the lessee to keep the asset.

This page uses the standard present-value method that suits equipment and business leases. US car leases are usually priced with a money factor and sales tax on each payment instead; for those, the Auto Lease Calculator follows the dealer's method.

How to use the lease calculator

  • Pick a tab: Find the payment (you know the rate) or Find the rate (you know the payment).
  • Currency: choose it at the top. Only the symbol changes.
  • Value of the asset: the price of what you lease, before any deposit. If you pay something up front, subtract it here.
  • Value left at the end (residual): what the asset is expected to be worth when the lease ends, which is often also the price at which you may buy it. It cannot be more than the value.
  • Lease length: years plus any extra months, for example 2 years and 6 months.
  • Interest rate (Find the payment): the yearly rate in percent. Monthly payment (Find the rate): the amount you were quoted.
  • Each payment is made (under More options): At the end of the month, the usual loan convention, or At the start (in advance), which suits leases that take the first payment at signing.

The lease payment formula, step by step

The payment comes from one idea: the asset's value today must equal the present value of all the lease payments plus the present value of the residual you hand back at the end. Solving that for the payment gives P = (V − R × (1 + r)^−n) × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n). Here is the machine example worked by hand.

  • Monthly rate: r = 7.5% ÷ 12 = 0.625% = 0.00625. Number of payments: n = 4 × 12 = 48.
  • Discount factor: (1.00625)^−48 = 0.74151, so the residual's present value is 12,000 × 0.74151 = 8,898.12.
  • Amount to be paid off in present-value terms: 48,000 − 8,898.12 = 39,101.88.
  • Payment: 39,101.88 × 0.00625 ÷ (1 − 0.74151) = $945.44 a month.
  • Check: 48 × 945.44 = $45,381.14 of payments; with the $12,000 residual that is $57,381.14 for a $48,000 asset, so the interest is $9,381.14.

A quick sanity check works for any lease. At 0% the payment is just the lost value spread evenly: (48,000 − 12,000) ÷ 48 = $750.00, and the calculator shows exactly that with no interest. Any rate above zero adds interest on the whole amount tied up, including the residual, which is why the payment at 7.5% is $195.44 higher.

How the residual value changes the payment

The residual value is the biggest lever after the price. A higher residual means less value to pay off, so the payment falls; but you also borrow the residual for the whole term, so the interest rises. The table keeps the $48,000 machine, 4 years and 7.5%, and changes only the residual.

Residual value is also called salvage value or lease-end value. For equipment, the lessor estimates it from how fast that kind of asset usually loses value; for cars, it comes from the leasing company's own residual tables. If the residual is set too high, the payment looks attractive but buying the asset at the end can cost more than it is worth.

Finding the interest rate in a lease offer

Lease quotes often show the payment but not the rate. On the Find the rate tab, type the value, the residual, the length and the payment, and the calculator searches for the yearly rate that makes them fit. A $32,000 car with an $18,500 residual and a $450 payment for 3 years contains a rate of 3.522% a year (0.2935% a month), with $2,700 of interest over the lease.

That rate is directly comparable with a loan's rate. As a cross-check, the dealer-style money factor method on the auto lease page, using the same 3.522% (a money factor of about 0.00147) and no tax, gives a payment of $449.11, within a dollar of the $450.01 this method gives. The two methods agree closely for ordinary leases.

Equipment lessors sometimes quote a lease rate factor instead: the monthly payment divided by the equipment cost. The machine example's factor is 945.44 ÷ 48,000 = 0.0197. A factor on its own hides the residual and the term, so turn it into a payment and use the Find the rate tab to see the real yearly rate.

Payments in advance, deposits, tax and buyouts

Many leases take the first payment at signing, so every payment is made at the start of its month. Choose At the start (in advance) and the payment drops slightly, because the lessor gets its money a month sooner: the machine's payment becomes $939.57 instead of $945.44, and the total interest falls to $9,099.27.

A deposit or down payment that reduces what you lease should be subtracted from the value of the asset. A refundable security deposit that you get back at the end is different: it does not change the payment, but you lose the use of that money during the lease.

Sales tax, VAT or GST charged on each payment is not included. Multiply the payment by 1 plus the tax rate: with 13% HST in Ontario, $945.44 becomes 945.44 × 1.13 = $1,068.35 a month. Some tax rules for leases differ, so ask the lessor how tax is charged.

To buy the asset before the end, the payoff is roughly the present value of the payments still to come plus the present value of the residual, at the lease's rate. Halfway through the machine lease, after 24 payments, that is about $31,343.30: a 2-year lease of $31,343.30 with the same $12,000 residual at 7.5% gives the same $945.44 payment. The lessor's quote may add fees.

Types of lease: equipment, property and cars

Equipment and business leases come in two broad kinds. A finance (capital) lease transfers most of the asset's risks and rewards to the lessee, often running for most of its useful life with a small residual or a purchase option. An operating lease is shorter, leaves a sizeable residual with the lessor, and is closer to renting. Under the IFRS 16 accounting standard, effective for periods beginning on or after 1 January 2019, a lessee records a right-of-use asset and a lease liability for every lease of more than 12 months unless the asset is of low value. The lease liability is a present value of the lease payments, the same kind of sum this calculator does.

Property leases are priced differently. A residential lease simply fixes the rent for a term, often 12 months. Commercial property leases are often described as gross, where the landlord's costs are included in the rent, or net, where the tenant also pays some or all of the property tax, insurance and maintenance. Neither has a residual value, so for rent use the Rent Calculator or compare with buying on the Rent vs Buy Calculator.

Car leases work like equipment leases, but US dealers use a money factor and tax each payment, and contracts add mileage limits and wear charges. Use the auto lease calculator for those, and this page for a quick check of the rate.

Leasing in Pakistan and other countries

In Pakistan, bank car leasing and car Ijarah end with the car being transferred to you, so the monthly rental works like a loan instalment. Use the Auto Loan Calculator for those, keeping in mind the State Bank of Pakistan's auto finance limits (30% minimum down payment and a Rs3 million cap per person, as reported in October 2025). This calculator suits equipment and machinery leases from banks, leasing companies and modarabas, where a residual or a security deposit is settled at the end.

For example, choose PKR and lease a Rs 3,000,000 generator for 3 years at 18% with a Rs 300,000 residual. The payment is Rs 102,111.47 a month; the 36 payments total Rs 3,676,012.85, so the lease costs Rs 976,012.85 in interest on top of the Rs 2,700,000 of value used up.

The same maths works in Canada, the UK, Australia, Sri Lanka or anywhere else: only the currency and the tax treatment change. Check how your lessor charges tax and whether payments are in advance, then set the options to match.

Tips and related calculators

  • Always find the rate. A low payment can hide a high rate or an unrealistic residual.
  • Compare the total cost (payments plus any buyout) with buying the asset on a loan over the same years.
  • Check whether payments are in advance or in arrears, and set the option to match the quote, or the rate you find will be slightly off.
  • Read the end-of-lease terms: purchase option price, return conditions, and any fees for damage or early termination.
  • For business leases, ask your accountant how the lease is treated for tax and in your accounts.

How it works

A lease is like a loan on only part of the asset. You borrow its full value, but at the end you hand back (or buy) something still worth the residual value, so your payments only have to cover the value it loses plus interest on the money tied up. The calculator finds the one level monthly payment whose present value, together with the present value of the residual, equals the asset's value today. In the second tab it works backwards: from a payment you were offered, it finds the yearly interest rate that payment contains.

The formula

  • Monthly rate r = yearly rate ÷ 100 ÷ 12; number of payments n = years × 12 + months
  • Payment P = (V − R × (1 + r)^−n) × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n), with V = asset value and R = residual value
  • At 0%: P = (V − R) ÷ n. Paid in advance: divide P by (1 + r)
  • Total interest = P × n + R − V
  • Finding the rate: the r for which P × (1 − (1 + r)^−n) ÷ r + R × (1 + r)^−n = V, found by halving the search range until it is exact

A worked example

A $48,000 machine with $12,000 left after 4 years at 7.5%: r = 0.075 ÷ 12 = 0.00625, n = 48, (1.00625)^−48 = 0.74151. P = (48,000 − 12,000 × 0.74151) × 0.00625 ÷ (1 − 0.74151) = $945.44 a month. The 48 payments add up to $45,381.14, and the interest is 45,381.14 + 12,000 − 48,000 = $9,381.14.

Questions

How do I calculate a lease payment?

Take the asset's value, subtract the present value of the residual, and spread the rest over the months as a level payment at the monthly rate. A $48,000 machine with a $12,000 residual over 4 years at 7.5% costs $945.44 a month. The calculator does the sum as you type.

What is the lease payment formula?

P = (V − R × (1 + r)^−n) × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n), where V is the asset value, R the residual, r the monthly rate and n the number of payments. For payments in advance, divide the result by (1 + r).

What is the residual value?

It is what the asset is expected to be worth when the lease ends. The lessor gets it back (or you pay that amount to keep it), so your payments do not have to cover it. A higher residual means a lower payment.

How do I find the rate in a lease offer?

Open the Find the rate tab and type the value, the residual, the length and the monthly payment you were offered. The calculator shows the yearly rate that makes those numbers fit, so you can compare it with a loan.

Is this the same as a car lease with a money factor?

Not quite. Car dealers in the US use a money factor and add sales tax to each payment. The two methods give payments within a dollar or two of each other, but use the Auto Lease Calculator for car deals so the tax and fees match.

Payment at the start or the end of the month?

The default is the end of each month, the usual loan convention. Many leases take the first payment at signing, which is paying in advance; choose that under More options and the payment drops a little.

How do I calculate a lease payment with tax?

Work out the payment here, then multiply it by 1 plus the tax rate charged on each payment. A $945.44 payment with 13% tax becomes $1,068.35. If tax is charged on the whole value at the start instead, add that amount to what you pay up front.

How do I calculate the lease buyout amount?

At the end of the lease it is usually the residual or purchase option price written in the contract. Earlier, it is roughly the present value of the payments still due plus the present value of the residual; halfway through the machine example that is about $31,343.30. Ask the lessor for an exact payoff quote.

How is a lease liability calculated under IFRS 16?

The lessee records a liability equal to the present value of the lease payments it still has to make, discounted at the rate in the lease or its own borrowing rate. This calculator's Find the rate tab helps you find the rate implicit in a lease; your accountant will confirm which rate applies.

What is lease financing?

It is a way to use an asset without buying it: a lessor buys the equipment, vehicle or machine and lets you use it for regular payments. It keeps cash free and can be simpler to arrange than a loan, but the total cost is usually higher than buying outright.

How does a car lease work in Pakistan?

Bank car leasing and Ijarah in Pakistan end with the car being transferred to you, so the instalment behaves like a loan payment. Use the auto loan calculator with the bank's rate, and remember the 30% minimum down payment reported under State Bank of Pakistan rules.
