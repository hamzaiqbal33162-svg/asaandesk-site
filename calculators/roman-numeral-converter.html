What Roman numerals are and how this converter helps

Roman numerals are the number system of ancient Rome, written with seven capital letters: I, V, X, L, C, D and M. This Roman numeral converter turns an ordinary number (an Arabic or Hindu-Arabic numeral such as 2026) into Roman numerals, and a Roman numeral such as MMXXVI back into a number, with the steps shown so you can check the working.

Roman numerals are still everywhere: on clock faces, in the names of kings, queens and popes (Elizabeth II), in Super Bowl and Olympic numbering, in film copyright years, in book chapters and the page numbers of a preface, in outlines, and in chemistry names such as iron(III) chloride. They are also popular for tattoos and engravings of birthdays and wedding dates, which is why the converter has a Date tab.

Arabic numerals reached Europe by the 11th century, but the change was slow: Roman numerals stayed in everyday use, even in business records, into the 14th and 15th centuries.

The rules for writing Roman numerals

  • Write from the largest value to the smallest and add: CLXV = 100 + 50 + 10 + 5 = 165.
  • A smaller letter in front of a larger one is subtracted. Only six such pairs are standard: IV (4), IX (9), XL (40), XC (90), CD (400) and CM (900).
  • Below 4,000, only I, X and C are put in front to subtract, and only from the next two letters up: I before V or X, X before L or C, C before D or M. So 49 is XLIX, not IL, and 99 is XCIX, not IC.
  • V, L and D are never repeated and never subtracted. VV would just be X, and VX is not a numeral at all; the converter refuses it with the reason.
  • I, X, C and M are not written more than three times in a row in the standard form, so 4 is IV rather than IIII and 40 is XL rather than XXXX.
  • There is no zero and there are no negative numbers or fractions in this system; the converter takes whole numbers from 1 up.

These are today's conventions, not laws handed down from Rome. Ancient inscriptions often used additive forms such as IIII and VIIII; on the Colosseum, IIII is used throughout, though gate 44 is labelled XLIIII. Many clock faces still show IIII for four o'clock. That is why this converter reads IIII as 4 but adds a note that IV is the standard form.

Worked examples: numbers, numerals and big values

Number to numeral. 1947 splits into 1,000 + 900 + 40 + 5 + 1 + 1, which become M + CM + XL + V + I + I, so 1947 is MCMXLVII. 49 splits into 40 + 9 and is XLIX.

Numeral to number. MCMLXXXVIII splits into M + CM + L + X + X + X + V + I + I + I, which add up to 1,988. XCIX is XC + IX = 99.

Numerals that do not exist. Type IL and the converter replies that I cannot stand in front of L; type VX and it explains that V cannot stand in front of X.

Large numbers. 12,345 is X̅I̅I̅CCCXLV: the barred XII means 12 thousands, followed by CCCXLV for 345. Where bars cannot be shown, the plain-text form is (XII)CCCXLV, and the typed form here is _X_I_ICCCXLV. 2,500,000 is M̅M̅D̅, and 4,000 is I̅V̅.

Dates in Roman numerals for tattoos, birthdays and engravings

A date in Roman numerals converts the day, the month and the year one by one. Pakistan's independence day, 14 August 1947, written day first with dots, is XIV.VIII.MCMXLVII. A wedding on 18 June 2027 written month first with slashes, as in the US, is VI/XVIII/MMXXVII; day first with a two-digit year it becomes XVIII.VI.XXVII.

Decide on the order before anything permanent is made. III.IV.MMXXVI is 3 April 2026 to someone who reads day first but 4 March 2026 to someone who reads month first. Many people write the full four-digit year to avoid confusion, and some keep the day and year in ordinary digits and write only the month in Roman numerals.

Before a tattoo or engraving, type the date into the Date tab, then paste the result back into Convert or Many at once to read it back. Check that there are no stray spaces or letters, and that the separator is the one you want.

Roman numerals in chemistry, Excel and on a keyboard

In chemistry, a Roman numeral in brackets after a metal's name gives its oxidation state, in what is called Stock nomenclature: FeCl₂ is iron(II) chloride and Fe₂O₃ is iron(III) oxide. Alfred Stock proposed the system in 1919 and IUPAC adopted it in 1940. To find the numeral for an ionic compound, work out the metal's charge from the other ions; in FeCl₃ the three chloride ions carry −3 in total, so the iron is +3 and the name is iron(III) chloride.

In Excel, =ROMAN(number) converts a number up to 3,999 into Roman numerals as text; =ROMAN(499) gives CDXCIX. An optional second argument picks shorter, non-standard styles, and numbers above 3,999 give a #VALUE! error, so use this converter for bigger values.

On a keyboard, simply type the capital letters I, V, X, L, C, D and M. Unicode also has special Roman numeral characters, but the Unicode standard itself advises building numerals from ordinary letters for most purposes, which keeps them searchable.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Writing 99 as IC or 49 as IL. Break the number by place value instead: 90 + 9 = XCIX, 40 + 9 = XLIX.
  • Writing 1990 as MXM. It is 1,000 + 900 + 90 = MCMXC.
  • Repeating V, L or D. 10 is X, 100 is C and 1,000 is M.
  • Mixing up the date order on a tattoo or ring. Agree on day first or month first, and write the year in full.
  • Expecting a numeral for zero. The Romans had none; the word nulla was used in medieval tables instead.

How it works

Type a number or a Roman numeral; the converter sees which one it is. Roman numerals are built from seven letters, I = 1, V = 5, X = 10, L = 50, C = 100, D = 500 and M = 1,000, written from the largest value to the smallest and added up. Six pairs are subtracted instead: IV = 4, IX = 9, XL = 40, XC = 90, CD = 400 and CM = 900. No letter is used more than three times in a row, so the largest plain numeral is MMMCMXCIX = 3,999; for larger numbers a bar over a numeral multiplies it by 1,000 (V̅ = 5,000), which reaches 3,999,999. The Date tab writes a day, month and year in numerals, and Many at once converts a whole list, one per line, in either direction.

The formula

  • Values: I 1, V 5, X 10, L 50, C 100, D 500, M 1,000 (a bar over a letter: × 1,000)
  • Number to numeral: take the largest of 1,000 900 500 400 100 90 50 40 10 9 5 4 1 that fits, write its numeral, subtract, repeat
  • Numeral to number: add the values, but subtract a letter that stands before a larger one (IV, IX, XL, XC, CD, CM)
  • Only I, X, C and M (or a barred one) may be subtracted, and only from the next two larger letters

A worked example

1947 = 1,000 + 900 + 40 + 7 = M + CM + XL + VII = MCMXLVII. The other way, MCMLXXXVIII = M + CM + L + XXX + VIII = 1,000 + 900 + 50 + 30 + 8 = 1,988. The date 14 August 1947 is XIV.VIII.MCMXLVII.

Questions

How do I convert a number to Roman numerals?

Split the number by place value, write each part from the chart and join them, largest first. 1947 is 1,000 + 900 + 40 + 7, which is M + CM + XL + VII = MCMXLVII. The converter shows the same steps.

How do I read Roman numerals?

Add the letters from left to right, but when a smaller letter comes before a larger one, subtract it instead. MCMLXXXVIII is 1,000 + 900 + 50 + 30 + 8 = 1,988.

What is IV in Roman numerals?

IV is 4: one before five. Likewise VI is 6, IX is 9 and XI is 11.

What is L in Roman numerals?

L is 50. 40 is XL, 60 is LX and 90 is XC.

What is 9 in Roman numerals?

9 is IX, one before ten. VIIII also appears in old inscriptions, but IX is the standard form.

Why does IIII give a note instead of an error?

IIII is read as 4, because the letters add up to 4, and it is even used on many clock faces. The standard form is IV, so the converter says so. Numerals that cannot mean anything, such as VX or IC, are refused with the reason.

How are numbers above 3,999 written?

With a bar (a vinculum) over the thousands: the barred part is multiplied by 1,000. 4,000 is I̅V̅, 10,000 is X̅ and 1,000,000 is M̅. To type a bar here, put _ in front of the letter: _V is V̅ = 5,000. Where a bar cannot be shown, the plain-text form puts brackets around the barred part, such as (IV) for 4,000.

Is there a Roman numeral for zero or for fractions?

No. The Romans had no zero digit (they wrote the word nulla) and used a separate system of twelfths for fractions, so this converter takes whole numbers from 1 to 3,999,999.

How do I write my date of birth in Roman numerals?

Open the Date tab, pick the date and choose the order and separator you want. 14 August 1947 is XIV.VIII.MCMXLVII day first, or VIII.XIV.MCMXLVII month first. Agree on the order before a tattoo or engraving: III.IV.MMXXVI is 3 April day first but 4 March month first.

How do I find the Roman numeral in a chemical name?

Work out the metal ion's charge from the rest of the formula and write it in Roman numerals in brackets: FeCl₃ has three Cl⁻ ions, so the iron is +3 and the name is iron(III) chloride.

Can I convert many at once?

Yes. In Many at once, type one number, numeral or date per line, up to 1,000 lines. Each line is converted in the right direction, a date such as 14-08-1947 is converted part by part, and you can copy all the results or download the table.
