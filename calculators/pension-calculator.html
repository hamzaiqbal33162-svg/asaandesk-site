What this pension calculator shows

A pension calculator helps you make the three choices most people with a workplace pension face at retirement: take a lump sum or a monthly pension, take a single-life pension or one that keeps paying your spouse, and retire now on a smaller pension or later on a bigger one. Each choice is a trade between money now and money later, so the calculator turns every option into its value today and shows a break-even age.

The break-even age is the key result. It is the age you would have to live to for one option to pay more than the other, after counting what the money could earn in the meantime and how much the pension rises each year. If you expect to live past it, the option that pays more later wins; if not, the option that pays more sooner wins.

These are the choices of a defined benefit pension, the kind that promises a set monthly amount. For a pension pot you build yourself, such as a 401(k) or a Voluntary Pension System account, use the Retirement Calculator or the Annuity Payout Calculator instead.

How to use the pension calculator, step by step

  • Pick a tab: Lump sum or monthly, Protect my spouse, or Retire now or later.
  • Lump sum or monthly: enter Your retirement age, the Lump sum offered and the monthly pension you would get instead (Or a monthly pension of).
  • Protect my spouse: enter Your retirement age and how long you expect to live (Your life expectancy), then for your partner the age they will be when you stop work (Spouse's age when you retire) and Spouse's life expectancy, and finally the Monthly pension, single life (stops when you die) and the Monthly pension, joint and survivor (keeps paying your spouse).
  • Retire now or later: enter the first age and its monthly pension (Retire at age), and the later age and its larger pension (Or work longer and retire at).
  • On every tab, enter the Yearly return if you invest the money, from −10% to 30%, and the Yearly cost-of-living rise of the pension (use 0 if it never rises).
  • Choose your currency, such as US dollar or Pakistani rupee; the maths is the same.

The lump sum is assumed to go into an IRA or another account where it is not taxed straight away. If you would pay tax when you take it, enter the amount you would keep after tax so the comparison is fair.

Lump sum or monthly pension: a worked example

You retire at 62 and your plan offers either $420,000 now or $2,450 a month for life, rising 2% a year. You think you could earn 5% a year investing the lump sum. The calculator shows a break-even age of 81: the pension has to be paid for 19 years before its value today ($426,177) passes the lump sum. To match that income from the lump sum you could take $2,831.75 a month for 19 years, after which it would be gone, while the pension carries on for life.

The return you assume moves the break-even age a lot, because a higher return makes the lump sum worth more. A pension without cost-of-living rises is also worth much less: at 7% a flat pension would have to be paid until 112 to match the lump sum.

Money is only part of the decision. A monthly pension cannot run out and protects you if you live a long time; a lump sum gives you control, flexibility for large costs and something to leave to your heirs, but you carry the investment risk. In the United States the Department of Labor notes that a lump sum may be taxed, and may face a penalty if you are under 59½, unless you roll it over.

Single-life or joint-and-survivor pension

A single-life pension pays more each month but stops when you die. A joint-and-survivor pension pays less while you are alive and then keeps paying your spouse. Under US rules for defined benefit and money purchase plans, the standard payment for a married participant is a qualified joint and survivor annuity, and the survivor's payment must be at least half of the payment during your joint lives; some plans offer 75%. To choose a pension without a survivor benefit, your spouse must sign a written consent witnessed by a notary or a plan representative.

Example: you retire at 66 and expect to live to 84; your spouse is 63 and expected to live to 88. The plan offers $2,900 a month single life or $2,550 joint and survivor, both rising 2% a year, and you could earn 4.5%. The calculator says joint and survivor is the better choice. The extra $350 a month, invested until you are 84, grows to $142,976, but the survivor pension still to come then is worth $290,525.

Life insurance is the alternative. If you died at retirement, a lump sum of $597,560 would replace the survivor pension, and your spouse would need cover for 25 years. Taking the single-life pension and buying term life insurance makes sense only if the policy costs less than $350 a month. If you expected to live to 90 instead of 84, the answer would flip to the single-life pension, because your spouse would then have fewer years left to be paid.

Retire now or later: the break-even age

Many pensions grow if you work longer. Suppose you can retire at 62 on $1,900 a month or at 67 on $2,700, rising 2.5% a year. Waiting means giving up five years of payments, so it only pays off if you live long enough to make them up. At a 4% return the break-even age is 90, where retiring at 62 is worth $543,505 and retiring at 67 is worth $543,531.

The lower the return you could earn, the sooner waiting pays off, because the early payments you give up would have earned less. With the same pensions the break-even age is 82 at a 0% return, 85 at 2%, 87 at 3%, 90 at 4% and 95 at 5%. Health, the work you would do in the extra years and other income all belong in the decision too; the calculator gives you the money side.

Defined benefit and defined contribution pensions

A defined benefit plan, funded by the employer, promises a set monthly benefit at retirement, usually based on your pay and years of service. A defined contribution plan, such as a 401(k), promises no set benefit: what you get depends on what was paid in and how the investments did. The three choices on this page are those of a defined benefit pension.

In the United States, if a private defined benefit plan ends without enough money, the Pension Benefit Guaranty Corporation (PBGC) guarantees vested benefits up to limits set by law each year; defined contribution accounts have no such guarantee. Your own contributions are always fully vested, while employer-funded benefits may need up to 5 years of service under cliff vesting.

Pensions in Pakistan: commutation, EOBI and VPS

Many Pakistani government and company pensions let you commute part of the pension: you give up a share of the monthly amount in return for a lump sum at retirement. The Lump sum or monthly tab answers the same question. For example, a lump sum of Rs 4,000,000 against Rs 40,000 a month from 60, with an assumed 10% return and 5% yearly increases, breaks even at 71: the monthly pension wins if you live past 71. This calculator does not apply the official government pension or commutation formulas, so take the amounts from your pension office or the published rules.

Private-sector workers insured with the Employees' Old-Age Benefits Institution (EOBI) are entitled to an old-age pension over age 60, or 55 for women, once contributions have been paid for at least 15 years, under section 22 of the EOBI Act 1976. Retiring early reduces it by half a percent for each month short of that age, for life.

The Voluntary Pension System is a defined contribution scheme regulated by the SECP. Under the Income Tax Ordinance 2001, as amended up to 30 June 2026, contributions earn a tax credit on up to 20% of taxable income, and up to 50% of the balance can be taken tax-free at retirement, with the rest going into an income payment plan or an annuity.

Tips and common pension mistakes

  • Use an honest life expectancy. Your family history and health matter more than averages, and planning for a long life is the safer side to err on.
  • Use a realistic return after fees. A high assumed return always makes the lump sum look better.
  • Check whether the pension rises with prices. A fixed pension loses buying power every year, which the cost-of-living field captures.
  • Count taxes on both sides. Enter the lump sum after any tax you would pay on it.
  • Ask your plan for its official quotes before deciding: lump sum, single life, joint and survivor at each percentage, and each retirement age. Most choices cannot be undone once payments start.

How it works

Every tab turns future pension payments into one value today, using the return you could earn by investing the money yourself and the yearly cost-of-living rise of the pension. The first tab adds up the monthly pension month by month from your retirement age and finds the first age at which it is worth more than the lump sum: live longer than that and the pension wins. The second tab prices the survivor protection of a joint-and-survivor pension: how big a lump sum would replace it, the most a term-life policy can cost to make the single-life pension plus insurance the better deal, and whether investing the extra single-life income beats the survivor pension. The third tab compares retiring earlier on a smaller pension with working longer for a bigger one.

The formula

  • Monthly rate from a yearly return R: r = (1 + R)^(1/12) − 1
  • Tab 1: value of the pension for Y years = Σ P × (1 + COLA)^(whole years) ÷ (1 + r)^m, over months m = 0 … 12Y − 1; break-even = retirement age + the first Y with value ≥ lump sum
  • Tab 2: survivor lump sum = Σ 12 × joint × (1 + COLA)^j ÷ (1 + R)^j for each year of your spouse's life from your retirement
  • Tab 2: difference invested = Σ 12 × (single − joint) × (1 + COLA)^j × (1 + R)^(n − 1 − j), n = your life expectancy − retirement age + 1
  • Tab 3: break-even = the first age L at which Σ 12 × later pension × (1 + COLA)^j ÷ (1 + R)^(j + gap) ≥ Σ 12 × earlier pension × (1 + COLA)^j ÷ (1 + R)^j

A worked example

Offered $420,000 now or $2,450 a month from age 62 that rises 2% a year, with a 5% return the monthly pension's value passes $420,000 after 19 years of payments ($426,177 by then). So if you expect to live to 81 or longer the pension is the better deal; if not, the lump sum is. Spending the lump sum evenly over those 19 years would pay $2,831.75 a month.

Questions

How do I calculate my pension?

A defined benefit pension is set by your plan's own formula, usually based on your pay and years of service, so ask your plan for the amount. Once you know the amounts on offer, this calculator compares them: lump sum or monthly, single or joint, earlier or later.

Should I take the lump sum or the monthly pension?

The break-even age answers it in money terms: if you expect to live past it, the monthly pension is worth more. A lump sum gives you control and something to leave to heirs; a pension cannot run out and protects you if you live a long time.

What is pension commutation?

Commutation means exchanging part of your monthly pension for a lump sum paid at retirement. Your pension is then smaller for life. Use the Lump sum or monthly tab with the lump sum and the part of the monthly pension you would give up to see the break-even age.

What does joint and survivor mean?

A joint-and-survivor pension pays a smaller amount while you are alive and keeps paying your spouse after you die. A single-life pension pays more but stops when you die. In US plans the survivor share must be at least 50%.

Can life insurance replace a survivor pension?

Sometimes. If a term-life policy that pays out the survivor lump sum costs less each month than the difference between the two pensions, taking the single-life pension plus the policy can leave your spouse just as protected and you with more money.

Is it better to retire early or later on a bigger pension?

Retiring later pays off only if you live past the break-even age. In our example of $1,900 a month at 62 or $2,700 at 67, that age is 90 at a 4% return but 82 at 0%.

When can I get the EOBI old-age pension in Pakistan?

Over age 60 for men and 55 for women, once contributions have been paid for at least 15 years, under section 22 of the EOBI Act 1976. Retiring earlier reduces the pension by half a percent for each month short of that age.

What return should I enter?

Use what you could realistically earn on the money yourself after fees. A higher return favours the lump sum or retiring earlier; a lower one favours the monthly pension or working longer.

Is the lump sum taxed?

Here it is assumed to be rolled over into an IRA or another untaxed account. If you would cash it out, income tax and possibly a 10% early-withdrawal penalty apply in the United States, so enter the amount you would keep.

Does the pension calculator work for Pakistan government pensions?

It compares the choices once you know the amounts, in rupees if you pick Pakistani rupee. It does not work out the government pension itself from pay and service, which follows the official rules of the federal or provincial government.
