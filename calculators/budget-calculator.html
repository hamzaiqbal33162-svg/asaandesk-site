How it works

Type your pay before tax and one overall tax rate, then open each spending group and fill in what you spend. Every line can be monthly or yearly, so you can type bills the way they arrive. The calculator turns everything into yearly amounts, takes tax off your income, adds up the spending and shows what is left each month. Saving into a retirement or college plan before tax costs you less than its face value, so it is counted after tax. It also shows how much of your income before tax goes to housing and debt (the debt-to-income ratio lenders look at) and how your take-home pay splits into needs, wants and saving.

The formula

  • Yearly amount = monthly amount × 12 (yearly lines as typed); monthly = yearly ÷ 12
  • Income after tax = income before tax × (1 − tax rate)
  • Saving group = (retirement + college saving made before tax) × (1 − tax rate) + investing + other saving
  • Left over = income after tax − all spending groups
  • Debt-to-income = (mortgage + property tax + rent + home insurance + building fee + car loan + card, student and other loan payments) ÷ income before tax
  • Housing ratio = (mortgage + property tax + rent + home insurance + building fee) ÷ income before tax
  • Group share = group total ÷ income after tax

A worked example

With $83,000 a year before tax and a 28% tax rate, take-home pay is $59,760. Rent of $1,400 a month and home insurance of $200 a year are $17,000 of housing a year; with a $250 car loan and a $250 student loan, debt and housing are $23,000, or 27.71% of income before tax, which counts as good.

Questions

Why is retirement saving counted at less than I put in?

Money paid into a pension, 401(k) or IRA before tax lowers your tax bill, so it costs your take-home pay only about amount × (1 − tax rate). Counting it that way keeps the budget honest when your income is entered before tax.

What debt-to-income ratio is good?

Under 36% of income before tax is comfortable. 36% to 43% is workable but tight; 43% is the usual top limit for a mortgage. Above 50% means more than half your pay goes to housing and debt.

What is the 50/30/20 rule?

A simple guide for take-home pay: about half for needs such as housing, transport, groceries and loans, up to 30% for wants such as eating out and holidays, and at least 20% for saving and paying debt off early.

Can I type some bills per year?

Yes. Every line has its own Monthly or Yearly switch inside the box. Insurance, car servicing, gifts and holidays are often easier to enter as a yearly amount.

Which tax rate should I use?

Use all income taxes together as a share of your pay. If you only know your take-home pay, type that as the salary and set the tax rate to 0.
