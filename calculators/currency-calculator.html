What the currency calculator does

This currency calculator converts money between 166 currencies at the day's mid-market rate, the midpoint between the prices at which currencies are bought and sold. Pick an amount and two currencies, and it shows the converted amount, the same amount the other way, the value of one unit of each currency, and that amount in the popular currencies at once.

A second tab, Your own rate, converts with the rate your bank, exchange company, card or money transfer service quotes and tells you how far it is from the market rate, in percent and in money. That is the quickest way to check whether an offer is fair before you change cash or send money home.

The rates come from ExchangeRate-API's free daily feed and load each time you open the page. Pakistani rupees, US dollars, euros, pounds and the Gulf currencies are at the top of the lists, so PKR conversions such as dollar to rupee, dirham to rupee or riyal to rupee take one click.

How to use it, step by step

  • Amount: the sum you have or want, for example 100 or 2,500. Decimals are fine.
  • From and To: the currency you have and the one you want. Every currency is listed by its three-letter code and name, popular ones first.
  • Market rate tab (the default): converts at the day's mid-market rate and shows the date and time of the rates used.
  • Your own rate tab: type how much of the To currency you get for 1 unit of the From currency, as your bank or exchange shop quotes it, for example 74.50 rupees for 1 dirham.

To reverse a pair, swap the From and To currencies; the reverse line already shows the other direction for the same amount.

Understanding the results

The headline is your amount in the To currency, with the rate for one unit underneath. The lines below show the same amount converted the other way, the value of 1 unit of each currency, and when the rates are from, marked either as live rates loaded with the page or as the saved daily rates the page falls back on if the live feed cannot load. If the rates in use are more than two days old, you see a warning.

With Your own rate, two lines replace the date: the market rate for the same pair, and the gap between your rate and the market in percent, with what the amount would be at the market rate.

Three tables follow: the amount in popular currencies, the amount in every currency (collapsed), and a cross-rate grid for US dollars, euros, pounds, rupees, dirhams and riyals. The tables always use market rates, because your own rate applies to one pair only.

How to calculate a currency conversion

A conversion is the amount times the exchange rate. If 1 euro is worth 1.098 US dollars, then 250 euros is 250 × 1.098 = 274.50 dollars, and going back you divide: 250 dollars ÷ 1.098 = 227.69 euros.

When neither currency is the dollar, use a cross rate. Rate tables usually quote everything against the US dollar, so divide the rate of the currency you want by the rate of the one you have. With 3.75 Saudi riyals and 276.460596 rupees per dollar, 1 riyal is 276.460596 ÷ 3.75 = 73.72283 rupees, and 2,000 riyals is 147,445.65 rupees.

To find the rate you were given, divide what you received by what you paid: if 500 pounds bought 180,000 rupees, the rate was 180,000 ÷ 500 = 360 rupees per pound.

  • Excel or Google Sheets, with the amount in A2 and the rate in B2: the converted amount is =A2*B2 and the reverse is =A2/B2.
  • Cross rate from two dollar rates, with the From rate in C2 and the To rate in D2: =A2*D2/C2.
  • Excel for Microsoft 365 can fetch a rate with the Currencies data type (type a pair such as USD/PKR and choose Data, Currencies), and Google Sheets has the GOOGLEFINANCE function; both say their quotes may be delayed and are for information, not trading.

Mid-market rate, bank rate and the spread

Every currency has two prices at any moment: the bid, at which dealers buy it, and the ask, at which they sell it. The mid-market rate is halfway between them, and it is the rate this calculator and most rate tables show. Banks, exchange companies, card networks and transfer apps sell to you nearer the ask and buy from you nearer the bid, and the difference, the spread, is part of what they earn, sometimes on top of a fee.

You can measure the spread you are offered with Your own rate. If an exchange shop gives 74.50 rupees per dirham when the market rate is 75.27858, the gap is (74.50 ÷ 75.27858 − 1) × 100 = −1.03%. On 1,500 dirhams you would get 111,750 rupees instead of 112,917.87, so the margin costs you 1,167.87 rupees.

Official reference rates are useful for checking but are not prices you can deal at. The European Central Bank publishes euro reference rates for 29 currencies at around 16:00 CET on working days and says they are for information only. The State Bank of Pakistan publishes weighted average customer exchange rates for the rupee.

For money sent abroad, the World Bank tracks the total cost of sending the equivalent of 200 US dollars, which depends on both the fee and the exchange rate a provider applies. The United Nations Sustainable Development Goal 10.c aims to bring that cost below 3% and to remove corridors costing more than 5% by 2030.

The dirham and the riyal move against the rupee almost exactly as the dollar does, because both are pegged to the US dollar: the Central Bank of the UAE holds the dirham at 3.6725 per dollar, and the Saudi Central Bank has kept the riyal at 3.75 per dollar since 1986. So when the dollar rises in rupees, a remittance from Dubai or Riyadh is worth more rupees too.

In Pakistan, cash is changed at banks and at exchange companies licensed and listed by the State Bank of Pakistan; their open-market cash rates can differ a little from the interbank rate. Paying abroad with a Pakistani debit, credit or prepaid card also carries advance income tax under section 236Y: 0.5% of the amount for people on the Active Taxpayers List and 1% for others, cut from 5% by the Finance Act 2026, according to the FBR rate card.

For a remittance, compare the rupees the receiver actually gets from two or three services, not only the rate on the screen: type each service's rate into Your own rate, then subtract any fee.

Currencies and codes of popular countries

Each currency has a three-letter code under the international standard ISO 4217, whose list is kept by SIX, the official maintenance agency. The first two letters usually name the country and the third the currency, as in PKR for the Pakistani rupee or USD for the US dollar; the euro, shared by many countries, is EUR.

Why exchange rates move

Most major currencies, such as the euro, pound and yen, float: their price is set mostly in the market by the supply of and demand for the currency from trade, investment and travel, and central banks step in mainly to smooth sharp swings. Others are pegged, held at a fixed rate to another currency, usually the US dollar, by the central bank, as with the dirham and the riyal.

A floating rate can move by a fraction of a percent in a day and by much more over months, which is why the calculator always shows the date and time of the rates it uses and warns you when they are old. For a large exchange or a contract in a foreign currency, check a current rate on the day.

How it works

Every rate is the number of units of a currency you get for one US dollar, taken from daily mid-market rates that load when you open the page. To go from one currency to another, the calculator divides the rate of the currency you want by the rate of the one you have, so any pair works, not only pairs with the dollar. The result shows the conversion both ways, the rate for one unit, and the same amount in other currencies. Banks, cards and exchange shops quote their own rate with a margin built in; the Your own rate tab converts with that rate and shows how far it is from the market rate.

The formula

  • Cross rate: 1 unit of A = (rate of B ÷ rate of A) units of B, both rates per 1 US dollar
  • Converted amount = amount × cross rate
  • The same amount the other way = amount ÷ cross rate
  • Your own rate: converted amount = amount × your rate; gap to the market = (your rate ÷ market rate − 1) × 100%

A worked example

With the saved rates of 20 November 2026, 1 US dollar = 3.6725 UAE dirhams and 276.460596 Pakistani rupees, so 1 dirham = 276.460596 ÷ 3.6725 = 75.27858 rupees. That makes 1,500 dirhams = 1,500 × 75.27858 = 112,917.87 rupees, and 1,500 rupees = 1,500 ÷ 75.27858 = 19.92599 dirhams.

Questions

How do I calculate a currency exchange?

Multiply the amount by the exchange rate for 1 unit. At 276.46 rupees per dollar, 300 dollars is 300 × 276.46 = 82,938 rupees; to go back, divide the rupees by the same rate. The calculator does both directions at once.

How do I calculate the exchange rate I was given?

Divide the amount you received by the amount you paid. If 1,000 dirhams gave you 74,800 rupees, your rate was 74.80 rupees per dirham. Type that into Your own rate to see how far it was from the market rate.

What is the currency conversion formula in Excel or Google Sheets?

With the amount in A2 and the rate in B2, the converted amount is =A2*B2 and the reverse is =A2/B2. For a cross rate from two dollar rates, use =A2*(rate of the To currency)/(rate of the From currency).

How fresh are the rates?

When you open the page it loads the latest mid-market rates, which are updated once a day, and the result shows the date and time they are from. If they cannot load, for example when you are offline, a saved copy is used, and you will see a warning if that copy is more than two days old.

What is the dollar rate in Pakistan today?

Open the calculator with US dollar to Pakistani rupee to see the day's mid-market rate and the time it was set. Banks and exchange companies quote their own buying and selling rates around it, and the State Bank of Pakistan publishes the weighted average interbank rate each day.

Why does my bank or exchange shop give me less?

The market rate is the midpoint between buying and selling prices. Banks, cards, money transfer services and exchange shops add a margin and sometimes a fee. Type the rate they quote in the Your own rate tab to see exactly what you get and how far it is from the market rate.

Can I convert between two currencies that are not the US dollar?

Yes. Pick any two currencies, for example UAE dirhams to Pakistani rupees. The calculator works out the cross rate from both rates against the dollar, which is how most rate tables do it.

Why do some currencies, like the UAE dirham, hardly move against the dollar?

They are pegged to the US dollar by their central bank. The UAE dirham is held at 3.6725 per dollar and the Saudi riyal at 3.75 per dollar, so their rates stay the same from day to day, and their rupee rates move only as the dollar moves against the rupee.

Is this rate good for sending money home?

Use it as a guide. Remittance services and banks each set their own rate and fees, so compare the amount the receiver actually gets. The Your own rate tab makes that comparison quick.

Can I convert at a past date's rate?

No, it uses the latest daily rates only. For a historical rate, look it up in a reference table such as the European Central Bank's euro rates or the State Bank of Pakistan's published rates, then enter it in the Your own rate tab.

What is the currency of Dubai, Japan or Turkey?

Dubai, like the rest of the United Arab Emirates, uses the UAE dirham (AED). Japan uses the yen (JPY) and Turkey the Turkish lira (TRY). The table above lists the currency and code for more countries.

Can I use it to check a card or payment app conversion?

Yes. Divide the amount charged by the amount you paid to get the rate the card or app used, or read the rate it shows before you confirm, and type it into Your own rate. The gap to the market rate is the margin you paid for the conversion.
