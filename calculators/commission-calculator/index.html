What a commission calculator works out

A commission calculator finds the pay a salesperson, agent or broker earns from a sale. In its simplest form, commission is a percentage of the sale price: sell something for 18,500 at 6% and you earn 1,110. The same formula can be turned around to find the sale price you need for a target commission, or the rate you were actually paid.

Many pay plans are more complex. Commission can rise with the size of the sale in tiers, come with a fixed base amount on every deal, or pay a set bonus once sales pass a threshold. This calculator handles both: the Simple tab for one rate, and the Tiers and base pay tab for up to eight tiers with an optional base commission.

How to use the commission calculator

  • Simple tab: fill in any two of Sale price, Commission rate and Commission, and leave the third empty. The calculator works out the missing one and shows the working.
  • Tiers and base pay tab: enter the Sale price, which can be one deal or your total sales for the period your plan uses.
  • Add a base commission (optional): tick it, then enter a fixed amount or switch it to % of the whole sale.
  • How the rest is paid: One rate or amount for a single percentage or fixed sum, or Tiers by sale size.
  • Number of tiers (1 to 8): for each tier enter its top limit (Tier 1: sales up to) and its commission as a % or a fixed amount. The last tier has no limit, and each limit must be higher than the one before.
  • Currency: pick dollars, rupees, pounds or another currency; the maths is the same.

If all three Simple boxes are filled in and they do not agree, the calculator uses the sale price and the rate and warns you that the commission you typed was not used.

Tiered commission, step by step

A tiered (or graduated) commission pays a higher rate on sales above set levels, and each rate applies only to the slice of the sale inside its tier. Take an $85,000 sale on a plan of 4% up to $30,000, 6% from $30,000 to $60,000 and 8% above $60,000, with a $500 base commission per sale.

The overall rate is 6.47%, below the top rate of 8%, because only the last $25,000 earns 8%. A flat 6% on the whole sale would pay $5,100. Because the rates work like tax brackets, crossing into a higher tier never lowers your pay: on this plan a $60,000 sale earns $3,000 and a $60,001 sale earns $3,000.08.

A tier can also pay a fixed amount instead of a percentage, which suits bonus plans. With 5% up to $100,000 and a fixed $750 once sales go above it, a $120,000 sale earns $5,000 plus $750, a total of $5,750.

Types of commission pay plans

  • Commission only (straight commission): all pay comes from sales. It can pay very well, but income falls to nothing in a month without sales.
  • Base salary plus commission: a fixed salary covers the basics, and commission is added on top. Work out the commission here and add your salary to it.
  • Tiered or graduated commission: the rate rises once sales pass each level, as in the example above.
  • Base commission per deal: a fixed amount or small percentage on every sale, then a rate or tiers on top; turn on Add a base commission.
  • Draw against commission: an advance paid each period and taken back out of later commissions; the commission itself is still worked out the same way.
  • Gross margin commission: some plans pay a percentage of the profit on a sale rather than the price. Enter the profit as the sale price to use the calculator for these plans.

Real estate commission and commission after tax

A real estate commission is a percentage of the property price, agreed in the listing or buyer agreement. In the United States, the National Association of Realtors states in its settlement guidance that compensation is not set by law and is fully negotiable. When a commission is shared between agents and brokerages, work out the full amount first and then each share: on a $425,000 home, a 2.5% share is $10,625. The same steps work in Pakistan or anywhere else; for example, an agreed 1% on a Rs 25,000,000 property is Rs 250,000.

Commission is taxable pay. In the United States the IRS treats commissions as supplemental wages, and for 2026 an employer may withhold federal income tax from them at a flat 22%, or must use 37% on supplemental wages above $1 million in a year. Withholding is not the final tax: what you owe depends on your total income for the year, so a commission paycheck can look smaller than expected even when your yearly tax is lower. Use the Take-Home Paycheck Calculator to see your pay after tax.

How it works

A simple commission is the sale price times the commission rate, so any two of the three give the third. Many sales jobs pay more for bigger sales with tiers: for example 4% on the first 30,000 and 6% on everything above it. Each tier's rate is paid only on the part of the sale that falls inside that tier, the same way tax brackets work, so moving into a higher tier never lowers your pay. Some plans also pay a base commission on every sale, a fixed amount or a small percent of the whole sale, on top of the rest.

The formula

  • Commission = sale price × commission rate ÷ 100
  • Sale price = commission ÷ (commission rate ÷ 100); rate = commission ÷ sale price × 100
  • Each tier: part of the sale inside the tier = smaller of (sale, tier top) − tier start; tier commission = that part × tier rate
  • A tier paid as a fixed amount pays it once the sale reaches into that tier
  • Total = base commission + all tier commissions; overall rate = total ÷ sale price × 100

A worked example

An $85,000 sale with 4% up to $30,000, 6% from $30,000 to $60,000 and 8% above it, plus a $500 base commission: the tiers pay 30,000 × 4% = $1,200, 30,000 × 6% = $1,800 and 25,000 × 8% = $2,000. With the base the total is $5,500, an overall rate of 6.47% of the sale, not 8%.

Questions

How do I calculate commission on sales?

Multiply the sale price by the commission rate as a decimal. At 6% on $18,500 that is 18,500 × 0.06 = $1,110. For tiered plans, work out each slice of the sale at its own rate and add them up, or let the Tiers and base pay tab do it.

How do I calculate the commission percentage or rate?

Divide the commission by the sale price and multiply by 100. A $2,775 commission on a $46,250 sale is 2,775 ÷ 46,250 × 100 = 6%. In the Simple tab, type the sale and the commission and leave the rate empty.

Does the top tier rate apply to the whole sale?

No. Each rate applies only to the part of the sale inside its own tier. With 4% up to 30,000, 6% to 60,000 and 8% above, an 85,000 sale earns 8% only on the last 25,000. The breakdown table shows every part.

How do I calculate commission in Excel?

Put the sale in one cell and the rate in another (as 6%), then multiply them, for example =B1*B2. For tiers, use one row per tier with =MAX(0, MIN(sale, top) − start) × rate and add the rows; the calculator's breakdown shows the values to check against.

How is real estate commission calculated?

It is the sale price times the agreed percentage, then split between the agents or brokerages as agreed. A 2.5% share of a $425,000 sale is $10,625. Rates are negotiable, so use the rate in your own agreement.

How much tax is taken out of a commission check?

In the United States, commissions are supplemental wages, and in 2026 employers may withhold federal income tax at a flat 22% (37% above $1 million a year), plus Social Security, Medicare and any state tax. Your final tax is settled on your yearly return.

What is a base commission?

An amount paid on every sale on top of the rest, either a fixed sum or a percent of the whole sale. Turn it on in the Tiers and base pay tab and pick the money symbol or % to choose which.

Can a tier pay a fixed amount instead of a percent?

Yes. Switch that tier to the money symbol. The fixed amount is paid once the sale reaches into that tier, which suits bonus-style plans such as an extra 750 for any sale above 100,000.
