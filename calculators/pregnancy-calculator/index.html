How it works

Pick the date you know: your due date, the first day of your last period (with your usual cycle length), the date and result of an ultrasound, the conception date, or an IVF embryo transfer. Each one is turned into the same starting point, the first day of a last period for a 28-day cycle, and every other date is counted from there in whole days. Today's date then gives how far along you are, the trimester, the average size of a baby that week, and how much of the 40 weeks has passed. The schedule lists all 42 weeks with their dates.

The formula

  • Start date S from a last period: S = first day of last period + (cycle length − 28) days
  • From a due date: S = due date − 280 days; from a conception date: S = conception − 14 days
  • From an ultrasound: S = scan date − (7 × weeks + days) at the scan
  • From an IVF transfer: S = transfer date − 14 − embryo age (day 3, 5 or 6)
  • Due date = S + 280 days (40 weeks); likely conception = S + 14 days
  • Pregnancy length = today − S, in weeks and days; week number = days ÷ 7 rounded up
  • Trimesters: weeks 1-12 first, 13-27 second, 28 onward third; progress = days ÷ 280

A worked example

The last period started on June 14, 2026 and cycles are 28 days. The due date is June 14 + 280 days = March 21, 2027, and conception was likely around June 28. On November 21, 2026, 160 days have passed: 22 weeks and 6 days, so this is week 23, in the second trimester, 57% of the way to the due date. An average baby that week is about 28.9 cm (11.38 in) long and weighs about 501 g (1.1 lb).

Questions

Why are pregnancy weeks counted from my last period?

The day you conceived is hard to know, but most people know when their last period started. Doctors count 40 weeks from that day, so conception falls in about week 3. With a longer or shorter cycle than 28 days, ovulation moves later or earlier, and the dates move with it.

Which date is the most accurate?

An early ultrasound, done before about 14 weeks, is the most reliable way to date a pregnancy, and an IVF transfer date is exact. A last period works well if your cycles are regular and you are sure of the date. If a scan and your period disagree, your doctor or midwife will tell you which date to use.

Will my baby be born on the due date?

Probably not on the day itself. The due date marks 40 weeks, and most babies are born in the two weeks before or after it. From 37 weeks a baby counts as early term, from 39 weeks full term, and from 41 weeks late term.

How big is my baby this week?

The sizes shown are averages from a published table of fetal length and weight. Up to week 19 the length is measured from head to bottom, and from week 20 from head to heel, which is why it jumps at week 20. Your own baby can be bigger or smaller and still be healthy.
