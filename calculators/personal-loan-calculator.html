What is a personal loan?

A personal loan is a lump sum you borrow from a bank, credit union, finance company or online lender and repay in equal monthly installments over a fixed term, usually a few years. Most personal loans are unsecured: no house or car stands behind them, so the lender relies on your income and credit record. People use them to consolidate card debt, pay for a wedding, medical bills, home repairs, education or a large purchase.

This personal loan calculator shows the monthly payment (often called the EMI, equated monthly installment), the total you will repay, the interest, the month the loan ends and the full schedule. It also turns an origination fee and loan insurance into a real APR, so you can compare offers that look alike on the surface.

Because the payment is fixed, a personal loan is easier to plan around than a credit card, where the balance and minimum payment move every month. The trade-off is that you commit to the payment for the whole term.

How to use the personal loan calculator

  • Choose your currency at the top: dollars by default, or rupees, pounds, dirhams, riyals and others.
  • Enter the Loan amount (default 20,000), the amount you sign for.
  • Enter the Interest rate as a yearly rate on the reducing balance (default 10% a year).
  • Set the Loan term in years and, if needed, extra months (default 5 years and 0 months). A 6-month loan is 0 years and 6 months.
  • Open Dates to set the First payment date; the schedule and payoff month follow from it.
  • Open Fees and insurance to add an Origination fee as a % of the loan or an amount, choose whether The fee is taken out of the loan or paid upfront, and add Loan insurance per month if the lender charges it.

The result updates as you type. Below it is a chart of what the loan costs in all, a chart of the balance falling, and the schedule by year and by month with the interest, principal and balance of every payment.

Fees, loan insurance and the real APR

The interest rate prices the money you borrow; the annual percentage rate (APR) also counts the required fees, so it shows the full yearly cost. US lenders must disclose the APR under the Truth in Lending rules (Regulation Z), and comparing APRs is the fairest way to compare loans with different fees.

An origination fee, also called a processing or arrangement fee, is charged for setting up the loan. Taken out of the loan, it means you receive less cash but still repay the whole amount. On the default loan, a 5% fee leaves you 19,000 and lifts the APR from 10% to 12.239%. Paying the same fee upfront gives the same APR, because either way the cash you keep is the loan minus the fee.

Loan insurance (often credit life or payment protection) is a monthly charge on top of the installment. On the default loan, 15 a month adds 900 over 5 years and lifts the APR to 11.509%. With the 5% fee as well, the cost of the loan is 7,396.45 and the APR 13.781%.

In the US, a lender must give you a disclosure before you sign a closed-end loan such as a personal loan. Regulation Z, section 1026.18, lists what it must show, and each item lines up with a number in this calculator, so you can check the offer line by line.

Worked examples: consolidating card debt and a loan in rupees

Paying off a credit card. The Federal Reserve's November 2026 G.19 release puts the average rate on a 24-month bank personal loan at 11.90% and on credit card accounts that pay interest at 22.36% (third quarter of 2026, preliminary). A 20,000 balance repaid in equal payments over 5 years at 22.36% costs 556.48 a month and 13,388.78 of interest. A 5-year personal loan at 11.90% costs 443.88 a month and 6,632.74 of interest, about half. Over 2 years at 11.90% the payment is 940.54 and the interest only 2,572.86.

A personal loan in Pakistan. A Rs 1,000,000 loan over 3 years at an example rate of 24% a year costs Rs 39,232.85 a month, Rs 1,412,382.69 in all and Rs 412,382.69 of interest. Add a Rs 5,000 processing fee paid upfront and Rs 1,000 a month of insurance, and the cost of the loan rises to Rs 453,382.69, with Rs 40,232.85 going out every month. Use your bank's quoted rate and charges instead of these examples.

Personal loans in Pakistan: eligibility, salary and rates

How much a Pakistani bank will lend depends mainly on your salary. The State Bank of Pakistan's consumer financing rules cap the debt burden ratio: since September 2021, all your consumer loan installments together may not exceed 40% of your net disposable income (take-home pay). Banks can set a lower limit and add their own salary, age and job-length rules.

To estimate your limit, take 40% of your net monthly salary and subtract the installments you already pay. With a Rs 200,000 salary and a Rs 30,000 car installment, that leaves up to Rs 50,000 a month for a new loan. At an example rate of 24% over 3 years, Rs 50,000 a month repays a loan of about Rs 1,274,442 (worked out on the present value tab of our finance calculator).

Bank rates generally move with the State Bank's policy rate, which was raised to 12% from 27 October 2026, so a quote from last year may be out of date.

Watch for flat rates. A flat rate charges interest on the full original amount for the whole term, while this calculator, like most lenders' statements, charges interest on the reducing balance. A Rs 1,000,000 loan at a 15% flat rate for 3 years means Rs 450,000 of interest and Rs 40,277.78 a month; on a reducing balance that payment equals about 25.976% a year. Ask the bank for the rate on the reducing balance, or the APR, before comparing.

What lenders look at, and how to pay less

  • Your credit record and score: on-time payments and low card balances usually mean a lower rate.
  • Income and job: lenders want steady income that covers the new payment, and may ask how long you have been in your job.
  • Debt-to-income ratio: your monthly debt payments, including the new loan, divided by your income. Check yours with the Debt-to-Income Ratio Calculator.
  • The amount and term: smaller, shorter loans are lower risk for the lender and may get better rates.
  • A co-signer or collateral: a co-signer with strong credit, or a secured loan against savings or a vehicle, can help you qualify, but the co-signer becomes responsible if you do not pay.
  • Compare at least three offers by APR, not by the monthly payment.
  • Borrow only what you need. A fee charged as a % of the loan grows with the amount.
  • Pick the shortest term whose payment you can comfortably afford.
  • Pay on time, every time; set up automatic payments if your bank offers them.
  • If you might repay early, ask about prepayment charges first, then use the Amortization Calculator to see what extra payments save.

Alternatives to a personal loan

  • A 0% or low introductory rate card for a balance you can clear before the offer ends.
  • A home equity loan or line of credit, which usually costs less because your home secures it, but you can lose the home if you cannot repay.
  • Borrowing from family, with the amount and repayment dates written down so nobody is surprised later.
  • Asking the hospital, school or seller for a payment plan, which may carry no interest.
  • Avoid payday loans. The CFPB describes them as short-term, high-cost loans, generally of 500 or less and due on your next payday, with fees under many state laws of 10 to 30 for every 100 borrowed.

How it works

Enter the loan amount, the yearly interest rate and the term in years and months. The calculator finds the fixed monthly payment, the total you repay, the interest, the payoff month and a full yearly and monthly schedule from your first payment date. Many lenders also charge an origination (processing) fee, either taken out of the loan (you get less cash but repay the whole amount) or paid upfront, and some add monthly loan insurance. Add them under Fees and insurance: the payment stays the same, but the cost of the loan and its real APR go up. The APR is the yearly rate at which your payments are worth exactly the cash you end up with.

The formula

  • Monthly rate r = yearly rate ÷ 100 ÷ 12; payments n = years × 12 + months
  • Monthly payment P = L × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n); at 0% interest P = L ÷ n
  • Fee = L × fee % ÷ 100, or the amount you type; cash received = L − fee when it is taken out of the loan
  • Cost of the loan = total interest + fee + insurance × n
  • Real APR = 12 × x, where x solves L − fee = (P + insurance) × (1 − (1 + x)^−n) ÷ x

A worked example

A 20,000 loan at 10% for 5 years: r = 0.10 ÷ 12 and n = 60, so P = 424.94 a month and 5,496.45 of interest in all. With a 5% fee taken out of the loan you receive 19,000 but still repay 60 × 424.94, so the real APR is the x that makes those payments worth 19,000: 12.239%.

Questions

How do I calculate a personal loan EMI?

Use EMI = L × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n), where L is the loan, r the yearly rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100 and n the number of months. For 20,000 at 10% over 60 months the EMI is 424.94. The calculator does this and shows the whole schedule.

How do I calculate personal loan interest?

Multiply the monthly payment by the number of payments and subtract the loan amount. For 424.94 × 60 on a 20,000 loan that is 5,496.45. Each month's interest is the balance still owed × the monthly rate, so it falls as you repay.

How do I calculate personal loan eligibility based on salary in Pakistan?

State Bank rules cap all consumer loan installments at 40% of your net disposable income. Take 40% of your take-home pay, subtract the installments you already pay, and the rest is the most a new installment can be; your bank may set a lower limit.

What is a good personal loan interest rate?

Anything below what you would otherwise pay, such as a credit card rate, and close to the market average for your credit. In the US, the Federal Reserve's average for a 24-month bank personal loan was 11.90% in the third quarter of 2026 (preliminary). Rates in Pakistan move with the State Bank's policy rate.

Why is the APR higher than my interest rate?

The interest rate only prices the money you borrow. The APR also counts the fee (and any loan insurance), so it shows the full yearly cost. With a fee taken out of the loan you repay the whole amount but receive less cash, which is the same as borrowing at a higher rate.

Is it better to have the fee taken out of the loan or to pay it upfront?

For the same fee the APR is the same either way, because in both cases the cash you keep is the loan minus the fee. Taking it out of the loan means you need no cash on day one; paying upfront means the full amount reaches your account.

Does this personal loan calculator show an amortization schedule?

Yes. It shows a yearly and a monthly schedule with the dates, interest, principal and balance after every payment, starting from the first payment date you set under Dates.

Can I add extra payments?

Not on this page, which keeps the installment fixed. To see how extra monthly, yearly or one-time payments shorten a loan and cut the interest, use the amortization calculator with the same amount, rate and term.

What is the difference between a flat rate and a reducing balance rate?

A flat rate charges interest on the original amount for the whole term, even as you repay it; a reducing balance rate charges only on what you still owe. A 15% flat rate over 3 years costs about the same as 26% on a reducing balance, so always compare on the same basis.

How long can a personal loan be?

Lenders set their own limits, and terms of a few years are common. This calculator accepts terms up to 30 years and any number of extra months, but a longer term always means more interest: 20,000 at 10% costs 5,496.45 over 5 years and 11,716.18 over 10.

Why does loan insurance push the APR up so much?

A monthly insurance charge is paid on top of every installment, so over a few years it can add up to a large share of the cost. The APR here counts it as a cost of borrowing. If the insurance is optional, leave it at 0 to see the loan alone.

Can I use it for a loan in my own currency?

Yes. Pick the currency symbol at the top. The maths is the same everywhere; check that your lender quotes a yearly rate on the reducing balance, because a flat rate charged on the original amount costs much more than the same rate on the balance.
