What the interest rate calculator finds

This interest rate calculator works out the yearly rate a loan really charges when you know only three things: how much you borrow, how long you pay, and the fixed monthly payment. It is the reverse of a loan calculator. Instead of turning a rate into a payment, it turns a payment into a rate.

That is useful whenever a seller or lender talks in instalments instead of rates. A car dealer quotes a monthly figure, a shop offers a phone for 12 easy payments, a friend proposes a repayment plan, or a bank shows the instalment but not the yearly cost. Type the numbers in, and the calculator shows the rate hidden inside them, the total interest and how the balance falls year by year.

An interest rate is the price of borrowing money, stated as a percentage of the amount borrowed for each year. A higher rate means each payment carries more interest and less repayment of the loan itself, so comparing rates is the fairest way to compare two loans of different sizes or lengths.

How to use the interest rate calculator

  • Currency: choose it at the top. The maths is the same in rupees, dollars or any other currency.
  • Amount borrowed: the cash you actually receive, or the cash price of the item you are buying on instalments. The default is 20,000.
  • Loan length: the number of years or months you will pay, with a switch for the unit. The length must come to a whole number of months, so type 4.5 years as 54 months. The default is 5 years.
  • Monthly payment: the fixed amount you pay every month, 380 by default.

The result appears as you type. If the payments add up to exactly the amount borrowed, the rate is 0%. If they add up to less, the rate comes out negative and the calculator warns you, because that usually means a number was mistyped. Rates above 1,000% a year are refused.

How the rate is worked out

A fixed-payment loan follows the amortization formula: loan = payment × (1 − (1 + i)^−n) ÷ i, where i is the monthly rate and n is the number of payments. Given a rate, the payment is easy to find. Going the other way is not, because i sits both inside a power and under the division, so there is no way to rearrange the formula into rate = something.

The calculator therefore searches. It tries a rate, checks whether the payments would clear the loan, and halves the range of possible rates again and again until the answer is exact to many decimal places. You can check the default answer yourself: at 0.44% a month, 380 × (1 − 1.0044^−60) ÷ 0.0044 comes to about 20,000 (20,000.23 with the rate rounded to 0.44%).

Small changes in the payment move the rate a lot, which is why it pays to check. The table keeps the default 20,000 over 5 years and changes only the payment.

Flat rate or real rate? Spotting hidden interest

Instalment offers are often described with a flat rate: the total interest divided by the amount borrowed and the number of years. The phone above costs $120 extra on $1,200 over one year, which sounds like 10%. But you do not owe the full $1,200 for the whole year; you pay part of it back every month. Measured on the balance you actually owe, the rate is 17.972%.

The same gap appears in the rupee loan. Rs 148,000 of interest on Rs 500,000 over 2 years is a flat 14.8% a year, yet the real rate is 26.249%. In both examples the real rate is about 1.8 times the flat rate, because on average you owe only a little over half the loan during its life. Always compare loans on the real rate this calculator gives, not on the flat figure.

Fees and cash discounts hide interest too. If the 20,000 loan charges a 600 fee taken out at the start, you only receive 19,400; enter that as the amount borrowed and the rate rises from 5.280% to 6.546%. A 0% plan of 12 payments of 100 is not free if the shop would sell the item for 1,100 in cash: enter 1,100 and the hidden rate is 16.376%.

Interest rate, APR and APY: what the numbers mean

  • Interest rate: the yearly percentage charged on the balance. A loan's quoted rate is normally the monthly rate × 12, which is what the headline shows.
  • APR (annual percentage rate): the CFPB describes it as a broader measure that includes the interest rate plus certain fees, such as points and some charges. With no fees the APR equals the interest rate; with fees, enter the amount you really receive to get close to it.
  • APY or effective rate: the yearly cost with compounding, shown here as Real yearly cost. Savings accounts in the US advertise APY; loans quote the lower nominal rate.
  • Simple or compound: simple interest is charged on the original amount only, compound interest on the balance including earlier interest. A level-payment loan charges each month's interest on the balance still owed.
  • Fixed or variable: a fixed rate stays the same for the whole loan, so the payment does not change. A variable rate moves with a benchmark, so this calculator then shows only the rate implied by today's payment.

What sets interest rates

The starting point is the central bank. In the US, the Federal Reserve sets a target range for the federal funds rate; its latest change, on 17 September 2026, put the range at 3.75% to 4.00%. In Pakistan, the State Bank of Pakistan sets the policy rate; after a long run of cuts from a peak of 22%, it stood at 12% from 27 October 2026. Banks price their loans and deposits above or below these rates, so check the latest decision before relying on these figures.

Inflation matters next. Lenders want to be paid back in money that still buys something, so the real interest rate is roughly the quoted rate minus inflation. A 7% loan when prices rise 3% a year costs about 4% in real terms.

Then come your own numbers. A stronger credit record, a secured loan (backed by a car, house or deposit), a larger down payment and a shorter term usually earn a lower rate. Shopping around matters too: the same borrower can be offered noticeably different rates by different lenders, and this calculator makes their instalment quotes easy to compare.

Tips and related calculators

  • Compare offers by rate, not by monthly payment: a longer loan always has a lower payment, even at a higher rate.
  • Enter the cash you actually receive after fees, or the cash price of the item, to see the true cost.
  • Check the length unit: 60 typed as years instead of months gives a very different answer.
  • For loans with a balloon payment, irregular payments or changing rates, the result is only an estimate, because the formula assumes equal monthly payments that clear the loan exactly.
  • In Excel or Google Sheets, =RATE(60, -380, 20000) × 12 gives the same 5.28% as the default here.

How it works

Enter the amount you borrow, how long you will pay (in years or months) and the fixed monthly payment. A level-payment loan has one interest rate that makes those payments clear the loan exactly at the end; there is no simple formula for it, so the calculator searches for that monthly rate to full precision and shows it as a yearly rate (the monthly rate times 12, as loans quote it). It also shows the yearly cost with compounding, the total you pay and the balance year by year.

The formula

  • Loan = payment × (1 − (1 + i)^−n) ÷ i, where i is the monthly rate and n the number of payments
  • The calculator solves this for i by narrowing the range of possible rates until it is exact
  • Yearly rate (APR) = 12 × i; real yearly cost (APY) = (1 + i)^12 − 1
  • Total paid = payment × n; total interest = total paid − loan

A worked example

Borrow $25,000 for a car and pay $495 a month for 60 months. The 60 payments total $29,700, so the interest is $4,700. The monthly rate that makes 495 × (1 − (1 + i)^−60) ÷ i equal 25,000 is i = 0.5831%, which is 12 × 0.5831% = 6.997% a year (7.226% once the monthly compounding is counted).

Questions

How do I calculate the interest rate on a loan?

You need the amount borrowed, the number of monthly payments and the payment. The rate is the one that makes payment × (1 − (1 + i)^−n) ÷ i equal the loan, which has to be found by trial; the calculator does that search for you. For 20,000 over 5 years at 380 a month it is 5.280% a year.

How do I calculate the interest rate per month?

Divide the yearly rate by 12: 6% a year is 0.5% a month. If you only know the payments, this calculator shows the monthly rate directly, for example 0.4400% for the default loan.

How do I convert a monthly interest rate to a yearly rate?

Multiply by 12 for the quoted yearly rate (2% a month is 24% a year). For the true yearly cost with compounding, use (1 + monthly rate)^12 − 1, so 2% a month costs 26.82% over a year.

How do I calculate the interest rate on a car loan?

Enter the amount financed (price minus down payment and trade-in), the number of months and the monthly payment. A $25,000 loan paid at $495 a month for 60 months works out at 6.997% a year.

What is the interest rate formula in Excel?

Use =RATE(number of payments, -payment, amount borrowed) for the monthly rate, then multiply by 12. =RATE(60, -380, 20000) × 12 returns 5.28%, the same as this calculator.

Why can't the rate be worked out with one formula?

The rate appears both inside a power and as a divisor in the payment formula, so it cannot be moved to one side by algebra. The calculator tries rates and narrows the range step by step (bisection) until the payments match your loan to many decimal places.

Is the result the same as the APR on my loan papers?

It is the APR if there are no fees. Lenders must include certain fees in the APR, which makes it higher than the plain interest rate. To include fees, enter the amount you actually receive as the amount borrowed: the rate you get is then close to the true cost.

What is the real yearly cost line?

Loans quote the monthly rate times 12. Because unpaid interest builds on itself each month, the true yearly cost is a little higher: 6% a year charged monthly costs 6.168% over a full year.

Why do I get a negative rate?

If your payments add up to less than the amount you borrow, the only rate that fits is below zero. That usually means a payment or the length was typed wrong; a real lender would not agree to it.

Can I use it for 0% offers and buy-now-pay-later plans?

Yes. If the payments add up exactly to the price, the rate is 0%. If a 0% plan costs more than paying cash, enter the cash price as the amount borrowed to see the hidden rate.

Can I use it for a fixed deposit or savings account?

Not directly, because it is built for loans repaid in equal monthly payments. For a deposit that grows to a maturity amount, use the CD calculator or the compound interest calculator instead.

What is the interest rate in Pakistan today?

The benchmark is the State Bank of Pakistan's policy rate, which is 12% from 27 October 2026 (it was cut from a peak of 22% and is reviewed at each monetary policy meeting). Bank loans are priced above it and deposits below it, and it changes at each monetary policy meeting, so check the latest SBP decision.
