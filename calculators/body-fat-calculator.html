How it works

Measure your neck, your waist (and your hips if you are a woman) with a soft tape, and enter your height, weight, age and sex. The U.S. Navy method turns the tape measurements and your height into an estimate of body density, and the Siri equation turns density into a body fat percentage. A second estimate comes from your BMI, age and sex. The category bands are the ones the American Council on Exercise uses, and the ideal value for your age comes from the Jackson and Pollock table.

The formula

  • Men: body fat % = 495 ÷ (1.0324 − 0.19077 × log10(waist − neck) + 0.15456 × log10(height)) − 450
  • Women: body fat % = 495 ÷ (1.29579 − 0.35004 × log10(waist + hip − neck) + 0.22100 × log10(height)) − 450
  • All lengths in centimetres (U.S. Navy method, Hodgdon and Beckett, Naval Health Research Center, 1984)
  • BMI method, adults: body fat % = 1.20 × BMI + 0.23 × age − 10.8 × sex − 5.4 (sex: 1 for men, 0 for women)
  • BMI method, under 18: body fat % = 1.51 × BMI − 0.70 × age − 3.6 × sex + 1.4
  • Fat mass = weight × body fat % ÷ 100; lean mass = weight − fat mass

A worked example

A man 178 cm tall with a 39 cm neck and an 86 cm waist: waist − neck = 47, log10(47) = 1.672 and log10(178) = 2.250, so body fat = 495 ÷ (1.0324 − 0.19077 × 1.672 + 0.15456 × 2.250) − 450 ≈ 16.4%, in the Fitness band for men. At 75 kg that is about 12.3 kg of fat and 62.7 kg of lean mass.

Questions

How do I measure my neck, waist and hips?

Use a soft tape, snug but not pressing into the skin, and breathe out normally. Neck: just below the voice box. Waist: men at the navel, women at the narrowest point. Hips: around the widest part of the buttocks. Measure twice and use the average.

How accurate is the tape method?

It is an estimate. For most people it lands within a few percentage points of a lab scan, but it can be further off for very muscular people or when the tape is placed differently each time. Use the same method and the same spots to track changes over time.

Why are the two results different?

The tape method uses your body shape, while the BMI method uses only height, weight, age and sex, so it cannot tell muscle from fat. When they disagree, the tape result is usually closer for active people.

What is a healthy body fat percentage?

It depends on sex and age. The American Council on Exercise puts average men at 18 to 24% and average women at 25 to 31%; athletes are lower. The ideal value shown here rises slowly with age. Ask a doctor about your own target.
