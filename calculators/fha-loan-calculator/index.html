What is an FHA loan, and what does this FHA loan calculator show?

An FHA loan is a US home loan insured by the Federal Housing Administration. The FHA does not lend money itself: it insures mortgages made by FHA-approved lenders, so the lender is protected if the borrower stops paying. Congress created the FHA in 1934, and in 1965 it was placed within HUD, the federal housing department. Because of that insurance, FHA loans accept small down payments, from 3.5% of the price, and lower credit scores than many other mortgages.

The borrower pays for the insurance in two ways: an upfront mortgage insurance premium (UFMIP) of 1.75% of the base loan, usually added to the loan, and an annual premium (MIP) paid monthly with the mortgage. This FHA loan calculator works out both with HUD's own rules, adds them to the principal and interest, and, if you want, adds property tax, home insurance, HOA fees and other costs to show the full monthly payment. It also builds the yearly and monthly schedule, shows what extra payments save, and compares paying every two weeks.

How to use the FHA loan calculator

  • Home price: the purchase price of the home.
  • Down payment: switch between % and $; FHA asks for at least 3.5% down. The base loan is the price minus this amount.
  • Loan term (years) and Interest rate (% a year). FHA purchase loans are usually 15 or 30 years.
  • FHA mortgage insurance (optional): the Upfront premium (UFMIP), 1.75% of the base loan by default; the Annual premium (MIP), left blank to use the HUD rate for your loan; Annual premium is paid, which defaults to HUD's rule (life of the loan, or 11 years with 10% or more down) but can be set to the whole loan, 11 years, 5 years, until the balance is 78% of the price, or not at all; and Monthly premium is worked out, either on each year's average balance (HUD) or on the starting loan, the same every month.
  • Taxes and costs (optional): tick Include property tax, insurance and other home costs, then enter Property tax per year (1.2% of the price to start), Home insurance per year ($1,500), HOA or building fee per year and Other costs per year, each as a % of the price or in dollars.
  • Yearly cost increases (optional): how fast property tax, insurance, HOA fees and other costs rise each year.
  • Extra payments (optional): Extra every month, Extra every year, a One-time extra payment, and More one-time payments as a list such as 5000 2028-06; 2500 2030-01.
  • Dates and comparison (optional): the First payment date, and Compare with paying every two weeks.

The calculator warns you when the down payment is under 3.5%, when the base loan is over the 2026 FHA limit for most counties or for any county, and when the term is longer than 30 years.

All amounts are in US dollars, because FHA is a US program, and the result updates as you type.

FHA mortgage insurance: UFMIP and the 2026 annual MIP rates

HUD Mortgagee Letter 2023-05, which still sets the rates in 2026, charges every FHA purchase loan an upfront premium of 1.75% (175 basis points) of the base loan amount. The annual premium depends on the loan term, the base loan amount and the loan-to-value ratio (LTV), which is the base loan divided by the price. A 3.5% down payment is an LTV of 96.5%; 10% down is 90%.

How HUD works out the monthly premium. Each loan year, HUD takes the average of the 12 scheduled balances for that year, multiplies it by the annual rate, divides by 1 plus the upfront factor when the UFMIP is financed, and divides by 12. That is why the monthly MIP falls a little every year. In the main example, the first-year premium is $153.98 a month, against $154.80 on the simple method (0.55% × $337,750 ÷ 12). Over 30 years, HUD's method gives $36,159.36 of MIP, while a flat amount every month would add up to $55,728.75.

The upfront premium is charged on the base loan, not the price. On the $350,000 home with 3.5% down, it is 1.75% of $337,750, or $5,910.63. Most borrowers add it to the loan. Paying it in cash instead (set the UFMIP field to 0 and keep the $5,910.63 aside for closing) lowers the payment from $2,115.98 to $2,079.58 and total interest from $418,091.30 to $410,900.54: financing the premium costs $7,190.76 of extra interest over 30 years on top of the premium itself.

These rates come from Mortgagee Letter 2023-05, which lowered the annual premium for FHA forward mortgages endorsed on or after 20 March 2023 and tied the loan-size line in the table to the national conforming loan limit. HUD reviews premium levels from time to time based on the health of its Mutual Mortgage Insurance Fund and the housing market, so check the current rate if your loan closes later.

Because the premium follows the falling balance, it shrinks slowly over a long loan rather than stopping when you reach 20% equity; the worked examples below show by how much.

Added up, the insurance in the main example costs $5,910.63 up front plus $36,159.36 of annual premiums over 30 years, a little over $42,000, which is the price of buying with 3.5% down instead of 20%.

FHA loan requirements and who qualifies

Credit score and down payment. Under HUD's rules, a minimum decision credit score of 580 or higher qualifies for maximum financing, which for an owner-occupant purchase is a 96.5% LTV (3.5% down). A score from 500 to 579 limits the loan to a 90% LTV, so at least 10% down. HUD Mortgagee Letter 2026-11 adds FICO 10T and VantageScore 4.0 alongside Classic FICO for case numbers assigned from 1 January 2027, but keeps these score limits unchanged.

Debt-to-income ratios. FHA loans are scored through FHA's TOTAL Mortgage Scorecard. When a loan is manually underwritten, HUD sets the highest housing and total debt ratios (housing payment ÷ income before tax, and all monthly debts ÷ income before tax) by credit score and compensating factors:

Maximum ratios for manually underwritten FHA loans (HUD Handbook 4000.1, as updated by ML 2026-11)
Credit scoreHousing / total debtWhat is needed
500 to 579, or no score31% / 43%No higher ratios allowed (33% / 45% for an energy-efficient home)
580 and above31% / 43%No compensating factors (33% / 45% for an energy-efficient home)
580 and above37% / 47%One of: verified cash reserves, a minimal increase in housing payment, or residual income
580 and above40% / 40%No discretionary debt
580 and above40% / 50%Two of: cash reserves, minimal payment increase, significant extra income not counted, residual income

In plain words, cash reserves are savings you still have after closing, a minimal increase in housing payment means the new payment is not much higher than what you pay for housing now, and residual income is what is left each month after taxes, debts and the new housing payment. Discretionary debt means debts such as credit cards that you pay off in full each month or do not carry at all.

Other rules that affect the cost. FHA loans must stay within the county loan limit, and the home must meet FHA's property standards. Sellers and other interested parties may pay up to 6% of the sales price toward your origination fees, other closing costs, prepaid items and discount points; anything above 6% reduces the price used for the loan. FHA loans can be prepaid in whole or in part at any time with no prepayment charge, under HUD's regulations (24 CFR 203.22).

FHA loan limits for 2026

FHA sets a maximum loan for every county, based on local home prices and the national conforming loan limit. For case numbers assigned from 1 January 2026 (HUD Mortgagee Letter 2025-23), the limits range between a floor for low-cost areas and a ceiling for high-cost areas. Alaska, Hawaii, Guam and the US Virgin Islands have higher limits to allow for building costs. HUD sets the one-unit ceiling at 150% of the national conforming loan limit that the Federal Housing Finance Agency publishes for Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac.

Whether you are buying in Florida, Texas, California, Ohio or Pennsylvania, the limit that counts is your county's, which can be anywhere between the floor and the ceiling. Look it up with HUD's FHA mortgage limits tool before you choose a price; the calculator warns you when the base loan is above $541,287 and blocks nothing, so you can still see the numbers.

The limit caps the base loan, so it also caps the price you can buy with the minimum down payment. In a county at the $541,287 floor, the highest price with 3.5% down is about $560,919 ($541,287 ÷ 0.965); at the $1,249,125 ceiling it is about $1,294,430. With a bigger down payment you can buy above those prices while keeping the base loan within the limit.

Worked examples

3.5% down on a $350,000 home. The base loan is $337,750, the loan with UFMIP is $343,660.63 and the payment at 6.25% for 30 years is $2,115.98. MIP starts at $153.98 a month and runs for the whole loan, adding $36,159.36 in total. With 1.2% property tax and $1,500 of insurance, the full monthly cost is $2,744.96, and total interest over 30 years is $418,091.30.

10% down on the same home. The base loan falls to $315,000, the payment to $1,973.45 and the MIP rate to 0.50%, starting at $130.55 a month. Because the LTV is 90%, MIP ends after 11 years (132 payments) and costs $15,958.20 in total, less than half of the 3.5% down case.

5% down on the same home. The base loan is $332,500, an LTV of exactly 95%, so the rate is 0.50% instead of 0.55%: the first monthly premium is $137.81 and the payment $2,083.09. MIP still runs for the life of the loan, because the LTV is above 90%, and adds up to $32,361.12.

A 15-year loan with 10% down. The payment rises to $2,748.15, but MIP drops to 0.15% ($38.63 in the first month, $3,743.40 in total) and total interest is $174,154.04, against $389,929.71 for the 30-year loan with the same down payment. The trade-off is a payment $774.70 a month higher than the 30-year loan with the same down payment.

A larger loan. An $800,000 home with 5% down has a base loan of $760,000, over the $726,200 line in HUD's table, so the MIP rate is 0.70% ($440.98 in the first month). That base loan is above the floor limit, so it is only possible in a county with a higher limit.

Extra payments and paying every two weeks. On the 3.5% down example, $200 extra every month clears the loan in 23 years 10 months, saves $100,977.50 of interest and cuts total MIP to $33,824.64. Paying half the payment ($1,057.99) every two weeks instead saves $92,887.57 of interest and pays the loan off in about 24.3 years.

How to calculate the FHA loan amount by hand. Start with the price and take off the down payment: $350,000 − $12,250 = $337,750, the base loan. Then add the upfront premium: $337,750 × 1.0175 = $343,660.63. That total is the loan the payment is worked out on.

How much can I afford, and the steps to an FHA loan

Start from the full monthly cost, not just principal and interest. In the main example that is $2,744.96 a month with MIP, tax and insurance. At the basic 31% housing ratio, it needs a gross income of about $8,855 a month, or $106,257 a year. A household earning $96,000 a year ($8,000 a month) would have a housing ratio of 34.31%, and with a $300 car payment a total debt ratio of 38.06%: within the 37% / 47% tier, so it would need one compensating factor, such as cash reserves, if the loan were manually underwritten.

To find a price that fits, lower the Home price until Monthly with MIP and costs is about 31% of your monthly income before tax, or use the house affordability calculator, which works backwards from income and debts. Remember that taxes and insurance differ a lot by county and state.

Once the numbers work, the process follows a set order. Each step is a chance to check the figures from this calculator against real quotes.

  • Check your credit score and savings: 580 or more opens the 3.5% minimum, and you also need cash for closing costs unless the seller pays part of them.
  • Get pre-approved by one or more FHA-approved lenders and confirm your county limit.
  • After you apply, each lender must give you a Loan Estimate within three business days, showing the estimated rate, monthly payment and total closing costs on the same standard form, which makes offers easy to compare.
  • Make an offer, and have the home appraised; FHA loans also require the property to meet FHA standards.
  • At least three business days before closing, the lender must give you a Closing Disclosure with the final loan terms, the projected monthly payments and the closing costs. Compare it with your Loan Estimate and with this calculator.
  • Close the loan. The upfront premium is usually added to the loan at this point, and the annual premium starts with your first payment.

FHA vs conventional loans, and other FHA options

The biggest difference is mortgage insurance. On a conventional loan, private mortgage insurance (PMI) can be cancelled on request when the balance is scheduled to reach 80% of the home's original value, and it must end automatically at 78%, or at the midpoint of the loan, under the rules the CFPB describes. FHA MIP follows HUD's table instead: with less than 10% down, it lasts for the life of the loan. Many FHA borrowers later refinance into a conventional loan to drop it once they have enough equity. Select Until the balance is 78% of the price under Annual premium is paid to see what a PMI-style end date would cost; in the main example MIP would stop after 145 payments and total $20,448.36.

FHA also insures other kinds of home loans. A 203(k) loan finances the purchase or refinance of a home together with its repairs: the Limited 203(k) allows up to $75,000 of rehabilitation costs (raised from $35,000 by Mortgagee Letter 2024-13, for case numbers from 4 November 2024), while the Standard 203(k) needs at least $5,000 of repairs and a HUD-approved 203(k) consultant. FHA also offers streamline refinances for existing FHA borrowers. This calculator covers standard purchase loans; for a refinance, use the refinance calculator.

Tips, related calculators and home loans outside the US

  • Charge the 1.75% upfront premium on the base loan, not on the home price.
  • Include MIP, property tax and insurance in the monthly figure you compare with your income.
  • Compare 3.5% down with 10% down: the extra 6.5% can end MIP after 11 years instead of 30.
  • Check your county's 2026 loan limit before shopping, especially in high-cost areas.
  • If your credit score is just under 580, raising it before you apply can cut the minimum down payment from 10% to 3.5%: on a $350,000 home that is $12,250 instead of $35,000.
  • Unless you put at least 10% down, plan as if the annual premium will be paid until you sell or refinance.
  • Ask sellers for closing-cost help within the 6% limit, and compare Loan Estimates from several FHA lenders.

How it works

Type the home price, your down payment (as a % or in dollars), the loan term and the interest rate. The base loan is the price minus the down payment. HUD adds an upfront premium of 1.75% of that base loan to the loan itself, so you borrow a little more than the base loan. The monthly payment is the level payment that clears this loan by the end of the term. On top of it comes the annual mortgage insurance premium (MIP): its yearly rate comes from HUD's table for your loan size, down payment and term, and it is split into monthly amounts. With less than 10% down it runs for the life of the loan; with 10% or more it stops after 11 years. Property tax, insurance, HOA and other costs, extra payments and the every-two-weeks comparison work as in our mortgage calculator.

The formula

  • Base loan B = home price − down payment
  • Upfront premium UFMIP = 1.75% × B; loan L = B + UFMIP
  • Monthly payment M = L × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n), with r = yearly rate ÷ 12 and n = years × 12
  • Annual MIP rate (HUD, term over 15 years): 0.55% with under 5% down, 0.50% with 5% or more (0.75% and 0.70% for base loans over $726,200)
  • Annual MIP rate (term of 15 years or less): 0.40% with under 10% down, 0.15% with 10% or more (larger loans: 0.65%, 0.40% or 0.15%)
  • HUD way: monthly MIP for a loan year = average of that year's 12 scheduled balances × rate ÷ (1 + 1.75%) ÷ 12
  • Simple way: monthly MIP = rate × B ÷ 12, the same every month

A worked example

A $350,000 home with 3.5% down has a base loan of $337,750. The upfront premium is 1.75% of that, $5,910.63, so the loan is $343,660.63. At 6.25% for 30 years the payment is $2,115.98 a month. The annual premium rate is 0.55% (under 5% down, term over 15 years), which is $153.98 in the first month on HUD's average-balance method. With property tax at 1.2% of the price ($350 a month) and $1,500 a year of home insurance ($125 a month), the full monthly cost is $2,744.96.

Questions

How do I calculate an FHA mortgage payment?

Take the price minus your down payment (the base loan), add 1.75% of it for the upfront premium, and work out the level payment on that loan for your rate and term. Then add the annual MIP (for example 0.55% of the balance a year, divided by 12) and your property tax and insurance. The calculator does all of this with HUD's tables.

How do I calculate FHA mortgage insurance?

The upfront premium is 1.75% of the base loan. The annual premium is the HUD rate for your term, loan size and down payment, applied each year to that year's average balance, divided by 1.0175 when the upfront premium is financed, then by 12. On a $337,750 base loan at 0.55% that is $153.98 a month in the first year.

Is the upfront premium charged on the home price or on the loan?

On the loan. HUD charges 1.75% of the base loan amount, which is the price minus your down payment. With 3.5% down on a $500,000 home that is 1.75% of $482,500, or $8,443.75, not 1.75% of the price. Some calculators use the price and overstate it.

How long do I pay the annual premium?

For FHA loans started since June 2013, the annual premium lasts for the life of the loan when you put down less than 10%, and for 11 years when you put down 10% or more. You can pick another period under FHA mortgage insurance to compare. Paying off the loan, or refinancing it into a conventional loan, ends it sooner.

Why does the monthly premium go down each year?

HUD works out the premium once a year from the average balance you will owe over the next 12 months. As the balance falls, so does the premium. Pick the simple method if you want the same amount every month, as many quotes show.

What are the FHA loan requirements?

A credit score of 580 or more allows 3.5% down; 500 to 579 needs 10% down. The loan must be within your county's limit, the home must meet FHA property standards, and your debt ratios must fit HUD's limits, which start at 31% for housing and 43% for all debts on a manually underwritten loan.

What is the FHA loan limit in 2026?

For a one-unit home, it is $541,287 in low-cost counties and up to $1,249,125 in high-cost areas, for case numbers from 1 January 2026. Two- to four-unit homes have higher limits. HUD's county lookup shows the limit where you are buying.

What is an FHA loan vs a conventional loan?

An FHA loan is insured by the government and accepts lower credit scores and 3.5% down, but charges an upfront premium and annual MIP that often lasts for the life of the loan. A conventional loan has no upfront FHA premium, and its PMI can be removed at 80% or ends at 78% of the original value.

Can I use this FHA loan calculator with closing costs?

The upfront premium is included automatically. Other closing costs, such as lender fees and title charges, are paid at closing and are not part of the monthly payment; check page 2 of your Loan Estimate for them, and remember that sellers may pay up to 6% of the price toward them.

What is the FHA loan interest rate today?

FHA rates are set by each lender, not by HUD, and they change with the market and with your credit score, down payment and points. Ask two or three FHA-approved lenders for a Loan Estimate on the same day and type the quoted rate here. Remember to add the 0.15% to 0.75% annual premium when you compare an FHA rate with a conventional one.

Can I pay off an FHA loan early?

Yes. HUD's rules let you prepay an FHA loan in whole or in part at any time with no prepayment charge. Use the extra payment fields to see how much interest and MIP an early payoff saves.

Can I use this as a home loan calculator for Pakistan?

No, FHA loans exist only in the US and the premiums here are HUD's. For a home loan in rupees, use the mortgage calculator with Pakistani rupee selected. Pakistani banks price home finance on KIBOR; Meezan Bank's Easy Home, for example, is Islamic co-ownership finance (diminishing musharakah) of up to 25 years, fixed for the first year and then repriced each year against 12-month KIBOR.
