The density in other units

What density is, in simple words

Density is how much mass is packed into each unit of space. A density calculator finds it from the mass and the volume of an object, or uses a known density to find the mass or the volume. Its symbol is the Greek letter ρ (rho), and the density formula is ρ = m ÷ V: mass divided by volume.

Two things of the same size can have very different masses. A litre of water has a mass of about 1 kg, while a litre of structural steel has about 7.85 kg, so steel is 7.85 times as dense. In physics and chemistry density helps identify materials, predict whether something floats, and convert between a weight and a volume.

Density units and how they convert

The SI unit of density is the kilogram per cubic metre (kg/m³). Its dimension is mass divided by length cubed, written M L⁻³. Chemistry and school physics often use grams per cubic centimetre, and since 1 mL is exactly 1 cm³, g/cm³ and g/mL are the same unit.

Density formula examples

  • Is the ring gold? A ring has a mass of 5.79 g and pushes the water in a measuring cylinder up by 0.3 mL. Density = 5.79 g ÷ 0.3 cm³ = 19.3 g/cm³ (19,300 kg/m³), very close to gold's 19.28.
  • A liquid: 500 mL of cooking oil weighs 458 g. The calculator turns 500 mL into 0.5 L and gives 916 g/L, a specific gravity of 0.916, so the oil floats on water.
  • Mass of a steel plate: a plate 2 m × 1 m × 10 mm holds 0.02 m³. With structural steel picked, m = 7,850 kg/m³ × 0.02 m³ = 157 kg.
  • Volume from mass: 1 kg of gold fills only 51.86 cm³, while 1 kg of aluminium would fill several times as much.
  • A cylinder: a solid aluminium rod with a 2 cm radius and 10 cm length has a volume of π × 2² × 10 = 125.66 cm³ (from our Volume Calculator). With aluminium picked, its mass is 339.29 g.

How to calculate density of a liquid, a gas or a shipment

Liquids: weigh an empty measuring jug, fill it to a mark, weigh it again and subtract. For example, if 10 litres of petrol measured this way weigh 7.4 kg, the density is 740 kg/m³ and the specific gravity 0.74, so it is lighter than water. Fuel density changes with temperature and blend, so use the figure on the fuel's data sheet for anything precise.

Gases: their density depends strongly on pressure and temperature, so they are not in the material list. For an ideal gas, ρ = P × M ÷ (R × T), with the pressure P in pascals, the molar mass M in kg/mol, the gas constant R = 8.314 J/(mol·K) and the temperature T in kelvin.

Freight: carriers work out shipping density in pounds per cubic foot. A pallet 48 × 40 × 50 inches is 96,000 in³; enter that with a weight of 450 lb and choose lb/ft³, and the calculator gives 55.56 ft³ and 8.1 lb/ft³. The freight class that density falls in is set by the carrier's tariff, so check their current table.

Density altitude, used by pilots, is a different idea: the altitude at which the standard atmosphere would have the same air density as today's air. It needs temperature and pressure, not a mass and a volume.

Specific gravity and density

Specific gravity is the density of a material divided by the density of water, so it has no unit. This calculator compares with water at 4 °C, 999.97 kg/m³, where water is densest. A specific gravity above 1 means the material sinks in fresh water; below 1 means it floats.

To get a density from a specific gravity, multiply it by 999.97 kg/m³. Going the other way, a liquid of which 1 L weighs 1.2 kg has a density of 1,200 kg/m³ and a specific gravity of 1.20004, so the two numbers are almost the same when density is written in g/cm³.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Mixing units: 5 g divided by 2 L is not 2.5 g/cm³ but 0.0025 g/cm³, because 2 L is 2,000 cm³. Let the unit menus do the conversion, or convert both to matching units first.
  • Using the density triangle: put m on top and ρ and V underneath. Cover the one you want: m = ρ × V, ρ = m ÷ V, V = m ÷ ρ.
  • Hollow or porous objects: a hollow pipe or a sponge has a lower overall density than its material. Measure the volume the object really fills, or the solid part only, depending on the question.
  • Temperature: most materials expand when warm and become slightly less dense; water at 20 °C is 998.21 kg/m³ against 999.97 at 4 °C.
  • Weight and mass: kitchen scales show mass in grams or kilograms, which is what the formula needs, even though we often call it weight.

How it works

Pick what you want to find, then enter the other two values, each with its own unit. Density tells you how much mass fits into a given volume, so the three are tied together by one rule: density equals mass divided by volume. The calculator first converts your numbers into matching units (for example pounds into kilograms, or gallons into cubic centimeters), then divides or multiplies, and shows each step. To find a volume or a mass you can also pick a common material, such as water, steel or gold, and its typical density is filled in for you.

The formula

  • Density: ρ = m ÷ V
  • Volume: V = m ÷ ρ
  • Mass: m = ρ × V
  • Exact unit factors: 1 lb = 0.45359237 kg, 1 in = 2.54 cm, 1 US gallon = 231 in³ = 3.785411784 L, 1 UK gallon = 4.54609 L, 1 carat = 0.2 g
  • Specific gravity = density ÷ density of water at 4 °C (999.97 kg/m³)

A worked example

A metal block has a mass of 8,900 kg and a volume of 1 m³: ρ = 8,900 kg ÷ 1 m³ = 8,900 kg/m³, close to copper or nickel. Going the other way, 2 US gallons of a liquid with a density of 7.85 g/cm³: 2 gal = 7,570.823568 cm³, so m = 7.85 × 7,570.823568 = 59,430.97 g, about 59.43 kg.

Questions

What is density?

Density is the mass packed into each unit of volume. A liter of water has a mass of about 1 kg, while a liter of steel has a mass of about 7.85 kg, so steel is about 7.85 times as dense. Dense materials feel heavy for their size.

How do I calculate density with mass and volume?

Divide the mass by the volume: ρ = m ÷ V. A 5.79 g ring that fills 0.3 cm³ has a density of 19.3 g/cm³. Choose Find density, enter both values with their units and the calculator converts and divides for you.

What is the density formula in physics and chemistry?

It is the same in both: density equals mass divided by volume, ρ = m/V. Physics usually writes the answer in kg/m³, the SI unit, and chemistry in g/cm³ or g/mL; 1 g/cm³ equals 1,000 kg/m³.

What is the density of water?

About 1,000 kg/m³ or 1 g/cm³. More precisely it is 999.97 kg/m³ at 4 °C, where it is densest, and 998.21 kg/m³ at 20 °C. Ice is lighter, at 916.72 kg/m³, which is why it floats.

Is g/mL the same as g/cm³?

Yes. One millilitre is exactly one cubic centimetre, so a density in g/mL is the same number in g/cm³. Both equal kilograms per litre, and 1 of them is 1,000 kg/m³.

Can I mix units, like pounds and liters?

Yes. Each value has its own unit menu. The calculator converts everything into matching units before it divides or multiplies, and the steps show each conversion, so you can check it by hand.

How do I calculate density from specific gravity?

Multiply the specific gravity by the density of water. With water at 4 °C (999.97 kg/m³), a specific gravity of 0.74 gives about 740 kg/m³. In g/cm³ the number stays almost the same: 0.74 g/cm³.

What is specific gravity?

It is the density compared with water at 4 °C. A specific gravity of 2.7 (aluminium) means the material is 2.7 times as dense as water. Anything below 1 floats in fresh water.

How do I calculate freight density?

Divide the shipment's weight in pounds by its volume in cubic feet. Enter the weight in lb and the size in in³ or ft³ on the Find density tab and choose lb/ft³; a 450 lb pallet of 48 × 40 × 50 inches comes to 8.1 lb/ft³.

Why does the density of a material change?

Temperature and pressure change it a little: most things expand when warm, so they become slightly less dense. Mixtures such as steel, wood or concrete also vary with their exact make-up. The material list gives typical values at room temperature.
