What the Army body fat calculator checks in 2026

This Army body fat calculator answers two questions. The first is the one that counts for soldiers today: does your waist-to-height ratio (WHtR) meet the Army standard? Under Army Directive 2026-13, in force since 7 July 2026, the waist at the navel divided by height must come out below 0.55. The height and weight screening tables, the tape tests and the supplemental body fat scans were all withdrawn.

The second answer is your body fat percentage from the Army one-site tape formula that was used from June 2023 until July 2026, together with the AR 600-9 limit for your age and sex. That number no longer decides pass or fail, but recruits, ROTC cadets and soldiers still search for it, older records use it, and it is a handy way to follow fat loss over time.

Both results are estimates from a tape measure. They are not a medical test, and the official result is always the one taken by trained Army personnel.

How to use this calculator, step by step

  • Choose U.S. units (pounds and inches) or metric (kilograms and centimetres) with the unit switch. The Army itself records inches and pounds; metric values are converted for you.
  • Pick Male or Female. The sex changes only the tape formula and its limit; the waist-to-height standard is the same for everyone.
  • Enter your age in whole years. The old body fat limits start at 17, so under 17 you see the body fat estimate without a limit.
  • Enter your weight. It is used only by the tape formula and rounded to the nearest pound, as the Army did.
  • Enter the waist at the navel: tape level around the belly button, snug on bare skin, read at the end of a normal breath out. For the tape formula it is rounded to the nearest half inch.
  • Enter your height without shoes. Height is used only for the waist-to-height ratio.
  • Read the gauge and the results at once; change any number to see how the pass line moves.

Each extra inch of height allows 0.55 in more waist. Soldiers are measured twice a year; anyone at or over the line gets a confirmation test by a different team the same day, and two results at 0.55 or more lead to a flag and enrolment in the Army Body Composition Program (ABCP). The Army announced a 180-day review period during which no one is separated for failing the new test.

The old tape test: formula and AR 600-9 body fat limits

From June 2023 the Army replaced its neck-and-waist tape with a single tape at the navel plus body weight (Army Directive 2023-11). The waist was the average of three readings rounded to the nearest half inch, the weight was rounded to the nearest pound, and the formula's answer was rounded to a whole percent. Men: −26.97 − 0.12 × weight + 1.99 × waist. Women: −9.15 − 0.015 × weight + 1.27 × waist.

A soldier who failed the tape could ask for a supplemental scan such as DXA, an InBody 770 or a Bod Pod when one was available. High scorers on the fitness test were excused from body fat checks altogether: 540 or more on the ACFT under Army Directive 2023-08, then 465 or more on the Army Fitness Test, with at least 80 points in every event, under Army Directive 2025-17. All of these routes ended with the 2026 directive.

Before 2023 the Army used neck and waist (plus hip for women) with height. Those circumference equations are the same family as the U.S. Navy method in our Body Fat Calculator, so use that page if you want an old-style neck-and-waist estimate.

Worked examples

Passes the new rule, failed the old one. A 21-year-old man is 5 ft 10 in (70 in), 165 lb, with a 36 in waist. His ratio is 36 ÷ 70 = 0.514, so he meets the 2026 standard. The tape formula gives −26.97 − 19.80 + 71.64 = 24.87, recorded as 25%. That is 3 points over the 22% limit for ages 21 to 27, or about 5 lb of fat (3% of 165 lb).

Fails the new rule, passed the old one. A 30-year-old man is 5 ft 5 in (65 in), 200 lb, with a 36 in waist. His ratio is 36 ÷ 65 = 0.5538, recorded as 0.553, which does not meet the standard; his waist would need to be under 35.75 in. The tape gave −26.97 − 24.00 + 71.64 = 20.67, about 21%, comfortably inside his 24% limit. The men's tape formula subtracts weight, so a heavy, short soldier could pass it, while the ratio ignores weight completely.

Metric example. A 30-year-old woman weighs 64 kg (141 lb), is 165 cm tall and has a 76 cm waist (29.9 in, rounded to 30 in for the tape). Her ratio is 76 ÷ 165 = 0.460, well below 0.55. The tape gives −9.15 − 2.115 + 38.10 = 26.8, about 27%, within the 34% limit for women aged 28 to 39.

Why the Army moved to waist-to-height ratio

Fat stored around the middle, including visceral fat around the organs, is linked more closely with diabetes and heart disease than weight alone. The UK's National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE guideline NG246, 2025) advises adults to keep their waist below half their height: a ratio of 0.4 to 0.49 is healthy, 0.5 to 0.59 means increased health risk and 0.6 or more means further increased risk.

The Army line of 0.55 therefore sits in the middle of the increased-risk band; meeting it is not proof of ideal health. The Army describes the ratio as simpler and more medically accurate than the tape tests. The Department of the Air Force made the same switch earlier: Airmen and Guardians have had to stay under 0.55 since 1 April 2023, but they measure the waist midway between the lowest rib and the top of the hip bone, not at the navel.

Measuring tips and common mistakes

  • Measure at the navel, not at the narrowest point of the waist or at belt level; on many people the navel line is the widest part of the stomach.
  • Keep the tape level all the way round. A tape that dips at the back adds an inch or more.
  • Breathe out normally and read the tape before the next breath. Do not suck in the stomach or push it out.
  • Take height barefoot, standing tall with heels together. Half an inch of shoe changes the limit by about a quarter inch of waist.
  • Measure on bare skin or a thin layer, at the same time of day each week, so changes are real rather than noise.
  • Do not treat the tape body fat as a lab result. A population formula can be several percentage points off for one person; compare your own numbers over weeks instead.

How it works

Since 7 July 2026 the U.S. Army checks body composition with one number only: the waist measured at the navel divided by height, both in inches. A ratio below 0.55 meets the standard; it is kept to three decimals and never rounded up, so 0.549 passes and 0.550 does not. Before that, from June 2023, the Army estimated body fat with a one-site tape at the navel plus body weight and compared it with a limit for age and sex. This calculator shows both: the current pass or fail, and the tape-test body fat with its old limit, which many people still use to track progress.

The formula

  • Waist-to-height ratio = waist at the navel (in) ÷ height (in); meets the standard below 0.550, cut (not rounded) to three decimals
  • Largest waist that still passes: just under 0.55 × height
  • One-site tape, men: body fat % = −26.97 − 0.12 × weight (lb) + 1.99 × waist (in)
  • One-site tape, women: body fat % = −9.15 − 0.015 × weight (lb) + 1.27 × waist (in)
  • Weight to the nearest pound, waist to the nearest half inch, body fat to the nearest whole percent
  • Body fat limits (AR 600-9): men 20% at 17 to 20, 22% at 21 to 27, 24% at 28 to 39, 26% at 40 and over; women 30%, 32%, 34% and 36%
  • Fat to lose = weight × (body fat − limit) ÷ 100

A worked example

A 21-year-old man, 5 ft 10 in (70 in) tall, 165 lb with a 36 in waist: 36 ÷ 70 = 0.514, below 0.55, so he meets the current standard. The tape formula gives −26.97 − 0.12 × 165 + 1.99 × 36 = 24.87, about 25% body fat, 3% over the old limit of 22% for his age, which is about 165 × 3% ≈ 5 lb of fat.

Questions

What is the new Army body fat standard in 2026?

Since 7 July 2026 the Army uses only the waist-to-height ratio: waist at the navel divided by height, both in inches, must be below 0.55. There is no separate body fat percentage limit any more, and weight is not part of the test.

Does the Army still use the tape test?

No. Army Directive 2026-13 withdrew the one-site tape test, the height and weight tables and the supplemental scans such as DXA. A tape measure is still used, but only to measure the waist for the ratio. The tape body fat is shown here for reference and tracking.

How do I calculate my Army body fat percentage with the old tape test?

Round your weight to the nearest pound and your navel waist to the nearest half inch. Men use −26.97 − 0.12 × weight + 1.99 × waist; women use −9.15 − 0.015 × weight + 1.27 × waist. Round the answer to a whole percent and compare it with the AR 600-9 limit for your age.

Is 0.55 a pass?

No. The ratio must be below 0.55. The Army records it to three decimals and drops any further digits instead of rounding, so 0.5499 counts as 0.549 and passes, while 0.550 fails.

How does the Army measure the waist for the ratio?

The tape goes level around the body at the navel, snug but not pressing into the skin, and is read at the end of a normal breath out. Height is taken without shoes. Results at or above 0.55 are checked again by another team on the same day.

What were the Army body fat requirements for women?

Under AR 600-9 the maximum was 30% at ages 17 to 20, 32% at 21 to 27, 34% at 28 to 39 and 36% at 40 and over, measured with the women's one-site tape formula. Since July 2026 women and men face the same rule: a waist-to-height ratio below 0.55.

Can a high fitness test score exempt me from the body composition check?

Not any more. The exemption for 465 or more points on the Army Fitness Test (with at least 80 in each event) was rescinded by the 2026 directive, so the ratio applies regardless of fitness test scores. The 540-point ACFT exemption from 2023 had already been replaced by the AFT rule in 2025.

Does the Army still use height and weight tables?

No. The height and weight screening tables were withdrawn with the 2026 directive, so weight alone no longer flags anyone. This calculator still asks for weight only because the old tape formula needs it.

What happens if I fail the waist-to-height test?

After a failed confirmation test the soldier is flagged and enrolled in the Army Body Composition Program until the standard is met. The Army said no one would be separated for this test during its first 180-day review period.

Is the Army tape body fat accurate?

It is an estimate from a population formula and can be several percentage points off for one person. Use it to follow your own changes over time rather than as a lab measurement such as DXA.
