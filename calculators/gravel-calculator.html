What the gravel calculator works out

A gravel calculator tells you how much gravel, crushed stone or sand you need to cover an area to a chosen depth. It gives the amount both as a volume, in cubic yards, cubic feet, cubic metres or litres, and as a weight, in tons, pounds, metric tons or kilograms, because suppliers sell by one or the other.

Gravel is the loose stone bigger than sand. On the Wentworth grain-size scale used by geologists, sand runs from 0.0625 to 2 mm, and anything coarser (granules, pebbles of 4 to 64 mm and cobbles of 64 to 256 mm) counts as gravel. Builders also use the word for crushed stone, which is quarried rock broken into angular pieces.

How to use the gravel calculator

  • Area to cover: choose Rectangle (length and width), Circle (diameter), or Total area if you already know the square feet, square metres, square yards or acres.
  • Depth: how thick the layer should be. Each box has its own unit menu, so you can mix feet for the area with inches for the depth.
  • Material: Gravel (1/4 to 2 in, 5 to 50 mm), Gravel mixed with sand, Dry sand, Wet sand, or Type my own density in kg/m³, g/cm³ or lb/ft³ if your supplier gives one.
  • Under "Price, bags and extra": a Price with how it is quoted (per cubic foot, cubic yard, cubic metre, litre, pound, short ton, kilogram, metric ton or bag), the bag size when buying bags, and an "Extra for settling and compaction" percentage.
  • The US / Metric switch sets the starting units: tons and cubic yards lead in the US view, metric tons and cubic metres in the metric view. A currency menu sets the symbol for the cost.

Understanding the results

Weight needed and Volume needed are the two headline figures. Below them you get the same amount in cubic feet and pounds (or litres and kilograms), the area covered, the number of bags when you price per bag, and the material cost when you enter a price. A collapsed "In every unit" table lists the volume, the weight and the area in all units at once.

The weight comes from the density of the material. Loose gravel has air between the stones; sand fills those gaps, so a mix of gravel and sand is heavier for the same volume. The calculator's densities give these weights for one cubic yard.

How to calculate how much gravel you need

The gravel formula is simple: volume = area × depth, then weight = volume × density. The only trap is units. Depth is usually measured in inches or centimetres while the area is in feet or metres, so convert the depth first: 3 inches is 0.25 ft and 10 cm is 0.1 m. One cubic yard is 27 cubic feet, so divide cubic feet by 27 to get yards.

That gives a handy shortcut for coverage. One cubic yard spread 2 inches deep covers 162 ft², 3 inches deep covers 108 ft², 4 inches deep covers 81 ft² and 6 inches deep covers 54 ft². In metric units, one cubic metre covers 20 m² at 5 cm, about 13.3 m² at 7.5 cm and 10 m² at 10 cm.

Worked examples: driveway, patio circle and trench

  • Gravel driveway: 60 ft long, 12 ft wide and 4 in deep, with 10% extra for settling. The area is 720 ft² and the volume 264 ft³ including the extra, which is 9.78 yd³ (8.89 yd³ before the extra). At 1,680 kg/m³ that weighs 13.84 short tons (27,688 lb), so at $35 per short ton the gravel costs $484.54.
  • Round seating area or fire-pit surround: a circle 12 ft across covered 3 in deep. The area is 113.1 ft² and the volume 28.3 ft³ (1.05 yd³, 1.48 tons). Bought in bags of 0.5 ft³ at $6.50, that is 57 bags for $370.50; buying loose by the yard would usually cost less.
  • French drain or trench: a trench 40 ft long, 1 ft wide and 18 in deep holds 60 ft³ (2.22 yd³, 3.15 tons of gravel). If a perforated pipe runs along the bottom, its own volume can be taken off, but the difference is small for a narrow pipe, and the spare gravel will settle anyway.
  • UK patio base in square metres: 25 m² at 5 cm deep with gravel and sand chosen gives 1.25 m³, or 2.1 tonnes; at £45 per tonne that is £94.50.

Gravel and aggregate in Pakistan

For a car porch, a lawn path or the base under a floor, switch to Metric and enter the sizes in metres and the depth in centimetres. For example, a porch 6 m by 4 m with a 10 cm layer of crushed stone needs 2.4 m³, which is 84.8 cubic feet and about 4.03 metric tons at the calculator's gravel density of 1,680 kg/m³.

If your supplier quotes crush or sand by the cubic foot (cft), choose "per cubic foot" under Price, pick PKR, and type the rate you were given. At an example rate of Rs 180 per cubic foot, the 84.8 cft above costs Rs 15,255.94. Rates vary by city, by quarry and by the size of the crush, and they change over time, so always use a current quote.

Ask whether a truck load is measured loose or compacted, and how many cubic feet it holds, so you can compare it with the calculator's figure.

Types of gravel and what they are used for

  • Pea gravel: small, smooth, rounded stones about the size of a pea. Good for paths, play areas and around plants, but it moves underfoot and needs edging.
  • Crushed stone: quarried rock, commonly limestone, dolomite or granite, broken into angular pieces that lock together. Used for road and driveway bases, concrete aggregate and drainage. Graded sizes are often sold by number, such as #57 stone; ask the supplier for its density and type it in.
  • Crusher run or road base: crushed stone mixed with stone dust, which packs hard and is the usual base layer for driveways and pavers.
  • River rock: large, smooth stones used for decoration, dry stream beds and around downspouts.
  • Sand: used under pavers and slabs, in concrete and mortar, and in play areas. Weigh wet sand and dry sand differently, as the material menu does.

How deep should the gravel be?

The right depth depends on the job and on whether the gravel sits on a firm base. As a rough guide, decorative beds and garden paths often use 2 to 3 inches (5 to 8 cm). A gravel driveway is usually built in layers: a base of larger crushed stone 4 to 8 inches (10 to 20 cm) deep, then a top layer of finer gravel 2 to 4 inches (5 to 10 cm) deep. Work out each layer separately when they use different stone, because their densities and prices differ.

Under concrete slabs and pavers, a compacted base of crushed stone helps drainage and spreads the load; the depth there is set by the slab design or your local building rules. Our Concrete Calculator works out the concrete itself.

Tips for ordering gravel

  • Add extra. Gravel settles and packs down when it is spread and rolled, and edges are rarely exact; many people order 10 to 15% more. Put the margin in the Extra box so every total includes it.
  • Check the ton. A US short ton is 2,000 lb (907.18 kg), while a metric ton is 1,000 kg (about 2,205 lb). Compare prices in the same unit.
  • Bags or bulk: bags are easy for small jobs, but for larger jobs bulk delivery is often cheaper and quicker; compare both prices in the calculator.
  • Moisture matters: wet material weighs more, so a load bought by weight after rain holds a little less stone.
  • Odd shapes: split the area into rectangles and circles, work out each one, and add them, or measure the total square footage with our Square Footage Calculator.

How it works

Choose the shape of the area (a rectangle, a circle or an area you already know), type its size and the depth of the layer, and pick the material. The volume and weight appear as you type: in the US view tons and cubic yards lead (how gravel is usually sold there), in the metric view metric tons and cubic meters. Every box has its own unit menu. Under Price, bags and extra you can enter your supplier's price per volume, per weight or per bag, and add a margin for the settling that happens when gravel is spread and compacted.

The formula

  • Area: rectangle = length × width; circle = π × (diameter ÷ 2)²
  • Volume = area × depth × (1 + extra %)
  • Weight = volume × density (gravel 1,680 kg/m³, gravel with sand 1,920, dry sand 1,500, wet sand 1,900)
  • 1 short ton = 2,000 lb = 907.18474 kg; 1 metric ton = 1,000 kg; 1 yd³ = 27 ft³ = 0.764554857984 m³
  • Bags = volume or weight ÷ bag size, rounded up; cost = amount in the price unit × price

A worked example

A rectangle 20 ft × 15 ft covered 4 in deep with gravel and sand: 300 ft² × 1/3 ft = 100 ft³ (3.7 yd³, 2.83 m³). At 1,920 kg/m³ that is about 5,437 kg, 11,986 lb or 5.99 short tons, so at $30 per short ton the gravel costs $179.79.

Questions

How do I calculate how much gravel I need?

Multiply the area by the depth to get the volume, then multiply by the density to get the weight. A driveway of 720 ft² at 4 inches holds 240 ft³, or 8.89 yd³, which is about 12.6 tons of gravel; with 10% extra it is 9.78 yd³ and 13.84 tons. The calculator does the unit conversions for you.

How many tons of gravel do I need?

Work out the volume, then use the density: one cubic yard of loose gravel weighs about 1.42 short tons, and one cubic metre about 1.68 metric tons. Gravel mixed with sand is heavier, at about 1.62 short tons per cubic yard.

How much does a yard of gravel cover?

It depends on the depth: one cubic yard covers 162 ft² at 2 inches, 108 ft² at 3 inches, 81 ft² at 4 inches and 54 ft² at 6 inches. One cubic metre covers 20 m² at 5 cm and 10 m² at 10 cm.

How do I calculate gravel for a driveway?

Enter the driveway's length, width and the depth of each layer. Work out the base of larger crushed stone and the top layer of finer gravel separately, add 10 to 15% for settling, and compare suppliers' prices per ton or per yard.

How do I calculate gravel for a circle?

Choose Circle and enter the diameter. The area is π × (diameter ÷ 2)²: a circle 12 ft across is 113.1 ft², and at 3 inches deep it needs 28.3 ft³, about 1.05 cubic yards.

How much gravel do I need for a French drain?

Treat the trench as a rectangle: its length and width as the area and its depth as the depth. A trench 40 ft long, 1 ft wide and 18 inches deep needs 60 ft³, about 2.22 cubic yards or 3.15 tons.

How deep should a gravel layer be?

Decorative paths and beds are often 2 to 3 inches (5 to 8 cm) deep. A driveway usually has a base of larger crushed stone 4 to 8 inches (10 to 20 cm) deep with a top layer of finer gravel 2 to 4 inches (5 to 10 cm) deep. Work out each layer separately if they use different stone.

Why does the weight depend on the material?

Loose gravel has air between the stones, so a cubic meter weighs about 1,680 kg, while gravel mixed with sand fills those gaps and weighs about 1,920 kg. Wet sand is heavier than dry sand. If your supplier gives a density, choose Type my own density and enter it.

Should I order extra gravel?

Usually yes. Gravel settles and packs down when it is spread and compacted, and edges are rarely exact, so many people add 10 to 15 percent. Put the margin in the Extra box and every total includes it.

How do I work out aggregate in cubic feet for a Pakistani supplier?

Switch to Metric, enter the area in metres and the depth in centimetres, then open the "In every unit" table for the cubic feet. A 6 m × 4 m porch at 10 cm needs 84.8 cubic feet; choose "per cubic foot" and PKR to price it at your supplier's rate.

What is the difference between a short ton and a metric ton?

A short ton, used in the United States, is 2,000 pounds (907 kg). A metric ton, or tonne, is 1,000 kg (about 2,205 lb). Check which one your supplier means before comparing prices.
