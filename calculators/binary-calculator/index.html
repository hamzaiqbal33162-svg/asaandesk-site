How it works

Binary numbers use only the digits 0 and 1; each place is worth twice the place to its right (1, 2, 4, 8, 16 …). Pick Calculate to add, subtract, multiply or divide two binary numbers (division gives a whole-number quotient and a remainder) or to combine them bit by bit with AND, OR and XOR. The answer is shown in binary, in decimal, in hexadecimal and in octal. The two converters change binary to decimal by adding up the place values, and decimal to binary by dividing by 2 again and again; a decimal fraction such as 10.5 becomes 1010.1.

The formula

  • Binary to decimal: d = bₙ×2ⁿ + … + b₂×2² + b₁×2¹ + b₀×2⁰ (+ b₋₁×2⁻¹ + … after the point)
  • Decimal to binary: divide by 2 and keep the remainders; read them from the last to the first
  • Fractions: multiply the part after the point by 2; each whole part (0 or 1) is the next digit
  • Division: a = q × b + r with 0 ≤ |r| < |b| (q rounded toward zero)
  • Two's complement of a negative n in w bits: 2ʷ + n

A worked example

10101010 + 11001100: in decimal 170 + 204 = 374, and 374 = 256 + 64 + 32 + 16 + 4 + 2, which is 101110110 in binary. To turn 170 into binary: 170 ÷ 2 = 85 r 0, 85 ÷ 2 = 42 r 1, 42 ÷ 2 = 21 r 0, 21 ÷ 2 = 10 r 1, 10 ÷ 2 = 5 r 0, 5 ÷ 2 = 2 r 1, 2 ÷ 2 = 1 r 0, 1 ÷ 2 = 0 r 1; reading up gives 10101010.

Questions

How do I read a binary number?

Start from the right: the places are worth 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 and so on. Add the values of the places that hold a 1. For 1011 that is 8 + 2 + 1 = 11.

How are negative binary numbers shown?

The answer keeps a minus sign, such as −101 for −5, which is the plain mathematical way. Computers store negative whole numbers in two's complement instead, so the result also shows the 8, 16, 32 or 64-bit two's complement form (−5 is 11111011 in 8 bits).

Can I convert decimals with a fraction part?

Yes. 10.5 is 1010.1 in binary because .1 in binary is one half. Some fractions never end in binary (0.1 becomes 0.000110011…), so the result shows the first digits and tells you which block repeats.

What do AND, OR and XOR do?

They compare the two numbers one bit at a time: AND gives 1 where both bits are 1, OR where either is 1, and XOR where exactly one is 1. They are used in programming for flags and masks, and the result lists the bits lined up so you can check.
