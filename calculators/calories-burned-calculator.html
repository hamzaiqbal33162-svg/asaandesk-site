What does calories burned mean?

Calories burned is the energy your body uses to do something, from sleeping to sprinting. This calories burned calculator estimates it for 106 activities, or for walking, running or cycling a set distance, from three things: how hard the activity is, how long you do it and how much you weigh.

The unit is the kilocalorie (kcal), which is the same thing as the Calorie with a capital C on food labels. So when a watch says 300 kcal and a snack bar says 300 Calories, they mean the same amount of energy. The calculator also gives the answer in kilojoules (kJ), the unit used on labels in many countries: 1 kcal is 4.184 kJ.

All the figures are estimates for an average adult doing the activity at a steady effort. They are very useful for comparing activities and planning, but they are not a measurement of your own body.

How to use the calories burned calculator

Pick Metric or US units at the top, then one of the two tabs. Body weight is asked on both, in kg or lb; enter your weight with everyday clothes.

  • By activity and time: choose the Type of activity (walking, running, cycling and swimming; gym and exercise classes; sports, dancing and games; outdoor and yard work; or home and daily life), then the Activity, then How long in hours, minutes and seconds.
  • Walking, running or cycling a distance: choose Walking, Running or Cycling, then How fast. Enter my speed or pace lets you type a speed in mph, km/h, metres or yards per second, or a pace in minutes per mile or per km. The presets are Slow, Moderate, Fast and Very fast (walking 2, 2.8, 3.5 and 4 mph; running 5, 6, 7.5 and 10 mph; cycling 13, 15, 17.5 and 21 mph). Then enter the distance in km, miles, metres or yards.
  • Results, activity tab: calories burned, the effort level in MET with the Compendium code and description, calories per hour and per minute, the total in kJ, and a table (tap to open) that compares every activity in the same group for the same time and weight.
  • Results, distance tab: calories burned, the time the distance takes, your speed in mph and km/h, your pace per km and per mile, the MET for that speed and the calories per hour at that speed.

The calories burned formula: MET × weight × time

A MET (metabolic equivalent of task) compares an activity with sitting quietly. By definition 1 MET is 3.5 ml of oxygen per kg of body weight per minute, which the Compendium treats as about 1 kcal per kg per hour. An activity of 4 MET therefore uses about four times as much energy as sitting, so the calories burned formula is simply MET × weight in kg × hours.

Some sources write the same idea per minute: MET × 3.5 × weight in kg ÷ 200. That version assumes slightly more energy per MET (1.05 kcal per kg per hour), so it gives about 5% more than this calculator. Neither is wrong; they round the oxygen-to-calorie conversion differently.

Walking, running and cycling by distance

On the distance tab the calculator first works out how long the distance takes at your speed, then uses the Compendium value for that speed on level ground. Faster is not always much costlier per kilometre: at 70 kg, 1 km at the moderate walking preset (2.8 mph) burns about 59 kcal in 13 minutes, and 1 km at the moderate running preset (6 mph) about 67 kcal in 6 minutes. Running mostly saves time.

  • Lahore, walking: a 62 kg woman who walks 5 km in a park at 5 km/h takes 1 hour, at 3.8 MET, and burns about 236 kcal.
  • Running 10 km: at 70 kg and a pace of 6:00 per km the run takes 1 hour at 9.3 MET and burns about 651 kcal.
  • Cycling: an 80 kg rider covering 20 km at 25 km/h (15.53 mph) needs 48 minutes at 10.0 MET and burns about 640 kcal, or 800 kcal an hour at that speed.
  • US units, walking: a 180 lb man walking 3 miles at 3.5 mph takes 51 minutes 26 seconds at 4.8 MET and burns about 336 kcal.
  • US units, one mile: at 160 lb, a 10-minute mile (6 mph) burns about 112 kcal.

The calculator warns when a speed does not fit the activity, for example walking above 5.5 mph (8.9 km/h), where race-walking values are used, or running below 2.6 mph, which is really walking.

Steps, treadmill, stair climber, elliptical and weights

  • Steps: turn steps into a distance first. Walk 10 normal steps, measure how far you went and divide by 10 to get your stride. With a 0.7 m stride, 8,000 steps is 5.6 km; at 4.8 km/h a 70 kg person takes 1 h 10 min and burns about 310 kcal.
  • Treadmill: use the distance tab with the speed shown on the machine. The Compendium values are for level ground, so a raised incline costs more than the calculator shows; treat the result as a minimum for incline walking.
  • Stair climber, elliptical and exercise bike: choose them under Gym and exercise classes. For 45 minutes at 75 kg the calculator gives about 523 kcal on a stair machine (9.3 MET), 281 kcal on an elliptical (5.0 MET), and 383 or 608 kcal on an exercise bike at moderate (101 to 125 watts) or vigorous (200 to 229 watts) effort.
  • Weight lifting: general training with 8 to 15 reps is 3.5 MET, while power lifting or body building at vigorous effort is 6.0 MET. For 45 minutes at 75 kg that is about 197 or 338 kcal. If you take long breaks between sets, count only the time you spend training.
  • Strength training still matters even though the calorie count is modest: WHO advises muscle-strengthening activities on 2 or more days a week for adults.

How many calories you burn in a day and at rest

To estimate a whole day, add up each part of it. A 65 kg person who sleeps 8 hours (520 kcal), sits reading or at a screen for 8 hours (520 kcal), cooks for 1.5 hours (195 kcal), walks at a moderate pace for 1 hour (247 kcal), shops for 1 hour (215 kcal), plays with children for 1 hour (228 kcal) and watches TV for 3.5 hours (228 kcal) uses about 2,150 kcal in 24 hours by the MET method.

Calories burned at rest are set mostly by your basal metabolic rate (BMR). The MET method assumes about 1 kcal per kg per hour at rest, which is 65 kcal an hour for that person. A formula that also uses height, age and sex can give less: for a 30-year-old woman of 160 cm and 65 kg, the Mifflin-St Jeor formula in our BMR Calculator gives 1,339 kcal a day, about 56 kcal an hour. For daily totals, the TDEE Calculator is usually the better tool.

MET figures are gross values: they include the energy you would have used anyway. For the extra energy an exercise adds, subtract 1 MET. The 10 km run above burns 651 kcal in an hour, of which about 70 kcal you would have burned sitting, so the net cost is about 581 kcal.

Calories burned and weight loss

A long-used rule of thumb from Wishnofsky (1958) is that about 3,500 kcal equals one pound of body weight, or about 7,700 kcal per kg. By that rule, burning an extra 300 kcal a day would take about 12 days to equal a pound. In practice weight changes more slowly than the rule predicts, because the body burns less as it gets smaller; a 2011 analysis in The Lancet by Hall and colleagues explains why.

Exercise is still one of the best things you can do for health. WHO advises adults to do 150 to 300 minutes of moderate activity, or 75 to 150 minutes of vigorous activity, every week, and its 2026 fact sheet says nearly a third of adults worldwide (31% in 2022) do not reach even the lower target. For weight loss, combine regular activity with a sensible eating plan from the Calorie Calculator.

Watch out for eating back exercise calories based on an optimistic number. A modest snack can easily match a 30-minute walk, which is 133 kcal at 70 kg.

How accurate is it? Watches, heart rate and other factors

Fitness watches and trackers estimate calories from heart rate, movement and your profile with their own formulas. In a 2017 Stanford study of seven wrist devices on 60 volunteers, six measured heart rate with an error under 5%, but none measured energy expenditure accurately: the best was off by 27% on average and the worst by 93%. So a big gap between this page and your Apple Watch or Fitbit is normal.

Heart rate itself is a guide to effort rather than to calories, because a fit person has a lower heart rate at the same work. The Target Heart Rate Calculator shows the heart rate zones that match moderate and vigorous effort.

  • Fitness and technique: practised exercisers move more efficiently, so they may burn a little less than the estimate.
  • Body make-up and age: more muscle raises energy use; less muscle in later life lowers it.
  • Terrain, wind, heat and load: hills, sand, headwinds and carrying weight all raise the real cost above the level-ground values.
  • Breaks: an hour of tennis or soccer often includes rest. Count only the active time for a fairer estimate.
  • Very low or high body weight: the calculator warns below 35 kg or above 160 kg, where the MET method is less reliable.

How it works

Every activity has a MET value: how many times more energy it takes than sitting still. The values here come from the 2024 Compendium of Physical Activities, the research list scientists use to compare activities; each activity shows its Compendium code so you can look it up. Multiply the MET by your weight in kilograms and the hours you spent, and you get kilocalories (the calories on food labels). For walking, running and cycling over a distance, the calculator works out the time from your speed and picks the Compendium value for that speed on flat ground.

The formula

  • Calories (kcal) = MET × body weight (kg) × time (hours)
  • 1 MET = 3.5 ml of oxygen per kg per minute ≈ 1 kcal per kg per hour
  • Wheelchair basketball uses the 2024 Wheelchair Compendium, where 1 MET = 0.992 kcal per kg per hour
  • By distance: time = distance ÷ speed, then the MET for that speed (for example running 6 to 6.3 mph = 9.3 MET, code 12050)
  • 1 kcal = 4.184 kJ

A worked example

A 70 kg person running 10 km at a pace of 6:00 per km (10 km/h, 6.21 mph) takes exactly 1 hour. That speed falls in the Compendium's 6 to 6.3 mph band, 9.3 MET, so the run burns 9.3 × 70 × 1 = 651 kcal, about 2,724 kJ.

Questions

How do I calculate calories burned?

Multiply the activity's MET value by your weight in kg and the time in hours. Moderate walking is 3.8 MET, so 30 minutes at 70 kg is 3.8 × 70 × 0.5 = 133 kcal. The calculator looks up the MET for 106 activities and does the sum for you.

How many calories do I burn walking?

It depends on your weight and speed. At 70 kg, a moderate walk of 2.8 to 3.4 mph burns about 266 kcal an hour and a brisk walk of 3.5 to 3.9 mph about 336 kcal an hour. Use the distance tab to get the figure for your own route and pace.

How many calories does running burn per mile or km?

Roughly 1 kcal per kg of body weight per km at a steady pace, or about 1.5 kcal per kg per mile. At 6 mph, a 70 kg runner burns about 67 kcal per km, and a 160 lb runner about 112 kcal per mile.

How do I calculate calories burned from steps?

Multiply your steps by your stride length to get a distance, then enter it on the distance tab with your walking speed. For example, 8,000 steps of 0.7 m is 5.6 km, which at 4.8 km/h burns about 310 kcal for a 70 kg person.

How many calories do you burn in a day?

It depends mostly on your size and how active you are. The example day on this page, built from everyday activities, comes to about 2,150 kcal for a 65 kg person, and resting energy (BMR) is usually the largest part. For your own number, add up your day here or use the TDEE calculator, which starts from your BMR.

Do the calories include what I would burn anyway?

Yes. MET values count all the energy used during the activity, including the energy your body uses at rest. For the extra calories from exercise alone, subtract about 1 kcal per kg per hour.

How accurate are these numbers?

They are good averages, not a measurement of you. Fitness, body make-up, technique, terrain and how hard you really work all move the real figure up or down. Use them to compare activities and plan, not to count every calorie.

Why is my Apple Watch or Fitbit number different?

Watches use their own formulas based on heart rate and movement, and a 2017 Stanford study found that wrist devices measured heart rate well but were off by 27% to 93% on calories. Neither number is exact, so pick one method and use it consistently.

How long does it take to burn 100 calories?

At 70 kg, about 23 minutes of moderate walking or just over 9 minutes of running at 6 mph burns roughly 100 kcal. A lighter person needs a little longer and a heavier person a little less.

What is kcal in calories burned?

Kcal means kilocalorie, the unit food labels call a Calorie. One kcal is 1,000 small calories or 4.184 kilojoules, and every result on this page is in kcal with kJ shown too.

Why does a heavier person burn more?

Moving a heavier body takes more energy, so the estimate grows with weight. At the same weight, a harder activity (higher MET) burns more per minute.

Why do some other calculators give different numbers?

Many use older MET tables, their own data or the per-minute formula, which gives about 5% more. This one uses the latest published research values (2024) and shows the code for each activity, so you can check where every number comes from.
