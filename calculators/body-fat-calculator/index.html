What is body fat percentage?

Body fat percentage is the share of your body weight that is fat. This body fat calculator estimates it from a few tape measurements with the U.S. Navy method, and a second time from your BMI, then shows your fat mass, lean mass, category and an ideal value for your age. If you weigh 80 kg and your body fat is 25%, you carry about 20 kg of fat and 60 kg of everything else: muscle, bone, organs and water.

Some fat is essential. It cushions organs, insulates nerves, stores vitamins A, D, E and K and makes hormones, and women naturally carry more of it than men for reproduction. Fat beyond that is storage fat. A moderate store is healthy; a large one, especially deep in the belly around the organs, raises the risk of diabetes and heart disease.

Body fat tells you more than BMI does, because two people with the same height and weight can have very different amounts of fat and muscle. It is still an estimate: tape and BMI methods usually land within a few percentage points of a lab scan, so use the same method each time and follow the trend. This is general information, not medical advice.

How to measure and use the body fat calculator

  • Choose Metric (cm and kg) or US units (inches, feet and pounds).
  • Pick Male or Female and enter your age, weight and height.
  • Neck: wrap a soft tape just below the voice box (Adam's apple), sloping slightly down at the front. Keep your head level and do not flare the neck.
  • Waist: men measure horizontally at the navel; women measure at the narrowest point between the ribs and the hips. Stand relaxed and read the tape after a normal breath out, without pulling the belly in.
  • Hips (women only): measure around the widest part of the buttocks with your feet together.
  • Keep the tape snug but not pressing into the skin, measure twice and use the average. Measure at the same time of day each time you track progress.

The results show your body fat by the Navy method with its category, fat mass, lean mass, the ideal body fat for your age (from age 20), how much fat you would lose to reach it, and a second estimate from your BMI. If the tape numbers are impossible, for example a waist smaller than the neck, you get a clear message instead of a result.

Body fat percentage chart for men and women

The calculator places you in the categories used by the American Council on Exercise (ACE). ACE describes 18% to 24% for men and 25% to 31% for women as typical for the average non-athlete. Women's bands sit about 7 to 8 points higher because of their larger share of essential fat.

Ideal body fat by age

Body fat tends to rise with age even at a steady weight, as muscle is slowly lost. The ideal value shown by the calculator comes from the Jackson and Pollock table (1985) and is interpolated between the ages listed; from 55 the 55-year value is used, and under 20 no ideal is shown.

Worked examples, including a South Asian waist check

  • Metric, Faisalabad: a 40-year-old man, 172 cm and 82 kg, neck 39 cm and waist 96 cm. The Navy method gives 24.6% body fat, in the Average band: about 20.2 kg of fat and 61.8 kg of lean mass. His ideal at 40 is 15.3%, which means losing about 7.6 kg of fat. The BMI method gives 26.3% from his BMI of 27.7. His waist of 96 cm is above the WHO line of 94 cm for men and the 90 cm line the International Diabetes Federation uses for South Asian men, and his waist-to-height ratio of 0.56 is in the increased-risk range.
  • Metric, Karachi: a 35-year-old woman, 160 cm and 66 kg, neck 33 cm, waist 84 cm and hips 102 cm. The Navy method gives 36.2%, in the Obese band, with 23.9 kg of fat and 42.1 kg of lean mass, although her BMI of 25.8 is only just over the normal range. Her ideal at 35 is 21.5%. Her waist of 84 cm is above the 80 cm line for women.
  • US units: a 28-year-old man, 5 ft 11 in and 180 lb, neck 16 in and waist 33 in. The Navy method gives 13.0%, in the Athletes band, yet his BMI of 25.1 counts as overweight and the BMI method gives 20.4%. This is the classic case where BMI misreads a muscular build.

How the U.S. Navy and BMI methods work

The Navy method was developed by James Hodgdon and Marcie Beckett at the Naval Health Research Center in 1984, from measurements of 602 Navy men and a companion study of 214 Navy women. Their equations estimate body density from the logarithm of the waist minus the neck (plus the hips for women) and of height, and the Siri equation converts density into fat percentage. A later Navy evaluation found the circumference equation predicted body fat better for Navy men than three skinfold equations and two bioimpedance equations it was compared with.

The BMI method uses the Deurenberg equations (1991), built from 1,229 people aged 7 to 83 whose body fat was measured by densitometry. The typical error was about 4.1 percentage points for adults and 4.4 for children, and the authors noted that the formulas slightly overestimate body fat in people with obesity. Their child formula was derived for ages 15 and under; this calculator applies it under 18. Because it knows nothing about your shape, the BMI method cannot tell a muscular person from a heavier one, so trust the tape result more when the two disagree.

Whatever method you choose, compare like with like: the same device, the same time of day and similar hydration. A change of 1 to 2 percentage points between two different methods may simply be the difference between the methods.

Health risks of body fat, and notes for South Asians

Too much body fat raises the risk of high blood pressure, type 2 diabetes, coronary heart disease, stroke, gallbladder disease, osteoarthritis, sleep apnoea and several cancers (CDC). Where the fat sits matters: fat around the waist is linked more strongly with these problems than fat on the hips and thighs, which is why waist size is checked alongside body fat.

South Asian adults tend to carry more fat at a given BMI. A 2002 review found 3 to 5 percentage points more body fat in Asian groups than in Europeans at the same BMI, and the Pakistan Dietary Guidelines for Better Nutrition (2018) flag a waist over 94 cm for men or 80 cm for women as a sign of higher risk.

  • WHO waist cut-offs: over 94 cm (men) or 80 cm (women) means increased risk; over 102 cm (men) or 88 cm (women) substantially increased risk. A waist-to-hip ratio of 0.90 or more (men) or 0.85 or more (women) also signals substantially increased risk.
  • International Diabetes Federation cut-offs for South Asian, Chinese and Japanese adults: over 90 cm for men and over 80 cm for women.
  • NICE (UK, 2025): keep your waist under half your height. A waist-to-height ratio of 0.5 to 0.59 means increased risk and 0.6 or more further increased risk.
  • A 2025 Lancet Commission recommends confirming obesity with a body measurement such as waist size or a direct fat measurement, not BMI alone, except at very high BMI.

How it works

Measure your neck, your waist (and your hips if you are a woman) with a soft tape, and enter your height, weight, age and sex. The U.S. Navy method turns the tape measurements and your height into an estimate of body density, and the Siri equation turns density into a body fat percentage. A second estimate comes from your BMI, age and sex. The category bands are the ones the American Council on Exercise uses, and the ideal value for your age comes from the Jackson and Pollock table.

The formula

  • Men: body fat % = 495 ÷ (1.0324 − 0.19077 × log10(waist − neck) + 0.15456 × log10(height)) − 450
  • Women: body fat % = 495 ÷ (1.29579 − 0.35004 × log10(waist + hip − neck) + 0.22100 × log10(height)) − 450
  • All lengths in centimetres (U.S. Navy method, Hodgdon and Beckett, Naval Health Research Center, 1984)
  • BMI method, adults: body fat % = 1.20 × BMI + 0.23 × age − 10.8 × sex − 5.4 (sex: 1 for men, 0 for women)
  • BMI method, under 18: body fat % = 1.51 × BMI − 0.70 × age − 3.6 × sex + 1.4
  • Fat mass = weight × body fat % ÷ 100; lean mass = weight − fat mass

A worked example

A man 178 cm tall with a 39 cm neck and an 86 cm waist: waist − neck = 47, log10(47) = 1.672 and log10(178) = 2.250, so body fat = 495 ÷ (1.0324 − 0.19077 × 1.672 + 0.15456 × 2.250) − 450 ≈ 16.4%, in the Fitness band for men. At 75 kg that is about 12.3 kg of fat and 62.7 kg of lean mass.

Questions

How do I measure my neck, waist and hips?

Use a soft tape, snug but not pressing into the skin, and breathe out normally. Neck: just below the voice box. Waist: men at the navel, women at the narrowest point. Hips: around the widest part of the buttocks. Measure twice and use the average.

What is the U.S. Navy body fat formula for men?

With all lengths in centimetres: body fat % = 495 ÷ (1.0324 − 0.19077 × log10(waist − neck) + 0.15456 × log10(height)) − 450. For a 178 cm man with a 39 cm neck and an 86 cm waist that gives about 16.4%.

What is the body fat formula for women?

The Navy formula for women adds the hips: body fat % = 495 ÷ (1.29579 − 0.35004 × log10(waist + hip − neck) + 0.22100 × log10(height)) − 450, in centimetres. Measure the waist at its narrowest and the hips at their widest.

How can I calculate body fat at home without equipment?

The BMI method needs only your height, weight, age and sex: adults use 1.20 × BMI + 0.23 × age − 10.8 × sex − 5.4, with sex 1 for men and 0 for women. It is shown as the second result. A cheap tape measure for the Navy method is more accurate for most people.

How accurate is the tape method?

It is an estimate. For most people it lands within a few percentage points of a lab scan, but it can be further off for very muscular people or when the tape is placed differently each time. Use the same method and the same spots to track changes over time.

Why are the two results different?

The tape method uses your body shape, while the BMI method uses only height, weight, age and sex, so it cannot tell muscle from fat. When they disagree, the tape result is usually closer for active people.

What is a healthy body fat percentage?

It depends on sex and age. The American Council on Exercise puts average men at 18 to 24% and average women at 25 to 31%; athletes are lower. The ideal value shown here rises slowly with age. Ask a doctor about your own target.

Is the body fat calculator accurate for Indians and Pakistanis?

The formulas were built mainly on Western groups, and studies show South Asians carry more body fat at the same BMI, so the BMI method may read low. The tape method measures your own shape, which helps, and adding a waist check (over 90 cm for men or 80 cm for women) gives a useful second signal.

What is the difference between fat mass and lean mass?

Fat mass is the weight of the fat in your body: weight × body fat % ÷ 100. Lean mass is everything else, including muscle, bone, organs and water. When you lose weight, the aim is to lose fat mass while keeping lean mass, which strength training and enough protein help with.

Can children and teens use this calculator?

The tape equations were made for adult Navy personnel, so for children the result is only rough, and no ideal value is shown under 20. For under-18s the BMI line uses a children's formula. A paediatrician's growth chart check is the better guide for young people.
