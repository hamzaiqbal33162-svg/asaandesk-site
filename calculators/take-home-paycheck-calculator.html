How it works

Enter your yearly pay, how often you are paid and your filing status. The calculator splits the year into paychecks and takes off Social Security (6.2% up to the yearly wage base), Medicare (1.45%, plus the 0.9% your employer withholds on pay above $200,000), your state and city rates, and your pre-tax deductions. Federal income tax follows the IRS percentage method for a 2020-or-later Form W-4: your taxable pay for the year, minus the standard deduction built into the tables and the deductions in Step 4(b), taxed at the brackets for your status, minus the Step 3 credits for children and other dependents. If you or your spouse have another job, the Multiple Jobs Worksheet amount is added. You also get the W-4 numbers to give your employer. If you are self-employed, it shows self-employment tax and the income tax to set aside from each payment instead.

The formula

  • Gross pay per paycheck = yearly pay ÷ paychecks a year (52 weekly, 26 every two weeks, 24 twice a month, 12 monthly, 260 daily)
  • Social Security = 6.2% × pay up to the wage base; Medicare = 1.45% × pay + 0.9% × pay above $200,000 (401(k) money counts, cafeteria-plan health money does not)
  • Federal withholding for the year = tax on (taxable pay + Step 4(a) − Step 4(b) − standard deduction) − Step 3 credits + Multiple Jobs amount
  • Step 4(b) = itemized deductions above the standard deduction (or cash gifts to charity) + extra standard deduction at 65+ + tips, overtime, car loan interest and senior deductions + other adjustments
  • Take-home pay = gross pay − federal − Social Security − Medicare − state − city − pre-tax deductions

A worked example

A single person earning $80,000 a year, paid every two weeks, with $6,000 a year going to a 401(k): taxable pay is $74,000, minus the $16,100 standard deduction = $57,900, which has $7,450 of 2026 tax, or $286.54 per paycheck. Social Security is 6.2% of $80,000 ÷ 26 = $190.77 and Medicare $44.62. Take-home pay is $3,076.92 − $286.54 − $190.77 − $44.62 − $230.77 = $2,324.23.

Questions

Why is my federal tax lower than my tax bracket suggests?

Only the part of your pay above the standard deduction is taxed, and it moves through the brackets: 10% on the first slice, 12% on the next and so on. So someone in the 22% bracket usually has well under 22% of their pay withheld.

Does my 401(k) lower Social Security and Medicare?

No. 401(k), 403(b) and TSP contributions lower income tax only. Pre-tax health insurance, HSA and FSA money taken through a cafeteria plan is free of income tax, Social Security and Medicare, which is why they are two separate boxes here.

What should I put on my W-4?

Use the numbers in the W-4 table under the result. Fill in Steps 3 and 4 on one W-4 only, the one for your highest-paying job. If you would rather not tell your employer about other income or a second job, leave Steps 2, 4(a) and 4(c) blank and pay the quarterly amount shown with Form 1040-ES instead.

I am self-employed. What do I set aside?

Choose self-employed. Nobody withholds for you, so the calculator shows self-employment tax (15.3% on 92.35% of your profit, less above the wage base) and the extra income tax your business adds after half of that tax and the qualified business income deduction. Pay it to the IRS each quarter.

Why does Social Security stop late in the year for high earners?

Social Security tax only applies up to the yearly wage base ($184,500 in 2026, $192,300 in 2027). This calculator spreads it evenly over the year; on real paychecks it is taken from each check until your pay reaches the limit, then stops.
