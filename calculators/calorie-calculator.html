How many calories do I need a day?

This calorie calculator estimates how much food energy you need each day to keep your weight steady, and how much to eat to lose or gain weight at a steady pace. It starts from four facts about you (sex, age, height and weight), adds how active you are, and returns your maintenance calories plus six targets: three for weight loss and three for weight gain.

A calorie, as used on food labels, is a kilocalorie (kcal): the energy that would warm one litre of water by about one degree Celsius. Your body spends that energy in three ways. Most of it keeps you alive at rest (your basal metabolic rate, or BMR), some is used to digest food, and the rest goes on movement, from walking to the shop to a game of cricket. The calculator estimates the first part with a published formula and covers the other two with an activity factor.

The answer is an estimate for a typical person with your numbers, not a measurement of your own body. Two people with identical inputs can differ by around 10% because of muscle mass, genes, sleep, illness and medicines. Use the result as a starting point, follow your weight for a few weeks, and adjust. This is general information, not medical advice; if you are pregnant, have diabetes, kidney disease or an eating disorder, plan your intake with a doctor or dietitian.

How to use the calorie calculator, step by step

  • Choose Metric (kilograms and centimetres) or US units (pounds, feet and inches) with the unit switch.
  • Pick Male or Female. The formulas give women a lower resting energy use at the same size, mainly because women carry less muscle on average.
  • Enter your age (15 to 80 years), weight and height. Weigh yourself in the morning, after the toilet and before eating, for the most repeatable number.
  • Choose how active you are. Exercise here means 15 to 30 minutes with your heart rate raised; intense exercise means 45 to 120 minutes; very intense means 2 hours or more. The options run from BMR only (no activity at all) through sedentary, light (exercise 1 to 3 times a week), moderate (4 to 5 times), active (daily, or intense 3 to 4 times), very active (intense 6 to 7 times) to extra active (very intense daily, or a physical job).
  • Open Formula and units if you want a different BMR formula. Mifflin-St Jeor is the default; the revised Harris-Benedict formula is also offered; Katch-McArdle asks for your body fat percentage and uses lean mass instead of height and age.
  • In the same section, Show energy in switches every result between calories (kcal) and kilojoules (kJ).
  • The second tab, kcal and kJ converter, turns any amount between food calories (kcal), small calories (cal), kilojoules (kJ) and joules (J).

Worked examples: how to calculate calories to lose weight

  • US units: a 34-year-old woman, 5 ft 5 in and 150 lb, exercising 1 to 3 times a week. Her BMR is about 1,381 kcal and her maintenance is 1,899 kcal a day. To lose 1 lb a week she would eat about 1,399 kcal (74% of maintenance); the 2 lb a week target of 899 kcal is flagged because it is under the 1,200 kcal minimum for women.
  • Metric, Karachi: a 28-year-old man, 172 cm and 82 kg, with a desk job and no exercise (sedentary). His BMR is 1,760 kcal, so maintenance is 2,112 kcal a day. Losing 0.5 kg a week means about 1,612 kcal a day. The 1 kg a week target (1,112 kcal) is under the 1,500 kcal floor for men, so a slower rate is the safe choice.
  • Moving more instead: the same man could keep eating 2,112 kcal and add exercise. Moving up to light activity (exercise 1 to 3 times a week) burns about 308 kcal more a day by the formula, roughly 0.28 kg a week; daily exercise burns about 616 kcal more, roughly 0.56 kg a week.
  • Metric, Peshawar: a 45-year-old woman, 155 cm and 70 kg, with light activity. Her maintenance is 1,764 kcal a day and her mild loss target (0.25 kg a week) is 1,514 kcal. The 0.5 kg a week target of 1,264 kcal is still above her 1,200 kcal floor, but it leaves little room for treats or festive meals, so many people in her position start with the mild rate.
  • Muscular build, Katch-McArdle: a 30-year-old man, 178 cm and 85 kg with 15% body fat, who trains hard 6 to 7 times a week. Mifflin-St Jeor gives a BMR of 1,818 kcal and maintenance of 3,135 kcal. Katch-McArdle, which counts his 72.3 kg of lean mass, gives 1,931 kcal and 3,330 kcal. For lean, muscular people the lean-mass formula usually gives the higher and more realistic figure, so he would base a mild gain target on about 3,580 kcal rather than 3,385 kcal.

The pattern in these examples is common: shorter and lighter people, and women in general, have lower maintenance needs, so the fast loss rate quickly runs into the safety floor. A larger deficit is easier to reach safely by adding activity than by cutting more food.

How many calories do men, women and teens need?

For a quick reference without a calculator, the Dietary Guidelines for Americans 2020-2025 (Appendix 2) list estimated daily needs by age, sex and activity. They are based on a reference man 5 ft 10 in tall weighing 154 lb (about 70 kg) and a reference woman 5 ft 4 in tall weighing 126 lb (about 57 kg). Moderately active means activity equal to walking about 1.5 to 3 miles a day at 3 to 4 miles per hour on top of daily life; active means more than 3 miles a day.

Pakistan's own guidance gives similar figures. The Pakistan Dietary Guidelines for Better Nutrition (2018) estimate that an adult man of 60 kg, with a BMR of 1,515 kcal, needs about 2,318 kcal a day when sedentary, 2,727 kcal with moderate to heavy activity and 3,485 kcal with vigorous activity. For a woman of 55 kg with a BMR of 1,241 kcal the figures are 1,899, 2,234 and 2,854 kcal a day.

Pregnancy and breastfeeding raise needs. The same US guidelines add nothing in the first trimester, about 340 kcal a day in the second and about 452 kcal in the third, then about 330 kcal a day in the first six months of breastfeeding and about 400 kcal in the second six months. This calculator does not add these amounts, so add them to your maintenance figure and follow your doctor's advice.

Teenagers aged 15 to 17 can use the calculator, but they are still growing, so they should not follow a weight-loss target without a doctor. For younger children, use the BMI Calculator to check growth on the charts instead.

Do needs really fall with age? The tables drop by a few hundred kcal between the twenties and the sixties, and the formulas subtract a little for every year. A large 2021 study in Science, which measured daily energy use with doubly labelled water in thousands of people, found that once body size and lean mass are taken into account, energy use stays fairly stable from about 20 to 60 and declines after 60. Much of the drop in middle age therefore reflects lost muscle and less movement, which strength training and daily walking can slow.

Calorie deficit and surplus: how fast can you lose or gain?

A calorie deficit means eating less energy than you burn, so the body draws on its stores; a calorie surplus is the opposite. The calculator uses the classic rule from Wishnofsky (1958) that about 3,500 kcal equals one pound of body weight (about 7,700 kcal per kg). That is where 500 kcal a day for 1 lb a week comes from.

The rule works well for the first weeks but overstates long-term loss. A 2011 Lancet analysis by Kevin Hall and colleagues showed that as you lose weight your body burns less, so the response to a fixed cut is slow, with a half-time of about one year. Put simply, a steady 500 kcal deficit gives fast early progress that gradually levels off unless you recalculate at your new weight. A follow-up study of contestants from a televised weight-loss contest found that their resting metabolism stayed lower than expected for their size six years later.

Weight-management guidelines from the American Heart Association, the American College of Cardiology and The Obesity Society (2013) suggest one of these approaches: about 1,200 to 1,500 kcal a day for women and 1,500 to 1,800 kcal for men, or a daily deficit of 500 to 750 kcal. Very low calorie diets, under 800 kcal a day, should be used only in a medical setting with monitoring.

What you lose matters as much as how much. A deficit takes some muscle along with fat, and resistance exercise plus enough protein limits that. The International Society of Sports Nutrition (2017) considers 1.4 to 2.0 g of protein per kg of body weight a day enough for most people who exercise, and notes that trained people cutting calories may benefit from more; the Protein Calculator shows a daily target.

  • Find your maintenance figure with the calculator and pick a target that stays above 1,200 kcal (women) or 1,500 kcal (men).
  • Keep the target for 2 to 3 weeks, weighing yourself under the same conditions several times a week; single readings jump around with water and food in the gut, so compare weekly averages.
  • Compare the average weekly change with the rate you chose. If you are losing faster or slower than planned, move the target by 100 to 200 kcal a day.
  • Recalculate after every 4 to 5 kg of change, because a lighter body needs less energy.
  • To gain weight, start with the mild or normal gain target (plus 250 or 500 kcal); the NHS suggests about 300 to 500 extra kcal a day for underweight adults. Pair it with strength training so more of the gain is muscle.

Zigzag calorie cycling: how the two plans work

Zigzag dieting, also called calorie cycling, means eating more on some days and less on others while the weekly total matches your target. The idea is that occasional higher days make a diet easier to live with and may soften the drop in metabolism that comes with steady restriction. Plan 1 keeps Saturday and Sunday near maintenance and lowers the five weekdays; plan 2 rises and falls through the week and never goes above maintenance or below your safety floor.

The research is thinner than the popularity. The MATADOR trial (2018) compared continuous dieting with two-week blocks of dieting alternated with two-week breaks at maintenance in men with obesity; among those who finished, the intermittent group lost more weight (14.1 kg against 9.1 kg). That trial used two-week blocks, not day-to-day changes, so it does not prove that zigzag days work better. What clearly matters is the weekly total.

To follow a plan day to day, decide in advance which days are higher and keep the difference in one or two meals, for example an extra chapati and a glass of milk on a high day rather than snacks all evening. Keep protein steady on every day, because the low days are when hunger is strongest. Weigh yourself on the same day of the week, since a high-calorie day can add water weight that is gone two days later.

Calories in common Pakistani foods

Counting calories is easier when you know the usual portions. The Pakistan Dietary Guidelines for Better Nutrition (2018) group foods into servings with average energy values, which suits home cooking where recipes vary. The rows marked USDA come from the US FoodData Central database, which measures single plain foods and is handy for checking a specific item.

Those guidelines also show where Pakistani calories come from: wheat supplies about 46 to 52% of daily energy, cooking oils about 13 to 14%, dairy 10 to 13% and sugars about 10%. They name sweets, bakery items, fried foods, fast food and heavy use of oil and ghee as the main reasons for rising overweight in the country.

Not all calories act the same: protein, carbs, fat, fibre and alcohol

Food labels do not burn the food to measure its energy. Under rules such as the EU food information regulation (1169/2011), the energy on the label is calculated from the grams of each nutrient times a fixed factor, shown in the table. Fat carries more than twice the energy of protein or carbohydrate per gram, which is why oily dishes add up so quickly.

Your body also spends energy digesting food. A 2004 review by Klaas Westerterp put this diet-induced thermogenesis at about 5 to 15% of daily energy use on a mixed diet, higher when the diet is rich in protein and lower when it is rich in fat; protein also helps you feel full. Processing matters too: in a 2019 NIH trial, people offered ultra-processed meals ate about 508 kcal a day more than when offered unprocessed meals matched for nutrients, and gained about 0.9 kg in two weeks.

For quality, the WHO (healthy diet fact sheet, 2026) advises keeping free sugars under 10% of energy (about 50 g on 2,000 kcal), ideally under 5%, total fat at 30% or less, saturated fat at 10% or less, trans fat at 1% or less and salt under 5 g a day, with at least 400 g of fruit and vegetables daily.

The Pakistan Dietary Guidelines make the same points in local terms: make half of your daily cereals whole grains, eat five servings of fresh vegetables and fruit a day, take two to three servings of milk and milk products, limit oil and fat in cooking, and cut down on sugar, soft drinks, sweets, bakery products and commercial fruit drinks. A day of 1,800 kcal from whole-wheat chapati, dal, vegetables, yogurt and fruit keeps you fuller and better nourished than the same 1,800 kcal from white bread, fried snacks and sweet tea.

Calories and joules: the joule is the SI unit of energy, and the food calorie is 1,000 small calories. Burning calories, and tips for counting them

Exercise energy is usually estimated with METs. One MET is about 1 kcal per kg of body weight per hour, so a walk rated 3.8 METs burns about 3.8 × your weight in kg each hour. The values below come from the 2024 Adult Compendium of Physical Activities and include the energy you would burn anyway at rest, so the extra over sitting still is a little lower.

  • Count drinks: sweet tea, juices, soft drinks and milkshakes add energy without filling you up much.
  • Measure for a week before you estimate by eye. Cups, spoons and a kitchen scale reveal how big a plate of biryani or a serving of oil really is.
  • Do not eat back every exercise calorie that a watch or app reports; those numbers are estimates too, and the activity factor already covers your usual exercise.
  • Keep protein in every meal and fill half the plate with vegetables, salad or fruit, which makes a lower target easier to hold.
  • Plan for occasions such as weddings, Eid and Ramadan iftars instead of abandoning the plan; a higher day balanced by lighter days keeps the week on track.
  • Re-run the calculator when your weight, job or exercise routine changes.

How it works

First the calculator estimates your basal metabolic rate (BMR): the energy your body burns at complete rest. It then multiplies the BMR by a factor for how active you are to get the calories that keep your weight steady (maintenance). Eating about 500 kcal a day less than that loses roughly 1 lb (0.5 kg) a week, because a pound of body fat holds about 3,500 kcal; 250 and 1,000 kcal give the slower and faster rates, and the same amounts added give weight gain. Any loss level under 1,500 kcal a day for men or 1,200 kcal for women is flagged, because going lower is only safe with a doctor. The zigzag plans spread the same weekly total unevenly over the days, which some people find easier to keep up.

The formula

  • Mifflin-St Jeor BMR: men 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age + 5; women the same − 161
  • Revised Harris-Benedict BMR: men 13.397W + 4.799H − 5.677A + 88.362; women 9.247W + 3.098H − 4.330A + 447.593
  • Katch-McArdle BMR: 370 + 21.6 × lean mass (kg), lean mass = weight × (1 − body fat % ÷ 100)
  • Maintenance = BMR × activity factor (1.2 sedentary to 1.9 extra active), rounded to whole kcal
  • Loss: maintenance − 250, − 500 or − 1,000 kcal a day; gain: + 250, + 500 or + 1,000 kcal a day
  • Zigzag plan 1: weekdays = (7 × target − 2 × maintenance) ÷ 5 (never under the safe minimum); weekend days take the rest of the weekly total
  • 1 kcal = 4.1868 kJ

A worked example

A 25-year-old man, 5 ft 10 in (177.8 cm) and 165 lb (74.8 kg), who exercises 4 to 5 times a week: BMR = 10 × 74.84 + 6.25 × 177.8 − 5 × 25 + 5 ≈ 1,740 kcal. Maintenance = 1,740 × 1.465 ≈ 2,549 kcal a day. To lose 1 lb a week he would eat about 2,549 − 500 = 2,049 kcal a day, 80% of maintenance.

Questions

How many calories should I eat to lose weight?

Start from your maintenance calories and subtract 250 to 500 kcal a day, which loses about 0.25 to 0.5 kg (0.5 to 1 lb) a week. Stay above about 1,200 kcal a day for women and 1,500 kcal for men unless a doctor supervises you. The calculator shows all three loss levels and flags any that are too low.

How do I calculate my calorie deficit?

Your deficit is your maintenance calories minus what you actually eat. If the calculator says you burn 2,100 kcal a day and you eat 1,600 kcal, your deficit is 500 kcal a day, or about 3,500 kcal a week. Track your weight for 2 to 3 weeks to see whether the real result matches.

What is a calorie surplus, and how much do I need to gain weight?

A surplus means eating more than you burn. About 250 to 500 kcal a day above maintenance gives a slow, steady gain of roughly 0.25 to 0.5 kg a week. Combine it with strength training and enough protein if you want the gain to be mostly muscle rather than fat.

Which BMR formula should I pick?

Mifflin-St Jeor is the default because studies find it the most accurate for most adults. The revised Harris-Benedict formula gives slightly higher numbers. Katch-McArdle uses your lean mass instead of height and age, so it suits people who know their body fat percentage, especially lean and muscular people.

Why is there a minimum of 1,200 or 1,500 calories?

Below about 1,200 kcal a day for women and 1,500 kcal for men it becomes hard to get enough protein, vitamins and minerals, and muscle loss speeds up. Very low calorie diets can be used, but only with a doctor checking on you.

What is a zigzag diet?

Also called calorie cycling: you eat more on some days and less on others, while the weekly total stays the same as eating your target every day. Some people find it easier to stick to and use it to get past a plateau. It does not burn more fat by itself; the weekly total is what counts.

How accurate is the result?

It is an estimate for an average person with your numbers. Real needs can be about 10% higher or lower. Use the result as a starting point, weigh yourself for 2 to 3 weeks, and adjust by 100 to 200 kcal a day if your weight does not move the way you planned.

How many calories are in a roti?

The Pakistan Dietary Guidelines count one chapati as a cereal serving of about 160 kcal, the same as two slices of bread. A commercially made whole-wheat chapati of 43 g has about 129 kcal in the USDA database. Size and any ghee or butter on top change the number a lot, so a large paratha can carry two to three times as much.

Why am I not losing weight on a calorie deficit?

The most common reasons are portions and oil that are larger than you think, drinks and snacks you did not count, and a starting estimate that was too high for your body. Water retention can also hide fat loss for a week or two. Weigh and log your food carefully for 2 weeks, then lower the target by 100 to 200 kcal or add more walking if the weekly average still has not moved.

Can teenagers use this calorie calculator?

It accepts ages 15 and over. Teenagers are still growing, so the maintenance figure is a fair guide, but they should not follow a weight-loss target without a doctor. US guidelines estimate about 1,800 to 2,400 kcal a day for girls and 2,400 to 3,200 kcal for boys aged 16, depending on activity.

How are calories in food calculated?

Labels multiply the grams of each nutrient by standard factors: 4 kcal per gram of protein or carbohydrate, 9 for fat, 7 for alcohol and 2 for fibre, then add them up. For a home recipe, add the calories of every ingredient and divide by the number of servings. Oil and sugar usually make the biggest difference.

What is the difference between kcal and kJ?

Both measure energy. Food labels in many countries list kilojoules; 1 kcal (one food Calorie) equals 4.1868 kJ in the International Table definition used here, or 4.184 kJ in the thermochemical one.
