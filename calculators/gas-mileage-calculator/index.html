What gas mileage means

Gas mileage is how far a car travels on a set amount of fuel. This gas mileage calculator measures your car's real figure from two odometer readings and the fuel you put in at a fill-up, so it reflects your own roads, traffic and driving rather than a lab test.

Different countries write it differently. The US uses miles per gallon (mpg), where higher is better. Pakistan, India and much of Asia talk about kilometres per litre (km/L), often called the fuel average. Europe, Canada and Australia use litres per 100 km (L/100 km), where lower is better. The calculator gives all of them at once.

How to calculate gas mileage with this calculator

  • Fill the tank to the top and write down the odometer reading (or reset a trip meter).
  • Drive as usual until the tank is at least half empty, then fill it to the top again at the same kind of pump if you can.
  • Choose US (miles, US gallons) or Metric (km, litres) with the switch at the top.
  • Enter "Odometer now", "Odometer at the last fill" and "Fuel put in to fill the tank", the amount on the second receipt.
  • Optionally type the price per gallon or per litre and pick your currency to see the cost of this fill and the fuel cost per mile or km.

If you used a trip meter, put 0 as the last reading and the trip figure as the reading now. A reading now that is lower than the last one gives a clear message instead of a negative mileage.

Worked examples in miles and in kilometres

In miles: the odometer read 45,210 at the last fill and 45,622 now, and 12.4 US gallons filled the tank at $3.19 a gallon. The car drove 412 miles, so the gas mileage is 412 ÷ 12.4 = 33.23 mpg, which is 7.08 L/100 km or 14.13 km/L. The fill cost $39.56, so fuel cost $0.096 per mile.

In kilometres, with rupees: a car in Lahore went from 23,400 km to 23,812 km, and 31.5 litres filled the tank at Rs 265 per litre (type the price on your own receipt, since fuel prices change). The fuel average is 412 ÷ 31.5 = 13.08 km/L, or 7.65 L/100 km. The fill cost Rs 8,347.50, which works out to about Rs 20.26 per kilometre.

Fuel cost for a trip and mileage reimbursement

Once you know your mileage, a trip's fuel is the distance divided by it, and the cost is that fuel times the price. Our Fuel Cost Calculator and Mileage Calculator do this for a road trip, including the driving time and a cost split between passengers.

Mileage reimbursement is a different thing. Employers and tax rules often pay a flat rate per mile that covers fuel, wear, insurance and depreciation, not only the fuel you measured here. For 2026 the IRS standard mileage rate for business use is 72.5 cents per mile from January 1 to June 30 and 76 cents per mile from July 1 to December 31; in 2025 it was 70 cents. The medical and moving rate is 20.5 cents and then 23.5 cents in 2026, and the charity rate 14 cents. These rates change, so check the IRS page for the year you claim.

To work out a claim, multiply the business miles from your log by the rate for the dates you drove. Keep a record of each trip's date, purpose and miles, because the claim rests on that log, not on your fuel receipts.

What lowers or raises your gas mileage

  • Driving style: according to fueleconomy.gov, hard acceleration, hard braking and speeding waste a lot of fuel: the loss is put at about 15-30% on the highway and 10-40% in city stop-and-go driving.
  • Speed: mileage usually drops quickly above 50 mph (80 km/h), and slowing down by 5 to 10 mph can improve it by 7% to 14%.
  • Idling: an idling engine can burn a quarter to a half gallon an hour (about 1 to 2 litres), depending on the engine and the air conditioner.
  • Weight: an extra 100 lb (45 kg) in the car can lower mpg by about 1%, more in small cars.
  • Short trips, cold engines, roof racks, under-inflated tyres and heavy use of the air conditioner all add to the fuel used.

Tips for an accurate mileage figure

  • Fill to the first click of the pump both times; topping off past it adds fuel that may not be counted the same way each time.
  • Average several fills. One tank can be skewed by a long highway trip or a week of city driving; adding the distances and the fuel of three or four fills gives a steadier result.
  • Bigger tyres change the odometer. A larger outside diameter covers more ground per turn than the odometer counts, so the car shows fewer miles than it drove and the mileage looks worse than it is. Use our Tire Size Calculator to see the error and correct the distance.
  • Compare like with like: a figure in UK gallons or in km/L is not the same number as US mpg, so convert first.
  • The car's trip computer is an estimate; the pump-and-odometer method on this page is the one to trust.

How it works

Fill the tank to the top and note the odometer. Drive as usual, fill the tank to the top again, and note the odometer and how much fuel went in. The distance between the two readings divided by the fuel put in the second time is your real fuel economy. Choose US units (miles and US gallons) or metric (kilometres and litres) with the switch at the top; the result is shown in miles per US gallon, miles per UK gallon, kilometres per litre and litres per 100 km. Add the price to see what the fill cost and what each mile or kilometre cost you.

The formula

  • Distance = odometer now − odometer at the last fill
  • mpg = miles ÷ US gallons; km/L = km ÷ litres
  • L/100 km = 100 ÷ (km/L) = 235.215 ÷ mpg (1 mile = 1.609344 km, 1 US gallon = 3.785411784 L)
  • Fill cost = fuel × price; cost per mile or km = fill cost ÷ distance

A worked example

The odometer read 12,000 miles at the last fill and reads 12,360 now, and 15 gallons filled the tank. That is 360 ÷ 15 = 24 mpg, or 9.8 L/100 km. At $3 a gallon the fill cost $45, so each mile cost $45 ÷ 360 = $0.125.

Questions

How do I calculate gas mileage?

Fill the tank, note the odometer, drive, fill it again and note the odometer and the gallons or litres added. Divide the distance by the fuel: 412 miles on 12.4 gallons is 33.23 mpg. The calculator also gives L/100 km, km/L and the cost.

What is the gas mileage formula?

Gas mileage = distance driven ÷ fuel used. In miles and US gallons that is mpg; in kilometres and litres it is km/L. Litres per 100 km = 100 ÷ km/L, or 235.215 ÷ mpg.

How do I calculate gas mileage in km?

Switch to Metric, enter the two odometer readings in kilometres and the litres added. Dividing the kilometres by the litres gives km/L, the fuel average used in Pakistan and India; 412 km on 31.5 litres is 13.08 km/L, or 7.65 L/100 km.

How do I calculate gas mileage for a trip?

Divide the trip distance by your car's mileage to get the fuel needed, then multiply by the price. The Fuel Cost Calculator and the Mileage Calculator on this site do it in one step, with a round trip and a cost split too.

What is the gas mileage reimbursement rate for 2026?

The IRS business rate is 72.5 cents per mile for January 1 to June 30, 2026, and 76 cents per mile for July 1 to December 31, 2026. Employers may use their own rate, and the IRS can change rates, so check the official page before you claim.

Why must I fill the tank to the top both times?

The fuel you put in at the second fill is exactly what the car burned since the first fill only when the tank is full both times. With a part fill the figure is off by the fuel left in or missing from the tank.

Why is my figure different from the official rating?

Official ratings come from standard lab tests. Real driving with traffic, short trips, high speed, cold weather and air conditioning usually uses more fuel. Your own measured figure is the one to use for trip planning.

How do I turn mpg into L/100 km?

Divide 235.215 by the mpg figure (US gallons). 24 mpg is about 9.8 L/100 km and 40 mpg is about 5.9 L/100 km. For UK gallons divide 282.481 instead. The calculator shows every unit for you.

Do bigger tires affect gas mileage?

They affect both the fuel used and the measurement. Larger, heavier tyres can use a little more fuel, and because the odometer counts fewer miles than the car really covers, the mileage you calculate looks lower than it is. Correct the distance first with the Tire Size Calculator.

What if the current reading is lower than the last one?

Then the readings are swapped or the trip meter was reset, and the calculator says so instead of showing a negative mileage. Enter the higher, later reading as the odometer now.
