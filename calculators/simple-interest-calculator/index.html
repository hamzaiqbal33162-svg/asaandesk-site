What simple interest is

Simple interest is interest worked out only on the original amount, called the principal. It never earns interest on interest, so the same amount is added every year (or every month) and the balance grows in a straight line. This simple interest calculator applies that rule, I = P × r × t, and can find any one of the four parts (end balance, starting amount, rate or time) from the other three, with every step written out.

In simple words: if you lend Rs 150,000 at 12% a year, you earn 12% of Rs 150,000, which is Rs 18,000, every year, whatever the balance has grown to. After 2 years that is Rs 36,000 of interest and Rs 186,000 in all. Compound interest, by contrast, would work out the second year's interest on Rs 168,000 instead.

How to use the simple interest calculator

  • Choose a tab for what you want to find: End balance, Starting amount, Interest rate or Time.
  • Currency: pick it at the top. Rupees, dollars or any other currency work the same way.
  • Starting amount (principal): the money lent, borrowed or deposited at the start.
  • End balance: the principal plus all the interest (shown on the tabs that need it).
  • Interest rate, with Rate is set to % a year or % a month. The Interest rate tab always answers per year and shows the monthly rate underneath.
  • Time, with Time is set to years or months. Decimals are fine, so 0.5 years is six months.

If the rate and the time use different units, for example a monthly rate and a time in years, the first step converts the time so both use the same period. The calculator refuses a 0% rate on the Time tab (the money would never grow) and an end balance below the starting amount, because simple interest can only add to the principal.

Understanding the results

Each tab shows its answer in the headline and the total interest underneath. The End balance and Starting amount tabs also give the interest each year (P × r), the Interest rate tab gives the rate each month, and the Time tab gives the answer in months as well as years.

The steps repeat the formula with your own numbers, which makes the page handy for checking homework. Below them, the Year by year (or Month by month) table lists the interest and the balance for every period; when the time is not a whole number of periods, the last row covers only the part left and earns that share of the interest. The donut chart splits the end balance into principal and interest, and the stacked chart shows the interest building up evenly on top of the principal.

The simple interest formula and its four forms

The basic formula is I = P × r × t: interest equals principal times rate times time, with the rate and the time in the same unit (a yearly rate with years, a monthly rate with months). Rearranged, it answers each of the other questions people ask, including the formula if the rate is missing.

Simple interest for days and between two dates

For a term in days, turn the days into years first. Banks use a day-count rule for this: dividing by 365 (actual/365) is common, while some markets divide by 360. For $10,000 at 7% for 90 days, the actual/365 time is 90 ÷ 365 = 0.246575 years; type that as the time and the interest is $172.60. Calling the same period 3 months gives 3 ÷ 12 = 0.25 years and $175.00.

For a loan or deposit between two dates, count the days first with the Day Counter, then divide by 365 and type the result as years. Always check which day count your lender or bank uses, because it decides the last few rupees or cents.

Simple interest vs compound interest

With compound interest, each year's interest is added to the balance and earns interest itself. Over one year the two are the same; after that compound interest pulls ahead, slowly at first and then quickly. Where simple interest is used, including loans

Simple interest appears in short personal loans between friends or family, some trade credit, bonds that pay their coupon out rather than reinvesting it, and school and exam maths at every level. Many savings accounts, fixed deposits and mortgages compound instead, so read the terms.

Loans with monthly payments need care. A so-called simple interest car or personal loan charges interest each period only on the principal still owed, and the payment is fixed, so the balance falls and so does the interest. That is not the same as this calculator, which assumes nothing is repaid until the end; for monthly payments use the Loan Calculator. A flat-rate loan, which charges P × r × t on the full amount for the whole term while you repay it monthly, costs much more than its flat rate suggests; the Interest Rate Calculator shows the real rate.

In Excel or Google Sheets, put P in A2, the yearly rate in B2 (as 12%) and the years in C2. The interest is =A2*B2*C2 and the end balance is =A2*(1+B2*C2). In Python it is a single line: interest = p * r * t.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Match the units: a yearly rate needs the time in years, a monthly rate needs the time in months. The calculator converts for you if you set the switches.
  • Type the rate as a percent here (12 for 12%); in a spreadsheet formula, use 12% or 0.12.
  • Interest each year is P × r; it never changes with simple interest, which is a quick way to check an answer.
  • To find the rate or the time, the end balance must include the principal, not just the interest.
  • For days, decide on 365 or 360 before you divide, and say which one you used.

How it works

Pick what you want to find with the tabs: the end balance, the starting amount, the interest rate or the time. Fill in the other three; the rate can be per year or per month and the time in years or months, and the calculator converts them to match. Simple interest is paid only on the starting amount, so the same interest is added every period and the balance grows in a straight line. The worked steps show the formula with your numbers.

The formula

  • Interest I = P × r × t (P = starting amount, r = rate per period, t = time in the same periods)
  • End balance A = P + I = P × (1 + r × t)
  • Starting amount P = A ÷ (1 + r × t)
  • Rate r = (A ÷ P − 1) ÷ t; time t = (A ÷ P − 1) ÷ r
  • A rate per year with a time in months: t = months ÷ 12

A worked example

Rs 150,000 lent at 12% a year for 2 years earns I = 150,000 × 0.12 × 2 = Rs 36,000, so the end balance is Rs 186,000. Going the other way, $15,000 that must reach $18,000 at 5% a year needs t = (18,000 ÷ 15,000 − 1) ÷ 0.05 = 4 years.

Questions

What is simple interest in simple words?

It is interest paid only on the amount you started with. If you lend 10,000 at 8% a year, you get 800 every year, no more and no less, however long the loan runs.

How do I calculate simple interest?

Multiply the principal by the rate and the time: I = P × r × t. Rs 150,000 at 12% a year for 2 years gives 150,000 × 0.12 × 2 = Rs 36,000 of interest. Add the principal to get the end balance.

What is the simple interest formula if the rate is missing?

Use r = (A ÷ P − 1) ÷ t, or r = I ÷ (P × t) if you know the interest. $12,000 that grows to $13,800 in 3 years earned 5% a year. Open the Interest rate tab to do it with steps.

How do I calculate simple interest for months?

Either use a monthly rate with the time in months, or divide the months by 12 and use the yearly rate. $8,000 at 1.5% a month for 9 months earns $1,080; the switches under the rate and time let you mix units.

How do I calculate simple interest for days or between two dates?

Count the days, divide by 365 (or 360 if your bank uses it) and type the result as years. $10,000 at 7% for 90 days is 10,000 × 0.07 × 90 ÷ 365 = $172.60.

How do I calculate simple interest on a loan?

If the whole loan is repaid at the end, use I = P × r × t here. If you repay monthly, the interest falls as the balance falls, so use a loan calculator; and if the lender charges a flat rate on the full amount, check the real rate with the interest rate calculator.

What is the difference between simple and compound interest?

Simple interest is always worked out on the starting amount, so you earn the same interest every year. Compound interest is worked out on the growing balance, so the interest itself earns interest. Over a long time compound interest gives much more; use the Compound Interest Calculator for it.

Where is simple interest used?

In short loans between people, some car and personal loans that quote a flat rate, bonds that pay out their coupons instead of reinvesting them, and many school maths problems. Most bank savings accounts and mortgages use compound interest.

Can I enter the rate per month?

Yes. Switch the rate to % a month. With a time in years, the calculator turns the years into months first (2 years = 24 months) so both use the same period. The rate tab always gives the answer per year, with the monthly rate under it.

How do I calculate simple interest in Excel?

With the principal in A2, the yearly rate in B2 and the years in C2, the interest is =A2*B2*C2 and the end balance is =A2*(1+B2*C2). Type the rate as a percentage, such as 12%.

Why is the last row of the table shorter?

If the time is not a whole number of periods, for example 6.5 years, the last row covers only the part that is left (half a year) and earns that share of a year's interest.
