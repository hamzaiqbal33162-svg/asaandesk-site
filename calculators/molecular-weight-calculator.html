What the molecular weight calculator does

This molecular weight calculator reads a chemical formula and adds up the mass of every atom in it, giving the molar mass in grams per mole (g/mol). Type any formula, from NaCl to K4[Fe(CN)6] or a hydrate such as MgSO4·7H2O, or pick one of 30 common compounds from the list.

Besides the molar mass you get the number of atoms and elements, the mass of a single molecule in grams, a table with each element's count, atomic weight, subtotal and share of the mass, and two charts that split the formula by atoms and by mass. The atomic weights are the 2024 abridged standard values from IUPAC's Commission on Isotopic Abundances and Atomic Weights (CIAAW).

How to enter a chemical formula

  • Pick a compound from the Compound list, or keep "Type a formula" and write it in the Chemical formula box.
  • Start each element with a capital letter and write any second letter small: Na, Cl, Mg, Fe. The formula is case-sensitive, so Co is cobalt but CO is carbon monoxide.
  • Put the number of atoms straight after the symbol (H2O, C6H12O6). Typed subscripts such as H₂SO₄ are read too.
  • Use round, square or curly brackets for groups that repeat, with the count after the bracket: Ca3(PO4)2 or [Co(NH3)6]Cl3.
  • Join the parts of a hydrate with a dot, a full stop, a star or a bullet: MgSO4·7H2O, MgSO4.7H2O or MgSO4*7H2O. A number in front multiplies the first part, as in 2CaSO4·H2O.
  • Leave out charges and states: type the neutral compound, such as KMnO4, not K+ and MnO4−.

How to calculate molecular weight step by step

Every molar mass is worked out the same way: count the atoms of each element, multiply each count by that element's atomic weight, and add the results. Brackets multiply everything inside them, and each water in a hydrate adds 2 hydrogens and 1 oxygen.

Sodium hydroxide, NaOH: 22.990 + 15.999 + 1.0080 = 39.997 g/mol. Sodium is 57.48% of its mass.

Calcium phosphate, Ca3(PO4)2: the bracket doubles the phosphate, so there are 3 Ca, 2 P and 8 O. 3 × 40.078 + 2 × 30.974 + 8 × 15.999 = 120.234 + 61.948 + 127.992 = 310.174 g/mol.

Oxalic acid dihydrate, H2C2O4·2H2O: H2C2O4 is 2 × 1.0080 + 2 × 12.011 + 4 × 15.999 = 90.034 g/mol, and the two waters add 2 × 18.015 = 36.03 g/mol. The total is 126.064 g/mol, of which water is 28.58%. Using the anhydrous 90.034 when the bottle holds the dihydrate is a common lab mistake.

Washing soda, Na2CO3·10H2O, shows how much water a crystal can hold: 105.988 g/mol of sodium carbonate plus 180.15 g/mol of water makes 286.138 g/mol, so 62.96% of its mass is water.

Atomic weight, molecular weight, molar mass and the mole

The atomic weight of an element (its relative atomic mass) is the average mass of its atoms, weighted by how common each isotope is, compared with one twelfth of the mass of a carbon-12 atom. It has no unit. CIAAW publishes the standard values and an abridged table rounded to five significant figures unless the natural spread of isotopes does not allow that, which is why chlorine is given as 35.45. Its 2024 table revised gadolinium, lutetium and zirconium, now 91.222.

The molecular weight (relative molecular mass) is the sum of the atomic weights in one molecule, also without a unit. The molar mass is the mass of one mole of the substance, in g/mol, and the same number gives the mass of one molecule in daltons (Da). Since the 2019 revision of the SI, a mole is exactly 6.022 140 76 × 10²³ particles, so for everyday work the three numbers are the same: water is 18.015 g/mol and one water molecule is 18.015 Da.

Molecular weight is therefore measured in g/mol for amounts of a substance, in daltons or kilodaltons (kDa) for single molecules such as proteins, and in kg/mol in strict SI units (18.015 g/mol = 0.018015 kg/mol). Elements with no stable isotope, such as technetium, have no standard atomic weight; the calculator uses the mass number of the longest-lived isotope in brackets, [97], and warns you.

Using molar mass: moles, grams, solutions and formulas

Molar mass links grams and moles: moles = mass ÷ molar mass, and mass = moles × molar mass. So 10 g of NaOH is 10 ÷ 39.997 = 0.25 mol, and 0.5 mol of NaCl is 0.5 × 58.44 = 29.22 g. To make 1 liter of a 0.1 mol/L sodium chloride solution you dissolve 0.1 × 58.44 = 5.844 g; the molarity calculator does this for any volume and concentration.

Molar mass also turns an empirical formula into a molecular formula. The empirical formula CH2O has a mass of 30.026 g/mol. If a compound with that empirical formula has a molar mass of 180.156 g/mol, it contains 180.156 ÷ 30.026 = 6 units, so its molecular formula is C6H12O6, glucose. Both have the same percent composition (40.00% carbon by mass), which is why a mass analysis alone cannot tell them apart.

Gases, air, proteins and DNA

For a gas, the ideal gas law gives a molecular weight formula from measurements: M = ρRT ÷ P, where ρ is the density, T the temperature in kelvins, P the pressure and R = 8.314 462 618 J/(mol·K). Run the other way, carbon dioxide's 44.009 g/mol means an ideal gas density of about 1.96 g/L at 0 °C and 101.325 kPa.

Air is a mixture, not a compound, so it has no formula to type. NASA lists dry air as 78.08% nitrogen and 20.95% oxygen by volume, with 9,340 ppm of argon and about 420 ppm of carbon dioxide, and gives a mean molecular weight of 28.97. Weighting this calculator's values for N2, O2, Ar and CO2 by those shares gives the same 28.97 g/mol.

Proteins and DNA are too large to type as formulas, so they are worked out from their sequence. Expasy's Compute pI/Mw adds the average masses of the amino acids plus one water molecule, because each peptide bond releases a water: glycine, C2H5NO2, is 75.067 g/mol, and the dipeptide glycylglycine, C4H8N2O3, is 2 × 75.067 − 18.015 = 132.119 g/mol. For nucleic acids, Thermo Fisher gives the approximate formulas MW ≈ n × 607.4 + 157.9 for double-stranded DNA of n base pairs, n × 303.7 + 79.0 for single-stranded DNA and n × 320.5 + 159.0 for single-stranded RNA of n nucleotides. A 1,000 bp double-stranded DNA is therefore about 607,558 Da, or 607.6 kDa.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Check capital letters first. CO2 is carbon dioxide; Co2 would be two cobalt atoms; a lower-case co2 is rejected with a hint.
  • Count brackets carefully: Ca3(PO4)2 has 8 oxygens, not 4. The steps under the result list the final count of each element, so a slip is easy to see.
  • Use the hydrate form that is on the label. MgSO4·7H2O (246.466 g/mol) is about twice the molar mass of anhydrous MgSO4 (120.361 g/mol), so weighing the wrong one gives a solution about half or twice as strong as planned.
  • Half or odd hydrates need whole numbers: write plaster of Paris, CaSO4·½H2O, as 2CaSO4·H2O (290.283 g/mol for two formula units, so 145.14 g/mol per CaSO4·½H2O).
  • Keep sensible precision. The atomic weights have five significant figures at most, so a molar mass quoted to three decimals is already as precise as the data allow.
  • For deuterated compounds, type D for deuterium (D2O is 20.027 g/mol) and T for tritium; their atomic masses are used.

How it works

Type the formula of a molecule or salt, or pick a common one. Write element symbols with a capital first letter and a small second letter (Na, Cl, Fe), put the number of atoms after each symbol, and use brackets for groups that repeat, such as Ca(OH)2 or Ca3(PO4)2. For a hydrate, join the parts with a dot: MgSO4·7H2O (a full stop or * works too). The calculator counts every element, multiplies each count by its standard atomic weight and adds them up. The table shows how much each element adds and its share of the mass and of the atoms.

The formula

  • Molar mass = sum over the elements of (count × standard atomic weight)
  • Brackets: every count inside (…)ₙ, […]ₙ or {…}ₙ is multiplied by n
  • Hydrates: MgSO4·7H2O = MgSO4 + 7 × H2O
  • Share of mass of an element = count × atomic weight ÷ molar mass × 100%
  • Mass of one molecule = molar mass ÷ Avogadro constant (6.02214076 × 10²³ per mole)

A worked example

Epsom salt, magnesium sulfate heptahydrate, MgSO4·7H2O: Mg 1 × 24.305 + S 1 × 32.06 + O 11 × 15.999 + H 14 × 1.0080 = 24.305 + 32.06 + 175.989 + 14.112 = 246.466 g/mol. The seven water molecules add 126.105 g/mol, which is 51.17% of the mass.

Questions

What is molecular weight in chemistry?

It is the total of the atomic weights of all the atoms in one molecule, also called the relative molecular mass. It tells you how heavy a molecule is compared with one twelfth of a carbon-12 atom. Sulfuric acid, H2SO4, has a molecular weight of 98.072.

What is molecular weight measured in?

Strictly it has no unit, but in practice it is given as a molar mass in grams per mole (g/mol), or in daltons (Da) or kilodaltons (kDa) for a single molecule. In SI base units the molar mass is in kg/mol, so 98.072 g/mol is 0.098072 kg/mol.

Are molecular weight and molar mass the same?

For everyday chemistry, yes. The molar mass is the mass of one mole in grams (g/mol); the molecular weight is the same number without a unit, or in daltons for a single molecule. Water is 18.015 g/mol, and one water molecule weighs 18.015 Da.

How do I calculate the molecular weight of a compound?

Count the atoms of each element, multiply each count by the element's atomic weight and add the results. For NaOH that is 22.990 + 15.999 + 1.0080 = 39.997 g/mol. The calculator shows the same steps for any formula you type.

What is the molecular weight of sodium chloride (NaCl)?

58.44 g/mol: sodium 22.990 plus chlorine 35.45. Sodium is 39.34% of the mass and chlorine 60.66%.

What is the molecular weight of CO2 and of oxygen?

Carbon dioxide, CO2, is 12.011 + 2 × 15.999 = 44.009 g/mol. Oxygen gas, O2, is 2 × 15.999 = 31.998 g/mol, while a single oxygen atom is 15.999.

Why does the formula have to be case-sensitive?

Because capital letters start a new element. Co is cobalt, but CO is carbon and oxygen (carbon monoxide). Likewise Sn is tin and SN would be sulfur and nitrogen.

Which atomic weights are used?

The 2024 abridged table of standard atomic weights from IUPAC's Commission on Isotopic Abundances and Atomic Weights (CIAAW), given to five significant figures. Elements with no stable isotope, such as technetium, have no standard atomic weight, so the mass number of their longest-lived isotope is used and shown in brackets.

How do I enter a hydrate or a complex?

Join the parts of a hydrate with a dot, a full stop or a star: MgSO4·7H2O, MgSO4.7H2O or MgSO4*7H2O all work. Square or curly brackets can hold round ones, as in [Co(NH3)6]Cl3. Leave out charges: type the neutral salt.

Can it calculate the molecular weight of a protein or DNA?

Only from a chemical formula. Proteins and DNA are usually worked out from their sequence instead: a protein is the sum of its amino acid residue masses plus one water, and double-stranded DNA is roughly 607.4 Da per base pair plus 157.9. A sequence tool such as Expasy's Compute pI/Mw does this for proteins.

What is the molecular weight of air?

Air is a mixture, so it has an average rather than a formula weight. NASA gives the mean molecular weight of dry air as 28.97, which matches a weighted average of nitrogen (28.014), oxygen (31.998), argon (39.95) and carbon dioxide (44.009) at their shares of the air.
