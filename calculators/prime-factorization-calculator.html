What prime factorization is

Prime factorization means writing a whole number as a product of prime numbers. A prime is a whole number above 1 whose only factors are 1 and itself (2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13 …); a composite number has more factors than that. So 84 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 7, or 2² × 3 × 7 with powers, and every number in that product is prime.

The fundamental theorem of arithmetic says that every whole number above 1 has exactly one prime factorization, apart from the order of the factors. However you split 360, by 2 first or by 10 first, you always end with three 2s, two 3s and one 5. That uniqueness is what makes prime factors useful for finding the HCF and LCM, simplifying fractions and square roots, and counting factors. This prime factorization calculator gives the answer with steps, as a product, in power form and as a factor tree.

Understanding the results

  • Prime factorization of n: the power form, such as 2³ × 3² × 5 for 360.
  • Prime factors: every prime in order, repeats included (2, 2, 2, 3, 3, 5).
  • As a product: the same list joined with × signs.
  • Different primes: each prime once (2, 3, 5).
  • Type of number: Prime number, Composite number, or Neither prime nor composite for 1. A prime gets itself as its factorization.
  • Number of factors: how many whole numbers divide n, from the powers: (3 + 1) × (2 + 1) × (1 + 1) = 24 for 360.
  • The steps show each division by the smallest prime that goes in, and the factor tree shows the same splits as branches, with the primes at the ends.

How to find the prime factorization, with steps

Division (ladder) method. Divide by the smallest prime that goes in exactly, write the answer underneath, and repeat until the answer is prime. This is what the calculator shows for 84:

  • 84 ÷ 2 = 42
  • 42 ÷ 2 = 21 (2 still goes in)
  • 21 ÷ 3 = 7 (2 no longer goes in, so try 3)
  • 7 is prime, so stop: 84 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 7 = 2² × 3 × 7

Factor tree method. Split the number into any two factors, then keep splitting each branch until every branch ends in a prime, and ring the primes. 360 can start as 36 × 10 or as 2 × 180; either way the ringed primes at the ends are 2, 2, 2, 3, 3 and 5. The calculator's tree always splits off the smallest prime, so each level is one step of the ladder.

To know when to stop testing, you only need primes up to the square root of what is left. For 97, √97 is about 9.8, and none of 2, 3, 5 or 7 divides it, so 97 is prime. Divisibility rules speed this up: 2 if the last digit is even, 3 if the digit sum is a multiple of 3, 5 if it ends in 0 or 5, and 11 if the alternating digit sum (for 1,001: 1 − 0 + 0 − 1 = 0) is a multiple of 11.

What prime factors are used for

  • HCF and LCM: write both numbers in primes; the HCF takes each shared prime at its lower power and the LCM takes every prime at its higher power. For 84 = 2² × 3 × 7 and 90 = 2 × 3² × 5, the HCF is 2 × 3 = 6 and the LCM is 2² × 3² × 5 × 7 = 1,260. The LCM Calculator does this for whole lists.
  • Simplifying fractions: 72/84 = (2³ × 3²) ÷ (2² × 3 × 7); cancel the shared 2² × 3 and 6/7 is left.
  • Simplifying square roots: pair up equal primes. √72 = √(2² × 2 × 3²) = 2 × 3 × √2 = 6√2.
  • Testing for squares and cubes: 1,764 = 2² × 3² × 7² has only even powers, so it is a perfect square, √1,764 = 2 × 3 × 7 = 42. 1,728 = 2⁶ × 3³ has powers that are multiples of 3, so it is a perfect cube, with cube root 2² × 3 = 12.
  • Counting and adding factors: add 1 to each power and multiply. 1,234,567,890 = 2 × 3² × 5 × 3,607 × 3,803 has 2 × 3 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 48 factors. The Factor Calculator lists them.
  • Cryptography: RSA, a public-key method that NIST approves for digital signatures, is built on a number that is the product of two very large secret primes. Multiplying them is easy, but no efficient method is known for splitting such a product back into its primes when it has hundreds of digits.

Large numbers, primes and how the calculator works

For small factors the calculator uses trial division: test 2, 3, 5, 7 and so on, up to the square root of what is left. A number that might be prime is checked with the Miller-Rabin test, which settles every number of the size this page accepts, and a large composite part with no small factors is split with Pollard's rho method.

That is why big examples come back at once. 600,851,475,143 = 71 × 839 × 1,471 × 6,857. 2⁶⁴ − 1 = 18,446,744,073,709,551,615, the largest 64-bit unsigned number, is 3 × 5 × 17 × 257 × 641 × 65,537 × 6,700,417. And 2,147,483,647, which is 2³¹ − 1, is reported as prime. A 24-digit number made of two very large primes can still take too long, and the page then says so rather than guess.

On a scientific calculator, Casio models such as the fx-991ES PLUS C have a FACT function that factorizes whole numbers of up to 10 digits; a prime factor of more than three digits is shown in brackets, not split. This page goes to 24 digits and shows every step.

For school, common mistakes and related calculators

Expressing a number as a product of its prime factors is a set skill in the Cambridge O Level Mathematics syllabus (4024, exams in 2025 to 2027), with 72 given as the example, alongside finding the HCF and LCM. Answers are usually expected in power form, 2³ × 3², in increasing order of the primes.

  • Stopping at a composite number: 2 × 2 × 21 is not finished, because 21 = 3 × 7.
  • Including 1 as a factor: 1 is not prime, so it never appears in a prime factorization.
  • Calling 2 composite because it is even: 2 is the only even prime.
  • Dropping a repeated prime: 72 is 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 × 3, not 2 × 3 × 3.

How it works

Type a positive whole number. Its prime factorization is the list of primes that multiply together to give it; every whole number above 1 has exactly one such list. The calculator divides by the smallest prime that goes in exactly, then repeats with the answer until a prime is left. The steps show this ladder of divisions and the factor tree shows the same splits as a picture.

The formula

  • n = p₁ × p₂ × … × pₖ with every p a prime, written in order from the smallest
  • Grouping equal primes gives the power form, such as 360 = 2³ × 3² × 5
  • Trial division by 2, 3, 5, 7, … up to the square root of what is left; larger factors are found with Pollard's rho method
  • Primality is checked with the Miller-Rabin test, which is exact for every number this tool accepts

A worked example

360 ÷ 2 = 180, 180 ÷ 2 = 90, 90 ÷ 2 = 45, 45 ÷ 3 = 15, 15 ÷ 3 = 5, and 5 is prime. So 360 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 × 5 = 2³ × 3² × 5.

Questions

How do I find the prime factorization of a number?

Divide by the smallest prime that goes in exactly (2, then 3, then 5 …), write down the prime, and repeat with the answer until the answer is prime. The primes you divided by, plus the last one, are the prime factorization. For 60: 60 ÷ 2 = 30, 30 ÷ 2 = 15, 15 ÷ 3 = 5, so 60 = 2² × 3 × 5.

What is the prime factorization of 36?

36 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 = 2² × 3². Both powers are even, which is why 36 is a perfect square (6 × 6), and it has (2 + 1) × (2 + 1) = 9 factors.

What is the prime factorization of 48?

48 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 = 2⁴ × 3. Halve it four times (48, 24, 12, 6, 3) and the 3 left over is prime.

What is the prime factorization of 84?

84 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 7 = 2² × 3 × 7. The ladder is 84 ÷ 2 = 42, 42 ÷ 2 = 21, 21 ÷ 3 = 7.

What is the division method of prime factorization?

It is the ladder method: write the number, divide it by a prime that goes in exactly, write the quotient below, and keep going until you reach 1 or a prime. The divisors down the side are the prime factors. The calculator's steps follow exactly this method.

What is a factor tree?

A picture of the splitting: the number sits at the top and branches into a prime (ringed) and the rest, which branches again until only primes are left. The ringed numbers at the ends of the branches are the prime factors.

Is there a formula for prime factorization?

No formula produces the primes directly; you have to search for them, which is why it is slow for huge numbers. Once you have them, n = p₁^a × p₂^b × … gives the number of factors as (a + 1)(b + 1)… and the sum of factors from (1 + p₁ + … + p₁^a)(1 + p₂ + … + p₂^b)….

How do I find the prime factorization of a large number?

By hand, use divisibility tests and trial division up to the square root, or ask for help from a tool: this one handles up to 24 digits using Miller-Rabin and Pollard's rho, so 600,851,475,143 = 71 × 839 × 1,471 × 6,857 comes back at once.

Why does 1 have no prime factors?

1 is neither prime nor composite. Multiplying no primes at all gives 1, so its list of prime factors is empty.

What does it mean when the answer is the number itself?

The number is prime: nothing other than 1 and itself divides it, so it is already its own prime factorization.

How large can the number be?

Up to 24 digits. Numbers with up to 20 digits always work quickly. A longer number made of two very large primes can take too long to split, and the tool then says so.
