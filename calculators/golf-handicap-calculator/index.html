What a golf handicap is

A golf handicap calculator turns your scores into a number that shows how well you can play, so that golfers of different skill can compete fairly. The lower the number, the better the player: a scratch golfer has a handicap of 0, and a bogey golfer, who averages about one over par per hole, is around 18.

Since 2020 most of the golfing world has used one set of rules, the World Handicap System (WHS), run by the USGA and The R&A. It has two numbers. Your Handicap Index measures your ability on a course of standard difficulty and goes with you everywhere. Your course handicap converts that index into the strokes you receive on one particular set of tees, because the same golfer scores higher on a long, tight course than on an easy one.

This page does both: the Course handicap tab works out your strokes for any tees, and the Handicap Index tab estimates your index from up to 20 rounds using the WHS table of differentials.

How to use the golf handicap calculator

  • Course handicap tab: type your Handicap Index (a plus handicap with a minus sign, so +2.1 is −2.1), then the Course Rating of the tees, the Slope Rating (55 to 155) and the Par. All three are printed on the scorecard or listed on the club's website for each set of tees.
  • Handicap Index tab: choose the Number of rounds, from 1 to 20, using your most recent rounds. The first five rows are pre-filled with sample rounds; replace them with your own.
  • For each round choose 18 holes or 9 holes, then type the Course rating, the Slope and your Score. Use your adjusted gross score: no hole counted worse than net double bogey.
  • Set PCC (playing conditions adjustment) for each round: −1, 0, +1, +2 or +3. It is 0 on most days; your club or handicap app shows it next to the round.
  • For 9-hole rounds use the 9-hole Course Rating (usually about half the 18-hole figure). The page warns if a rating looks like the wrong kind for the holes you chose.

Understanding the results

Course handicap gives Your course handicap as a whole number, with the unrounded value, the Index × Slope ÷ 113 part and the Course Rating − Par part shown separately. A plus course handicap means you give strokes back to the course. A note reminds you that many competitions use a playing handicap, a share of the course handicap set by the format of play.

Handicap Index shows Your Handicap Index, the rule used (for example Average of the lowest 2 of 6, minus 1.0), how many 18-hole differentials were counted, the lowest differentials and their average. Worked examples: course handicap and Handicap Index

Course handicap. A golfer with an index of 9.6 plays tees with a Course Rating of 70.4, a Slope Rating of 124 and par 72. 9.6 × 124 ÷ 113 = 10.53, and 70.4 − 72 = −1.6, so the course handicap is 8.93, rounded to 9 strokes. A beginner with an index of 32.5 on tees rated 68.9, slope 115, par 70 gets 33.08 − 1.1 = 31.98, so 32 strokes.

Plus handicap. A strong player with a +1.8 index (typed as −1.8) on hard tees rated 73.1 with a slope of 138 and par 72 gets −2.2 + 1.1 = −1.10, a course handicap of +1: one stroke added to the gross score instead of taken off.

Handicap Index from five rounds. Scores of 91, 86, 94, 83 and 89 on courses with these ratings and slopes: 71.2/127, 70.5/122, 72.0/131, 69.8/119 and 71.2/127. The differentials are 17.6, 14.4, 19.0, 12.5 and 15.8. With five differentials WHS uses the lowest one, so the Handicap Index is 12.5.

Six rounds. Add a sixth round of 87 on a 70.9/125 course on a day with PCC +1. Its differential is (113 ÷ 125) × (87 − 70.9 − 1) = 13.7. Now the rule is the average of the lowest 2, minus 1.0: (12.5 + 13.7) ÷ 2 − 1.0 = 12.1.

Only three rounds. Of the first three rounds (17.6, 14.4 and 19.0), the lowest is 14.4, and with three differentials 2.0 comes off, giving 12.4.

Nine-hole rounds and the 2024 change

A 9-hole round gives its own differential from the 9-hole Course Rating and Slope. On this page each two 9-hole rounds, in the order you enter them, are added together into one 18-hole differential. For example, a 44 on a 35.6/123 nine gives 7.7 and a 42 on a 35.1/118 nine gives 6.6; together they make one 18-hole differential of 14.3. With three 18-hole rounds plus that pair, you have four differentials, so the index is the lowest (14.3) minus 1.0, which is 13.3.

Since 2024 the official system can also turn a single 9-hole score into an 18-hole differential by adding an expected score for the other nine based on your index on the day. That needs data this page does not have, so a lone 9-hole round here waits for a partner and does not count yet.

Course Rating, Slope Rating and the 113 standard

The Course Rating is the average good score of a scratch golfer from a set of tees in normal conditions, such as 70.4. The Slope Rating measures how much harder the course plays for a bogey golfer than for a scratch golfer. Slope Ratings run from 55 to 155, and a course of standard difficulty has a slope of 113, which is why 113 appears in both formulas.

On a course with a slope of 113 and a Course Rating equal to par, your course handicap equals your Handicap Index. A slope above 113 gives you more strokes and a slope below 113 gives fewer; a Course Rating above par adds the difference, and one below par takes it away.

What the official index adds, and common mistakes

  • Caps. The official index cannot rise too quickly: increases more than 3.0 above your lowest index of the past 365 days are cut by half (the soft cap), and the total increase is limited to 5.0 (the hard cap). This page does not have that history, so it does not apply the caps.
  • Exceptional scores. A round far below your index triggers an extra reduction in the official system; this page does not model it either.
  • Gross versus adjusted score. Type the adjusted gross score, with every hole capped at net double bogey (par + 2 + any strokes you receive on that hole), not the raw total. A Stableford card shows the same idea: a hole worth 0 points is at least net double bogey.
  • Wrong tees. Use the rating and slope of the tees you actually played; the same course can have very different figures from the back and front tees.
  • Old rounds. Only your most recent 20 scores count, so drop older rounds when you add new ones.

How it works

The Course handicap tab turns your Handicap Index into the strokes you get on one set of tees: type the index and the tees' Course Rating, Slope Rating and par, which are on the scorecard or the club's website. The Handicap Index tab works out an index from your most recent rounds, up to 20. For each round enter the Course Rating, the Slope Rating, your adjusted gross score (your score with no hole worse than net double bogey) and the playing conditions adjustment (PCC) your club published, usually 0. Every round gives a score differential; the index is the lowest one, or the average of a few of the lowest, depending on how many rounds you have, with a small reduction when you have only 3, 4 or 6. Nine-hole rounds show their own differential, and each two of them are added into one 18-hole differential in the order you entered them. A single 9-hole round waits for a partner. The official system also applies caps that need your full scoring record, so your club's figure can differ.

The formula

  • Course handicap = Handicap Index × Slope Rating ÷ 113 + (Course Rating − Par), rounded to the nearest whole number
  • Score differential = (113 ÷ Slope Rating) × (adjusted gross score − Course Rating − PCC), rounded to 1 decimal
  • Handicap Index from n differentials: 3 → lowest 1 − 2.0; 4 → lowest 1 − 1.0; 5 → lowest 1; 6 → average of lowest 2 − 1.0; 7-8 → lowest 2; 9-11 → 3; 12-14 → 4; 15-16 → 5; 17-18 → 6; 19 → 7; 20 → average of the lowest 8
  • Two 9-hole rounds: their differentials are added into one 18-hole differential; the index is at most 54.0

A worked example

With a Handicap Index of 9.6 on tees with a Course Rating of 70.4, a Slope Rating of 124 and par 72: 9.6 × 124 ÷ 113 = 10.53, plus 70.4 − 72 = −1.6, gives 8.93, so you get 9 strokes on those tees.

Questions

How do I calculate my golf handicap?

Work out a score differential for each recent round: (113 ÷ Slope) × (adjusted score − Course Rating − PCC). Then use the WHS table: with 20 rounds, average the lowest 8; with fewer, use fewer and sometimes subtract a small adjustment. The Handicap Index tab does all of this for up to 20 rounds.

What is the difference between a Handicap Index and a course handicap?

Your Handicap Index measures your ability on a course of standard difficulty and travels with you. The course handicap is the number of strokes you get on one particular set of tees, found from your index and that course's Slope Rating, Course Rating and par.

How do I calculate a golf handicap differential?

Subtract the Course Rating and any PCC from your adjusted gross score, then multiply by 113 and divide by the Slope Rating. A 91 on a 71.2/127 course gives (113 ÷ 127) × 19.8 = 17.6.

How many rounds do I need for a Handicap Index?

At least 54 holes: three 18-hole rounds, or the same from 9-hole rounds added in pairs. With fewer than 20 rounds a smaller share of your best differentials is used, and with 3, 4 or 6 rounds a small reduction is taken off.

How do I calculate a golf handicap for 9 holes?

Mark the round as 9 holes and use the 9-hole Course Rating and Slope. Each two 9-hole differentials are added into one 18-hole differential, which then counts like any other round.

What is an adjusted gross score?

Your score after capping any bad hole at net double bogey: par plus 2 plus any strokes you get on that hole. It stops one disaster hole from pushing your handicap up.

What is the playing conditions calculation (PCC)?

On days when the course plays much harder or easier than normal, for example in strong wind, the handicap system adjusts every differential by -1 to +3. Your club or app shows it with the round; it is 0 on most days.

What is a scratch golfer?

A golfer with a handicap of 0, expected to play to the Course Rating. A bogey golfer has a handicap of about 18.

How do I enter a plus handicap?

Type it with a minus sign: a +2.1 index is -2.1. A plus course handicap is shown with a + sign and means you give strokes back to the course.

Why is my club's index different from this one?

The official index also applies the soft and hard caps, exceptional-score reductions and the 2024 rule for single 9-hole rounds, all of which need your full scoring record. Use this page to understand and check the maths.
