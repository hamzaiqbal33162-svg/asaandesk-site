What volume is

A volume calculator measures how much three-dimensional space a solid takes up, or how much a container can hold. In maths, volume is counted in cubic units: a cube 1 meter on each side has a volume of 1 cubic meter (1 m³), and a box 5 ft by 3 ft by 4 ft holds 60 of those 1-foot cubes, so its volume is 60 ft³.

In physics and science, volume is one of the basic properties of matter, alongside mass. The two are linked by density: density = mass ÷ volume, so volume = mass ÷ density. In chemistry, liquid volumes are usually given in liters (L) and milliliters (mL), and a milliliter is exactly one cubic centimeter.

Capacity is the same idea applied to a container: how much water, fuel or grain it can hold. Tanks, bottles and pools are labelled in liters or gallons rather than cubic meters, so this page gives every answer in liters and US gallons straight away, with a table of other units below. (In trading, "volume" means the number of shares or contracts bought and sold, a different idea that this page does not cover.)

Volume grows faster than size. Doubling every length of a solid multiplies its volume by 2 × 2 × 2 = 8, while its surface area only grows 4 times. A tank twice as long, wide and deep holds eight times as much water, which is why a small change in a measured size can change a volume answer a lot.

How to use the volume calculator

  • Pick the shape from the tabs: Sphere, Cone, Cube, Cylinder, Box, Capsule, Spherical cap, Frustum, Ellipsoid, Pyramid or Tube.
  • Choose the Unit: feet, inches, yards, miles, meters, centimeters, millimeters, kilometers and smaller units down to angstroms. Every size box uses it.
  • Sphere, Cone, Cylinder and Capsule ask for a Radius (r), half the width across. Cone, Cylinder, Box, Frustum and Pyramid ask for a Height (h).
  • Box needs Length (l), Width (w) and Height (h); Cube needs one Edge length (a); Pyramid needs the Base edge (a) of its square base.
  • Capsule asks for the Height of the straight part (h), the cylinder between the two half spheres. Frustum asks for the Top radius (r), the Bottom radius (R) and the Height (h).
  • Spherical cap: fill in any two of Base radius (r), Ball radius (R) and Cap height (h). Ellipsoid: type the three semi-axes a, b and c, each half of a full width.
  • Tube: type the Outer diameter (d1), the Inner diameter (d2) and the Length (l). The answer is the volume of the wall; if you swap the two diameters by mistake, the page swaps them back and says so.
  • Mixed units: open Units and tick Use a different unit for each length. A cylinder with a radius of 2 ft and a height of 6 in then shows 6.2831853071796 ft³ and 10,857.344210806 in³.

Understanding the results and volume units

The headline gives the volume in cubic units of your length unit, followed by the same amount In liters and In US gallons. The steps put your numbers into the formula and show the exact multiple of π when it is short, such as 36π, before the rounded decimal. A collapsed table lists the volume in cubic meters, cubic centimeters, liters, milliliters, cubic feet, cubic inches, cubic yards, US gallons and UK gallons, and a labelled picture shows which size goes where.

Volume units are lengths cubed, so their conversion factors are the length factors cubed. Since 1 ft = 12 in, 1 ft³ = 12 × 12 × 12 = 1,728 in³, and since 1 m = 100 cm, 1 m³ = 1,000,000 cm³.

Answers keep 14 significant digits, and the liter and gallon lines are rounded a little further for reading. Round the final figure to match how precisely you measured: sizes taken with a tape to the nearest centimeter do not justify a volume to the nearest milliliter.

Two patterns make these easy to remember. Every prism-like solid with straight sides (box, cube, cylinder) is base area × height. Every solid that narrows to a point (cone, pyramid) is one third of that. The sphere, cap and ellipsoid are the round ones with 4/3 in front.

Cube, box and rectangular tank volume

For a cube, V = a³: a box with 3 ft edges holds 3 × 3 × 3 = 27 ft³. For a rectangular box, cuboid or rectangular prism, multiply the three sides: V = l × w × h. These two cover rooms, cartons, aquariums, water tanks, trenches and slabs.

Underground water tank: a tank in a Lahore house measures 8 ft long, 6 ft wide and 5 ft deep inside. Its volume is 8 × 6 × 5 = 240 ft³, which the page converts to 6,796.0432 L, about 6,800 litres or 1,795 US gallons. Measure the inside, not the outside, because walls and the slab take up space.

Aquarium: a tank 60 cm long, 30 cm wide and 36 cm high holds 60 × 30 × 36 = 64,800 cm³, which is 64.8 L. Fill it a few centimeters below the rim and subtract gravel and decorations, so the real amount of water is less.

Room volume for air conditioning or ventilation: a room 12 ft × 10 ft × 10 ft has 1,200 ft³ of air, about 33.98 m³.

Concrete, gravel and soil are usually bought by the cubic meter or cubic yard. A slab 10 ft by 10 ft and 4 in thick, entered with mixed units, is 33.333333333333 ft³, or 1.2345679012346 yd³. The Concrete Calculator adds bags and waste for you.

Swimming pool: a pool 10 m long and 5 m wide with an average depth of 1.5 m holds 10 × 5 × 1.5 = 75 m³, which is 75,000 L. For a sloping floor, use the average of the shallow and deep ends as the height.

Cylinder, pipe, tube and capsule volume

A cylinder is a circle pushed straight up, so its volume is the circle's area times the height: V = πr²h. With r = 3 ft and h = 4 ft that is π × 9 × 4 = 36π ≈ 113.10 ft³, about 3,202.56 L.

Overhead water tank: a round plastic tank with an inside radius of 0.6 m (1.2 m across) and a height of 1.2 m holds π × 0.6² × 1.2 = 1.3571680263508 m³, which is 1,357.168 L. Tanks are often sold by capacity, so measuring the inside like this lets you check the label.

Water in a pipe: the water inside a pipe is a cylinder with the pipe's inner radius. A pipe with a 2 inch inner diameter (r = 1 in) and 100 ft long (1,200 in) holds π × 1² × 1,200 = 3,769.9111843078 in³, about 61.78 L or 16.32 US gallons.

Tube wall (the material itself): the Tube tab gives the volume between the outer and inner diameters, π(d1² − d2²)l ÷ 4. A tube 10 cm across outside, 9 cm inside and 600 cm long contains 8,953.5390627309 cm³ of material. Multiply by the material's density to get its mass.

Capsule: a cylinder with a half sphere on each end, the shape of many gas tanks and medicine capsules. With a radius of 0.5 m and a straight part 2 m long, V = π × 0.5² × 2 + 4/3 × π × 0.5³ = 2.0943951023932 m³, about 2,094 L. Note that the straight part does not include the rounded ends.

A horizontal cylinder that is only partly full needs the area of a circular segment instead of the full circle: V = L × (r² × arccos((r − d) ÷ r) − (r − d) × √(2rd − d²)), where d is the depth of the liquid. A tank with r = 0.6 m and L = 2 m holds 2,261.95 L when full, but at a depth of 0.3 m (a quarter of its height) it holds only about 442.21 L, under a fifth, because the bottom of a round tank is narrow. At half depth it holds exactly half, 1,130.97 L.

Sphere, spherical cap and ellipsoid volume

A sphere's volume is 4/3 πr³. A ball with a radius of 10 cm holds 4,188.7902047864 cm³, just under 4.19 L. Because the radius is cubed, doubling it makes the volume eight times bigger, so a ball twice as wide holds about 33.5 L.

Archimedes showed that a sphere fills exactly two thirds of the smallest cylinder that fits around it. A sphere of radius 3 has 36π; the cylinder of radius 3 and height 6 around it has 54π, and 36π ÷ 54π = 2/3.

A spherical cap is a slice off the top of a ball: a dome, a lens, or the liquid in the bottom of a round-bottomed flask. Its volume is 1/3 πh²(3R − h), where R is the ball's radius and h the cap's height. If you know the base radius r instead, the page finds the missing value from r = √(2Rh − h²). A hemispherical tank is a cap with h = R: with R = 3 m it holds 18π ≈ 56.55 m³, half of a full sphere.

An ellipsoid is a stretched sphere with three semi-axes a, b and c. Its volume is simply 4/3 πabc, which becomes the sphere formula when all three are equal. An egg-like shape with semi-axes of 2, 1.5 and 1 cm has 4/3 × π × 2 × 1.5 × 1 = 4π ≈ 12.57 cm³. If you measured full lengths instead of halves, use V = π/6 × L × W × H, about 0.5236 × L × W × H; the semi-axes are just those lengths halved.

Cone, frustum and pyramid volume

A cone holds exactly one third of the cylinder with the same base and height: V = 1/3 πr²h. An ice cream cone 6 cm across (r = 3 cm) and 10 cm deep holds 30π ≈ 94.25 cm³, about 94 mL. The default cone (r = 3, h = 4) has 12π, one third of the 36π cylinder.

A frustum is a cone with the tip cut off flat: a bucket, a flower pot, a lampshade or a tapered tank. Its volume is 1/3 πh(r² + rR + R²). A bucket with a top radius of 15 cm, a bottom radius of 11 cm and a depth of 28 cm holds 14,983.302562521 cm³, almost exactly 15 litres. Do not average the two radii: a cylinder of radius 13 cm would give 14,866 cm³, a little too little.

A pyramid also takes one third of its box: V = 1/3 × base area × height, so a square pyramid is 1/3 a²h. The default (a = 6, h = 4) is 1/3 × 36 × 4 = 48.

With its base of about 230.3 m and original height of about 146.6 m, the Great Pyramid of Giza had a volume of about 2,591,795 m³ by this formula, roughly 2.6 million cubic meters of stone and core.

Why one third? A cube can be cut into three identical square pyramids that meet at one corner, each with a face of the cube as its base and the cube's edge as its height. Each must therefore hold a³ ÷ 3. The same one-third rule holds for every cone and pyramid, whatever the shape of the base, as long as the height is measured straight up.

Shapes not on the tabs, irregular objects and volume from mass

  • Triangular prism: area of the triangle × length, V = ½ × base × triangle height × length. A tent with a triangular end 2 m wide and 1.5 m high, 3 m long, holds ½ × 2 × 1.5 × 3 = 4.5 m³.
  • Any prism or cylinder-like solid, such as a hexagonal nut or an L-shaped beam: area of the end × length. Work out the end with the Area Calculator.
  • Oval (elliptical) tank lying on its side or standing up: π × a × b × length, where a and b are half the two widths of the oval. An oval tank 1.6 m wide and 1 m high (a = 0.8, b = 0.5) and 3 m long holds π × 0.8 × 0.5 × 3 = 3.7699 m³, about 3,770 L.
  • Pit or trench with sloping sides: use the prismoidal formula V = h ÷ 6 × (A₁ + 4Aₘ + A₂), with the top area A₁, the bottom area A₂ and the area Aₘ halfway down. A pit 4 m × 3 m at the top, 2 m × 1 m at the bottom and 1.5 m deep has a middle of 3 m × 2 m, so V = 1.5 ÷ 6 × (12 + 24 + 2) = 9.5 m³.
  • Hemisphere: half of 4/3 πr³, which is 2/3 πr³. Use Spherical cap with the cap height equal to the ball radius.
  • Composite solids: split them into pieces from the tabs and add the volumes, for example a cylinder tank with a cone bottom. Subtract any hollow parts.
  • Irregular objects: sink the object in a measuring jug or a tank of water and read how much the level rises. The rise in volume is the object's volume, the method traditionally credited to Archimedes.
  • From mass and density: V = m ÷ ρ. Water has a density close to 1 kg per litre, so 25 kg of water takes about 25 L. For other materials, divide the mass by the density from a data sheet, keeping the units matched (kg and kg/m³ give m³).

Common mistakes and tips

  • Using the diameter as the radius. A tank 1.2 m across has r = 0.6 m; using 1.2 gives four times the real volume.
  • Mixing units in one formula, such as a radius in feet and a height in inches. Use the mixed-units option or convert first.
  • Converting cubic units with the length factor. 1 m³ is 1,000,000 cm³, not 100 cm³, and 1 ft³ is 1,728 in³, not 12.
  • Measuring the outside of a container. Walls take space; use inside measurements for capacity.
  • Using the slant height of a cone or pyramid. Volume needs the straight-up height from base to tip.
  • Mixing up US and UK gallons. They differ by about 20%.
  • Rounding π to 3.14 early. Keep π, as the steps do, and round the final answer.
  • Not sanity-checking the answer. Before trusting a result, estimate it: a round tank about 1 m across and 1 m high is a little under a 1 m cube, so it should hold somewhat less than 1,000 L (the exact figure is 785.40 L). An answer ten times bigger or smaller usually means a radius and diameter mix-up or a unit slip.

How it works

Pick the shape, choose the unit and type its sizes; the volume appears as you type, with the same amount in liters and US gallons, a labelled picture and the formula worked out with your numbers. When the answer is a neat multiple of π it is shown that way too (for example 15π). If your sizes come in different units, such as a radius in feet and a height in inches, open Units and give each box its own unit: the volume is then worked out once in each unit you used, every length converted first, so you can check it either way. For a spherical cap, fill in any two of base radius, ball radius and cap height and the third is found for you.

The formula

  • Sphere: V = 4/3 × π × r³
  • Cone: V = 1/3 × π × r² × h
  • Cube: V = a³; rectangular box: V = l × w × h
  • Cylinder: V = π × r² × h; tube (hollow cylinder): V = π × (d1² − d2²) × l ÷ 4
  • Capsule: V = π × r² × h + 4/3 × π × r³ (a cylinder with a half sphere at each end)
  • Spherical cap: V = 1/3 × π × h² × (3R − h), with r = √(2Rh − h²) and R = (h² + r²) ÷ 2h
  • Conical frustum: V = 1/3 × π × h × (r² + rR + R²)
  • Ellipsoid: V = 4/3 × π × a × b × c; square pyramid: V = 1/3 × a² × h
  • Units: 1 in = 2.54 cm exactly, 1 ft = 12 in, 1 L = 1,000 cm³, 1 US gallon = 231 in³ = 3.785411784 L

A worked example

A cone with a base radius of 3 m and a height of 5 m: V = 1/3 × π × 3² × 5 = 15π, about 47.12 cubic meters, which is 47,124 liters.

Questions

What is the formula for volume?

It depends on the shape. A box is length × width × height, a cylinder πr²h, a sphere 4/3 πr³, a cone 1/3 πr²h and a pyramid 1/3 × base area × height. The table above lists all eleven shapes on this page with an example.

How do I find how much water a tank holds?

Choose Cylinder for a round tank (radius and height) or Box for a rectangular one (length, width, height), and read the volume in liters or US gallons straight under the main answer. Use the inside measurements of the tank.

How do you calculate the volume of a cylinder?

Square the radius, multiply by π, then by the height: V = πr²h. A cylinder 1.2 m across and 1.2 m high has r = 0.6 m, so V = π × 0.36 × 1.2 = 1.357 m³, which is 1,357 liters.

How do you calculate the volume of a cube?

Multiply the edge length by itself three times: V = a³. A cube with 3 ft edges holds 27 ft³, and one with 10 cm edges holds 1,000 cm³, exactly 1 liter.

How do I convert cubic feet to liters?

Multiply by 28.316846592, the exact number of liters in a cubic foot. So 240 ft³ is about 6,796 L. The page does this for you under every answer.

How many liters are in a cubic meter?

Exactly 1,000. A cubic meter is a cube 100 cm on each side, which is 1,000,000 cm³, and a liter is 1,000 cm³.

How do I calculate volume from mass and density?

Divide the mass by the density: V = m ÷ ρ. With mass in kilograms and density in kilograms per cubic meter, the answer is in cubic meters. For water, 1 kg takes up almost exactly 1 liter.

How do I calculate the volume of a room?

Multiply the floor length by the width and the ceiling height. A room 12 ft × 10 ft × 10 ft has 1,200 ft³, about 34 m³. Use the Box tab and read the cubic meters in the table.

What is the volume of a triangular prism?

Area of the triangular end times the length: ½ × base × height × length. A prism with a triangle 2 m wide and 1.5 m high, 3 m long, has 4.5 m³.

My sizes are in different units. What should I do?

Open Units and switch on a different unit for each length, then pick the unit next to every box. The volume is given in each unit you used, so a radius of 2 ft with a height of 6 in shows the answer in cubic feet and in cubic inches.

Why is the answer shown with π?

Round shapes have π in their formula. When the number in front of π is short, such as 15π, it is shown as well, because that is the exact answer; the long decimal next to it is the same value rounded to 14 significant digits.

Which two values does the spherical cap need?

Any two of the base radius, the ball radius and the cap height. With a base radius and a ball radius two caps are possible (a shallow one and a deep one); the shallow one is used and the height of the deep one is given in a note.
