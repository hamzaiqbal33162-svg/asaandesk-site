What does a finance calculator do?

A finance calculator solves time value of money problems: how much a sum grows to, what a loan payment is, what interest rate a deal really charges, how long a goal takes, or what a future amount is worth today. It works like the five money keys on a financial calculator used in banking and finance classes: N, I/Y, PV, PMT and FV.

You enter any four of the five values and this online finance calculator finds the fifth. The same tool handles a car loan, a home loan, a savings plan, a pension payout or a single deposit, because they all follow the same equation. Under the answer you get the totals, a year-by-year table, a period-by-period schedule and a chart of the balance.

The idea behind it is simple: money now is worth more than the same money later, because money now can earn interest. The equation links today's amount, the payments in between and the amount at the end through the interest rate per period.

Two more settings sit under Periods and timing. Payments per year (P/Y) is 12 for monthly and 1 for yearly. Compounding per year (C/Y) is how often interest is added, usually the same as P/Y. Payments are made at the end of each period (END) for most loans, or at the start (BGN) for rent-style payments and many savings plans.

How to use the finance calculator

  • Choose the tab for the value you want: Future value (FV), Payment (PMT), Interest rate (I/Y), Periods (N) or Present value (PV).
  • Enter the other four values. Type money you pay out as a negative number and money you receive as a positive number.
  • Open Periods and timing if payments are not monthly, if interest is compounded on a different schedule, or if payments are made at the start of each period.
  • Pick your currency. The maths is the same in dollars, rupees or pounds.
  • Read the answer, then check Sum of all payments, Total interest, Rate per period and Time span, and open the schedules for the detail.

When you solve for I/Y, the result shows three rates: the yearly rate compounded C/Y times (the APR-style quote), the effective yearly rate (APY) and the rate per period.

Getting the signs right

The signs are the part that trips most people up. Think of them from your own point of view: cash that leaves your pocket is negative, cash that comes to you is positive. A problem where every amount has the same sign has no answer, because money cannot grow without something going in and something coming out.

Worked examples

Savings (the defaults). Put in 10,000 now and 200 at the end of every month for 120 months at 6% compounded monthly. The future value is 50,969.84: you put in 34,000 (10,000 plus 120 × 200) and earn 16,969.84 of interest, which the calculator shows as negative because it is interest you earn. Switch the timing to BGN and the future value rises to 51,133.72, since each deposit earns one more month.

A home loan payment. On the Payment tab, PV = 200,000, N = 360, I/Y = 6% and FV = 0 give PMT = −1,199.10 a month. Over 30 years you repay 431,676.38, so 231,676.38 is interest.

A car finance payment in Pakistan. A Rs 3,150,000 car loan over 36 months at an example rate of 18% a year gives PMT = −Rs 113,880.05 a month and Rs 949,681.65 of interest. Your bank's rate will differ; ask for it in writing.

The real rate of an offer. A lender offers 20,000 now for 60 payments of 400. On the Interest rate tab, PV = 20,000, PMT = −400 and FV = 0 give 7.420% a year (7.678% effective). Total interest is 4,000.

How long a goal takes. Saving Rs 10,000 a month at 10% to reach Rs 1,000,000 takes N = 73.039 months, about 6 years 1 month.

What a future sum is worth today. With P/Y and C/Y set to 1, 100,000 received in 20 years at 5% a year has a present value of −37,688.95: the amount you would have to invest today.

Finance charge, APR and compounding

The finance charge is the cost of credit in money. Under the US Truth in Lending rules (Regulation Z, section 1026.4) it includes interest and charges such as points, loan fees and appraisal or credit report fees that the creditor requires. For a loan with no fees, the Total interest row is the finance charge: N × PMT plus PV, which is 4,000 in the 20,000 example above.

Compounding matters when P/Y and C/Y differ. The calculator first turns the yearly rate into the equal rate for one payment period. If interest is added twice a year but you pay monthly, set P/Y to 12 and C/Y to 2. A rate compounded more often grows faster, which is why the effective rate (APY) is higher than the quoted yearly rate.

Loan rates and limits in Pakistan

Bank lending rates in Pakistan generally move with the State Bank of Pakistan's policy rate, which the Monetary Policy Committee raised to 12% with effect from 27 October 2026. Many car and home loans are priced as KIBOR plus a margin and reset over time, so the I/Y you enter is only a snapshot; run the calculator again at a higher and lower rate to see the range.

For car finance, the State Bank cut the longest term to 3 years for cars above 1,000cc and 5 years for cars up to 1,000cc in May 2022, and requires at least a 30% down payment. Home finance can run for up to 30 years under its housing finance rules (updated 18 August 2026).

Common mistakes

  • Entering every amount as positive. A loan needs PV and PMT with opposite signs.
  • Mixing years and periods. N counts payments, so a 5-year monthly loan is 60, not 5.
  • Typing the rate per month into I/Y. I/Y is the yearly rate; the calculator divides it for you.
  • Leaving P/Y at 12 for yearly cash flows. For one payment a year, set P/Y and C/Y to 1.
  • Forgetting fees. A loan's true cost includes fees; add them to the amount you repay or compare offers by APR.

How it works

Pick the value you want to find with the tabs, then fill in the other four. Like a financial calculator, every amount has a sign: money you pay out or put in is negative, money you receive is positive. A loan you take is a positive PV with negative payments; savings are a negative PV and negative payments with a positive FV. Under Periods and timing, P/Y is how many payments a year, C/Y how many times a year interest is added, and END or BGN says whether each payment is made at the end or the start of its period. The calculator turns the yearly rate into a rate per payment period, solves the time value of money equation, and lists what happens in every period.

The formula

  • Rate per period i = (1 + I/Y ÷ 100 ÷ C/Y)^(C/Y ÷ P/Y) − 1, which is I/Y ÷ 100 ÷ P/Y when C/Y = P/Y
  • PV × (1 + i)^N + PMT × (1 + i × t) × ((1 + i)^N − 1) ÷ i + FV = 0, with t = 1 for BGN and 0 for END
  • At 0%: PV + PMT × N + FV = 0
  • N = ln((PMT′ − FV × i) ÷ (PMT′ + PV × i)) ÷ ln(1 + i), where PMT′ = PMT × (1 + i × t)
  • I/Y has no closed form: the rate is found by narrowing it down until the equation balances; APR = ((1 + i)^(P/Y ÷ C/Y) − 1) × C/Y and APY = (1 + i)^P/Y − 1
  • Total interest = −(PV + N × PMT + FV)

A worked example

Borrow 200,000 (PV = +200,000) over 360 monthly payments at 6% with nothing left at the end (FV = 0). With P/Y = C/Y = 12, i = 0.06 ÷ 12 = 0.005, so PMT = −200,000 × 1.005^360 ÷ ((1.005^360 − 1) ÷ 0.005) = −1,199.10 a month. The 360 payments add up to 431,676.38, so 231,676.38 of it is interest.

Questions

How do I calculate a loan payment with a finance calculator?

Open the Payment tab, enter the loan as a positive PV, the number of payments as N, the yearly rate as I/Y and 0 as FV. For 200,000 over 360 months at 6%, PMT is −1,199.10; the minus sign means you pay it.

How do I calculate the finance charge on a loan?

Multiply the payment by the number of payments and subtract the amount borrowed, then add any required fees. For 60 payments of 400 on a 20,000 loan that is 24,000 − 20,000 = 4,000. The calculator shows this as Total interest.

How do I calculate the interest rate on a loan?

Use the Interest rate (I/Y) tab with the loan as PV, the payment as a negative PMT, N and FV = 0. The calculator narrows the rate down until the values balance, and shows the yearly rate, the effective rate and the rate per period.

Why are some amounts negative?

The signs show which way the money moves for you. Money you pay out, like a deposit or a loan payment, is negative; money you receive, like a loan you are given or savings you take out, is positive. If PV, PMT and FV all have the same sign, no interest rate can balance them.

Can I use this as a car finance calculator in Pakistan?

Yes. Choose rupees, enter the amount financed (price minus down payment) as PV, the months as N and the bank's yearly rate as I/Y. For the down payment and the loan in one step, the auto loan calculator also works in rupees.

What are P/Y and C/Y?

P/Y is the number of payments a year (12 for monthly) and C/Y the number of times a year interest is added. They are usually the same. When they differ, the yearly rate is first turned into the equal rate for one payment period.

What is the difference between END and BGN?

END means each payment is made at the end of its period, as with most loans. BGN means at the start, as with rent or savings paid in at the start of each month; each payment then earns one more period of interest.

Why can N have decimals?

The number of periods that makes the values balance is rarely a whole number. The schedule then ends with a part period: a part payment and the interest that brings the balance to exactly the future value.

What do APR, APY and rate per period mean in the I/Y answer?

The rate per period is what the balance grows by each period. APR is that rate as a yearly rate compounded C/Y times a year, the way rates are usually quoted. APY is the same growth compounded once a year, so it is the yearly growth you actually get.

Is this the same as the TVM keys on a BA II Plus or HP 12C?

It follows the same five-key method and sign rules, with P/Y, C/Y and BGN/END settings, so you can use it to check homework or exam practice. Rounding of displayed values may differ slightly from a handheld calculator.
