METADATA & PRIVACY · FAQ

Questions about removing metadata

Short answers about privacy, quality, AI labels, Content Credentials and the formats the tools support.

OverviewHow it worksFAQAI image metadataPhoto metadata
What is metadata?

Information stored inside a file next to the content itself: for a photo the camera, the date, the place and the editing app; for a document the author and the company; for a song the title and cover picture. You do not see it when you open the file, but anyone who receives the file can read it.

Are my files uploaded?

No. The tools run in your browser: the page downloads the tool's code, and your files are read and cleaned on your device. You can check in your browser's developer tools (Network tab) that no request carries your file. The only time anything read from a file leaves your device is the optional map in Photo Privacy Check: when you press "Show on a map", the photo's position is sent to OpenStreetMap to draw the map.

Does removing metadata lower the quality?

Not with "Keep quality", the default for pictures: the hidden blocks are cut out and the coded picture is copied byte for byte, so every pixel stays the same. Videos and audio are never re-encoded either. Only "Fresh copy" draws a picture again into a new file.

What is the difference between "Keep quality" and "Fresh copy"?

"Keep quality" removes the metadata blocks and keeps the file's own picture data, so the format and the pixels stay exactly the same. "Fresh copy" decodes the picture and saves a new JPG, PNG or WebP from the pixels alone; use it when a file is damaged or uses a block the first mode cannot cut out.

Will removing metadata hide that a picture was made with AI?

No, and it is not meant to. It removes hidden text such as prompts, settings, generator names and Content Credentials, which is useful for privacy. Visible labels such as "AI info" or "Made with AI" and watermarks drawn into the pixels stay, and platforms may still recognise AI-made pictures by other means.

Do I still have to label AI-made content after cleaning it?

Yes, where a law or a platform rule asks for it. Removing metadata only removes hidden data from your copy of the file; it does not change what you have to disclose when you publish realistic AI-made or AI-edited pictures, videos or audio.

What are Content Credentials (C2PA)?

A signed record inside a file that states which app made or changed it and how, for example "created with a generative AI model" or "cropped in an editor". The C2PA Checker shows what such a record states; it does not verify the signature.

Can I remove the GPS location from a video?

Yes. The Video Metadata Cleaner removes the location that phones store in MP4 and MOV files, the camera make and model, dates, titles and tags, and the GPS and sensor tracks some action cameras record, without re-encoding the video.

Do WhatsApp, Instagram or Facebook remove metadata?

Many apps remove most of it when they send or post a photo, but not always all of it, and files sent as documents, by email or through cloud links usually keep everything. If it matters, clean the file yourself before you send it.

Which formats are supported?

Pictures: JPG, PNG, WebP, HEIC and AVIF. Videos: MP4, MOV, M4V, WebM and MKV. Documents: DOCX, XLSX, PPTX (and their macro versions) and PDF. Audio: MP3, M4A and FLAC.

Why do my photos stay upright after cleaning?

By default one tiny tag that holds only the orientation is kept, because many phones store a photo sideways and rely on that tag to show it upright. It contains no personal data. You can untick it to remove it too.

How can I check that nothing else changed?

Open the original and the cleaned copy in Image Compare. It compares every pixel and lists every metadata field that differs. You can also run the cleaned file through Photo Privacy Check to see that nothing is left.

Is it free, and are there limits?

Everything is free with no sign-up. The limits come from the browser: up to 50 pictures and 300 MB at a time, videos up to 2 GB, documents up to 100 MB and audio up to 500 MB.

Does it work on a phone?

Yes, in current Chrome, Safari, Edge and Firefox on Android, iPhone, Windows and Mac. Large videos are faster on a computer.
